  2) Parts of microscope
  (i)Eyepiece:

An eyepiece is that part of an optical system, which is directed to
the viewer. It is a construction of at least one or more lenses. The
function of the eyepiece in a microscope is to convert the real-
enlarged-intermediate-image from the objective into an enlarged-
virtual-image. It contains the ocular lens, which the user looks
through to see the magnified specimen. The ocular lens has a
magnification that can range from 5x to 30x, but 10x or 15x is
the most common setting.

  (ii)      Objective lenses:

    It   combines    with     the   eyepiece   lens   to   increase
     magnification levels. Microscopes generally feature three
     or four objective lenses, with magnification levels ranging
     4x to 100x.

  • Objective revolvers are used in microscopes with multiple
     objective lenses that have different magnification factors.

  • An objective (lens) is that part of an optical system, which is
     directed to the object. It´s task is to collect the light rays
     that are reflected from the observed item. The objective
     generates a real-optical image.

     Long

• This can refer to multiple layers of different material, including

material. In this type of filtration, a mixture of liquid and insoluble

Cold filtration:
• Cold filtration makes use of very low temperatures, often by
using an ice bath.
Some substances, such as fatty acid particles, become suspended
in the mixture as they cool down, which then allows us to filter
them out more easily.
Hot filtration:
• This is often used for crystalline compounds that contain
impurities. The way this filtration is done is by melting down the
crystalline compound, removing the impurities as the substance is
still in liquid form, and finally recrystallizing the now pure
substance. Often, it is recommended that the apparatus used in
this filtration be heated up so that the filtered substance doesn’t
crystallize in the funnel and block the flow.

  5) Advantages of SEM?
• The scanning electron microscope has many advantages over
traditional microscopes.

• The SEM has a large depth of field, which allows more of a
specimen to be in focus at one time.
• The SEM also has much higher resolution, so closely spaced
specimens can be magnified at much higher levels.
• Because the SEM uses electromagnets rather than lenses, the
researcher has much more control in the degree of magnification.
• Provides strikingly clear images, make the scanning electron
microscope one of the most useful instruments in research today.
  6) Cationic and anionic exchangers?

Cationic exchangers:

• Cationic exchangers possess negatively charged group, and
these will attract positively charged cations. These exchangers
are also called “Acidic ion exchange” materials, because their
negative charges result from the ionization of acidic group.

Anionic exchangers:
Anionic exchangers have positively charged groups that will
attract negatively charged anions. These are also called “Basic
ion exchange” materials. Ion exchange chromatography is most
often performed in the form of column chromatography. However,
there are also thin layer chromatographic methods that work
basically based on the principle of ion exchange.
