   1- DNA Damage:
1. DNA can be damaged by oxidizing agents, alkylating agents and high-energy electromagnetic radiation
such as ultraviolet light and X-rays.
2. DNA is damaged due to excessive levels of Reactive Oxygen Species. Normal level of ROS may be
necessary for memory and learning.
3. DNA damaged is related to cancer and other diseases.

   2- Electrochemical properties of DNA bases
All four bases of DNA can be oxidized and produce their oxidation signals at highly positive potentials on carbon electrodes.
The one electron reduction potentials of DNA nucleosides are

1. Guanine 1.29

2. Adenine 1.42

3. Cytosine 1.6

4. Thymine 1.7

   3- Drawbacks of Label Free Electrochemical Detection

1. It requires high potential to detect the Guanine oxidation.

2. High background signals.

3. Non-specific adsorption of DNA target containing Guanine bases.

   4- Features of Glassy Carbon (Allotropic form of Carbon)
1. High-purity

2. Excellent Stability as high as at 3,000 °C in vacuum

3. Stable at 500 °C in the air

4. Inert against Chemical erosion

5. Impermeability to Gas and Solution

6. Significant Hardness/ Strength

7. Favorable electric conducting property

8. Highly Resistant against Inorganic and Organic salts

9. Nonporous

10. Good Bio-Compatibility

   5- Electrochemical Detection HIV target DNA
1. A single-stranded PNA molecule is non-covalently linked the graphene surface with a pyrene linker group.

2. The complementary DNA-strand binds with HIV target DNA.

3.2D grephene with excellent electronic properties help to detect this hybridization event at a very low noise level.

Advantages:
The changes in oxidation and reduction (with and without hybridization) are bases of the detection.
Label-free electrochemical DNA hybridization method is

1. Simple

2. Rapid

3. Highly sensitivity

4. Reproducibility
5. Long-term stable.

   6- Floropore and Quencher
1. Fluorophore is attached at one end of one arm and Quencher is attached at the end of other arm.
2. Quenching is a process which decreases the fluorescence intensity of a substance.
3. When the fluorophore and quencher are in close proximity, the quencher absorbs the energy emitted from an excited
fluorophore, thereby suppressing its emission.

Designing and principle:

Molecular beacons do not fluoresce in their free form.

In the absence of targets, the probe is dark, because the stem places the fluorophore close to the non-fluorescent quencher.

Quencher and fluoropore transiently share electrons, eliminating the ability of the fluorophore to fluoresce.
Example of Quencher Cl, Acrylamide, NaI.

Working

1. When the probe encounters a target molecule, it forms a probe-target hybrid.

2. The hybrid is longer and more stable than the stem hybrid.

3. After hybridization the quencher moved away from the fluorophore.

4. As the quencher is away from the fluorophore, the fluorescence of the fluorophore is the restored.

   7- Hairpin DNA probe (HDP)
Hairpin DNA probes (HDPs) are specially designed ssDNA and have excellent sensing specificity. HDP is immobilized on the
electrode surface.
1. Hairpin DNA probe does not bind with the enzyme probe until it hybridizes with the target molecule.

2. Binding of target molecule with the DNA leads to the generation of signals by the enzyme.

   8- Lab On A chip (LOC)
1. A lab-on-a-chip is a device that can perform the functions of a laboratory on a single chip of only millimeters to a few
square centimeters in size.

2. LOCs deal with the handling of extremely small liquid volumes up to Pico liters.

3. Lab-on-a-chip devices are a subset of micro-electro-mechanical systems (MEMS) devices.

Development of LOC technology

1. The development of LOC technology is related to human genome sequence project, where fast running technologies were
established to sequence the whole human genome.

2. Later this approach was used to establish LOC by Defense Advanced Research Project Agency (DARPA) of USA for military
applications.

Components of LOC technology

For understanding the LOC technology it is require studying the following in detail.
1. Materials for chip

2. Lithography

3. Capillary Electrophoresis

4. Microfluidics

5. Introduction of sample/Reagent onto LOC

6. Mixing of Reagents in LOC

7. Electronic circuits

Advantages:
1. Required less sample volumes for diagnostics

2. Low fluid volumes consumption

3. Less waste

4. Lower reagents costs

5. Fast response

6. Fast Analysis

7. Small heat capacity

8. Fast heating

   9- Reynolds Number:

1. Reynolds number is a dimension less quantity.

2. Reynolds number compares the effect of the momentum of a fluid to the effect of viscosity.

3. The Reynolds number is the ratio of inertial forces to viscous forces.

4. It can predict if a flow condition will be laminar or turbulent.

Reynolds Number = Inertial Force / Viscous Force
V = velocity of the fluid
μ = viscosity of fluid
L = length or diameter of the fluid.

   10-Photoresist Types
A photoresistor is a type of resistor whose resistance decreases when the intensity of light increases.
    Positive photoresisters:
On the exposure of light positive photo resister are dissolved.
    Negative photo resisters:
These photoresistor are not dissolved on exposure to light.

Example: Epoxy based polymers.

   11-Etching
Etching in micro-fabrication is the removal of unwanted photoresister from the wafer. There are two methods of etching.
   1- Wet Etching
Hydroflouric acid is used to bath the treated silicon wafers for wet etching.
   2- Plasma Etching
Standard conditions for such as pressure under vacuume is created for etching.

   12-Freeze drying

This technique is also known as lyophilization. It involves the removal of water molecules from the sample by
1. Freezing the sample

2. Lowering the pressure creating a vacuum.
