Q. No. 2: What is the significance of using 10% buffered formalin solution for
preservation?

Significance of 10% Buffered Formalin Solution for Preservation:

   1. Tissue Morphology: Preserves cellular structure by cross-linking proteins, ensuring the integrity of the
      specimen.
   2. Prevents Decay: Stops enzymatic activity and microbial growth, preventing decomposition.
   3. Buffered Stability: Maintains a neutral pH (~7.0), avoiding tissue shrinkage or distortion caused by
      acidity.
   4. Processing Compatibility: Suitable for histological procedures like staining and microscopy.
   5. Long-Term Use: Ideal for prolonged preservation without altering tissue properties.

Buffered formalin is a reliable and widely used fixative for maintaining biological specimens.

Q. No. 3: How would you determine meristic counts and fish body ratios of fish for data
sheet? Sketch and paste diagram.

To determine meristic counts and fish body ratios of fish for a data sheet, follow these steps:

1. Determining Meristic Counts

Meristic counts are discrete, countable features of fish, such as:

   •   Fin rays (e.g., dorsal, pectoral, pelvic, anal, and caudal fins).
   •   Scales (e.g., lateral line scales, scales above/below lateral line).
   •   Gill rakers on the gill arch.
   •   Vertebrae (requires skeletal preparation or imaging).

Procedure:

   1. Place the fish on a flat surface or dissect under a microscope.
   2. Use a magnifying lens to count:
          o Number of fin rays in each fin.
          o Number of scales along the lateral line and above/below it.
          o Gill rakers on the first gill arch.
   3. Record the counts in a table or data sheet for compariso

2. Determining Fish Body Ratios

Body ratios are relative proportions of various body parts, such as:

   •   Total length (TL): Distance from snout tip to caudal fin end.
   •   Standard length (SL): Distance from snout tip to caudal peduncle.
   •   Head length (HL): Distance from snout tip to gill cover.
   •   Body depth (BD): Maximum vertical distance of the body.

   •   Eye diameter (ED): Horizontal length of the eye.

Procedure:

   1. Use a measuring scale or digital calipers for accurate measurements.
   2. Calculate ratios, e.g.:
         o SL/TL = Standard length divided by total length.
         o HL/SL = Head length divided by standard length.
   3. Record ratios in the data sheet.

Q. No. 1: What is the significance of fish museums? (2 marks)

Significance of Fish Museums:

   1. Biodiversity Documentation: Preserve fish species for studying diversity, distribution, and
      conservation.
   2. Research and Education: Aid in taxonomy, genetics, and ecological studies; provide educational
      resources.
   3. Species Identification: Serve as reference collections for identifying new or rare species.
   4. Conservation Efforts: Help monitor endangered species and support conservation programs.
   5. Historical Records: Maintain specimens as a historical record of fish populations over time.

Q. No. 2: What are the types of scales present in fish? Give examples (2 marks)

Types of Scales in Fish:

   1. Placoid Scales: Found in cartilaginous fish, these are tooth-like structures with enamel and dentine.
         o Example: Sharks and Rays.
   2. Cosmoid Scales: Thick and hard, made of cosmine, a dentine-like substance.
         o Example: Extinct lungfish and coelacanths.
   3. Ganoid Scales: Rhomboid-shaped scales covered with ganoine, a shiny, enamel-like substance.
         o Example: Gars and Sturgeons.
   4. Cycloid Scales: Thin, smooth-edged, and circular scales found in soft-finned bony fish.
         o Example: Salmon and Carp.
   5. Ctenoid Scales: Similar to cycloid but with tiny comb-like projections on the edges.
         o Example: Perch and Tilapia.
