                       WHAT IS LANGUAGE?

Topic 001: Introduction

What is linguistics?
Answer: Linguistics is the scientific study of language.
What are some purposes of language?
Answer: Language is used to express thoughts, emotions, communicate with
others, fulfill needs, and maintain culture.
How can language be defined?
Answer: Language can be defined as a verbal, physical, biologically innate form
of communication.
What is the challenge in defining "language"?
Answer: Defining "language" is challenging due to its varied translations in
different languages.
What is linguistics, and how is language defined in the context of this field?
A1: Linguistics is the scientific study of language, encompassing its various
aspects and functions. Language can be understood as a complex tool used to
express inner thoughts, emotions, abstract ideas, and to communicate with others.
It serves multiple purposes, including fulfilling needs, maintaining culture, and

Topic 002: Language: A Purely Human and Non-instinctive Communication

What does Sapir's definition say about language?
Answer: Sapir defines language as a purely human, non-instinctive way of
communicating ideas, emotions, and desires using symbols.
How is "idea" in Sapir's definition problematic?
Answer: The term "idea" is vague and imprecise in Sapir's definition.
What does "non-instinctive" mean in Sapir's definition?
Answer: "Non-instinctive" means language is not inherited but learned.
Why does Sapir exclude cries and groans from his definition?
Answer: Sapir excludes cries and groans because they are involuntarily produced,
unlike voluntary symbols.
According to Sapir, what is the essence of language, and how does his
definition of language face certain limitations?
Sapir's definition of language posits that it is a uniquely human and non-
instinctive method of conveying ideas, emotions, and desires through voluntary
symbols. However, this definition is not without flaws. The terms "idea,"
"emotion," and "desire" are rather broad and may not fully encompass the range of
communication accomplished through language. The concept of "idea" especially
lacks precise definition. "Purely human" signifies that language is exclusive to
humans and distinct from animal communication. While animals do have
communication systems, they differ significantly from human language. "Non-
instinctive" denotes that language is not inherited but learned, as opposed to being
passed down genetically. Additionally, "voluntarily produced" excludes
involuntary vocalizations like cries or groans from the scope of language in this
definition.

Topic 003: Language: A Symbol System

What constitutes a language system?
Answer: A language system includes symbols and rules for communication, such
as speech sounds, grammar, and pragmatics.
What differentiates language from communication?
Answer: Language is a symbolic form of communication, while communication
encompasses various forms like scent, gesture, and tone.
What is the significance of each language's encoding?
Answer: Every language encodes human experience uniquely, and the loss of a
language results in the loss of a way of thinking.
How can a language be defined as a system, and what distinguishes language
from other forms of communication?
A language can be characterized as a system of arbitrary vocal symbols that
enable communication within a social group. This system comprises symbols and
rules that encompass speech sounds, writing symbols, grammar rules (e.g.,
pronouns, tense), parsing, and pragmatics. An essential aspect is the arbitrary
connection between linguistic symbols and their meanings. Language provides a
context for understanding symbols. While language is a type of communication,
it's important to differentiate between the two. Communication involves sharing
information using various mediums like scent, song, gesture, tone, writing,
painting, or language. Language specifically refers to symbolic communication.

Topic 004: Behavioristic View of Language

How does Hall define language from a behavioristic perspective?
Answer: Hall defines language as the institution through which humans
communicate using habitually used oral-auditory symbols.
What is the influence of behaviorism on Hall's definition?
Answer: Behaviorism influenced Hall's use of "habitually used" symbols in the
context of language.
How does the behavioristic view of language, as introduced by Hall, define
language, and what are the historical influences on this perspective?
Hall's definition describes language as the medium through which humans interact
and communicate by employing habitually used oral-auditory arbitrary symbols.
