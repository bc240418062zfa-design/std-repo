Short Questions
1. Enlist two approaches of family domain

   1. Family members approach (father, mother, child, tutor, etc.)
   2. Dyadic approach (father→mother, mother→child, grandfather→father, etc.)

2. Differentiate between additive and subtractive bilingualism

      Additive bilingualism: Both languages are encouraged equally; provides cognitive
       advantages.
      Subtractive bilingualism: One language is promoted at the expense of the other; may
       lead to cognitive disadvantages.

3. Five domains of family

Common family-domain participants:

      Father
      Mother
      Child
      Governess
      Tutor

4. How to investigate the family domain in terms of speaker?

Investigate through:

      Family-member approach (individual speakers)
      Dyadic approach (speaker-hearer pairs/role relations)

5. What is meant by Indexicality in linguistics?

Indexicality refers to the social meanings conveyed by language forms, linking speech to social
identity, group membership, status, or context.

6. Difference between balanced bilingualism and dominant bilingualism

      Balanced bilingualism: Equal proficiency in both languages.
      Dominant bilingualism: Greater proficiency in one language.

7. Enlist domain levels of socio-psychological analysis

      Setting
      Situation

Bilinguality is the psychological state of an individual who has access to more than one linguistic

      Formal level (surface linguistic forms)

15. Define Relative Competence

Relative competence refers to proficiency in both languages, either balanced or dominant.

16. Define Institutional competence with reference to bilingualism

Institutional competence is the ability to use languages appropriately within institutional domains
such as school, government, courts, and administration.

17. Define language contact

Language contact occurs when speakers of different languages interact and communicate with
one another.

18. Three time periods of sociolinguistics in bilingualism

      Childhood bilingualism
      Adolescent bilingualism
      Adult bilingualism

19. Swain’s common storage model

Swain proposed that bilinguals possess separate language stores linked to a common conceptual
store.

20. How language is stored in a bilingual brain?

Languages are stored in separate language systems connected to a common conceptual store
through translation and association links.

21. Bilingual competitive job market

Bilingualism increases employability by providing access to a wider range of jobs and
international opportunities.

Long Questions (5 Marks)
1. Five Domains of Family

The family domain is a crucial area of language behavior because multilingualism often begins
in the family. Researchers analyze language use among family members such as father, mother,
child, tutor, and governess. Family interactions strongly influence language maintenance and
language shift. The family domain provides encouragement and protection for multilingual
development.

2. How to Investigate the Family Domain in Terms of Speaker?

Researchers use two approaches:

   1. Family-member approach (father, mother, child, tutor, etc.).
   2. Dyadic approach, examining speaker-hearer relations such as father-to-mother, mother-
      to-child, grandfather-to-father, etc.
      The second approach is more comprehensive because it studies role relations and
      communication patterns.

3. Problems Researchers Face Due to Their Identity While Researching
Language Shifting

Researchers may face:

      Bias due to social identity.
      Difficulty gaining trust.
      Influence of cultural background.
      Problems interpreting language choices objectively.
      Challenges in maintaining neutrality during data collection.

4 & 7. Multilingualism as an Important Resource at Personal and Societal Levels

At the societal level, multilingualism promotes national unity while preserving ethnic languages.
