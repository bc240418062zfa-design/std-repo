    1. What is E-Business?
The term electronic Business or e-business referred to the electronic exchange of information / data or documents both within the organization and with external resources to support business process. E-Business is an umbrella activity of moving from physical file storage to computer storage with the purpose of making business process more efficient. With efficiency comes improved business performance due to low cost of managing business processes. E-business includes vendor communication, order processing, e-procurement, supply chain and other business areas. Interestingly Online Transfer of Payments is part of E-Business also.
The electronic storage of data not only helped in improving the volume/speed of data storage but it also improved the pace of processing and sharing information with the relevant stakeholders ultimately improving business decision making.

    2. What is E-Commerce?
E-commerce is the activity focusing specifically on online buying and selling of the products. Being narrower in scope, e-commerce is more concentrated towards buying and selling of the products instead of business processes. Some E-commerce experts also include pre and post sales activities in the scope of e-commerce as well. E-commerce includes transaction among Business-to-Business (B2B), Business-to-Consumer (B2C) and Consumer-to-Consumer (C2C)
 This is how e-commerce works:

    1. A customer tries to order online and his browser communicate with the store web server
    2. Web server sends the order to the order manager
    3. The order manager forward the query to database to check the availability of the product
    4. If the item is not in stock, customer is informed and system may go ahead to order supplies
    5. The database confirms that the product is available
    6. Order manager communicates with merchant system to take payment
    7. Merchant system communicates with bank system for payment processing
    8. Bank system confirm that the customers has enough funds to buy the product
    9. The merchant system gives a go ahead to the transaction
    10. The order manager confirm the success of the transaction
    11. The web server shows the customer that the order has been processed
    12. The order manager send request to warehouse to dispatch product
    13. Delivery vehicle collects the product from warehouse and deliver
    14. The warehouse system sends and email to customer to tell that the product is on the way
    15. The goods are delivered to customer

    3. Difference between E-Commerce and E-Business
These two terms are used interchangeably but in real these are total opposite of each other. E-Business is superset and E-Commerce is subset from functionality viewpoint. Make it simpler, e-business is wider in scope and its focus in on all business processes conducted online. On the other side, e-commerce is narrower in scope and its main focus is buying and selling only. For example, email marketing to existing and prospective customer is E-Business; technology here is being used as an enabler while growth in revenue totally via Internet is E-Commerce (like Alibaba which uses email marketing too).

E-commerce today is the buzz word in the business and offers very promising benefits in terms of business growth, profit maximization, business reach and reaching international boundaries.     5. Sell-Side E-Commerce: Transactional E-Commerce
The main focus of the sell side e-commerce website is the enable sale of product, either offline or online. Another major intent is to provide information related to the product(s) to the customers but the main business contribution of such sites is through sale of the product. Few examples would be:
    1. www.amazon.com
    2. www.mercedes-benz.com/en/
Amazon is a perfect example of sell side transactional e-commerce that provide customers information related to the products and at the same time it enables them to purchase the product they want.
    6. Sell – Side E-Commerce: Service Oriented Relationship Building Websites
Another type of sell side e-commerce websites are service oriented and relationship building websites. Their major purpose is to provide information to all the customers in order to promote the business. The information is disseminated usually through newsletter and emails. Leads are generated first through these websites followed by sales. These websites are also very effective in identifying potential customers. Website of EY, Ernst & Young (www.ey.com), is a perfect example. EY is the world’s 3rd most largest and successful accounting firm in the world. They don’t sell anything online but they generate potential customer by showcasing their success.
