Creating Fixed-Choice Test Items: Matching Exercises

Matching test items are also selection items specially used to measure a student's ability to identify the relationship
between a set of similar items, each of which has two components.

Advantages Matching Exercises

       Ease of construction
       Measure a large amount of related factual material in a relatively short time

Limitations of Matching Exercises

       Restricted to the measurement of factual information
       Highly susceptible to the presence of irrelevant clues
       Difficulty of findings homogenous material

                                                    Lecture: 24

Suggestions for Constructing Matching Exercises

       Use only homogeneous material in the single matching exercise
       Include an unequal number of responses and premises
       Arrange the list of responses in logical order
       Indicate the directions the basis for matching the responses

Forms of Supply Type

       Short answers
       Completion items

They only differ in form of presentation. Short answers are presented in the form of direct questions while completion
items are incomplete statements.

Both of them can be answered with a word, phrase, number, or symbol.

                                                    Lecture: 26

Advantages of Short Answer/Completion Questions

       Teacher wants to control the possibility of guessing
       Teacher wants students to complete a large number of items in a fairly short time
       The possibility of guessing the correct answers to these questions is greatly reduced when compared with true-
        false questions

Disadvantages

       Difficult to frame questions for one specific answer
       Not usable to measure complex learning outcomes

Constructed Response Test Items

Subjective test items (essay questions) are constructed response type questions that can be the best way to measure the
students' higher order thinking skills. Such as applying, organizing, synthesizing, integrating, evaluating, or projecting.

Essay type questions are divided into two types

    1. Restricted Response Items
    2. Extended Response Items

Examples:

        Analysis of relationship.
        Compare and contrast positions.
        Explain cause-effect relationship

Guidelines for Constructing Restricted Response Essay Type Items

        Prepare a tentative scoring key in advance
        Do not employ optional questions
        Verify a question‘s quality by writing a trial response to the question
        Evaluate essay responses anonymously
        Specify the value and an approximate time limit for each question

                                                      Lecture: 29

Advantages of essay type items (Extended response questions)

        Effective for assessing higher order abilities
        Comparatively less time consuming
        Emphasizes essential communications skills

Scoring Rubrics provide two benefits

        They support the examination to the extent to which the specified criteria has been reached.
        They provide feedback to students concerning how to improve their performance

Types of Scoring Rubrics

        The analytic (scoring key, point or rating) method
          The analytic scoring requires developing a list of major elements that students are expected to include in an
            ideal answer of a question
          Decide the number of points awarded to students when they include each element
          When assigning number to each element, be sure the total points should match with the essay‘s total value
        The holistic (also called global, sorting or rating) method
          The holistic scoring rubric requires making a judgment about the overall quality of each student‘s response
            to an item.
          No need to mark each specific content element that a student included in the answer.

Item Analysis

It reveals if an item is:

       Too easy
       Too difficult
       Failing to show a difference between skilled and unskilled examinees

The two most common statistics reported in Item Analysis

    1. Item difficulty (which is a measure of the proportion of examinees who responded to an item correctly)
    2. Item discrimination (which is a measure of how well the item discriminates between examinees who are
       knowledgeable in the content area and those who are not).

Distractor analysis - provides a measure of how well each of the incorrect options contributes to the quality of a
multiple choice item.

Appropriate Time for Item Analysis

    1. After administration, but before scoring – it helps the test developers identify errors in the scoring key or defects
