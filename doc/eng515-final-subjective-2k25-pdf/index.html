What are countable and uncountable nouns?
Countable nouns
1. Countable nouns can be singular or plural.

2. A singular countable noun must have a, or the, or a word like this, my, each,
every, Fred’s in front of it.

3. Many, several, both, a few, these, those, two, three, etc. are only used in front of
plural countable nouns.
4. Each, every, a, another, one is only used in front of singular countable nouns.

5. People is a plural countable noun.
Uncountable nouns
1. Uncountable nouns cannot be plural.
2. Sometimes an uncountable noun does not need the, this, etc. in front of it.

3. Much is only used in front of uncountable nouns.

Difference between coherent and cohesive?
Coherent

   o Refers to the overall meaning and logic of the text
   o Concerned with the organization and structure of ideas
   o Focuses on the content and message
   o Ensures that the text makes sense and flows logically
Cohesive

   o Refers to the linguistic connections between words, phrases, and sentences
   o Concerned with the use of language to link ideas together
   o Focuses on the form and syntax
   o Ensures that the text is grammatically correct and flows smoothly

A situational syllabus is an approach to language teaching that is based on real-

learners how to use language in meaningful ways that reflect authentic, everyday
