i. Literal Anthropomorphism: the simplest form of anthropomorphism is when

Interphase
It occupies about 90% of total cell cycle.
                                              A R

                                   F D

ii. S (synthesis) phase: in this period, an exact copy (replica or duplicate) of double-stranded

                             S
chromatid. (A chromatid is a copy of a chromosome produced by replication).
Gap phase 2 (G2): in this period, chromosomes begin condensation; cell begins to assemble
the structures that it will later use to move the chromosomes to opposite poles (ends) of the cell.

Checkpoints
There are three checkpoints in the cell cycle:
i. The first checkpoint (G1): it determines whether all conditions are favorable for cell
division to proceed otherwise cell doesn’t enter S phase
ii. The second checkpoint (G2): ensures that all chromosomes and DNA has been
replicated properly otherwise bars the cell entry to the mitotic phase.
iii. The final checkpoint (M): occurs in the middle of mitosis. It ensures that all copied
chromosomes are arranged appropriately to be separated to opposite sides of the cell.
DNA: The Genetic Material
A molecule that serves as the genetic material must have certain characteristics to explain the
properties of life:
i. Able to code for the sequence of amino acids in proteins and control protein synthesis.

ii. able to replicate itself prior to cell division.
iii. must be in the nuclei of eukaryotic cells.
iv. able to change over time to account for evolutionary change
Only one molecule, DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid), fulfills all of these requirements.

Helicase enzyme unwinds the DNA by breaking the hydrogen bonds between the
nitrogenous base pairs.
Replication forks are formed at each replication origin as the DNA unwinds.
Topoisomerases remove the over-winding or supercoiling produced during unwinding.
RNA Primers are formed by the enzyme primase.
