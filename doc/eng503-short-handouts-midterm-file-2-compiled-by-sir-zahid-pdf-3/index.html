                 PSYCHOLOGY OF LANGUAGE

Topic 029: Introduction to Psycholinguistics
What is Psycholinguistics?
Psycholinguistics explores the relationship between the human mind and
language, treating language users as individuals and examining how mental
processes influence linguistic performance.
What are the objectives of Psycholinguistics?
Psycholinguistics aims to understand the language process, study linguistic
behavior patterns, and draw from related areas like neurolinguistics, phonetics,
and semantics.
What is codification in Psycholinguistics?

What is the field of Psycholinguistics, and what does it explore?
Psycholinguistics is the field that explores the relationship between the human
mind and language. It treats the language user as an individual, emphasizing how
linguistic performance is determined by mental processes.
Topic 030: Language/Speech Production
Describe the Model of Language Development by Levelt.
Levelt's Model of Language Development consists of four stages:
conceptualization, formulation, articulation, and self-monitoring.
What happens during the "Formulation" stage in language production?
During formulation, the process gets closer to the final output, focusing on word
choice and sentence structure decisions.
Describe the Model of Language Development proposed by Levelt in 1989.
Levelt's model views language production as a linear progression of four stages:
Conceptualization - The stage where the initial idea for speech originates, though
the details remain vague.
Formulation - The phase where thoughts become more precise and lead to the
choice of words and sentence structures.
Articulation - The stage where the linguistic plan is executed by the speech
system's muscles, producing sounds.
Self-monitoring - The final stage involves self-correction and self-repair in speech
production.
Topic 031: Lexical Processing
What is the purpose of visual word recognition in lexical processing?
Visual word recognition is fundamental for constructing meaning from text, as it
involves recognizing words and accessing their meanings.

What are content words in lexical processing?
Content words, such as nouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs, carry dictionary
meanings and are essential for understanding text.
What are the three areas of study within lexical processing?
Lexical processing research falls into three areas:
Lexical Entries - Information stored about lexical items in the mind.
Lexical Storage - How lexical items are stored in relation to each other.
Lexical Access - The process enabling the retrieval of lexical items.
Topic 032: Text/Discourse Comprehension
What are the two levels of discourse structure discussed in the context of
comprehension?
The two levels are local discourse structure (microstructure) and global discourse
structure (macrostructure), both contributing to text coherence.
How do linguistic cues influence discourse comprehension?
Linguistic cues, like connectives and pronoun referents, guide the reader's
understanding and the construction of coherent mental representations.
How does Discourse Context and Linguistic Cues influence Discourse
Comprehension?
Successful Discourse Comprehension involves making inferences to connect ideas
within and across discourse contexts. Linguistic cues, like connectives and
pronouns, guide the construction of memory for discourse, helping in coherence
and comprehension.
Topic 033: Language Development
At what age do infants start cooing and laughing?
Infants start cooing around 6 weeks of age and laughing at around 16 weeks.

Topic- 034: Introduction to Second Language Acquisition (SLA)
What is a second language, and what does it encompass?
A second language is any language other than the learner's native language or
mother tongue, encompassing both languages of wider communication and
foreign languages.
What is Second Language Acquisition (SLA) in general terms?
SLA in general terms refers to the process of learning a second language.
What does SLA specifically include, and how is it different from formal
education?
SLA includes the theory of the process by which we acquire a second language,
primarily a subconscious process during communication. It differs from formal
education, which involves more conscious language learning processes.
What are second languages often referred to as, and what is their aim or goal?
Second languages are also called target languages (TL), which are any languages
aimed or learned.
