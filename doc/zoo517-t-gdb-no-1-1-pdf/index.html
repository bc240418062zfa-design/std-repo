Question: 1
Discuss the digestive system in different invertebrates?

Solution:
Introduction:
Invertebrates are animals without a backbone.

Their digestive systems vary depending on their structure and way of life.

Some have no digestive organs, while others have well-developed systems.

Digestion can be inside cells (intracellular) or in a body cavity (extracellular).

1.Porifera (Sponges)
Type: No true digestive system

System: Simple; no mouth or stomach

Process: Water flows through pores; special cells (choanocytes) trap and digest food inside the cells
(intracellular digestion).

2.Cnidaria (Hydra, Jellyfish)
Type: Incomplete digestive system (one opening)

System: Gastrovascular cavity

Process: Food enters and exits through the same opening. It is digested in the cavity (extracellular), and
nutrients are absorbed by body cells.

3.Platyhelminthes (Flatworms)
Type: Incomplete digestive system

System: Branched gut with one opening

Process: Food enters and exits from one opening. Some parasitic species absorb nutrients directly from
the host, as they lack a digestive system.

4.Nematoda (Roundworms)
Type: Complete digestive system

System: Tube-like structure with mouth and anus

Process: Food enters through the mouth, is digested in the gut, nutrients are absorbed, and waste exits
through the anus.

5.Annelida (Earthworms)
Type: Complete and organized digestive system

System: Mouth → Pharynx → Esophagus → Crop → Gizzard → Intestine → Anus

Process: Food is swallowed, stored, ground, digested, and absorbed in order. Waste is passed out from
the anus.

6.Arthropoda (Insects, Crabs, Spiders)
Type: Complete and specialized system

Systems: Foregut, Midgut, Hindgut

Process: Food is chewed, partially digested in the foregut, digested and absorbed in the midgut, and
waste is removed through the hindgut.

7.Mollusca (Snails, Octopus, Clams)
Type: Complete digestive system

System: Includes mouth, stomach, intestine, and digestive glands

Process: A radula scrapes food (in some), digestion occurs in the stomach and intestine, and nutrients
are absorbed.

8.Echinodermata (Starfish, Sea Urchins)
Type: Complete but unique system

System: Mouth, stomach (extendable in starfish), intestine, anus

Process: Starfish push their stomachs out to digest food outside their bodies; sea urchins chew food with
Aristotle’s lantern.

Conclusion:
Invertebrate digestive systems range from very simple (like sponges) to advanced (like arthropods).

Some use cells for digestion, while others have complete systems with separate mouth and anus.

Each type of digestive system fits the animal’s feeding method and environment.

This variety shows how invertebrates adapt to survive in different habitats.
