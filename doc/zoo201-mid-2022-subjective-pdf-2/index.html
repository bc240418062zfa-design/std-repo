Q 2. Characteristics of nematoda?

Ans:-
Characteristics of Nematoda:
•Their body is bilaterally symmetrical and triploblastic.
•They are cylindrical in shape.

•They exhibit tissue level organization.
•Their body has a cavity or pseudocoelom.
•The alimentary canal is distinct, with the mouth and the anus.

•They are sexually dimorphic.

Q. 3 Oligochaetes?
Ans:-

Oligochaeta is a subclass of animals in the phylum Annelida, which is made up of many types
of aquatic and terrestrial worms, including all of the various earthworms.

Q1. What is Cnidocytes?

Ans:-
A cnidocyte is an explosive cell containing one giant secretory organelle called a cnidocyst
that can deliver a sting to other organisms. The presence of this cell defines the phylum
Cnidaria.

Q 2. Earthworm?
Ans:-
An earthworm is a terrestrial invertebrate that belongs to the phylum Annelida. They exhibit
a tube-within-a-tube body plan, are externally segmented with corresponding internal
segmentation, and usually have setae on all segments. … Earthworms are commonly found in
soil, eating a wide variety of organic matter.

Q 3. Three major clades of deutrostomes:-

Ans:-
The three major clades of deuterostomes are the echinoderms, the hemichordates, and the
chordates.

Q 4. Life cycle of Apicomplexans?

The apicomplexa have complex life cycles that are characterized by three distinct processes:
sporogony, merogony and gametogony.

Q 1 characteristics of Annelids?
Ans:-

Characteristics of Annelida
1.The Annelids are coelomate and triploblastic.
2.They exhibit organ system level organization.
3.Their body is segmented.

4.They respire through their body surface.

Q 2. Define siphon?
Ans:-

Definition:-
The tubular organ through which water is ejected from the gill cavity of a cephaloid.

Q 3. Circulatory system of cephalopod?
Ans:-
The core of the cephalopod circulatory system is a series of three beating hearts. This trio of
hearts connects to a high pressure system of veins, arteries, and capillaries – unique among
all mollusks. These are almost like the parts of the human heart that pump blood to our
lungs.

Q 4. Monoecious and dioecious?
Ans:-
Monoecious:-
Monoecious organisms are those containing both male and female reproductive organs.

Dioecious:-
These are organisms that produce either male or female reproductive organs and gametes
but never both at the same time.

Q 1. Define pseudocoelomates with example?
Answer:-
Definition:-
A pseudocoelomate is an organism with body cavity that is not derived from the mesoderm,
as in a true coelom, or body cavity.
Examples:-
The nematodes, rotifers, gastrotrichs, and introverts.

Q 2. Life cycle of Apicomplexans?
Ans:-
Most Apicomplexans have a complex life-cycle, involving both asexual and sexual
reproduction. Typically, a host is infected by ingesting cysts, which divide to produce
sporozoites that enter the host’s cells. Eventually, the cells burst, releasing merozoites which
infect new host cells.

Q 3. Two common characteristics of Annelids and arthropods?
Ans:-

1.Both Annelida and Arthropoda consist of segmented body structures.
2.Both Annelida and Arthropoda consist of a hydrostatic skeleton.

Q 4. Copulatory function in oligochaeta?

Ans:-
Sexually mature oligochaetes have a clitellum, which is a modification of a section of the
body wall consisting of a glandular, saddlelike thickening near the gonopores. During
copulation, the clitellum secretes a mucus that keeps the worms paired while sperm are
being exchanged.
