      Important Question for subjective
                  Midterm 2021-2022
1) What is associative behavior? 2 marks
Associative Learning “A learning that involves making associations
between experiences”
Example
• A mouse bites a brightly colored caterpillar
• It tastes bad
• Mouse avoids attacking insects of similar color in future.

    Associative Learning
• Ability to associate one environmental feature (such as a color) with
another (such as a foul taste)
2) Two examples of morphological response .2 mark
Example:
• Chameleon change color when it feel threatened.
• Arctic hare change its coat color from blue-grey to white for better
camouflage during winters and turn again to blue-grey in spring
   3) What is learned behavior? 3 marks
      Thorpe defined learning as a long lasting adaptive change in
      behavior resulting from past experience; hence it is acquired
      during the life time of an individual
      Learned behaviors

• modified by experience
• variable, changeable
• flexible with a complex & changing environment

4) Fixed Action pattern. 3 marks
   • It is a sequence of unlearned, innate behavior that is
   unchangeable.
   • Once initiated; it is usually carried to completion.

      • It is hardwired, however, not purely genetic, may improve with
      experience.
      • FAP is triggered by an external sensory stimulus known as “sign
      stimulus”.
   5) Explanation of innate behavior with example. 5 marks
      i. automatic, fixed, “built-in”, no “learning curve”
     ii. despite different environments, all individuals exhibit the
behavior
    iii. Examples: Early survival, reproduction, kinesis, taxis.
Examples
         Web making in spiders
         Nest building in birds
         Fighting among male stickleback fish
6). Imprinting. 5 marks
“Formation of a long-lasting behavioral attachment to a particular
individual or object at a specific stage in life”
Imprinting
• A type of learning behavior
• A very young animal develops attachment with the very first object it
has visual, auditory or tactile experience
• It thereafter follows that object
Example
• Seagulls
• Sensitive period for a parent to bond with its young lasts one to two
days
• If bonding does not occur, parent will not care for the infant, leading to
death of the offspring
Imprinting—Example

• Classic experiment of Konard Lorenz with geese
• Konard Lorenz made goslings to imprint on him
• Goslings followed him as though he was their mother

7).Habitat of dolphin. (2)
   Dolphins are found all over the world; generally in shallow cold
   waters as well as the warm tropical waters
8).Kinesis (2)
   Kinesis: “a change in activity or turning rate in response to a change
   in location”.
   Kinesis—Example:
   • Kinesis in sow bugs in response to variation in humidity.
   • Move more rapidly in dry areas and become slow in humid areas.
    • They survive better in moist areas.
9). Enlist the type of learning. (3)
   Habituation
   Imprinting
   Classical conditioning
   Trial and error learning
   Latent learning
   Insight
   Reasoning
   Cognition
10)... Characteristics of learned behaviors. (3)
   Acquired during the life of an organism due to constant experience
   Experience dependent and can be modified through experience
   Learning is flexible
   Learning behaviour differs from individual to individual, to
    individual among the same species hence not species specific

   Learned behaviour is certainly not inherited, though the ability to
    learn is almost certainly inherited as it is dependent on the
    development of the nervous system of the organism which is
    inherited.
   All organisms have the ability to learn at least to some extent.
   However learning in humans surpasses all other animals. Almost
    everything we do has been learned. No other species surpasses the
    humans in their amount and range of information that is acquired
    through learning.
11). Note on experiment of Angelo d’Arrigo. (5)

Angelo d'Arrigo extended the technique used by Konrad Lorenz.

• He noted that the flight of hang-glider is very similar to the flight
patterns of migratory birds.
• Both use updrafts of thermal currents to gain altitude that then permits
soaring flight over distance.
• He used this method to reintroduce threatened species of raptors.
• As birds hatched in captivity have no mentor birds to teach them
traditional migratory routes, D'Arrigo hatched chicks under the wing of
his glider and they imprinted on him. Then, he taught the fledglings to
fly and to hunt.
• The young birds followed him not only on the ground (as with Lorenz)
but also in the air as he took the path of various migratory routes

12). Characteristics of innate behavior. (5)
• Pattern of behavior is inherited. It is passed on from parents to
offspring
• It is unlearned behaviout.

• It occurs in all the members of a species hence it is species specific and
predictable.
• It is not dependent on past experience as it is an inborn response to a
stimulus.
• It takes place in individuals even when kept in isolation away from
their fellow members.
• Innate behavior has high adaptability and survival value
13).Animal orientation long question
Animal Orientation: “Ability of an animal to determine its position in
space and move predictably with respect to specific stimuli”
Animal Orientation: Examples:
• Standing up after falling down (orientation against gravity)
• Orienting towards incoming light
• Acoustic orientation
• Olfactory orientation
Sensory Motor Basis of Orientation:
• Animal orientation is a complex process.
• Requires integration of sensory input and coordination of motor output.
Input-Output Integration:
Integration includes:
• Receiving information through sensory receptor neurons.
• Processing and correlating it in the CNS.
• Forming a response through muscles that cause orienting movement
