Long's classroom centered
Classroom research is a recent trend that has been emerging due to severe need of designing
lessons according to specific needs of learners. As pointed out by Long (1983), classroom
centered research is ‘eminently practical’ because it is ‘concerned with what actually goes on
in the classroom as opposed to what is supposed to go on’ (p. 284). By definition, Classroom-
Centered Research (CCR) views classroom as the ‘object’ of research, and not simply the
‘setting’ for research. The CCR determines not only what happens in the classroom but also
the conditions that can affect teaching and learning processes. It is observed that sometimes
simple research results do not leave any impact unless they are empirically tested

Types of intelligence
Sternberg (1985, 2002) distinguishes three types of intelligence:

1. Analytical — the ability to analyze, compare, and evaluate

2. Creative — the ability to produce novel solutions to problems

3. Practical — the capacity to adapt, to shape, and to select environments suited to one’s
abilities

Cognitive motivation
Cognitive motivation refers to the mental processes that drive individuals to achieve goals,
solve problems, and engage in activities that they find intellectually stimulating or

    challenging. Unlike basic physiological drives, cognitive motivation is rooted in higher-order
    thinking and involves various cognitive functions such as planning, decision-making, and
    problem-solving.

    Understanding cognitive motivation is essential for designing effective educational programs,
    workplace environments, and personal development strategies. By recognizing the cognitive
    factors that influence motivation, individuals and organizations can create conditions that
    foster engagement, persistence, and achievement.

    Characteristics of competence
    Competence encompasses a range of characteristics that enable an individual to perform tasks
    effectively and achieve desired outcomes. Here are key characteristics of competence:

1. Skill Proficiency

2. Knowledge

3. Ability to Apply Knowledge

4. Experience

5. Adaptability

6. Problem-Solving Skills

7. Emotional Intelligence

8. Motivation and Commitment

9. Self-Regulation

    By developing these characteristics, individuals can enhance their competence and achieve
    success in various aspects of their personal and professional lives.

    Differences in traditional and 21th century classroom environment
    The shift from traditional classroom environments to 21st-century classroom environments has
    brought significant changes in teaching methods, learning experiences, and the overall
    educational atmosphere

    Traditional Classroom Environment:

   Role of Teacher: The teacher is the primary source of knowledge, delivering information
    through lectures.

   Student Role: Students passively receive information and are expected to memorize and
    recall it.

   Testing: Emphasis on standardized tests and exams to measure student performance.

   Grading: Grades are the primary measure of student achievement.

    21st-Century Classroom Environment:

   Role of Teacher: Teacher acts as a facilitator or guide, encouraging students to explore and
    discover.
   Student Role: Students actively participate, collaborate, and engage in their learning process.
   21st century classroom requires a learning environment where teaching and learning are
    facilitated and higher-order thinking skills are developed. The effectiveness of ESP learning
    is specific to the construction of knowledge in a social context (Duffy & Jonassen, 1991),
    such as, classrooms and language laboratories ‘where students join in manipulating materials
    and, thus, create a community of learners who built their knowledge together’ (Dewey, 1966).

    Characteristics of research
    Alami (2015) proposed five main characteristics of research within applied linguistics
    namely:

1) Empirical
2) Logical
3) Reductive
4) Planned
5) Imaginative

    Whereas, McDonough and McDonough (2005) sum up research in four features:

1) interest
2) originality
3) specificity
4) Dissemination of both research questions and findings.

    Nunan (2005, p. 226-227) contribution in determining the nature of the term rests on the
    concept of enquiry: product and process.

    Write Kolb’s Learning Style Model
    Kolb’s model is based on a four-stage learning cycle which proposes that the learning styles
    contain concrete vs. abstract and active vs. reflective dimensions. Concrete experiences rely
    on accommodating and diverging components as feelings specific whereas abstract
    conceptualization focuses on converging and assimilating as thinking and doing continuum.
    Both active and reflective domains are observed on the processing continuum.

   Explain Myers-Briggs Model
   The Myers-Briggs Model (Briggs & Myers, 1975; or Briggs et al., 1980) classifies the
   individuals according to their preferences on scales derived from the theories of psychological
   types developed by Carl Jung who believes that behaviour is never random and reflective of
   one’s distinctive mental capacities. According to the Myers-Briggs Model, learners may be:

1) Extroverts: Learn best when they can work with a friend and learn by trying something
   themselves
2) Introverts: Learn best when they can find quiet places to work and have enough time to
   reflect on.
3) Sensors: Learn best when they can ask their teacher to explain. They like to use computers,
   watch films etc.
4) Intuitors: Learn best when they can find ways to be imaginative and creative.
5) Thinkers: Learn best when they have limited time to do their work, or when they are able to
   put information in a logical order.
6) Feelers: Learn best when they can work with a friend and have opportunities to choose topics.
   They care about and help others.
