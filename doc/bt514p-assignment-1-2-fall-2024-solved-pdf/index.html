Q. No. 1: A student of analytical chemistry is tasked with weighing 9g of NaCl
in the laboratory. Describe step- By-step how the student ensures precise
measurement and safety throughout the experiment, detailing each
Precautionary measure and instrument used.

Answer:

To weigh 9g of NaCl in the laboratory, the student should follow these steps to ensure precision
and safety:

   1. Preparation and Safety:
          o Wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) such as a lab coat,
              safety goggles, and gloves to prevent contact with the chemical.
          o Ensure the weighing area is clean and free from contaminants to avoid inaccurate
              readings.
   2. Instrument Setup:
          o Use a top-loading balance or analytical balance for precise measurement.
          o Calibrate the balance if needed or ensure it is already calibrated.
          o Place the balance on a stable, vibration-free surface to avoid disturbances during
              weighing.
   3. Selection of Weighing Container:
          o Use a clean, dry weighing dish or beaker to hold the NaCl. Ensure the container
              is appropriate for the balance being used.
   4. Taring the Balance:
          o Place the weighing container on the balance and press the tare button to zero the
              weight. This ensures the balance reads only the mass of the NaCl.
   5. Weighing NaCl:
          o Using a clean, dry spatula, carefully add NaCl to the container.
          o Add the NaCl gradually to avoid exceeding 9g and adjust until the balance reads
              exactly 9.00g.
   6. Post-Weighing Precautions:
          o Close the NaCl container tightly after use to prevent contamination or spillage.
          o Transfer the NaCl to the designated area or experimental setup promptly and
              safely.
   7. Cleaning and Final Check:
          o Clean the spatula, weighing container, and the surrounding area to maintain
              laboratory hygiene.
          o Recheck the balance for any residual material and ensure it is reset for the next
              use.

By following these steps, the student ensures precise measurement while maintaining safety and
laboratory standards.

Q. No. 2: Why are micropipettes and Glass filters used in analytical
chemistry?

Answer:

   1. Micropipettes:
         o Purpose: Micropipettes are used to measure and transfer very small, precise
             volumes of liquids (typically in microliters).
         o Significance: They ensure accuracy and repeatability, making them essential for
             experiments that require precise liquid handling, such as preparing solutions,
             dilutions, or performing titrations in analytical chemistry.
   2. Glass Filters:
         o Purpose: Glass filters are used for filtration to separate fine particles or impurities
             from liquids or gases.
         o Significance: They are chemically resistant, ensuring they do not react with the
             substances being filtered. They are ideal for applications requiring high purity and
             precision, such as sample preparation or analysis in analytical chemistry.

Q. No. 3: How many significant figures are in each of the following
measurements, Convert each measurement to

Its equivalent scientific notation or decimal form.

• 0.0120 mol HCl

• 605.3 mg CaCO3

• 1.043 × 10–4 mol Ag+

• 9.3 × 104 mg NaOH

1. 0.0120 mol HCl
a) Significant Figures:

- Leading zeros are not significant

- Trailing zeros after a decimal point are significant

- Therefore, 0.0120 has 3 significant figures (1,2,0)

b) Scientific Notation:

   •   0.0120 = 1.20 × 10⁻² mol HCl

2. 605.3 mg CaCO₃

a) Significant Figures:

- All non-zero digits are significant

- The decimal point determines the last significant digit

- Therefore, 605.3 has 4 significant figures (6,0,5,3)

b) Scientific Notation:

   •   605.3 = 6.053 × 10² mg CaCO₃

3. 1.043 × 10⁻⁴ mol Ag⁺

a) Significant Figures:

- All non-zero digits are significant

- Zeros between non-zero digits are significant

- Therefore, 1.043 has 4 significant figures (1,0,4,3)

b) Decimal Form:

   •   1.043 × 10⁻⁴ = 0.0001043 mol Ag⁺

4. 9.3 × 10⁴ mg NaOH

a) Significant Figures:

- All non-zero digits are significant

- Therefore, 9.3 has 2 significant figures (9,3)

b) Decimal Form:

   •   9.3 × 10⁴ = 93,000 mg NaOH
