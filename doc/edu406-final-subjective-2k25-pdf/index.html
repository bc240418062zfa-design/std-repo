What is necessary for the reflective practitioner to become a teacher?

For a reflective practitioner to become a teacher, they must develop a combination of knowledge,
skills, and attitudes that foster continuous growth and improvement. Here are the key
components:

   1) Deep Subject Knowledge
   2) Pedagogical Skills
   3) Reflective Practice
   4) Emotional and Social Intelligence
   5) Commitment to Professional Development
   6) Ethical and Reflective Decision-Making
   7) Adaptability and Innovation

Name six phases of Dewey extension model?

Dewey suggests aspects (phases) of reflective thought:

   ❖ Suggestions
   ❖ Intellectualization
   ❖ Hypothesis Forming
   ❖ Clarity
   ❖ Imaginative action
   ❖ Testing the hypothesis

Components of Supervision?

Supervision is a crucial aspect of professional development, and it involves several key
components. Here are the main components of supervision:

   ❖ Instructional Supervision
   ❖ Clinical Supervision
   ❖ Developmental Supervision
   ❖ Professional Development
   ❖ Supportive Supervision
   ❖ Leadership and Support
   ❖ Ethical and Legal Considerations
   ❖ Educational Supervision

Write key components of Socratic method?

The Socratic method is a powerful teaching and learning approach that encourages critical
thinking, reflection, and intellectual exploration. Here are the key components of the Socratic
method:

   ❖ Questioning (Dialectical Method)
   ❖ Critical Thinking
   ❖ Active Participation
   ❖ Inquiry and Reflection
   ❖ Collaborative Dialogue
   ❖ Inductive Reasoning
   ❖ Continuous Learning
   ❖ Intellectual Humility

Inter-professional learning?

Inter-professional learning (IPL) refers to an educational approach where individuals from
different professional backgrounds learn together to foster collaboration, improve teamwork, and
enhance service delivery, particularly in healthcare, education, and social services.

Key Components of Inter-Professional Learning:

   1) Collaborative Learning

   2) Teamwork and Communication

   3) Role Awareness

   4) Problem-Solving and Critical Thinking

   5) Patient/Client-Centered Approach (for healthcare and social services)

   6) Ethical and Professional Responsibility

4d cycle name and briefly explain?

   1) Discover: The identification of organizational processes that work well
   2) Dream: The envisioning of processes that would work well in the future
   3) Design: Planning and prioritizing process that work well
   4) Destiny: The implementation of the proposed design

Components of appreciative inquiry AI?

Appreciative Inquiry (AI) is the study and exploration of what gives life to human systems when
they function at their best

It consists of the following key components:

   1) The 4D Cycle (Core Process of AI)
   2) Positive Core (Strength-Based Focus)
   3) Generative Questions
   4) Collaboration and Inclusivity
   5) Constructivist Approach

What is the importance of self-regulated learning in teaching?

Self-Regulated Learning (SRL) is essential in teaching because it empowers students to take
control of their own learning process, leading to better academic performance, motivation, and
lifelong learning skills. Here’s why SRL is important in teaching:

   1) Enhances Student Autonomy
   2) Improves Academic Performance
   3) Develops Critical Thinking and Problem-Solving Skills
   4) Boosts Motivation and Engagement
   5) Supports Lifelong Learning
   6) Enhances Teaching Effectiveness
   7) Promotes Autonomy and Independence

lowest level of Bloom's Taxonomy?

The lowest level of Bloom's Taxonomy is:

Remembering (Knowledge)

This level involves recalling or remembering previously learned information, such as facts,
definitions, or concepts.

What is higher-order learning?

Higher-order learning refers to complex cognitive processes that involve critical thinking,
problem-solving, and creativity. It goes beyond basic recall and memorization, requiring students
to analyze, evaluate, and synthesize information to create new knowledge and understanding.

Key Aspects of Higher-Order Learning:

   ❖ Critical Thinking
   ❖ Problem-Solving
   ❖ Creative Thinking
   ❖ Application & Transfer
   ❖ Metacognition
   ❖ Evaluation

Why establish a community of practice in school?

Establishing a Community of Practice (CoP) in a school fosters collaborative learning,
professional growth, and a shared sense of purpose among educators and students. A CoP is a
group of individuals who share a common interest and engage in collective learning to improve
their practice.

Key Reasons for Establishing a Community of Practice in Schools:

   ❖ Enhances Teacher Collaboration & Professional Growth
   ❖ Improves Student Learning Outcomes
   ❖ Encourages a Culture of Continuous Learning
   ❖ Supports School Improvement & Innovation
   ❖ Builds a Sense of Community & Shared Purpose

3 stages of dialogic diary?

A dialogic diary is a reflective writing tool that fosters deep thinking, critical analysis, and
meaningful dialogue between learners and educators or peers. It involves writing, responding,
and engaging in dialogue over time.

Stages:

   1) Uncertainty
   2) Enthusiasm
   3) Lack of Enthusiasm
   4) Habit and Learning

What are benefits to employ "Community of practice" in classrooms?

Employing a Community of Practice (CoP) in classrooms benefits both teachers and students
by fostering collaboration, shared learning, and continuous improvement. It creates a
supportive learning environment where individuals engage in meaningful discussions and
develop deeper understanding.

Benefits of Using a Community of Practice in Classrooms

           1) Enhances Collaborative Learning
           2) Improves Critical Thinking & Reflection
           3) Strengthens Teacher-Student & Peer Relationships
           4) Encourages Lifelong Learning & Professional Development
           5) Supports Diverse Learning Needs
           6) Fosters Innovation & Problem-Solving
