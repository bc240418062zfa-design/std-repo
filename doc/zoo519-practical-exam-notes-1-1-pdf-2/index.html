                            Subjective Part

1. What is hemoglobin and what is its major function?
Ans: Hemoglobin is a conjugated protein composed of the basic protein globin linked
to 4 heme molecules. It transports oxygen and carbon dioxide in the blood.

2. What are the different compounds of hemoglobin?
Ans: The different compounds of hemoglobin are oxyhemoglobin,
carboxyhemoglobin, methamoglobin, sulfhemoglobin and cyanmethemoglobin.

3. What is the principle of cyanmethemoglobin method for estimation of
hemoglobin?
Ans: In this method, blood is mixed with Drabkin's solution which converts all forms
of hemoglobin to cyanmethemoglobin. The absorbance of this solution is then
measured and compared to a standard solution to determine hemoglobin levels.

4. What is a hemocytometer and how is it used to count RBCs?
Ans: A hemocytometer is a device used to count cells visually under a microscope.
The Neubauer chamber contains counting grids which help count RBCs in a diluted
blood sample.

5. What happens during muscle contraction and relaxation?
Ans: During muscle contraction, myosin head attaches to actin filament causing the
filaments to slide over each other leading to shortening. During relaxation, the myosin
head detaches and the muscle returns to its resting state.

6. What are the different parts of the brain and their major functions?
Ans: The forebrain is involved in complex functions like thinking and decision
making. The midbrain controls reflex actions and eye movements. The hindbrain
controls vital functions like respiration and heart rate.

7. What are the different stages of the estrous cycle?
Ans: The four stages of the estrous cycle are - proestrus, estrus, metestrus/diestrus
and anestrus. These stages involve changes in ovarian activity and hormonal levels
which prepare the female for ovulation and pregnancy.

8. What is a dissolved oxygen meter and how is it used to measure DO levels?
Ans: A DO meter measures the voltage produced when oxygen reacts with the sensor
components. Higher voltage indicates higher DO. The probe is moved continuously
in water to get an accurate reading.

9. What is the normal blood glucose range in humans?
Ans: The normal blood glucose range in humans is between 60-140 mg/dL (3.3-7.8
mmol/L). Below 60 mg/dL is considered hypoglycemia while above 140 mg/dL is
termed as hyperglycemia.

10. What are the symptoms of hypoglycemia?
Ans: Symptoms of hypoglycemia include racing pulse, cold sweats, pale face, hunger,
shivering, weakness, restlessness and difficulty concentrating.

11. What are the different types of diabetes?
Ans: The two main types of diabetes are type 1 diabetes which involves destruction
of insulin producing cells and type 2 diabetes which involves insulin resistance.

12. How does exercise affect blood pressure?
Ans: Exercise causes an increase in oxygen demand leading blood vessels to dilate
and heart to pump faster to increase blood flow. This leads to a temporary rise in
systolic blood pressure.

13. What is the role of pituitary gland in regulating estrous cycle?
Ans: Pituitary gland produces FSH and LH which act on the ovary to stimulate
follicular development, ovulation and corpus luteum formation during the estrous
cycle.

14. Which part of the brain is responsible for vital functions like respiration
    and heart rate?
Ans. The hindbrain which includes parts like medulla oblongata and pons regulates
vital functions like breathing, heart rate and blood pressure.

15. What is the effect of temperature on oxygen consumption in aquatic
    animals?
Ans: Lower temperature reduces oxygen consumption in aquatic animals like fish
whereas higher temperature increases their oxygen demand.

16. What are the components of a sphygmomanometer used to measure blood
    pressure?

Ans: The components of a sphygmomanometer include an inflatable cuff, a pressure
gauge, a pump bulb to inflate the cuff, and a valve to release pressure.

17. What is the physiological basis of nerve-muscle preparation?
Ans: Nerve muscle preparation demonstrates the functional unit of skeletal muscle
activity which is the motor unit composed of nerve axon branches connected to
muscle fibers through the neuromuscular junction.

18. Which part of the brain is associated with memory formation and storage?
Ans: The hippocampus located in the temporal lobes of the brain plays a key role in
the consolidation of short-term memory into long-term memory.

19. Why are frequent measurements of dissolved oxygen levels important in
    aquaculture?
Ans: In fish farming, dissolved oxygen levels can change rapidly due to limited
oxygen solubility, rapid utilization and slow replenishment which makes frequent
monitoring critical.

20. What is the role of GnRH in regulating the estrous cycle?
Ans: GnRH from the hypothalamus stimulates the anterior pituitary to produce FSH
and LH which induce follicular development, ovulation and corpus luteum formation
during the cycle.

21. What is the auscultatory gap in blood pressure measurement?
Ans: The auscultatory gap refers to the disappearance of Korotkoff sounds during
deflation of the sphygmomanometer cuff leading to underestimation of systolic blood
pressure.

22. Which type of diabetes involves autoimmune destruction of insulin
    producing cells?
Ans: Type 1 diabetes mellitus involves autoimmune mediated destruction of
pancreatic beta cells which produce insulin.

23. Name the different lobes of the cerebral hemispheres.
Ans: The lobes of the cerebral hemispheres are - frontal, parietal, temporal and
occipital.

24. Why is hemoglobin measurement important in diagnosing diseases?
Ans: Hemoglobin concentration determines the oxygen carrying capacity of blood.
Its measurement is used to diagnose different types of anemia.

25. What is the utility of a kymograph in studying cardiac activity?
Ans: A kymograph produces graphical recordings of variations in cardiac muscle
tension providing information about the force and frequency of heart contraction.
