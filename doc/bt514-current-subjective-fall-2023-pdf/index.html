Q1: What is chemical analysis?
Chemical analysis includes any aspect of chemical characterization, starting from collection of
sample, extraction, sample preparation, instrumental analysis to statistical evaluation of collected
data.

Q2: Define complementary color?
When white light falls upon a sample, either reflection or absorption takes place. If only a
portion of the light is absorbed, the color of the sample is determined by the reflected light (is
called Complementary Colors).

Q3: Function of hollow cathode lamp?
HCL is a vacuumed Tube bearing Cylindrical cathode And some noble gas at very low pressure.
It generates photons of particular metal for which cathode is made.

Q4: Why transition metals and organic molecules show color?
Transition metals are colored because of d-d transitions while organic Molecules are colored
because of conjugation.

Q8: Note on UV spectroscopy?
UV spectroscopy, specifically UV-Vis spectroscopy, is an analytical technique that measures the
absorption of ultraviolet (UV) and visible (Vis) light by various substances. This method
provides valuable insights into the molecular structures and concentrations of chemicals within a
sample.

Principle:
When UV or Visible light is absorbed by a substance, the electrons in the molecule move from
their ground states to higher energy levels. Different molecules have unique absorption patterns
depending on their electronic configurations.

Instrument & its components:
The instrument used in this technique is UV spectrophotometer and has following components:

Light source

Monochromator

Sample compartment

Detector

Readout Device

Beer-Lambert law:
Absorption of light is directly proportional to both the concentration of the absorbing substance
and the path length of the light through the sample. Mathematically, this relationship is expressed
as:

A= ε*b*C
Where

A represents the absorbance,

ε is the molar absorptivity ,
C denotes the concentration, and

b stands for the path length.

(Note: It’s your choice whether you write Beer-Lambert law or just write till components.
You can also write applications of UV spectroscopy instead of Beer-Lambert law)

Q9: Note on ICP-OES?
ICP/OES stands for Inductively Coupled Plasma Optical Emission Spectroscopy. It is a type of
atomic emission spectroscopy.

In this technique, atomization source is changed to inductive coupled plasma. Temperature of
plasma can reach up to 10,000 K. Risk of chemical interference is low. 70 elements can be
determined to ppb (parts per billion) level.

Working of ICP:
The sample is prepared in liquid form as in case of FAAS (Flame Atomic Absorption
Spectroscopy).

Sample is injected through nebulizer at 0.5 to 1.5 mL/min.

The emitted spectral lines pass through monochromoator.
The separation in MS is on the basis of mass to charge ratio.

Q10: Importance of oxidation in chemical reactions?
Oxidation is defined in terms of loss of electrons, gain of oxygen, loss of hydrogen, or an
increase in oxidation number.

Oxidation reactions are essential in chemistry, influencing organic synthesis by creating
functional groups essential for drugs and materials. In natural processes like photosynthesis,
oxidation is vital for energy production in plants. These reactions play a crucial role in redox
titrations for analytical purposes. Oxidation contributes to chemical synthesis, enabling the
creation of diverse compounds and innovative materials. In daily life, oxidation affects food
spoilage and wastewater treatment, while in combustion, it powers energy production.

Additionally, oxidation is integral to cellular respiration, extracting energy from nutrients.
Overall, these reactions are crucial, driving advancements in biology and technology.

Q11: Define solid phase extraction and its purpose in sample preparation for
chromatographic analysis.
Solid phase extraction is a sample preparation process by which compounds that are dissolved or
suspended in liquid mixture are separated from other compounds in the mixture according to
their physical and chemical properties.
