Lesson 5
   The Continuing Evolution of Virtual Worlds for
                            Language Learning
What are Virtual Worlds?

What is a virtual world (VW)?

A virtual world is an online 3D environment where users control avatars and
interact with other avatars and objects. It exists primarily for social interaction.

What are some key characteristics of a virtual world (VW)?

Key characteristics of a VW include an online 3D environment, avatars, real-time
interactivity, 24-hour accessibility, persistence, social space, and a large number of
users.

A Brief History of VWs

What were some early works that inspired the development of virtual worlds?

Works like "Neuromancer" (1984) and "Snow Crash" (1992) inspired the
development of virtual worlds.

What were some early text-based virtual worlds and their features?

"Colossal Cave Adventure" (1975-1976) and "MUD" (1978) were early text-based
virtual worlds that introduced multiplayer online interactions.

How did MOOs (MUDs, Object-Oriented) differ from MUDs, and how were
they used in education?

MOOs introduced object-oriented programming, allowing for more customization,
and they were used in education for virtual classroom spaces.

Overview and Categorization of Virtual Worlds Today

Why is it difficult to determine the exact number of virtual worlds?

The distinction between virtual worlds and massively multiplayer online role-play
games (MMORPGs) is not always clear, making it challenging to count them.

What kind of data do virtual worlds typically provide about their users?

Virtual worlds usually provide data on the number of registered accounts but not
necessarily active users.

What are the five key areas used to categorize virtual worlds?

Virtual worlds are categorized based on age level, game level (low, mid, or high),
socialization level (low, mid, or high), education focus (low, mid, or high), and
technical requirements (low, mid, or high).

teaching gain prominence?

Research into the effects of virtual worlds on language learning and teaching

What are some shared themes in research on virtual worlds and language

Research indicates that virtual worlds enhance collaboration among learners and

How do avatars in virtual worlds impact learners?

How can virtual worlds blur the line between the virtual and real worlds for

Immersion in virtual worlds can blur the line between the virtual and real worlds in

How have virtual worlds evolved over time?

Virtual worlds have evolved with improved graphics, user interfaces, and
communication possibilities.

What changes are expected to shift virtual worlds toward artificial reality

Upcoming changes may shift virtual worlds into more fully developed artificial

Lesson 6
     Authoring Language-Learning Courseware
What is Language Courseware?

What is language courseware?

Language courseware refers to software applications designed for language
learning, encompassing self-study programs and those integrated into teacher-led
courses, ranging from simple activities to comprehensive online courses.

How has the role of language courseware evolved over time?

Answer: Language courseware has evolved from standalone exercises to
integrated online learning environments, often delivered through course websites
with materials provided by publishers.

What are "intelligent language tutors" in the context of language courseware?

Answer: "Intelligent language tutors" (ILT) are advanced language-learning
software that offer personalized interactions between learners and computers,
often incorporating artificial intelligence (AI) to enhance the learning experience.

Courseware at the Core of Early CALL

When did the development of language courseware start?

Answer: The development of language courseware dates back to the 1960s, with
early experiments involving computer-based language learning.

What was PLATO, and how was it used for language courseware?

Answer: PLATO was a system at the University of Illinois used for language
courseware in the 1960s. It featured text-based exercises, touch-sensitive
screens, and interactions focused on grammar and vocabulary.

Why was it essential for early language instructors to learn programming
languages?

Answer: Early language instructors were encouraged to learn programming
languages to have full control over the design and functionality of their
courseware, allowing customization to their curriculum and students' needs.
