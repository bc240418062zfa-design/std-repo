What are Three dimensions of syllables?
The three dimensions of syllables are:

1. Onset: The consonant sound or cluster that begins the syllable.

2. Nucleus (or Peak): The vowel sound that forms the core or center of the syllable.

3. Coda: The consonant sound or cluster that ends the syllable.

What are Three plosive sounds?
The three plosive (or stop) sounds in English are:

1. /p/ (voiceless bilabial plosive)

2. /t/ (voiceless alveolar plosive)

3. /k/ (voiceless velar plosive)

What is Source-filter mechanism?

The source-filter mechanism is a model of speech production that explains how sounds are
generated in the human vocal tract. It consists of two main components:

1. Source: The vocal cords (or glottis) produce a sound source, such as a buzz or a vibration,
through vocal cord vibration.

2. Filter: The vocal tract, including the mouth and nose, acts as a filter that modifies the sound
source, shaping its frequency characteristics and producing the desired speech sounds.

This mechanism allows for the production of a wide range of speech sounds, from vowels to
consonants.

Voiced and voiceless sounds?
Voiced and voiceless sounds are classified based on the presence or absence of vocal cord
vibration:

Voiced Sounds

1. Produced with vocal cord vibration

2. Vocal cords are close together and vibrate

3. Examples: /b/, /d/, /g/, /z/, /v/

Voiceless Sounds

1. Produced without vocal cord vibration

2. Vocal cords are apart and do not vibrate

3. Examples: /p/, /t/, /k/, /s/, /f/

Praat software?
Praat is a free, open-source software for speech analysis, created by Paul Boersma and David
Weenink. It's widely used in phonetics, linguistics, and speech therapy.

Praat allows users to:

1. Record and edit speech sounds

2. Visualize and analyze acoustic properties (e.g., pitch, intensity, spectrograms)

3. Measure and annotate speech segments

4. Perform statistical analyses

Praat is available for Windows, macOS, and Linux.

Types of phonations?
There are mainly four possible glottis/larynx settings or types of phonations:

   ❖ Voiceless – when the folds are open apart and the air passing through the glottis freely (/t/
     or /p/).

   ❖ Voiced – when the folds are tight together and there is vibration during the air passage
     though the glottis (e.g., /b/ or /d/).

   ❖ Creaky voice – when there is a slight opening in the front and the arytenoid cartilages are
     tight together, so that the vocal folds can vibrate only at the anterior end (the small
     opening at the top).

   ❖ Breathy or murmuring sound – when the vocal folds are apart but still, they are
     vibrating - a breathy voice is like a whisper except voice.

Three types of sounds related to approximants?
The three types of sounds related to approximants are:

1. Liquids: /l/ and /r/ sounds, which are made by allowing air to flow freely through the mouth.

2. Glides: /j/ and /w/ sounds, which are made by moving the tongue or lips towards a specific
position.

3. Semivowels: Sounds that are intermediate between vowels and consonants, such as /j/ and /w/.

Types of articulatory targets?

   1) Place of articulation

   3) Type of articulation

what is the function of first formant in the vowels?
The first formant (F1) is a critical acoustic cue for vowel identification, providing information
about vowel height, backness, and phonological contrasts.

what are the three sounds in the umbrella approximant?
The umbrella approximant, also known as the "w" sound, is actually a combination of three
distinct sounds:

1. Bilabial Approximant: /w/ (as in "wet")

2. Labiovelar Approximant: /ʊ̯/ (a blend of lip rounding and velarization)

3. Velar Approximant: /ɰ/ (a soft, velar sound)

Together, these three sounds blend to form the characteristic "w" sound in many languages.
