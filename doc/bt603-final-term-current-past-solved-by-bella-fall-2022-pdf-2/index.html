Enlist six trace elements used in fermentation.

Trace elements are essential nutrients required by microorganisms for their growth and metabolism during
fermentation. Here are six trace elements commonly used in fermentation:

    •   Iron (Fe)
    •   Zinc (Zn)
    •   Manganese (Mn)
    •   Copper (Cu)
    •   Cobalt (Co)
    •   Molybdenum (Mo)

Write down micro and macro minerals used in fermentation.

Minerals play an essential role in fermentation, both at the micro and macro levels. Micro minerals, such as iron,
zinc, and copper, act as cofactors for enzymes involved in fermentation, allowing for the proper functioning of
metabolic processes. Macro minerals, such as calcium and magnesium, are important for maintaining pH balance
and providing structural support for microorganisms during fermentation. Overall, minerals are crucial for optimal
fermentation performance and the production of high-quality fermented products.

Method of antibiotic screening.

The method of antibiotic screening typically involves the following steps:

    •   Collection of microorganisms: A variety of microorganisms, including bacteria and fungi, are collected from
        different sources such as soil, water, and plants.
    •   Isolation of strains: Individual strains of microorganisms are isolated and grown in culture.
    •   Testing for antibiotic production: The isolated strains are tested for their ability to produce antibiotics using
        various methods such as the agar well diffusion assay, disk diffusion assay, and broth dilution assay.
    •   Identification of active compounds: The active compounds produced by the microorganisms are identified
        using techniques such as chromatography and spectrophotometry.
    •   Characterization of active compounds: The active compounds are further characterized for their chemical
        and physical properties, as well as their efficacy against specific bacterial or fungal strains.
    •   Optimization of production: The conditions for optimal production of the antibiotic are determined, and the
        yield of the active compound is increased.

Overall, antibiotic screening involves a combination of microbiological, biochemical, and chemical techniques to
identify and characterize new antibiotics.

Differentiate between aerobic and anaerobic fermentation.

Aerobic fermentation requires oxygen, while anaerobic fermentation occurs in the absence of oxygen. During
aerobic fermentation, glucose is broken down completely into carbon dioxide and water, producing a large amount
of energy in the form of ATP.

Anaerobic fermentation involves the breakdown of glucose into smaller molecules such as lactic acid or ethanol,
which produces less ATP. Anaerobic fermentation is commonly used in the production of certain foods and
beverages such as yogurt, cheese, and beer.

How oxygen is supplied to the microbial culture during fermentation.

During fermentation, oxygen is supplied to microbial cultures through various methods such as agitation, sparging,
or the use of oxygen-enriched air. Agitation involves stirring or shaking the culture to increase the contact between
the microbial cells and the air. Sparging involves bubbling air or oxygen into the culture to provide a continuous
supply of oxygen. Oxygen-enriched air can also be used to supply oxygen to the culture. The method used depends
on the specific fermentation process and the type of microorganisms being cultured.

                                                          OR

Oxygen is normally supplied to microbial cultures in the form of air, this being the cheapest available source of the
gas. The method for provision of a culture with a supply of air varies with the scale of the process:

    ▪   Laboratory-scale cultures may be aerated by means of the shake-flask technique where the culture (50 to
        100 cm3) is grown in a conical flask (250 to 500 cm3) shaken on a platform contained in a controlled
        environment chamber.
    ▪   Pilot- and industrial-scale fermentations are normally carried out in stirred, aerated vessels (fermenters).

Write and explain stoichiometric equation.

Stoichiometric equations are balanced chemical equations that represent the relationship between the reactants
and products in a chemical reaction. The coefficients in the equation represent the number of molecules, atoms, or
ions involved in the reaction. For example, the stoichiometric equation for the combustion of methane (CH4) in the
presence of oxygen (O2) is:

                                              CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O

This equation shows that one molecule of methane reacts with two molecules of oxygen to produce one molecule of
carbon dioxide and two molecules of water. The stoichiometric equation is important because it allows us to
determine the amount of reactants needed to produce a certain amount of product, and vice versa. It also helps us
to understand the chemical properties of a substance and the nature of the chemical reactions that it undergoes.

Explain biological and physical problems due to formation of excessive foam in fermentation.

Excessive foam formation during fermentation can cause both biological and physical problems. Biologically, the
foam can inhibit the exchange of gases between the fermentation broth and the surrounding air, which can impair
the growth and metabolism of microorganisms involved in the fermentation process. Physically, the foam can cause
spills, overflows, and blockages in equipment and pipes, which can disrupt the operation of the fermentation
process and lead to contamination and product loss.
