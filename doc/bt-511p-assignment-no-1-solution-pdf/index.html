Question # 1:
Label the name of the Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) shown in the below
picture. Also, describe their use/function and mention the BSL levels in which
they should be used. (2+2+1=5 marks)

Solution:

Labeling and Description of Personal Protective Equipment
(PPE)
(a) Gloves
Name: Disposable Gloves
Function:

  •   Protect hands from direct contact with infectious materials, harmful chemicals, or contaminated
      surfaces.
  •   Prevent cross-contamination between patients or samples.

Use in BSL Levels:

  •   BSL-1 to BSL-4
      (Used in all biosafety levels; material and layering depend on risk level.)

(b) Safety Goggles
Name: Protective Goggles
Function:

  •   Shield eyes from chemical splashes, aerosols, or infectious droplets.
  •   Provide side protection which regular glasses lack.

Use in BSL Levels:

  •   BSL-2 to BSL-4
      (Required when there's a risk of splashes or airborne pathogens.)

(c) Surgical Mask
Name: Surgical Face Mask
Function:

  •   Protects mouth and nose from droplets.
  •   Reduces transmission of microorganisms from the wearer to the environment.

Use in BSL Levels:

  •   BSL-1 and BSL-2
      (Not suitable for protection against aerosolized agents; limited use in higher BSLs.)

(d) N95 Respirator
Name: N95 Respirator Mask
Function:

  •   Filters ≥95% of airborne particles, including pathogens.
  •   Offers a tight seal around nose and mouth, unlike surgical masks.

Use in BSL Levels:

  •   BSL-2 (aerosol-generating procedures), BSL-3
      (Not sufficient for BSL-4 unless combined with other protective gear.)

(e) Lab Coat
Name: Laboratory Coat
Function:

  •   Protects skin and clothing from chemical and biological spills.
  •   Acts as a barrier against contamination.

Use in BSL Levels:

  •   BSL-1 and BSL-2
      (BSL-3 requires solid-front or wrap-around gowns; BSL-4 uses full-body suits.)

(f) Face Shield
Name: Full Face Shield
Function:

  •   Protects entire face from splashes, sprays, and potentially infectious materials.
  •   Worn over goggles and masks for extra protection.

Use in BSL Levels:

  •   BSL-2 and BSL-3
      (Especially during procedures with high splash risk.)

(g) Respirator with Exhalation Valve
Name: Reusable Respirator (e.g., P100 or other high-filtration masks)
Function:

  •   Provides high-level filtration for airborne particles.
  •   Exhalation valve helps reduce heat and moisture buildup.

Use in BSL Levels:

  •   BSL-3
      (More commonly used in high-risk aerosol-generating environments.)

(h) Full-Body PPE Suit with PAPR
Name: Positive Pressure Suit (also known as PAPR Suit)
Function:

  •   Fully encapsulated suit that provides maximum protection.
  •   Equipped with a powered air-purifying respirator (PAPR) system to ensure clean airflow.

Use in BSL Levels:

  •   BSL-4
      (Used in maximum containment labs handling deadly, aerosol-transmitted pathogens.)

(i) Full PPE Ensemble
Name: PPE Ensemble (Gloves, Gown, Goggles, and Mask)
Function:

  •   Demonstrates a standard PPE setup showing correct layering of gloves, gown/lab coat, surgical
      mask/N95, and protective goggles.
  •   Protects multiple exposure points including mucous membranes, skin, and respiratory
      pathways.

Use in BSL Levels:

  •   BSL-3
      (Used in high-risk laboratory procedures involving aerosol-transmitted pathogens, without the
      need for a full BSL-4 suit.)

Question # 2:

  a) How do you justify that the molecular weight of NaHCO₃ is 84.007 g/mol?
     (2 marks)
  b)How do you prepare a 50mM working solution (Final volume=100 mL)
     using the above 10M stock solution NaHCO₃. (3 marks)

Solution (a):
Justification of the Molecular Weight of NaHCO₃ (Sodium
Bicarbonate)
     The molecular weight (also called molar mass) of a compound is calculated by summing the
atomic masses of all atoms present in its chemical formula. For sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO₃), the
formula includes:

  •   1 atom of Sodium (Na)
  •   1 atom of Hydrogen (H)
  •   1 atom of Carbon (C)
  •   3 atoms of Oxygen (O)

The standard atomic weights of these elements are:

  •   Sodium (Na) = 22.989 g/mol
  •   Hydrogen (H) = 1.008 g/mol
  •   Carbon (C) = 12.011 g/mol
  •   Oxygen (O) = 15.999 g/mol

Now, calculating the molecular weight:

Molecular weight of NaHCO₃ =
22.989 (Na) + 1.008 (H) + 12.011 (C) + (15.999 × 3 for O)
= 22.989 + 1.008 + 12.011 + 47.997
= 84.005 g/mol

Due to rounding of atomic weights in periodic tables, this is commonly reported as 84.007 g/mol.
