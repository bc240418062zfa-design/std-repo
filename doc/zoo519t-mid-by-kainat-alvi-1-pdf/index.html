           PHYSIOLOGY (ZOO519T)
CURRENT MIDTERM PAPER FALL 2023(23-DEC)

                             SHORT QUESTIONS(3)

   1)    Define physiology & also name only all laws obey by
         physiology.

   ❖ Physiology
• Study of functions of tissues, organs and organ systems of animals. • To understand
the mechanisms operating in animals.
   ❖ Physiology Obeys the Laws of Physics and Chemistry

   o Ohm’s Law
   o Boyle’s Law
   o Ideal Gas Law
   o Law of Gravity
   o Kinetic & potential energy
   o Inertia
   o Momentum
   o Velocity
   o Drag

   2)    Conformers and its types

   ❖ Conformers
• Animals that conform to external changes • Are unable to maintain homeostasis for
internal conditions
   ❖ Types

   o Osmoconformers
   o Oxyconformers
   o Thermoconformers

   3)    Define Facilitated Diffusion.
Passive diffusion through the membrane down the conc. Gradient with the help of
transport proteins. • It is a passive process, not requiring energy in the form of ATP.
   4)    Antiporter
Antiporters are coupled transporters that transfer two solutes in opposite directions.
   5)    Action Potentials & Importance

   ❖ Definition

• Action potentials are the type of signals that neurons use to send information over
long distances. • Actions potentials are large but brief changes in membrane potential
that are propagated along axons without decrement.
  ❖ Importance of Action Potentials
• APs are responsible for every sensation, every memory, every thought — indeed every
impulse. • They control:
   o Effector responses
   o Activation of voltage-gated ion channels
   o Muscle contraction
   o Exocytosis

  6)    How action potential is generated?

  ❖ Stimulation
• When a stimulus is received at axon hillock, it causes some voltage-gated Na+
channels to open in the neuronal membrane. • Resultantly Na+ start to diffuse in. •
This results in a local depolarization due to increase in the Vm(voltage across the
neural membrane).• A depolarization in the range of -55 to -30 mV is the threshold that
triggers an AP. • If the intensity of stimulus is less, it causes a subthreshold
depolarization that fails to generate an AP.
  7)    What is neurofibrils write its function?

  ❖ Neurofibrils
• Delicate threads running from cell body into the axon and dendrites.
  ❖ Neurofibrils— Functions
• Give support and shape to the neuron. • Transport substances from cell body to the
distal cell processes.
  8)    Synapses
• Junctions between axon terminals of one neuron and the dendrites of another
neuron. • transmission of signals takes place.
  ❖ Types of Synapses

  1) Electrical synapses
  2) Chemical synapses

  9)    Quantal Release of Neurotransmitter
• Neurotransmitters are generally released in tiny packets called quanta. • Each
quantum may consist of about 2000 to 10,000 molecules of transmitter molecules.
  10) Few facts about myosin?

  ❖ Myosin Molecule
• A myosin molecule consists of two identical heavy chains. • These chains are coiled
together to form a long tail. • Myosin also has two globular heads, made from two
heavy chains and three or four calcium-binding light chains. • The heads form cross
bridges between the thick and the thin myofilaments during contraction.

Cross bridges are formed when actin (A) and myosin (M) bind and form a stable
complex actomyosin (AM).
        A + M = AM
• This happens in the absence of ATP.
Cross-bridges b/w myosin heads & actin pull the thin filament toward the center of
sarcomere. • The force for pulling is produced by the partial rotation of myosin heads
  11) Function of Myosin
Myosin is a protein found in muscles that plays a crucial role in muscle contraction. It
forms cross-bridges with actin filaments, allowing them to slide past each other. This
sliding action shortens the muscle fibers, resulting in muscle contraction. Myosin acts
like a motor, using ATP as an energy source to generate the force needed for muscle
movement.
  12) Rigor Mortis
• After death, human and other animal’s bodies gradually become rigid. • This
condition is called rigor mortis. • This rigidity happens because ATP’s are not available
in dead body for detachment of actin and myosin, so muscles cannot relax.
  13) How the Acetylcholine act as a Excitatory/Fast
      neurotransmitter?

  ❖ Release of Acetylcholine
Ach is excitatory at the neuromuscular junctions • It opens channels that allow Na+ to
flow in and K+ to flow out.
• At the neuromuscular junction, motor neurons release acetylcholine as
neurotransmitter. • ACh binds to receptors (ligand-gated ion channels) in postsynaptic
muscle fibers. • Opening of these channels causes movement of sodium (Na+) and
potassium (K+) ions.
  14) Parathormone (PTH)?
It is secreted by parathyroid gland. Increases blood Ca2+ levels; decreases blood
phosphate level
  15) How steroid hormone synthesized?
• Steroid hormones are synthesized from cholesterol. • They consist of four fused
carbon rings including three cyclohexyl rings and one cyclopentyl ring combined into a
single structure.
• Examples: hormones of adrenal cortex (cortisol and aldosterone) hormones of
ovaries (estrogen and progesterone) and hormone of testes (testosterone).

                             LONG QUESTIONS(5)

  1)     Define homeostasis and write its five Examples?

  ❖ Homeostasis—Definition

• To keep internal fluctuations in a narrow range. • The ability to protect internal
environment from the harms of fluctuations in external environment. • Homeostasis
does not mean to keep a fixed internal environment.
   ❖ Homeostasis—Examples

   o    Homeostatic regulation of water
   o    Homeostatic regulation of temperature
   o    Regulation of pH
   o    Regulation of glucose concentration
   o    Regulation of osmotic pressure
   o    Regulation of oxygen level
   o    Regulation of ion conc.

   2)     Describe Feedback Control System & its Types.

   ❖ Feedback Control Systems Definition
• Mechanism of self-regulation of Biological processes.
