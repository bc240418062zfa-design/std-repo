Define Pesticides:
Pesticides are chemical substances used to control or eliminate pests, such as insects, weeds, and
fungi, in order to protect crops, livestock, and public health from their detrimental effects.

Mechanism of resistant in plants, tissue culture:
Resistant mechanisms in plants involve genetic adaptations that enable them to withstand the
harmful effects of pests or diseases. These mechanisms can include physical barriers, production
of toxic compounds, and altered biochemical pathways. This enables the production of disease-
free and genetically identical plants, providing a valuable tool for breeding. Plants can also
produce toxic compounds, like alkaloids or terpenes, that deter pests or inhibit pathogen growth.
Another mechanism involves the activation of defense responses, such as the production of
antimicrobial proteins or phytohormones, to fight off invaders. Additionally, plants can undergo
genetic changes, including the activation of resistance genes or the modification of metabolic
pathways, to enhance their resistance against specific threats.

Tissue culture:
Tissue culture is a laboratory technique that involves the propagation of plant cells, tissues, or
organs in a controlled environment to produce genetically identical and disease-free plants for
various applications.

Application of DNA based marker:
DNA-based markers have a wide range of applications across different fields. In agriculture, they
are used for marker-assisted breeding, genetic mapping, and identifying desirable traits in crops.
In medicine, they assist in diagnosing genetic disorders, determining drug responses, and
predicting disease risks. In forensic science, DNA markers aid in criminal investigations and
identifying individuals. They are also utilized in evolutionary studies, conservation genetics, and
studying population dynamics. Additionally, DNA markers play a role in biodiversity
assessment, paternity testing, and tracking the spread of infectious diseases.

Germplasm preservation:
Germplasm is living tissue from which new plants can be grown. It can be a seed or another
plant part a leaf, a piece of stem, pollen or even just a few cells that can be turned into a whole
plant. Germplasm preservation refers to the collection, storage, and maintenance of plant or
animal genetic resources to ensure their long-term availability for future use in breeding,
research, and conservation purposes.
