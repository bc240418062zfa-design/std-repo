    1. Lecture 2 – Usefulness of Microorganisms:
       How do the small size and rapid reproduction of microorganisms make them ideal for industrial and
       environmental processes?

Microorganisms’ small size allows cultivation in compact bioreactors, maximizing surface-to-volume
ratios for efficient nutrient and oxygen transfer. Their rapid reproduction means biomass and target
metabolites accumulate quickly, shortening production cycles. Together, these traits reduce spatial and
temporal resource demands, lowering operational costs. In industrial fermentation, microbes synthesize
enzymes, biofuels, pharmaceuticals, and food ingredients in days rather than months. Environmentally,
their fast growth accelerates pollutant degradation in bioremediation, wastewater treatment, and
composting. Genetic engineering can further enhance these capabilities, enabling scalable, controllable
processes that address global energy, healthcare, and environmental challenges with minimal land use and
rapid responsiveness to changing conditions.

    2. Lecture 5 – Scope of Microbial Biotechnology I:
       What are some promising applications of microbial biotechnology in agriculture, and how do they
       contribute to food security?

1. Biofertilizers

     •   Rhizobia, Azospirillum and phosphate-solubilizing bacteria
     •   Enhance N‐fixation and P-availability, reducing synthetic inputs

2. Mycorrhizal & PGPR Inoculants

•    Improve nutrient uptake and root growth
•    Boost drought and salinity tolerance

3. Biopesticides

•    Bacillus thuringiensis and Trichoderma species
•    Target pests and diseases, lowering chemical residues

4. Microbial Consortia & Seed Coatings

•    Restore soil microbiome balance
•    Protect seedlings and promote early vigor
