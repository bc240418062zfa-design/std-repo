Topic 091: Middle English Syntax

What resulted in the leveling of inflections in Middle English?

Leveling of inflections resulted in syntactic and semantic relationships that had been
signaled by the endings on words.
How did Old English and Middle English differ in expressing grammatical
functions of consecutive nouns?

In Old English, grammatical functions were clear from the endings, while in Middle
English, their functions might become uncertain.

What was the most direct way to avoid ambiguity in Middle English syntax?

The most direct way was through limiting the possible patterns of word order.

Describe the shift observed in Middle English syntax.

 COMPILED BY SIR ZAHID                                                             1

There was a shift from a highly synthetic stage in Old English to the highly analytic
stages of Late Middle English and Modern English.

What period does the Peterborough Chronicle span, and what changes can be
traced in its text?
The Peterborough Chronicle spans from 1070 to 1154, and it shows a significant loss
of inflections followed by a corresponding rigidity of word order.

Topic 092: Middle English Vocabulary

What influenced the loss of inflections and simplification of grammar in Middle
English vocabulary?

The loss of inflections and simplification of grammar was influenced directly by the
use of French.

What aspects are significant in understanding the nature of the interchange of
words between French and English?

Mutual & frequent, no sudden change, varying tempo, and a slow beginning.

Give examples of French words influencing fashion and social life in Middle
English.

Examples include fashion, dress, apparel, gown, robe, garment, and others.
Which areas of life in Middle English were influenced by French words related
to government and administration?

Words such as government, crown, state, empire, parliament, and titles of offices
like chancellor and treasurer.

What were some Middle English words borrowed from French related to law
and the military?
Legal terms: felony, trespass, assault; Military terms: army, navy, battle, captain,
lieutenant.

Topic 093: Borrowings in Middle English

 COMPILED BY SIR ZAHID                                                            2

How did literature contribute to the entrance of French words into Middle
English?

Literature was a channel for the entrance of French words, and Middle English
literature was based directly on French originals.

How did Latin borrowings differ from French borrowings in Middle English?

Latin borrowings were less popular and generally entered through the written
language, often through literature.

Topic 094: The Rise of Standard English
Which dialect contributed the most to the formation of standard written
English?

The East Midland dialect contributed the most.

What were some characteristics of the East Midland dialect that made it a basis
for standardization?

It represented a compromise in sounds and inflections, and it was less conservative
than the Southern dialect and less radical than the Northern.

What role did the universities, Oxford and Cambridge, play in the rise of
Standard English?
The presence of universities in the East Midland region, particularly Oxford and
Cambridge, contributed to its intellectual development.

Topic 095: Importance of London Standard

Why was London significant in the rise of Standard English?
London was the political capital, commercial center, seat of judicial tribunals, and a
focus of social and intellectual activities in England.

How did London's economy contribute to the spread of Standard English?

London's economy served as an "engine of communication and exchange,"
facilitating the distribution of ideas and information.

 COMPILED BY SIR ZAHID                                                             3

What factors made London English influential, and what was the influence
reciprocal?

London's status as a political and economic hub made its English influential, and the
influence was reciprocal as people took back forms and usages from the city.

What significant event in 1476 contributed to the spread of London English?

The introduction of printing in 1476 played a crucial role in the dissemination of
London English, with Caxton using the current speech of London.

By when had the London standard been accepted in most parts of the country,
and what was a key reason for this acceptance?

In the latter part of the fifteenth century, the London standard had been accepted, at
least in writing, with the introduction of printing being a key reason.
