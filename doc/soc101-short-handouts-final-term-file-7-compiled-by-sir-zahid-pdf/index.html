Lesson-31

                               RELIGION-I

Topic-168: The Sacred and Profane

What did Durkheim find essential to the sociological understanding of religion?

Answer: Durkheim found that people establish a line between the sacred and
profane, where the sacred inspires awe, reverence, and respect, while the profane
refers to common, accepted behavior in daily life.

How did Durkheim differentiate between the sacred and profane?

Answer: The sacred involves unique aspects of social life that evoke reverence,
while the profane refers to common, everyday behavior. Objects can be considered
sacred by a group, leading to differences in perception between groups.

Give an example illustrating the difference in sacredness between groups.

COMPILED BY SIR ZAHID                                                          1

Answer: In Hinduism, cows are considered sacred, and farmers in America may view
them as valuable animals but not hesitate to send them to slaughter when their milk
production declines.

Topic-169: Religious Symbols, Beliefs and Rituals

Why do all religions have holy symbols?

Answer: Holy symbols, including phrases, places, meals, etc., enable interaction
with the sacred, which exists outside the world of senses.

What are two potent religious symbols for Christians?

Answer: Bread and wine are considered potent religious symbols for Christians,
symbolically transformed into the flesh and blood of Christ during church rituals.

What is the purpose of rituals in religion?

Answer: Rituals are formal representations that distance participants from the
ordinary, focusing attention on the sacred. They include prayers, chants, dances,
fasting, and sacrifice.

Topic-170: The Difference between Religion and Magic

How do religious and magical rituals differ in terms of objectives?

Answer: Religious rituals have long-term collective objectives, while magical rituals
are solitary actions with immediate ends.

What distinguishes magic from prayer and sacrifice?

Answer: Magic is a ritual attempt to compel supernatural entities or forces to
influence events in the natural world, unlike prayer and sacrifice that seek
intervention on one's behalf.

Give an example of where magic is common in contemporary society.

Answer: Magic is common in high-risk activities like exams, sports, or gambling,
where individuals may use rituals or lucky charms alongside preparation and hard
work.

Topic-171: Global Religious Diversity

What are the four ideal types in the classification system of religious ideas?

Answer: Animatism, Animism, Theism, and Ethical religions based on abstract
ideals.

What is animatism, and how is it prevalent in contemporary industrial culture?

Answer: Animatism holds that supernatural forces govern the cosmos. Modern
forms include beliefs in crystals, pyramids, and lucky charms, reflecting a belief in
mana-like abilities.

Name the three most dominant monotheistic religions.

Answer: Islam, Christianity, and Judaism.

What is the commonality among Judaism, Christianity, and Islam in terms of
belief?

Answer: They are monotheistic religions, affirming the presence of a solitary, all-
powerful deity.

Topic-172: Religion and Society: Three Perspectives

What period saw the emergence of sociology, and why was it a time of
upheaval?

Answer: The nineteenth century, marked by the Industrial Revolution; upheaval due
to societal changes.

Name three pioneering sociologists who contributed to the study of religion in
sociology.

 COMPILED BY SIR ZAHID                                                            3

Answer: Emile Durkheim, Karl Marx, Max Weber.

Topic-173: Religion and Functionalism

According to Durkheim, what stage did society advance to, and what did
individuals believe during this stage?

Answer: Transition from the theological stage to the positive stage; belief in
scientific principles.

What did Durkheim identify as exemplifying religion in its purest form?

Answer: Totemism, a religion practiced by Australian Aborigines.

How did Durkheim argue that religion fosters societal cohesion?

Answer: Through shared norms, symbols, rituals; psychological support, comfort,
morale, inspiration.

Topic-174: Religion from the Conflict Perspective

According to Karl Marx, what was the role of religion in society?

Answer: A tool by which the wealthy and powerful maintain privileged positions;
serves as the opiate of the people.

How did Marx view the relationship between economic base and religious
ideas?

Answer: Dominating class controls religious ideas, promoting submission and
acceptance of injustice.

How do conflict theorists extend Marx's ideas regarding religion?

Answer: Acknowledge the possibility that subordinate groups may use religious
beliefs to subvert social norms and bolster their power.

According to Max Weber, what did his study of faiths reveal about the
relationship between religion and capitalism?

Answer: Religious values and beliefs can encourage economic and social change.

What concept did Weber introduce to explain the success of early Calvinist
capitalists?

Answer: The Protestant ethic, emphasizing ascetic moral ethic and self-denial.
