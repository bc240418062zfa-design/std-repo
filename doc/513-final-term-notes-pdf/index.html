Content-Based Instruction

It is the teaching of content or information in the language being learned with little or no direct effort to teach the
language separately from the content being taught.

Principles of Content-Based Instruction

    1. Content-Based Instruction better reflects learners' needs for learning a second language
    2. People learn a second language more successfully when they use the language as a means of acquiring
       information, rather than mastering the language for its own sake.

Assumptions of Content-Based Instruction

    1. Language is purposeful
       Role of language as a vehicle for learning content entails the centrality of linguistic entities longer than single
       sentences, because the focus of teaching is how meanings are constructed and communicated through texts and
       discourse.
    2. Language is text and discourse-based
       In a content-based class, students are often involved in activities that link the skills. Hence, students might read
       and take notes, listen and write a summary, or respond orally.
    3. Language use draws on integrated skills
       Language is used for specific purposes. The purpose may be academic, vocational, social, or recreational but
       it gives direction, shape, and ultimately meaning to discourse and texts
    4. Students learn best when instruction addresses students' needs
    5. Teaching builds on the previous experience of the learners

Student goals of an immersion program include
(1) Developing a high level of proficiency in the FL

(2) Developing positive attitudes toward foreign language speakers and their culture(s)

(3) Developing English language skills commensurate with expectations for a student's age and abilities

(4) Gaining designated skills and knowledge in the content areas of the curriculum

Immigrant On-Arrival Programs - focus on the newly arrived immigrants in a country and their need for survival.
Such learners typically need to learn how to deal with differing kinds of real-world content as a basis for social survival.

Students with Limited English Proficiency (SLEP) programs – these are governmentally mandated programs to serve
especially those children whose parents might be served by the on-arrival programs, but more generally designed to
provide in-class instruction to those whose language competence is insufficient to participate fully in normal school
instruction.

Language for Specific Purposes (LSP) - a movement that seeks to serve the language needs of learners who need
language in order to carry out specific roles (e.g., student, engineer, technician, and nurse) rather than master the
language for its own sake.

LSP/EST subfields

       ESP (English for Specific Purposes)
       EOP (English for Occupational Purposes)
       EAP (English for Academic Purposes)

Assumptions of Task-based instruction

      The focus is on process rather than product.
      Learners learn language by interacting communicatively and purposefully
      Basic elements are purposeful activities and tasks
      Activities and tasks of a task-based syllabus are sequenced according to difficulty
      Activities and tasks can be either: those that learners might need to achieve in real life; those that have a
       pedagogical purpose specific to the classroom

Difficulty of a task depends on a range of factors

      Previous experience
      Complexity of the task
      Language required
      The degree of support available

Task-based training identified several key areas of concern

      Analysis of real-world task-use situations
      The translation of these into teaching tasks descriptions
      The detailed design of instructional tasks
      The sequencing of instructional tasks in classroom training

Four major categories of team performance function

   1. Orientation functions (processes for generating and distributing information necessary to task accomplishment
      to team members)
   2. Organizational functions (processes necessary for members to coordinate actions necessary for task
      performance)
   3. Adaptation functions (processes occurring as team members adapt their performance to each other to complete
      the task).
   4. Motivational functions (defining team objectives and "energizing the group" to complete the task)
