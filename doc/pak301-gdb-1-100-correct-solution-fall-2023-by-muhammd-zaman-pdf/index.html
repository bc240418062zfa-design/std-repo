Solution:
The decision of the Government of Pakistan to deport Afghan refugees
may have complex economic implications:

1. Economic Pressure: The mass deportation of Afghan refugees has
  led to increased prices for moving goods across the border, causing a
  more than doubled cost for transportation. This puts economic
  pressure on businesses relying on cross-border trade and logistics.
2. Job Market: Afghanistan's economy already struggles to provide
  enough jobs for its population, and the influx of deported refugees
  may exacerbate unemployment issues in both countries.
3. Deportation Costs: The deportation process itself incurs costs,
  including resources for enforcement and logistics, impacting
  Pakistan's budget and resources.
4. Regional Stability: Mass deportations pose risks to regional stability,
  as highlighted by international observers. Economic challenges can
  contribute to geopolitical tensions in the region.
5. Confiscation of Funds: Reports suggest that Pakistan is confiscating
  most of the refugees' money during deportation, potentially leading to

6. Connectivity Benefits: On the contrary, some argue that connectivity

  economic development, but the abrupt deportations may hinder
  diplomatic and economic cooperation.
7. Humanitarian Concerns: Deportations raise humanitarian concerns,
  and the international community's response may influence economic
  relationships and aid to Pakistan.
8. Trade Disruptions: The deportation policy has disrupted trade flows,
  impacting businesses dependent on cross-border trade and affecting
  Pakistan's economy negatively.
9. Diplomatic Relations: Abrupt deportations can strain diplomatic
  relations, affecting potential economic partnerships and
  collaborations in the future.
10.   Public Perception: The public perception of the deportation
  policy can influence consumer behavior and investor confidence,
  impacting various sectors of the economy.
11.   Legal Implications: Deportations may lead to legal challenges,
  incurring additional costs and potential compensation, affecting the
  government's financial stability.
