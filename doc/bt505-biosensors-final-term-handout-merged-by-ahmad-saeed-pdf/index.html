Lesson 1 : Characteristics of Biosensor

Summary/Handouts

Introduction

Some characteristics of biosensors must be considered before making them. These characteristics
are:

       Sensitivity
       Selectivity
       Stability
       Reproducibility
       Response
       Biocompatibility
       Low background

Sensitivity is the response per unit. It is the response of the bio receptor element to the
Analyte. Selectivity is the response of receptors elements to the analyte. It changes with a change
in the concentration of the analyte. Stability is the degree of susceptibility to ambient
disturbances. There must be internal and external stability. Reproducibility is the identical
response. It provides high reliability that is characterized by precision and sensitivity. The
biosensor must be user friendly and should give accurate results. The biosensor must have low
noise. Accuracy and precision are very important for low noise. Another important property is
LOD (Limit of detection). This is the minimum amount that a biosensor can detect.

In this topic, students will learn about:

       Characteristics of biosensors
       Important features of biosensors
       Limit of detection

       Internal and external stability

Lesson 2 : Analyte Background

Summary/Handouts

Introduction

Biosensors are designed to detect targeted molecules at the analytical stage. These targeted
molecules or elements are known as Analyte. Analyte could be a toxin, molecule and pathogen
or other. Analyte binds with its bio receptor molecule and generated a recognizable signal. For
example, a glucose molecule is an Analyte in Glucometer that generates a signal after binding
with its bio receptor element ‘glucose oxidase’. The biosensors available in the market can detect
glucose, lactic acid, uric acid, cortisol and bacteria.

Chemistry and biochemistry play important roles in designing biosensors. The characteristics of
analyte, bio receptor elements, and support material must be completely understood to design a
biosensor. Clinical chemistry is an important branch of biochemistry and chemistry. It provided
many important detection methods for biological and chemical molecules. For example, a rapid
urine test for glucose detection by dipstick method. The dipstick consists of a plastic strip on
which the chemical is fixed in dry form. The application of the sample detects glucose in urine.

Physical components are basic units of bio sensors. These are:

       Detector
       Signal converter
       Amplifier
       Measuring of signals
       Display unit

In this topic students will be able to learn:

       Basic principles involved in designing biosensors
       Chemistry of biosensors and analyte
       Physical components of biosensors
       Role of basic sciences in designing of biosensors

Lesson 3 : Components of a Biosensor

Summary/Handouts

Introduction

Biosensors consist of two types of components: Biological components or Biological
Recognition Element (BRE) and Physical components consisting of input and output devices.
Proteins are used as Biological Recognition Element (BRE). Functional proteins are most
important. Two types of functional proteins are used in biosensors at a large scale. These are
enzymes and antibodies. Proteins are liable to denaturation that can lose binding capacity at high
temperatures and pH. The enzymes (proteins) are biological catalysts produced by living cells.
These proteins bind to specific receptors known as substrates at specific receptor sites. The other
functional proteins are antibodies. Antibodies (blood proteins) are produced by the immune
system and can bind with counteracting antigens. These are Y-shaped. Besides this, microbial
cells, tissues, and viruses can be used as BRE. These are sources of enzymes. They can
regenerate after immobilization of cells. Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) is also used as BRE in
biosensors.

Physical components of biosensor include:

       Detector
       Transducer
       Amplifier
       Display Unit

The signals generated in biosensors are usually weak that are amplified by the amplifier. The
detector detects the signals produced by the biorecognition process. Detector sent signals
towards the transducer and the transducer converts signals from physical form to electronic
signals and vice versa. The display unit of the biosensor consists of input and output devices.
These signals are processed after amplification and displayed through LCDs or small printers in
a user-friendly manner.

In this topic, students will learn about:

       Components of biosensors
       Types of biological and physical components
       How biological components work
       The functionality of physical components
