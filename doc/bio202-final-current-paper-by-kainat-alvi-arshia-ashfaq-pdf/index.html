                                SHORT QUESTIONS(3)
   1) Types of lipoprotein
The lipoprotein particles include
      1) chylomicrons (CM),
      2) very-low-density lipoproteins (VLDL),
      3) low-density lipoproteins (LDL), and
      4) high-density lipoproteins (HDL).

   2) Components of nucleotides
Nucleotides are composed of three main components:
   1) a nitrogenous base (adenine, guanine, cytosine, thymine, or uracil),
   2) a pentose sugar (ribose or deoxyribose), and
   3) a phosphate group.
These components come together to form the building blocks of nucleic acids like DNA and RNA.
   3) Write three modified bases?
These unusual bases(also called modified bases) include
   1) Dihydrouracil
   2) Pseudouridine
   3) Thymine.

   4) Functional group of pyrimidines and purines?
The most important functional groups of pyrimidines and purines are
      1) ring nitrogens,
      2) carbonyl groups, and
      3) exocyclic amino groups.

   5) Three names of polyunsaturated fatty acid
Polyunsaturated fatty acids contain more than one double bond • These include:
   1) Linoleic acid (18: 2Δ9, 12 )
   2) α-Linolenic acid (18 : 3Δ9, 12, 15)
   3) Arachidonic acid series (20 : 4Δ5, 8, 11, 14)

   6) Enzyme activity in three steps
• Enzyme-catalyzed reactions have three basic steps:

      1) binding of substrate: E+S ES
      2) conversion of bound substrate to bound product: ES       EP
      3) release of product : EP   E+P

   7) Mention inorganic cofactors
The inorganic cofactor involved in coenzymes include:
          a. Zinc (Zn)
          b. Iron (Fe)
          c. Magnesium (Mg)
          d. Copper (Cu)
          e. Manganese (Mn)

   8) Name Of three pyrimidine
•Pyrimidines include:
   A. Cytosine C—in both DNA and RNA
   B. Thymine (T)—only in DNA

   C. Uracil (U) —only in RNA
         a. Cytosine is 2-oxy-4- amino-pyrimidine
         b. Thymine is 2,4-dioxy-5- methyl-pyrimidine
         c. Uracil is 2,4-dioxypyrimidine

   9) Pepsin PH
pepsin, a digestive enzyme in the stomach, is maximally active at pH 2.
   10)        Concentration of tRNA in cell
tRNAs compose roughly 20% of total cellular RNA • There are at least 20 species of tRNA molecules in
every cell.
   11)        Number of carbon atom in Stearic acid
Stearic acid is a saturated fatty acid, meaning it does not contain any double bonds in its carbon
chain. It consists of 18 carbon atoms in total.
   12)        Monosaccharides
Monosaccharides (simple sugars) are those which cannot be hydrolyzed further into simpler forms.
e.g: Glucose
   13)        Polysaccharides with two example
These are complex carbohydrates made up of multiple monosaccharide units. Examples include
starch (energy storage in plants), glycogen (energy storage in animals), and cellulose (structural
component in plant cell walls).
   14)        How fireflies produce light?
The light produced by fireflies is the result of a reaction involving the protein luciferin and ATP,
catalyzed by the enzyme luciferase.
   15)        Result of reduction of glutamate
• Removal of glutamate residue from LTC4(4 is subscript) forms LTD4 • LTD4 on removal of glycine
becomes LTE4
   16)        Hypercholesterolemia
"hypercholesterolemia," which is a condition characterized by high levels of cholesterol in the blood.
This condition can increase the risk of heart disease and other cardiovascular issues.
   17)        Effect of increase in GC base pairs
• The higher the content of GC base pairs, the higher the melting point of the DNA. • This is because
GC base pairs, with three hydrogen bonds, require more heat energy to dissociate than AT base pairs.
   18)        Enantiomers and diastereomers
Stereoisomers that are mirror images of each other are called enantiomers
Pairs of stereoisomers that are not mirror images of each other are called diastereomers
   19)    Diff. B/w Tyrosine Tryptophan and phenyl alanine on ring
     structure
Tyrosine: Amino acid with phenyl group and hydroxyl (-OH).
Tryptophan: Amino acid with indole ring and nitrogen atom.
Phenylalanine: Amino acid with simple benzene ring.
   20)        Cis-isomerism
Cis-isomerism refers to the arrangement of atoms or groups on the same side of a double bond in a
molecule. It is important because it affects the physical and chemical properties of the molecule, such
as its melting point, boiling point, and reactivity.
   21)        Mention the name and symbol of alanine arginine
The name and symbol of alanine are “Alanine” and “Ala” respectively. For arginine, the name and
symbol are “Arginine” and “Arg” respectively.
   22)        Allosteric hemoglobin
Allosteric hemoglobin refers to the ability of hemoglobin to undergo conformational changes in
response to the binding of molecules, such as oxygen or carbon dioxide, at sites other than the active
site. This allows for the regulation of oxygen binding and release in different tissues.

   23)        Eicosanoid
 Eicosanoids are a large group of lipid messengers with potent effects on every tissue in the body.
Eicosanoids are derived from metabolism of 20- carbon, polyunsaturated fatty acids (eicosanoic
acids). Eicosanoids are derived from either Omega-3 (ω-3) or Omega-6 (ω-6) fatty acids. Arachidonic
acid is the most common precursor of the eicosanoids.
   24)        Structure of the amino acid
The structure of an amino acid includes a central carbon atom (α-carbon) bonded to an amino group
(NH2), a carboxyl group (COOH), a hydrogen atom (H), and a variable side chain known as the R
group. The R group determines the specific characteristics of each amino acid.
   25)        Bond between DNA strand
The bond that holds the two strands of DNA together is a hydrogen bond. These hydrogen bonds form
between complementary nitrogenous bases: adenine (A) with thymine (T) and cytosine (C) with
guanine (G). This base pairing is a key structural feature of the DNA double helix.
