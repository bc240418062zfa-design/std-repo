                    EDU505
Terms and Explanations:

  1. Educational Development: The process of improving the structure, curriculum, and
      teaching methods to meet societal and individual needs.
  2. Education Policy: Guidelines and principles developed by governments to structure and
      regulate education systems.
  3. Colonial Education: Educational systems established during colonial rule, often
      prioritizing the colonizer’s language and values over indigenous traditions.
  4. Identity Crisis: A conflict in cultural or personal identity often caused by the introduction
      of foreign education systems.
  5. Wardha Scheme: A scheme advocating free and compulsory education for children aged
      7–14 in their mother tongue.
  6. Vidya Mandir Scheme (VMS): A basic education initiative focused on rural areas,
      criticized for its perceived alignment with Hindu cultural ideals.
  7. Sharif Commission: A 1959 report aimed at reorganizing Pakistan's education system to
      promote nationalism and modernity.
  8. National Integration: The role of education in uniting diverse communities within a
      country.
  9. Homogenization: Efforts in education to create a unified national identity, often at the
      expense of regional or cultural diversity.
  10. Ayub Khan's Educational Reforms: Policies prioritizing modernization and national
      identity through education in Pakistan during his military rule.

More Specific Terms from Policy and Philosophy:

  11. Philosophy of Education: The study of education’s purpose, process, and ideals, often
      influenced by national ideology.
  12. Manifest Functions of Education: Recognized and intended roles, such as skill
      development and socialization.
  13. Latent Functions of Education: Unintended consequences, like reinforcing social
      hierarchies or creating social networks.
  14. Religious Neutrality in Education: Policies ensuring that no specific religion dominates
      public education systems.
  15. Praeparatio Evangelica: The concept of preparing minds through education to later accept
      religious doctrines.
  16. Educational Sociology: The study of how education interacts with and influences societal
      structures and values.

  17. Dyarchy in Education: Shared control of educational governance between British
      officials and local authorities in colonial India.
  18. Aligarh Scheme: A proposal for Muslim-centric education, focusing on independence
      from non-Muslim influences.
  19. National Curriculum: A standardized framework outlining what students across a nation
      should learn.
  20. Democratic Education: An approach prioritizing participation, equality, and the
      development of critical thinking.

  21. Basic Education: Education focusing on fundamental skills, such as literacy, numeracy,
      and moral education, often tailored to rural or underprivileged communities.
  22. Modernization through Education: Using education to align a nation with contemporary
      global trends in technology, economy, and governance.
  23. Two-Nation Theory: The ideological basis for the creation of Pakistan, emphasizing the
      need for separate educational structures for Muslims and Hindus.
  24. Civic Education: Teaching students about their rights, responsibilities, and roles in society
      to foster good citizenship.
  25. Character Building: A primary aim of education focused on developing moral and ethical
      qualities in students.
  26. Religious Instruction: Education related to specific religious doctrines, often debated in
      secular versus religious education contexts.
  27. Secular Education: An education system free from religious influences, focusing on
      universal values and skills.
  28. Cultural Heritage in Education: Preserving and promoting national culture, traditions,
      and languages through the curriculum.
  29. Missionary Schools: Educational institutions established by missionaries to spread
      Christianity, often accompanied by Western education practices.
  30. Teacher Training Institutes: Institutions aimed at improving teaching skills and
      methodologies for educators.

Educational Aims and Philosophies:

  31. Self-Realization: The process of achieving one's potential through education, as
      emphasized in spiritual and philosophical traditions.
  32. Vocational Education: Preparing students for specific trades or professions to meet
      economic demands.
  33. Social Aims of Education: Using education to develop individuals who contribute to
      societal welfare and progress.
  34. Individual Aims of Education: Focusing on personal growth and fulfillment, balancing
      societal expectations.
  35. Complete Living Aim: An educational philosophy advocating for preparation in all
      aspects of life, from personal well-being to societal responsibilities.
  36. Adjustment Aim: Preparing students to adapt to changing environments and societal
      needs.

   37. Harmonious Development: Ensuring balanced growth in physical, mental, emotional, and
       social aspects of an individual.
   38. Leisure Aim: Educating students to use their free time productively and for personal
       enrichment.
   39. Knowledge Aim: Highlighting the importance of acquiring knowledge as a tool for
       personal and societal progress.
   40. Spiritual Aim: Promoting inner growth, morality, and enlightenment through education.

Education and National Identity:

   41. National Character Building: Shaping a unified identity among citizens through
       education policies and curriculum.
   42. Patriotism in Education: Instilling a sense of national pride and loyalty in students.
   43. Education for Nation-Building: Using schools as tools to develop a cohesive and
       progressive society.
   44. Language as a Unifier: Promoting a national language in education to strengthen cultural
       and political unity.
   45. Islamic Education: Incorporating Islamic values and teachings into the curriculum to align
       with Pakistan’s national ideology.
