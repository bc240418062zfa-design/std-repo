What is Fermentation? Explain its types and applications in detail
Fermentation is a metabolic process in which organisms convert organic substrates—most
commonly carbohydrates—into simpler compounds, regenerating the reduced coenzyme NAD⁺
in the absence (or limitation) of oxygen. In essence, it is an anaerobic pathway that allows cells
to produce energy (ATP) when oxidative phosphorylation is not possible or efficient.
Fermentation pathways are harnessed not only by microorganisms (yeasts, bacteria, certain
fungi) but also occur in animal cells (e.g., muscle) under hypoxic conditions.

   1. Ethanol Fermentation
         o Pathway: Glycolysis converts glucose → 2 pyruvate, then pyruvate
             decarboxylase yields acetaldehyde + CO₂; alcohol dehydrogenase reduces
             acetaldehyde → ethanol while oxidizing NADH → NAD⁺.
         o Significance: Widely used in brewing (beer, wine), baking (CO₂ for dough rise),
             and industrial bioethanol production.
   2. Lactic Acid Fermentation
         o Homolactic: 2 pyruvate → 2 lactic acid via lactate dehydrogenase; yields 2 ATP
             per glucose, regenerating NAD⁺.
         o Heterolactic: Employs the phosphoketolase pathway, producing equimolar lactic
             acid, ethanol, and CO₂.
         o Significance: Central to yogurt, cheese, sauerkraut, kimchi, sourdough, and
             pickling industries.
   3. Propionic Acid Fermentation

      o Significance: Industrial production of solvents (acetone, butanol), bioplastics

2. Classification by Organism
  •   Yeast Fermentations: Predominantly ethanol and CO₂; critical to alcoholic beverage and
      baking industries.
  •   Bacterial Fermentations: Diverse products: lactic acid by lactic acid bacteria (LAB),
      propionic acid by propionibacteria, butyric acid by clostridia.
  •   Fungal Fermentations: Certain molds (e.g., Aspergillus spp.) produce enzymes, organic
      acids, and secondary metabolites.

3. Industrial and Biotechnological Applications
  1. Food & Beverage Production
        o Dairy: Yogurt and cheese rely on LAB to acidify milk, coagulate proteins,
            develop flavor.
        o Vegetable Fermentation: Sauerkraut, pickles, kimchi; preservation via
            acidification and probiotic enrichment.
        o Alcoholic Beverages: Beer, wine, spirits; ethanol fermentation underpins global
            brewing and winemaking.
  2. Biofuel & Renewable Energy
        o Bioethanol: Large-scale fermentation of sugarcane, corn, and cellulosic biomass
            by yeast to produce fuel additive for transportation.
        o Biogas Production: Anaerobic digestion in waste treatment yields methane-rich
            biogas via sequential fermentative steps.
  3. Pharmaceuticals & Fine Chemicals
        o Organic Acids: Lactic acid (polylactic acid bioplastics), propionic acid
            (preservatives), butyric acid (flavorings).
        o Solvents: Acetone, butanol from clostridial fermentations for coatings, extraction,
            chemical synthesis.
        o Antibiotics & Vitamins: Streptomyces fermentation yields antibiotics; P.
            freudenreichii produces vitamin B₁₂.
