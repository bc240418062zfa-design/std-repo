                                              Week 1
                                           The Profession

       Introduction
       Professionalism
       Traits of a Professional
       Applying Professionalism in Daily Life

    1. Introduction

Profession: A job that needs special training and a degree (e.g., Doctor, Engineer, Teacher).

Professional: A person who does a professional job, follows high standards, and has the right skills to do
their work.

Professional Responsibilities

In the IT world, being a professional is not just about writing code. It includes:

       Managing networks and making software.
       Keeping hardware and software updated.

Important Point: An engineer must have "Professional Knowledge" AND "Professional Behavior."

    2. Professionalism:

It is not just about what you know; it is a way of thinking and living.

Traits of a Profession

A job is called a "Profession" if it has these 4 things:

    1. Special Skills: It needs learning and practice.
    2. Helping Society: The goal is to help people, not just earn money.

   3. High Quality: Always trying to do the best work.
   4. Giving Back: Sharing your knowledge to help the community.

A Professional Behaves Ethically

Ethics is more than just following the law. It is about doing what is "right."

      Breaking the Law: If you break the law, you can go to jail or pay a fine.
      Breaking a Moral: If you do something bad, people will not respect you (it ruins your reputation).
      Breaking an Ethic: If you do the wrong thing, you will feel bad in your heart and mind (it ruins your
       conscience).
      Main Point: A person can break all three (Law, Morals, and Ethics) at the same time.

   3. Traits of a Professional

Being a professional means people expect these 10 good qualities from you:

   1. Seriousness: Take your work seriously. Do not treat it like a joke.
   2. Wanting to do Better: Always try to improve your work every day.
   3. Handling Problems: When something goes wrong suddenly, stay calm and find a solution.
   4. Communication Skills: Speak and write in a way that is clear, short, and confident.
   5. Enthusiasm: Show excitement and a positive attitude toward your work.
   6. Helpfulness: Always be ready to help your teammates.
   7. Taking Initiative: Don't wait for someone to tell you what to do. Start the work yourself.
   8. Cool Under Pressure: Stay calm and happy even when you have too much work or stress.
   9. Stay Focused: Keep your eyes on your goal and do not get distracted.
   10. Don’t Follow, Lead: Do not just follow others. Try to show the way and be a leader.

   4. Applying Professionalism in Real Life

Scenario # 1
You are the owner of a software engineering company. Your employees (engineers) want you to pay for
them to attend training.
How would you respond in a way that is legal, moral, and ethical?

Answer: "I support your request for training because it helps you improve and helps our company grow.
To be fair and professional, we will pay for the course, but we will have a clear agreement: the skills you
learn must be used to help our current projects, and you will share your new knowledge with the rest of
the team."

Explanation:

      Legal: You create a clear, written agreement or contract before paying for the training.
      Moral: You show that you care about your employees' future and their "wanting to do better".
      Ethical: You are being fair to everyone and "giving back" to your staff to make the profession
       stronger.

Scenario # 2
You are the owner of a software engineering company. Your employees (engineers) want you to let them
do pro bono work for a local non-profit organization on company time.
How would you respond in a way that is legal, moral, and ethical?

Answer: "I support this because helping our community is a great way of giving back to society. We will
allow a set number of hours per month for this project to make sure our own company's work stays
focused and on time. This way, we stay helpful to others while remaining professional and serious about
our business goals".

Explanation:

      Legal: You clearly define the hours allowed so it does not hurt the company's contracts or payroll.
      Moral: You show a society-centric motivation, proving that your company cares about more than
       just money.
      Ethical: You are balancing your responsibility to your clients with your responsibility to the public.

Scenario # 3
You are a software engineer at a company where management routinely encourages you and your
colleagues to use pirated software.
How would you respond in a way that is legal, moral, and ethical?

Answer: "I cannot use or install pirated software because it goes against our professional code of ethics.
Using illegal software puts the company at legal risk and hurts our reputation if we are caught. I suggest
we look for free Open Source alternatives or buy the proper licenses so we can work safely and honestly."

Explanation:

      Legal: You are protecting the company from lawsuits and fines for using stolen software.
      Moral: You are showing Integrity (honesty) by refusing to steal someone else's work.
      Ethical: As a professional, you must follow the rule of "Giving back to society". Stealing software
       hurts the developers who made it.
