 Coherence: Coherence refers to the quality of being logical and consistent, e.g., 'This
   essay, on the whole, lacks coherence'.
 Context Models: Context models are special kinds of experience models. They represent
   communicative episodes in which we participate, often as part of other everyday episodes
   (conversation at breakfast, meeting at work, etc.).
 Conversation Analysis: Conversation analysis (CA) is an approach to the study of social
   interaction, embracing both verbal and non-verbal conduct, in situations of everyday life.
 Critical Linguistics: Critical Linguistics (CL) is “an approach to language study which
   uses linguistic techniques to investigate the ideologies (value systems and sets of beliefs)
   which underlie texts”
 Dialectical Relational Approach: According to Dialectical Relational Approach CDA is
   based upon a view of semiosis as an irreducible element of all material social processes
   (Williams 1977). Semiosis is viewed as an element of the social process which is
   dialectically related to others.
 Dialectics: The art or practice of arriving at the truth by the exchange of logical arguments.
 Discourse: Discourse is a unit of language usually longer than a sentence but the deduced
   meaning is beyond the level of sentence.
 Interpersonal Meanings: The interpersonal meanings are concerned with the interaction
   between speaker(s) and addressee(s).

 Lexicon: A lexicon is the knowledge that a native speaker has about a language.
 Mental Model: A mental model is an explanation of someone's thought process about how
   something works in the real world.
 SFL: Systemic Functional Linguistics (SFL) is an approach to linguistics that considers
   language as a social semiotic system.
 Situation Models: Situation models represent the situation a discourse is about, refers to
   and hence may also be called semantic models.
 Systematic Functional Linguistics: Systemic functional linguistics (SFL) is an approach
   to linguistics that considers language as a social semiotic system.
 The Discourse-Historical Approach: The Discourse-Historical Approach considers
   discourse analysis not just to be a method of language analysis, but a multidimensional
   project incorporating theory, methods, methodology and empirically based research
   practices that yield concrete social applications.
 Turn taking: The end of one speaker’s turn and the beginning of the next’s frequently
   latch on to each other with almost perfect precision and split-second timing.
