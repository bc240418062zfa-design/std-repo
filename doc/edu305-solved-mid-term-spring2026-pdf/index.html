Short Questions
1. Teacher’s role in Laissez-faire approach

In the laissez-faire approach, the teacher exercises minimal control, does limited planning, and
allows students considerable freedom. The teacher focuses mainly on content delivery and pays
less attention to classroom discipline and management.

2. Strategies to identify needs of problem students

      Maintain student profiles and behavior portfolios.
      Observe students carefully in class.
      Monitor peer interactions.
      Coordinate with parents and counselors.
      Seek support from administrators and staff.

3. Rules for maintaining classroom discipline

      Establish rules at the beginning of the year.
      Develop rules with students.
      Reinforce rules regularly.
      Apply rules fairly and consistently.
      Define consequences for non-compliance.

4. Being a teacher, how do we monitor student work?

Teachers monitor learning through formal assessments, informal assessments, project work,
continuous observation, feedback, and assessment portfolios.

5. How students can self-regulate behavior and play an active role in classroom
management

Students can self-regulate by controlling emotions, reflecting on their behavior, solving problems
responsibly, participating in group activities, and following classroom rules.

6. Why should teachers prepare differentiated tasks?

Differentiated tasks accommodate learner differences, ensure participation of all students, reduce
frustration, and improve learning outcomes.

7. Importance of classroom rules

Classroom rules create discipline, establish boundaries, guide behavior, and support effective
teaching and learning.

8. Why do teachers make students’ seating fixed and use name cards?

Fixed seating and name cards help teachers identify students, maintain order, monitor behavior,
manage attendance, and reduce disruptions.

9. How can teachers control aggressiveness of unmet students?

Teachers should identify causes of aggression, provide counseling support, maintain caring
relationships, teach self-control, and involve parents when necessary.

10. Being a teacher, how to manage/control a classroom?

      Establish rules and routines.
      Create a positive climate.
      Monitor learning continuously.
      Organize seating and materials.
      Reinforce positive behavior.

11. Why is professional development important for teachers?

Professional development improves teachers’ classroom management, discipline handling, rule
setting, organization, and behavior management skills.

12. Importance of communication skills

Communication skills help teachers explain concepts clearly, build positive relationships, resolve
conflicts, and promote effective learning.

13. Effective learning

Effective learning occurs when content knowledge, pedagogy, classroom management, and
social-emotional learning work together in a positive environment.

14. How can a teacher manage student talk?

Teachers should establish communication rules, encourage turn-taking, use hand-raising
procedures, and ensure respectful discussions.

15. How would you engage students in class?

Use varied teaching methods, group work, cooperative learning, discussions, projects, and
meaningful tasks based on students’ interests.

Long Questions
1. How should teachers maintain good behavior of students in class?

Teachers should establish rules at the beginning of the year, develop rules collaboratively with
students, reinforce expectations regularly, apply rules consistently, monitor behavior
continuously, and provide positive reinforcement. Building respectful relationships and
promoting self-control also help maintain discipline.

2. Why is it important to plan variety tasks?

Variety tasks reduce boredom, maintain interest, accommodate different learning styles,
encourage participation, and improve classroom discipline. Students become more engaged when
activities vary in format and interaction patterns.

3. Effective Teaching Skills

Effective teaching skills include:

      Strong content knowledge
      Pedagogical competence
      Classroom management skills
      Communication skills
      Monitoring and assessment abilities
      Ability to motivate students
      Social-emotional understanding

4. Stakeholders’ Role

Stakeholders include students, teachers, parents, administrators, principals, and the community.
They help maintain discipline, identify problems, follow school policies, build rapport, and
support effective learning environments.

5. Dimensions of Classroom Organization

The main dimensions are:

      Seating arrangements
      Teacher position
      Bulletin boards
