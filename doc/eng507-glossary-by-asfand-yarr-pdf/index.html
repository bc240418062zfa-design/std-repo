1. Advanced tongue root (ATR) :Having the root of the tongue pulled forward so
   as to widen the pharynx (and, often, to raise the body of the tongue nearer to the
   roof of the mouth). Pharyngeal sounds are [-ATR], as the pharynx is narrowed.
2. affricate :A stop followed by a homorganic fricative.
3. allophone :A variant of a phoneme. The allophones of a phoneme form a set of
   sounds that (1) do not change the meaning of a word, (2) are all very similar to
   one another, and (3) occur in phonetic contexts different from one another—for
   example, syllable initial as opposed to syllable final. The differences between
   allophones can be stated in terms of phonological rules.
4. alternations :Variations in words that can be described in terms of phonological
   rules; for example, the difference between [ aI ] and [ I ] in divine, divin(ity).
5. alveolar :An articulation involving the tip or blade of the tongue and the alveolar
   ridge, as in English [ d ] in die.
6. alveolar ridge :The part of the upper surface of the mouth immediately behind
   the front teeth.
7. alveolo-palatal :Post-alveolar consonant produced with significant raising of the
   front of the tongue toward the palate.
8. ambisyllabic :Belonging to two syllables. A consonant such as [ p ] in happy is
   sometimes said to be ambisyllabic.
9. apical :An articulation involving the tip of the tongue.
10. approximant :An articulation in which one articulator is close to another but
   without the tract being narrowed to such an extent that a turbulent airstream is
   produced. In many forms of English, / j, l, r, w / are approximants.
11. articulator :One of several parts of the vocal tract that can be used to form
   speech sounds.

12. aspiration :A period of voicelessness after the release of an articulation, as in
   English in pie [ pÓaI].
13. assimilation :The change of one sound into another making it more similar to
   a neighboring sound, as in the change of underlying [ n ] to [ m ] in input [ ·ImpÁt
   ] or of underlying [ z ] to [ Z ] in does she [ ·dØZSi ].
14. back vowel :Vowel in which the body of the tongue is in the back part of the
   oral cavity (mouth). The vowels [ u, o, O, A ] form a set of back reference vowels.
15. bilabial :An articulation involving both lips, as in English [ m ] in my.
16. binary feature :A feature (for example, Lateral) that can be used to classify
   sounds in terms of two possibilities.
17. breathy voice :Another name for murmur; a type of phonation in which the
   vocal folds are only slightly apart so that they vibrate while allowing a high rate
   of airflow through the glottis, as in Hindi [ bH ].
18. broad transcription :A transcription that does not show a great deal of
   phonetic d etail; often a simple phonemic transcription.
19. cardinal vowels :A set of reference vowels first defined by Daniel Jones. The
   vowels of any language can be described by stating their relationships to the
   cardinal vowels.
20. citation form :The form a word has when it is cited or pronounced in
   isolation.
21. click :A stop made with an ingressive v elaric airstream, such as Zulu [ = ].
22. closed syllable :A syllable with a consonant at the end, as the first syllables
   in English magpie, pantry, completion.
23. coarticulation :The overlapping of adjacent articulations.
24. coda :The consonants occurring after the vowel in a syllable.

25. contour tone :A tone in a language such as Chinese that must be specified as
   a gliding movement within the pitch range.
26. coronal :A term for sounds articulated with the tip or blade of the tongue
   raised toward the teeth or the alveolar ridge (or, sometimes, the hard palate), such
   as [ T, s, t ].
27. creaky voice :See laryngealization.  declination :Gradual intonational pitch
   fall over the course of an utterance.
28. dental :Refers to sounds made with the tongue touching the teeth.
29. diacritic :Small added mark that can be used to distinguish different values
   of a symbol. For example, the addition of [ º] distinguishes a velarized from a
   nonvelarized sound, as in [ l º ] as opposed to [ l ].
30. diphthong :A vowel in which there is a change in quality during a single
   syllable, as in English [ aI 8] in high.
31. dorsal :Describing a sound articulated with the back of the tongue.  dorsum
   :The back of the tongue.
32. downdrift :The tendency for the pitch to fall throughout an intonational
   phrase.
33. ease of articulation :A phonetic force that impacts linguistic sound systems
   so that patterns that are easier to produce are more likely than difficult patterns.
34. ejective :A stop made with an egressive glottalic airstream, such as Hausa [ k'
   ].
35. epenthesis :The insertion of one or more sounds in the middle of a word, such
   as the pronunciation of sense as [ s”nts ].

36. exemplar theory :The idea that phonetic categories are represented in the
   mind as a set of all of the examples of the category that the speaker has produced
   and/or heard.
37. feature :A phonetic property of a sound. Some features like Coronal are fairly
   general, while others like Laminal Dental are more specific.
38. filter :In acoustic phonetics, a filter is an acoustic tube that enhances some
   frequency components of a sound source while decreasing the amplitudes of
   other components.
39. flap :An articulation in which one a rticulator, usually the tongue tip, is drawn
   back and then allowed to strike against another articulator in returning to its rest
   position.
40. formant :A resonating frequency of the air in the vocal tract. Vowels are
   characterized by three formants.
41. frequency :The rate of oscillation in air pressure in a periodic sound wave.
