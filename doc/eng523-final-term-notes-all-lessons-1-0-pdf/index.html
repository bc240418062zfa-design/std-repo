Question: What is one of the central concerns of political discourse
analysis?
Answer: The central concern is how language represents the world to the
public through various linguistic forms.

Question: How is language used in attributing meaning to individuals
and groups in political discourse?
Answer: Language is used to attribute meaning to individuals and groups
by describing their social practices and actions.

Question: According to cognitive science, what is the nature of
mental representation?
Answer: Cognitive science suggests that mental representation is, at least
in part, metaphoric in nature.

Question: How do people use metaphor in everyday life, according to
cognitive science?
Answer: Metaphor is persistent in everyday life and is used not just in
language but in thought and action.

Question: What role do basic-level metaphors play in comprehension,
as suggested by cognitive science?
Answer: Basic-level metaphors allow us to comprehend and draw
inferences about concepts using our knowledge of familiar domains.

Question: How does cognitive linguistics view metaphors?
Answer: Cognitive linguistics sees metaphors as a mode of representation
and thought, not solely a linguistic phenomenon.

Question: Why do we use metaphors when thinking about abstract
ideas, as per Lakoff and Johnson?

Answer: We use metaphors to represent abstract ideas because they are
difficult to represent directly and are better understood through concrete
concepts.

Question: What are some arguments presented in favor of metaphoric
representation?
Answer: Metaphoric representation helps us refer to abstract concepts,
quantify them, and identify specific aspects, making them more
understandable.

Question: Why is the issue of metaphoric representation considered
interesting and radical?
Answer: The issue of metaphoric representation is interesting and radical
because it challenges traditional views of how language and thought are
interconnected.

Question: How has metaphoric representation influenced
psychological research?
Answer: Metaphoric representation has influenced psychological research,
particularly in theories of idiom and metaphor comprehension.

Question: What are the advantages and disadvantages of discussing
metaphoric representation?
Answer: The advantage is gaining insights into how abstract ideas are
represented, but the disadvantage is the challenge of defining and
discussing verbal metaphors.

Question: How did critical linguistics emerge in the late 1970s?

Answer: Critical linguistics emerged from Halliday's functional linguistic
theory, viewing language as a "social fact" displaying politics and ideology
through grammatical structure.

Question: What is Critical Discourse Analysis (CDA) and how is it
related to critical linguistics?
Answer: CDA is a broader analytic program analyzing language to uncover
power abuse and inequality in society, and it relates to critical linguistics as
a part of this program.

Question: How does Van Dijk define CDA, and what is the aim of this
approach?
Answer: Van Dijk defines CDA as a form of critically driven theory and
practice aiming to study power abuse and inequality using various
methods.

Question: What are some criticisms of the critical approach in CDA?
Answer: The approach has been criticized for potentially confirming
preconceived notions and for using linguistic analysis to support claims of
power abuse.

Question: Who are some influential scholars in the field of political
discourse and CDA?
Answer: Scholars like Norman Fairclough, Ruth Wodak, and Teun van Dijk
have been leaders in both political discourse and CDA.

Question: How does the critical analyst view political discourse?
Answer: The critical analyst views political discourse as the use of
language and discursive positioning to hide or distribute responsibility and
designate specific individuals or groups for political purposes.

Question: What is the relationship between political discourse and
phonetics/phonology?
Answer: There has been relatively little attention given to how politicians
use phonetic and phonological features of language for political purposes.

Question: How does Margaret Thatcher's speech modification
illustrate the impact of phonetics in political persuasion?
Answer: Margaret Thatcher modified her speech to appeal to voters and
enhance her political image.

Question: How does the study of prosody in political interviews reveal
politicians' communication style?
Answer: Politicians tend to reflect a positive and fluid communication style
in political interviews.
Question: How have prosodic features been harnessed in political
debates to achieve specific effects?
Answer: In political debates, politicians use prosodic features such as pitch,
emphasis, and focus to convey assertiveness, irony, emotion, and
hyperbole to achieve specific political goals.

Question: How does advertising exemplify the diverse roles of
discourse in society?
Answer: Advertising serves as a form of discourse that informs, influences,
and relates to different social spheres and activities.

Question: Why does advertising sometimes have a perceived
marginal status in discourse analysis?
Answer: Advertising's ability to draw inspiration from various sources and
its ultimate commercial aims contribute to its perceived marginality.
