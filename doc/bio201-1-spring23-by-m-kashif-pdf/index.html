a) Explain journey in to the Cell in your own words and understanding. (5)

   Journey into the Cell: Exploring the Fascinating World of Cellular Life

Introduction:

Taking a journey into the cell means to visit the fascinating world inside the cell
with it’s each organelle working to manage the whole cell city allows us to delve
into the intricacies of cellular life and gain a deeper understanding of its
functions. In this note, we will explore the cell's composition, organelles, and
processes that contribute to its remarkable functionality.

1) The Cell Membrane & Cytoplasm _ Entry Gate & Transport System

Every cell has a unique structure and composition that enables it to carry out specific functions.
The cell is enclosed by a plasma membrane, a selectively permeable barrier that controls the entry
and exit of substances. Inside the cell, we find the cytoplasm, a jelly-like substance that fills the
space between the plasma membrane and the nucleus. The cytoplasm houses various organelles,
including the nucleus, mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, and many others.

2: The Nucleus - Command Center of the Cell

The nucleus is often referred to as the control center of the cell. It contains the cell's genetic
material in the form of DNA, which encodes the instructions for cell functioning. Within the
nucleus, we find the nucleolus, responsible for ribosome production, and the chromatin, which
consists of DNA tightly wound around proteins called histones. The nucleus regulates gene
expression and plays a vital role in cell reproduction.

3: Mitochondria - Powerhouses of the Cell

Mitochondria are small, rod-shaped organelles known as the powerhouses of the cell. They are
responsible for generating energy in the form of ATP through a process called cellular respiration.
Mitochondria have their own DNA and can replicate independently within the cell. Their unique
structure, with inner and outer membranes, allows them to carry out their energy-producing
function efficiently.

4: Endoplasmic Reticulum and Golgi Apparatus - The Protein Factory

The endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and Golgi apparatus work together in the synthesis, modification,
and transport of proteins within the cell. The ER is a network of membrane-enclosed tubules and

sacs involved in protein synthesis and lipid metabolism. It can be rough, with ribosomes attached
to its surface, or smooth, lacking ribosomes. The rough ER is involved in the synthesis of
membrane proteins and proteins for export, while the smooth ER contributes to lipid metabolism
and detoxification.

The Golgi apparatus consists of a series of flattened membrane sacs called cisternae. It receives
proteins from the ER and modifies them through processes such as glycosylation and
phosphorylation. The Golgi apparatus then packages these proteins into vesicles and directs them
to their appropriate destinations within or outside the cell.

5: Lysosomes – The Garbage Disposal Centre of Cell

Lysosomes are membrane-bound organelles responsible for cellular waste management and
recycling. They contain various enzymes that break down cellular waste, damaged organelles, and
foreign substances. Through this process, lysosomes contribute to cellular renewal and the
maintenance of a healthy cell environment. Lysosomal storage disorders can occur when these
enzymes are deficient, leading to the accumulation of waste materials within cells.

6: Cytoskeleton - Cellular Structure and Movement

The cytoskeleton is a dynamic network of protein filaments that provides structure and enables
movement within the cell. It consists of microtubules, microfilaments, and intermediate filaments.
Microtubules are hollow tubes involved in cell division and intracellular transport. Microfilaments,
made of actin, contribute

to cell shape and movement. Intermediate filaments provide mechanical support and help anchor
organelles within the cell.

7.Hormones & Neurotransmitters _Cell Communication and Signaling

Cells communicate with each other through complex signaling processes. This communication can
occur through direct cell-to-cell contact or by signaling molecules such as hormones and
neurotransmitters. Signaling pathways within the cell involve various molecules, including
receptors, enzymes, and secondary messengers. These pathways regulate processes such as cell
growth, differentiation, and response to external stimuli.

8. Cell Division - The Continuity of Life

Cell division is essential for growth, development, and the renewal of cells. The two primary
methods of cell division are mitosis and meiosis. Mitosis is the process by which a cell duplicates
its genetic material and divides into two identical daughter cells. Meiosis, on the other hand, is a
specialized form of cell division that occurs in reproductive cells and leads to the formation of
gametes.

Conclusion:

Embarking on a journey into the cell reveals the remarkable complexity and organization that
underlies all living organisms. From the nucleus, mitochondria, and other organelles to cellular
communication and division, every aspect of the cell contributes to the functioning and survival
of life. Exploring the cell allows us to appreciate the beauty and intricacy of the microscopic world
and gain insights into the fundamental processes that drive life itself.

b) Why mitochondria having their own DNA and what important role does they
play in cellular functions?

Why Mitochondria have their own DNA and function of Mitochondrial DNA

Mitochondria having their own DNA is a result of their evolutionary origin. It is believed that
mitochondria were once free-living bacteria that formed a symbiotic relationship with eukaryotic
cells. Over time, this symbiotic relationship became so integral that mitochondria became
permanent residents within cells.
