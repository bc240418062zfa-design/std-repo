                       IMPORTANT QUESTIONS

1. What is villi? Villi are small, finger-like projections lining the small intestine that
   increase the surface area for absorption. They contain blood vessels and lymphatics
   (lacteals) to transport nutrients absorbed from digested food.
2. Types of cells in the heart The heart contains several types of cells, including
   cardiomyocytes (contractile cells), pacemaker cells (e.g., sinoatrial node cells),
   endothelial cells (lining blood vessels), and fibroblasts (providing structural support).
3. Intestinal lumen absorption and secretion (3) o Absorption: Nutrients like
   glucose, amino acids, and fatty acids are absorbed. o           Secretion: Mucus and
   digestive enzymes are secreted. o       Electrolyte balance: Ions like Na+ and Cl- are
   absorbed or secreted to maintain balance.
4. Peptide and amine hormones Peptide hormones (e.g., insulin) are composed of
   amino acid chains and act on cell surface receptors. Amine hormones (e.g.,
   adrenaline) are derived from amino acids like tyrosine and also bind to surface
   receptors to exert their effects.
5. Hypercapnia. How animals respond to it? (9) Hypercapnia, elevated CO2 levels in
   the blood, triggers an increase in respiratory rate and depth to expel excess CO2.
   Animals may also increase blood flow to tissues and adjust bicarbonate levels to
   maintain pH balance.
6. Alimentary canal The alimentary canal, or gastrointestinal tract, is a continuous tube
   from the mouth to the anus, including the mouth, esophagus, stomach, intestines, and
   anus, responsible for digestion and nutrient absorption.

7. P wave and P-R or R-T wave interval (12) o                P wave: Represents atrial
    depolarization. o         P-R interval: Time from the start of atrial depolarization to the
    start of ventricular depolarization. o R-T interval: Represents the duration of
    ventricular depolarization and repolarization.
8. Triads in skeletal muscles Triads consist of a T-tubule flanked by two terminal
    cisternae of the sarcoplasmic reticulum, facilitating the transmission of action
    potentials to the sarcoplasmic reticulum, triggering calcium release for muscle
    contraction.
9. Hormone structure and function Hormones can be peptides, steroids, or amines,
    and they regulate various physiological processes by binding to specific receptors on
    target cells, initiating signaling cascades that alter cell function and gene expression.
10. Smooth muscles (2) Smooth muscles are involuntary, non-striated muscles found in
    the walls of hollow organs (e.g., intestines, blood vessels). They contract to facilitate
    movements like peristalsis and regulate blood flow.
11. Tubular reabsorption (8) Tubular reabsorption in the kidneys involves reclaiming
    water, ions, and nutrients from the filtrate back into the blood, primarily occurring in
    the proximal tubule, loop of Henle, distal tubule, and collecting duct.
12. Note on urea and uric acid (3)

•   Urea: A nitrogenous waste product formed in the liver from ammonia and excreted by
    the kidneys.
•   Uric acid: A waste product from purine metabolism, less soluble than urea, and
    excreted in urine.

13. pH regulation in detail pH regulation involves buffering systems (bicarbonate,
    phosphate, proteins), respiratory control (CO2 expulsion), and renal mechanisms (H+
    excretion and bicarbonate reabsorption) to maintain blood pH within a narrow range
    (7.35-7.45).
14. Describe absorptive surface in small intestine The small intestine's absorptive
    surface is enhanced by villi, microvilli, and circular folds, collectively increasing
    surface area for maximal nutrient absorption from digested food.
15. Structure of nAChRs Nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (nAChRs) are ligand-gated
    ion channels composed of five subunits forming a pore, activated by acetylcholine
    binding, allowing Na+ and Ca2+ influx and depolarization of the cell membrane.
16. Pacemaker cells and its types Pacemaker cells, primarily in the sinoatrial (SA) node,
    generate spontaneous action potentials to regulate heart rate. Other types include
    atrioventricular (AV) node cells and Purkinje fibers.
17. Motility and its two mechanisms (3)

•   Peristalsis: Wave-like muscle contractions moving contents through the GI tract.
•   Segmentation: Rhythmic contractions mixing and breaking down food in the
    intestines.

18. Clinical factors of GFR (4 lines each question)

•   Age: GFR declines with age due to a decrease in nephron number and renal function.
•   Sex: GFR varies with sex, generally higher in males due to greater muscle mass and
    metabolic demand.

•   Health conditions: Diseases like diabetes, hypertension, and chronic kidney disease
    can reduce GFR.
•   Hydration status: Dehydration can lower GFR by decreasing blood volume and renal
    perfusion.

19. Proenzyme and example (3) A proenzyme (zymogen) is an inactive precursor of an
    enzyme that requires a biochemical change to become active. For example,
    pepsinogen is secreted by the stomach lining and converted into the active enzyme
    pepsin by the acidic environment in the stomach, aiding in protein digestion.
