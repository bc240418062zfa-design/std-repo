Q. Define language learning.

Language learning refers to the process of acquiring a language through structured exposure,
practice, and instruction. It differs from natural language acquisition in that it often involves
conscious effort and formal education.

Q. Define language shock.

Language shock refers to the period of discomfort and confusion experienced by individuals when
exposed to a new language environment. It is often part of the silent period in second language
acquisition, where learners initially struggle to process and produce the new language.

Q. What is meant by language awareness in learning a second language?

Language awareness in second language learning refers to a learner's ability to consciously
recognize, understand, and reflect on language structures, usage, and cultural aspects. This
awareness improves metacognition and enhances language acquisition.

Q. Explain habitual thought.

Habitual thought refers to routine ways of attending to objects and events, categorizing them,
remembering them, and reflecting upon them. It contrasts with specialized thought, which is more
domain-specific or learned within particular cultural contexts.

Q. Define the interface between mass nouns and count nouns.

The distinction between mass nouns (e.g., water, air) and count nouns (e.g., chairs, books) affects
cognition and language structure. In English, mass nouns cannot be pluralized directly but can be
counted through expressions like "buckets of sand".

Q. Explain duality patterns in language.

Duality of patterning refers to the linguistic principle that language operates at two levels:

   ❖       Phonemes (basic sound units) combine to form
   ❖       Morphemes (meaningful units of language).

Q. What is the interface between first and second language acquisition?

The interface between first and second language acquisition refers to how knowledge of an initial
language influences the learning of another language, often causing transfer effects or interference.

Q. What is the constructivist approach to motivation?

The constructivist approach to motivation emphasizes that learning is an active, constructive
process where learners build their understanding through experiences and social interactions.

Q. List the key elements of culture.

   ❖      Language
   ❖      Traditions
   ❖      Beliefs
   ❖      Social norms
   ❖      Art and literature

Q. What are three inadequacies of the idea that "language is essential for thought"?

   ❖      Some cognitive processes occur without language.
   ❖      Non-verbal individuals (e.g., deaf individuals before learning sign language) still think.
   ❖      Concepts and emotions can exist before linguistic expression.

Q. Differentiate between sequential bilingualism and simultaneous bilingualism.

   ❖      Simultaneous bilingualism: Learning two languages from infancy.
   ❖      Sequential bilingualism: Learning a second language after acquiring a first one.

Q. Name three sound-based languages.

   ❖      English
   ❖      French
   ❖      Spanish

Q. What is the difference between induction and explication?

   ❖      Induction: Learning from specific examples to general rules.
   ❖      Explication: Directly explaining concepts through instruction.

Q. Define content words vs. functional words.

   ❖      Content words: Carry meaning (e.g., nouns, verbs, adjectives).
   ❖      Functional words: Provide grammatical structure (e.g., prepositions, conjunctions).

Q. What are the two main branches of constructivism?

   ❖       Cognitive constructivism (Piaget)
   ❖       Social constructivism (Vygotsky)

Q. What are the main factors in reading disorders?

   ❖       Phonological processing issues
   ❖       Visual processing difficulties
   ❖       Cognitive deficits

Q. Define whole-word recognition.

Whole-word recognition refers to the ability to recognize words as single units rather than
decoding them letter by letter.

Q. What is meant by language politics?

Language politics refers to how language is regulated, promoted, or suppressed by governments
and institutions.

Q. What is the term for the removal of a damaged part of the brain?

The term is lobectomy or lesion removal surgery.
