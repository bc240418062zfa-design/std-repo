Module 01: Introduction to CALL

  1. Computer-Assisted Language Learning (CALL)
     CALL refers to the use of computers and associated technologies in language teaching
     and learning. It encompasses tools like desktops, laptops, and mobile devices used to
     enhance language acquisition.
  2. Computer-Aided Language Instruction (CALI)
     An alternative term for CALL, commonly used in the U.S., focusing on the instructional
     aspect of computer-assisted learning.
  3. Multimedia
     Combines text, audio, video, and graphics in CALL activities, providing a rich and
     interactive learning environment.
  4. Internet Applications
     Includes tools like email, chat rooms, and the World Wide Web to promote real-time
     communication and access to authentic materials.
  5. Tutor vs. Tool Roles
     The computer acts as a tutor by providing instruction and feedback (e.g., grammar
     exercises) or as a tool for learners to explore and communicate (e.g., web searches or
     collaborative projects).

Module 02: Scope of CALL

  6. Technology-Driven CALL
      A critique that CALL is often focused more on technological advances than on
      pedagogical or theoretical considerations.
  7. Language Learning Theories
      CALL incorporates various theories, such as behaviorism, constructivism, and socio-
      cultural theory, to design activities.
  8. Normalization
      A state where technology becomes fully integrated into teaching and learning, much like
      traditional tools like textbooks or whiteboards.
  9. Educational Objectives
      Clear goals set for CALL, such as improving communication skills, learning vocabulary,
      or practicing grammar.
  10. Learner Autonomy
      CALL promotes self-directed learning by providing tools and resources that learners can
      access at their own pace.

Module 03: Brief History of CALL - I

  11. Behavioristic CALL
      The earliest phase of CALL (1950s-1970s), based on behaviorist theories, focusing on
      drills and repetitive practice.
  12. Drill-and-Practice Model
      A teaching approach where learners repeatedly practice specific language points until
      mastery.
  13. Mainframe Computers
      The large computers used in the early days of CALL, typically available only at research
      institutions.
  14. PLATO System
      An early CALL system developed in the 1960s, providing interactive grammar and
      vocabulary exercises.
  15. Simulations
      Interactive scenarios where learners practice language in real-life contexts, fostering
      problem-solving and communication skills.

Module 04: Brief History of CALL - II

  16. Communicative CALL
      The second phase of CALL (1970s-1980s), emphasizing meaningful communication over
      rote memorization.
  17. Multimedia CALL
      The use of multimedia technologies (video, audio, graphics) in the 1990s to provide an
      immersive learning experience.
  18. Artificial Intelligence in CALL
      Incorporating AI to create adaptive learning systems that respond to individual learner
      needs.
  19. Videodisc Technology
      A precursor to CD-ROMs, used in the 1980s for high-quality video content in CALL.
  20. Compact Disc Read-Only Memory (CD-ROM)
      Replaced videodiscs by offering more compact and versatile storage for multimedia
      CALL programs.

Module 05: Models for CALL

  21. Dunkin and Biddle’s Model
      A framework originally for classroom teaching, adapted to analyze the variables in
      CALL environments.

  22. Presage Variables
      Factors like teacher training and experience that influence the use of CALL.
  23. Context Variables
      The settings in which CALL occurs, such as classrooms, homes, or virtual environments.
  24. Process Variables
      The interactions between learners, teachers, and technology during CALL activities.
  25. Product Variables
      Outcomes of CALL, including language proficiency and learner engagement.

Module 06: The Role of the Teacher

  26. Teacher Training in CALL
      The specialized preparation teachers need to integrate CALL effectively into their
      classrooms.
  27. Integration of CALL
      Blending CALL activities seamlessly into broader language teaching curricula.
  28. Feedback in CALL
      The immediate and automated responses learners receive in CALL activities, crucial for
      learning.
  29. Classroom vs. Virtual Contexts
      CALL adapts to both traditional classrooms and online or distance learning
      environments.
  30. Teacher as Facilitator
      In CALL, teachers guide and support learners rather than delivering content directly.

Module 07: Learner Interaction

  31. Computer-Mediated Communication (CMC)
      Using technology for real-time or asynchronous communication, such as video calls or
      email, to practice language skills.
  32. Interactive Learning
      A learner-centered approach in CALL that promotes active engagement with tasks and
      peers.
  33. Learner Motivation
      CALL often enhances motivation by making language learning enjoyable and interactive.
  34. Task-Based Learning
      A method where learners complete real-world tasks using CALL tools, such as creating
      presentations or conducting online research.
  35. Collaboration in CALL
      Encouraging teamwork through CALL platforms, fostering peer learning and
      communication.

Module 08: Designing CALL Activities

  36. CALL Task Design
      Creating activities that align with learning objectives and effectively use technology.
  37. Pedagogical Frameworks
      Theories and principles guiding the development of CALL activities, such as
      constructivist or behaviorist approaches.
  38. Authentic Materials
      Using real-world texts, videos, and other materials in CALL to improve language
      acquisition.
  39. Learner-Centered Design
      CALL activities focused on meeting individual learner needs and preferences.
  40. Evaluation Metrics
      Methods to assess the effectiveness of CALL activities, including learner performance
      and engagement.
