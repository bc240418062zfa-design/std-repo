Antigen and Epitope.
Antigen:
 A foreign substance that provokes immunity is called an antigen. The definition of antigen is a
harmful substance which enters the body which causes the body to make antibodies as a response
to fight off disease. More specifically called as an immunogen. Proteins are best antigen, though
glycoproteins and lipoproteins can also act as antigens. Carbohydrates and lipid alone are poor
antigens.
 Epitopes:
 Specific regions on an antigen. In other words, any antigen has multiple regions each one of
those acts as a small antigen. These are called epitopes. These epitopes are also called as
antigenic determinants. o Most antigens have MW >10,000 D. Molecules that are less than
10,000 D are generally poor antigen.

Role of Thin Clonal Selection?
According to this theory, B cells are generated in the bone marrow randomly without seeing the
antigen. When such a B cell encounters its specific antigen in the body and start secreting
antibodies. A beautiful example to understand clonal selection theory is like going to a shoe store
and select a shoe size that fits you very well. Note, that the shoe that you selected was made
randomly without any regard to your foot size.

Define and Types of Algae. / describe Rhodophyta, bacillariophyta,
chlorophyta, phaeophyta.
Algae are simple eukaryotic organisms lacking roots, stems, or leaves, commonly found in
water. They exhibit a wide diversity of forms, ranging from single-celled to multicellular
structures. Algae are classified into several types based on characteristics such as color and cell
composition:
    1. Phaeophyta (Brown Algae): Multicellular, brownish, containing cellulose and alginic
        acid cell walls, and capable of rapid growth.
    2. Rhodophyta (Red Algae): Predominantly multicellular, red in color, with cellulose cell
        walls, storing glucose polymer for agar production.

   3. Chlorophyta (Green Algae): Unicellular or multicellular, containing chlorophyll a and
      b, believed to be ancestral to terrestrial plants.
   4. Bacillariophyta (Diatoms): Unicellular, brownish, with pectin and silica cell walls,
      storing oil and producing domoic acid.
   5. Dinoflagellates: Unicellular plankton with cellulose in the membrane, possessing
      chlorophyll a and c, and carotene, storing starch.
Inflammation and Write Its Consequence? / Characteristics:
Inflammation is a local defensive response to tissue damage, manifesting as redness, pain,
swelling, and heat. It involves the release of inflammatory mediators like histamine, kinins,
prostaglandins, leukotrienes, and cytokines, including TNF-alpha. These mediators induce the
release of acute phase proteins from the liver. The consequences of inflammation include
mobilizing immune cells to the site, initiating tissue repair, and isolating harmful agents. While
essential for healing, chronic inflammation can lead to various diseases.
Types of Symbiosis?
Interaction between two different organisms living in close physical association is called
symbiosis.
    1. Mutualism: Both are benefited Parasitism: one is harmed.
    2. commensalism: One is benefited, other is unharmed.
    3. Parasitism: one is harmed.
Characters of antigen?
Antigens are molecules recognized by the immune system, often found on pathogens. They
possess specific molecular features called epitopes that induce immune responses. Antigens can
be proteins, carbohydrates, nucleic acids, or lipids, provoking the production of antibodies and
cellular immune responses.
Define Culture, Culture Medium, Inoculation?
Microbial growth in the lab is called a culture. A nutrient material that supports the growth of
microbes in the lab is called a culture medium. Microbes introduced into a culture medium that
initiate growth of organisms are known as inoculum.
Define Five Kingdom Classification of Organisms.
The Five Kingdom Classification classifies organisms into five groups: Monera (prokaryotic
bacteria), Protista (single-celled eukaryotes), Fungi (multicellular, absorptive heterotrophs),
Plantae (multicellular, photosynthetic autotrophs), and Animalia (multicellular, ingestive
heterotrophs). This system, proposed by R.H. Whittaker, categorizes living organisms based on
their cellular structure, nutrition, and reproduction methods.
How disease effect normal to healthy person:
Disease can adversely impact a normal, healthy person by disrupting physiological functions.
Symptoms, ranging from mild to severe, can arise, affecting daily activities and overall well-
being. The immune system responds to combat the pathogen, potentially causing fatigue, fever,
and inflammation. If left untreated, diseases can lead to complications, organ damage, or chronic
conditions, impacting the individual's physical and mental health. Effective medical intervention
is crucial to restore health and prevent long-term consequences in otherwise healthy individuals.
Viral Replication and Its Mechanism? Stages of Viral Replication?
 Viral replication involves six steps:
1= Attachment, 2= Penetration, 3= Uncoating, 4= Replication, 5= Assembly, 6= Release.
During uncoating, replication, and assembly, the viral DNA or RNA incorporates itself into the
host cell's genetic material and induces it to replicate the viral genome.
Write three characteristic of bacterial cell wall?
   1. Peptidoglycan Structure: Bacterial cell walls contain peptidoglycan, a polymer with
      alternating sugar chains, providing rigidity.
   2. Protection: The cell wall protects against osmotic stress.
   3. Shape Determination: It contributes to bacterial shape and maintains structural integrity.
