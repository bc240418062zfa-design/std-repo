                              Subjective Questions
1. What are the different biosafety levels and what precautions are taken at each level?
Biosafety levels 1-4 have increasing levels of precautions. BSL-1 involves basic training and equipment. BSL-2
adds restrictions on access and uses safety cabinets. BSL-3 involves strict training and containment procedures.
BSL-4 has the strictest containment methods for highly dangerous pathogens.

2. Name three pieces of equipment used in a microbiology lab and their purpose.
Equipment used includes inoculating loops to transfer microorganisms, a microscope to view microorganisms,
and an autoclave to sterilize media and equipment using steam heat and pressure.

3. What is the purpose of Gram staining and how does it work?
Gram staining differentiates between Gram positive and Gram negative bacteria. It uses a primary stain, mordant,
decolorizer, and secondary stain. Gram positive bacteria retain the primary stain while Gram negative bacteria
take up the secondary stain.

4. What safety precautions should be taken when performing antimicrobial sensitivity testing?
Use aseptic technique, flame sterilize inoculating tools between use, avoid cross-contamination between samples,
use protective equipment like gloves and goggles.

5. What are four types of culture media used in microbiology?
Types include synthetic (chemically defined), non-synthetic (not fully defined), selective (inhibits certain
bacteria), differential (allows distinction of bacteria by colony characteristics).

6. What are four phases of the bacterial growth curve?
The four phases are lag phase with no growth, log/exponential rapid growth phase, stationary phase where growth
plateaus, and death phase where cell numbers decline.

7. How can microbial cell numbers be quantified by plating?
Cells are spread on agar plates to grow into colonies arising from individual cells that can be counted. Cell
concentration is calculated using the colony count and the dilution factor.

8. What is the purpose of biochemical tests in microbiology?
Biochemical tests analyze bacteria's ability to utilize substrates, produce metabolites and enzymes. They help
differentiate between bacterial groups and aid identification.

9. What precautions should be taken when sterilizing by autoclaving?
Place caps loosely to allow steam penetration and prevent explosion, use recommended temperatures and times
(121°C for 15 min), allow cooling before opening to prevent burns.

10. How does the Gram staining procedure differentiate bacteria?
Gram positive bacteria retain the crystal violet dye after decolorization while Gram negative bacteria lose the dye
and take up the safranin counterstain, appearing red under the microscope.

11. What are four physical methods of sterilization?
Heat methods include autoclaving with steam, dry heat ovens, and incineration. Radiation methods include UV
light and ionizing radiation. Filtration also physically removes microbes.

12. What is the purpose of microscopy in studying microorganisms?
Microscopy allows visual examination of microbial cell, colony, and growth characteristics to aid identification
and understanding of microbes. Different microscopy methods provide enhanced details.

13. Name the four IMViC biochemical tests. What does each test for?
The IMViC tests are: Indole (tryptophanase enzyme), Methyl Red (mixed acid fermentation), Voges-Proskauer
(butanediol fermentation), and Citrate (citrate utilization).

14. What safety guidelines should be followed when working at Biosafety Level 2?

Guidelines include restricted lab access, biohazard signs, impervious lab surfaces, presence of a safety cabinet
and autoclave, wearing gloves and lab coat.

15. What are selective and differential culture media used for?
Selective media inhibits unwanted bacteria, enrichment media facilitates pathogen growth. Differential media
allows distinction of bacteria based on colony characteristics.

16. What are four chemical sterilization methods?
Chemical methods include alcohols like ethanol, aldehydes like formaldehyde, oxidizing agents like hydrogen
peroxide, and ethylene oxide gas.

17. What are the components of a light microscope?
Components include ocular lens, objective lenses, stage, mirror/light source, condenser, diaphragm to adjust light
intensity, coarse and fine adjustment knobs.

18. What safety guidelines should be followed when performing microbiology experiments?
Guidelines include following aseptic technique, avoiding contamination between samples, flaming inoculating
tools between use, wearing protective equipment, and properly disposing waste.

19. What is the purpose of the Kirby-Bauer antibiotic sensitivity test?
It determines bacterial sensitivity or resistance to various antibiotics, seen as zones of inhibition on agar plates.
This guides effective antibiotic treatment.

20. What are mechanical or filtration methods for microbial sterilization?
Filtration uses membrane filters or sintered glass with specific pore sizes to physically remove bacteria from
thermolabile solutions and gases.

21. What are the characteristics examined when studying colony morphology of bacteria?
Colony characteristics examined include shape, elevation, margin, size, surface appearance, texture/consistency,
pigmentation, and opacity.

22. What is the purpose of a Durham tube in carbohydrate fermentation testing?

A Durham tube collects gas produced from carbohydrate fermentation, allowing differentiation between
fermentative and non-fermentative organisms.

23. How can citrate utilization be detected through biochemical testing?
Growth on Simmons citrate agar turns the bromothymol blue indicator from green to blue, signalling alkaline
byproducts from citrate breakdown.
