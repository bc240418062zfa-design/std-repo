                         IMPORTANT QUESTIONS

1. Hot start PCR (8)
    A method to improve PCR specificity by preventing the polymerase from
activating until the reaction temperature is high enough. This is achieved by using
modified enzymes or adding a heat-activation step before the PCR cycle begins.

2. Suicide PCR
   A PCR technique used to prevent contamination by ensuring that once a sample
has been amplified, it cannot be re-amplified. This is done by using primers that bind
only to the target DNA, reducing the risk of false positives.

3. Giemsa staining and its uses (7)
   Giemsa stain is used in cytogenetics for chromosomal analysis, particularly in
identifying chromosomal aberrations and banding patterns. It helps visualize the
structure of chromosomes and detect abnormalities such as deletions, duplications,
and translocations.

4. Polymorphism
   The occurrence of two or more different alleles at a locus within a population,
resulting in genetic variation. This variation can be in the form of single nucleotide
polymorphisms (SNPs), insertions, deletions, or structural variants.

5. Blending and particulate hypothesis
    Blending hypothesis suggests that genetic material from parents mixes to produce
offspring with intermediate traits. Particulate hypothesis, proposed by Mendel, states
that genes are discrete units that maintain their integrity through generations.

6. RNA purification and its procedure (5)
    RNA purification involves isolating RNA from biological samples using methods
like phenol-chloroform extraction or silica column-based kits. The process includes
cell lysis, separation of RNA from DNA and proteins, and RNA precipitation and
washing.

7. Symbols of insertion, inversion, deletion mutations
   Insertion mutations are represented by "ins," inversion mutations by "inv," and
deletion mutations by "del." These notations help describe changes in the DNA
sequence.

8. Diameter and length of Diptera chromosome
   Diptera chromosomes, particularly polytene chromosomes in Drosophila, can be
up to 200 micrometers long with a diameter of about 0.5-2 micrometers, providing
high-resolution visualization of gene activity.

9. Steps of organic method (4)
   The organic method involves cell lysis, extraction with organic solvents (phenol-
chloroform), phase separation to isolate nucleic acids, and precipitation of DNA or
RNA with alcohol.

10. Western blotting (7)
   Western blotting is a technique used to detect specific proteins in a sample. It
involves protein separation by SDS-PAGE, transfer to a membrane, blocking,
incubation with primary and secondary antibodies, and detection using
chemiluminescence or colorimetric methods.

11. Epistasis with example (2)
   Epistasis occurs when one gene's expression masks or modifies the effect of
another gene. For example, in Labrador retrievers, the E gene determines if the fur
has pigment, while the B gene affects the pigment color.

12. Linked single nucleotide and causative single nucleotide polymorphism
   Linked SNPs are near causative SNPs and inherited together. Causative SNPs
directly influence phenotypic traits or disease risk by altering gene function.

13. HGNC rules for substitution, range of residue duplication, deletion and
insertion (2)

    HGNC nomenclature for substitutions is "c.76A>T," while duplication, deletion,
and insertion are represented as "dup," "del," and "ins," respectively, e.g.,
"c.76_78dup," "c.76_78del," and "c.76_77insA."

14. Differentiate between hot start PCR and suicide PCR (9)
   Hot start PCR prevents premature enzyme activation using heat-activation,
improving specificity. Suicide PCR minimizes contamination risk by preventing re-
amplification of products using target-specific primers. Both enhance PCR accuracy
but in different contexts.

15. Which molecular technique is used to study protein? (4)
    Western blotting is commonly used to study protein expression, modifications,
and interactions by separating proteins via electrophoresis and detecting specific
proteins using antibodies.

16. Cytokinesis in plant cells
   Cytokinesis in plant cells involves the formation of a cell plate, which begins in
the center of the cell and grows outward, eventually fusing with the cell membrane to
divide the cell into two daughter cells.

17. Down syndrome
   Down syndrome is a genetic disorder caused by the presence of an extra copy of
chromosome 21 (trisomy 21), leading to intellectual disability, characteristic facial
features, and various health issues.

18. Additional mutation
   Additional mutations refer to secondary genetic changes that occur in the
presence of an existing mutation. These can modify the original phenotype or lead to
new traits and conditions.

19. VNTR and its applications
   Variable number tandem repeats (VNTRs) are short DNA sequences repeated in
tandem. They are used in forensic analysis, genetic mapping, and studying genetic
diversity due to their high variability among individuals.

20. Prophase l of meiosis
   Prophase I is the first stage of meiosis I, where homologous chromosomes pair up
and exchange genetic material through crossing over, forming tetrads. This increases
genetic diversity in the resulting gametes.
21. Metaphase of mitosis

   During metaphase, chromosomes align at the cell's equatorial plate, attached to spindle
fibers from opposite centrosomes. This alignment ensures accurate chromosome segregation
into daughter cells during anaphase.

22. Comparison between mitosis
   Mitosis produces two identical diploid cells for growth and repair, while meiosis
produces four genetically diverse haploid gametes for sexual reproduction. Mitosis
involves one division, meiosis involves two.
