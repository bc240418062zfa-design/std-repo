 Imperfect fungi?
Imperfect fungi, also known as Deuteromycetes, are a diverse group of fungi that do not have a known
sexual reproductive stage. Instead, they reproduce asexually through various methods such as
fragmentation, budding, or spore production. As a result of this lack of sexual reproduction, their
classification is based solely on their physical characteristics and not their genetic makeup. Many
common molds, such as Aspergillus and Penicillium, belong to this group.

Write a note on ascomycetes?

Ascomycetes are a diverse group of fungi characterized by the presence of specialized structures called
“asci” that contain “ascospores”. These fungi can be found in a wide range of habitats and play important
ecological roles such as decomposing organic matter and forming symbiotic relationships with plants.
Some ascomycetes are also important pathogens of plants, animals, and humans. Examples of
ascomycetes include morels, truffles, and yeasts like Saccharomyces cerevisiae, which is used in bread
making and beer brewing.

Uses of Citric Acid?

Citric acid, a weak organic acid found in citrus fruits, has a wide range of uses across several industries.
Some of its main uses include:
     Food and Beverage Industry: Citric acid is commonly used as a flavouring agent,
         preservative, and acidulant in the food and beverage industry. It is used in soft drinks, jams,
         jellies, candies, baked goods, and processed foods to enhance flavour, improve texture, and
         prolong shelf life.
     Pharmaceutical Industry: Citric acid is used as a excipient in pharmaceutical formulations. It
         is used to adjust the pH of medicines, as a chelating agent, and to mask the unpleasant taste of
         some medications.
     Cosmetics Industry: Citric acid is used in the cosmetics industry as a pH adjuster,
         preservative, and to exfoliate the skin. It is commonly used in skin care products, shampoos,
         and conditioners.
     Cleaning and Detergent Industry: Citric acid is used as a chelating agent and a water softener
         in cleaning and detergent products. It is commonly used in dishwashing detergents, laundry
         detergents, and all-purpose cleaners.
     Industrial Applications: Citric acid is also used in several industrial applications such as
         metal cleaning, water treatment, and as a cement retarder. In summary, citric acid has a wide
         range of applications in several industries due to its unique properties as an acidulate,
         preservative, chelating agent, pH adjuster, and water softener.

 This suppression of glycolysis by oxygen is a regulatory phenomenon referred to as the Pasteur effect,

How foams are produced?          3 Marks

 Foaming is largely due to media proteins that become attached to the air–broth interface where they
 denature to form stable foam. If uncontrolled the foam may block air filters, resulting in the loss of
 aseptic conditions; the fermenter becomes contaminated and microorganisms are released into the
 environment.

How many ways cell can be harvested?              3 Marks

 Broth conditioning: Broth conditioning techniques are mostly used in association with sedimentation
 and centrifugation for the separation of cells from liquid media.
 Sedimentation: Sedimentation is extensively used for primary yeast separation in the production of
 alcoholic beverages, and in waste-water treatment.
 Centrifugation: If instead of simply using gravitational force to separate suspended particles, a
 centrifugal field is applied, the rate of solid–liquid separation is significantly increased and much smaller
 particles can be separated.
