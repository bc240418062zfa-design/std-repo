   1. How is the DNA pellet diluted by organic method?
Dilute the pellet
   o Add 50-100 uL of low T.E. (Tris HCl 10 mL, EDTA 0.2mM) or DEPC water
   o Place the tubes in a shaking water bath at 70°C for one hour so that nucleases were inactivated.
     Finally DNA will store at –20oC.

   2. What is use to dilute the pellet in DNA extraction
In DNA extraction, the pellet is typically diluted with:
   o TE buffer (Tris-EDTA)
   o DEPC water (Diethyl pyrocarbonate-treated water)
These solutions help to rehydrate and stabilize the DNA pellet.

   3. Steps of DNA extraction?

   1- Lysis of Red Blood Cells, RBC
   2- Digestion step (Lysis of White blood cells, WBC)
   3- Phase Separation step (Extraction of Protein)
   4- DNA Precipitation
   5- Washing with ice cold Ethanol
   6- Dilute the pellet

   4. Principal of Northern Blotting
Northern blotting involves the use of electrophoresis to separate RNA samples by size and detection
with a hybridization probe complementary to part of or the entire target sequence. The term ‘northern
blot’ actually refers specifically to the capillary transfer of RNA from the electrophoresis gel to the
blotting membrane. However, the entire process is commonly referred to as northern blotting.

   5. Southern blotting
Southern blot is a method used in molecular biology for detection of a specific DNA sequence in DNA
samples.
Principle: Southern blotting combines transfer of electrophoresis-separated DNA fragments to a
filter membrane and subsequent fragment detection by probe hybridization.

   6. Principal of Southern Blotting
Southern blotting is a laboratory technique used to detect specific DNA sequences in a sample. It
involves cleaving the DNA with restriction enzymes, which cut the DNA at specific recognition sites,
resulting in fragments of varying lengths. These fragments are then separated by gel electrophoresis,
transferred to a membrane, and hybridized with a labeled probe to detect the desired DNA sequence.

   7. Western Blotting
A technique used to detect the presence of a specific protein in a complex protein mixture. The
western blot (sometimes called the protein immunoblotting) is a widely used analytical technique
used to detect specific proteins in a sample of tissue homogenate or extract.

   8. Procedure of Western Blotting .

   1) Proteins are separated by size using a special gel (SDS-PAGE).

   2) The separated proteins are transferred to a special paper (nitrocellulose).
   3) The paper is treated with a generic protein to prevent unwanted binding.
   4) An antibody that targets a specific protein is added, carrying an invisible enzyme or dye.
   5) A colorless substrate is applied, which the enzyme converts into a visible colored product.
   6) The resulting color reveals the location of the specific protein on the paper, allowing it to be
      identified and photographed.

   o Proteins are separated by gel electrophoresis, usually SDS-PAGE.
   o The proteins are transferred to a sheet of special blotting paper called nitrocellulose, though
     other types of paper, or membranes, can be used. The proteins retain the same pattern of
     separation they had on the gel.
   o The blot is incubated with a generic protein (such as milk proteins) to bind to any remaining
     sticky places on the nitrocellulose. An antibody is then added to the solution which is able to
     bind to its specific protein. The antibody has an enzyme (e.g. alkaline phosphatase or
     Horseradish peroxidase) or dye attached to it which cannot be seen at this time.
   o The location of the antibody is revealed by incubating it with a colorless substrate that the
     attached enzyme converts to a colored product that can be seen and photographed.

   9. Advantages and disadvantages of Western blotting?

   ❖ Advantages of Western Blotting:
1. Highly sensitive and specific
2. Can detect small amounts of protein
3. Can identify multiple proteins at once
4. Confirmatory test for diseases like HIV and BSE
5. Helps diagnose and monitor diseases
   ❖ Disadvantages of Western Blotting:
1. Time-consuming and labor-intensive
2. Requires specialized equipment and expertise
3. Can be expensive
4. May produce false positives or negatives
5. Not suitable for detecting very large or very small proteins

   10.        What are the advantages and disadvantages of PCR

   ❖ Advantages of PCR:
1. Selectively isolates specific DNA sequences
2. Amplifies tiny amounts of DNA into large quantities
3. Helps diagnose diseases by detecting specific DNA sequences
4. Quick and efficient process
   ❖ Disadvantages of PCR:
1. DNA polymerase can make errors, leading to mutations
2. Primers can bind nonspecifically, causing incorrect results
3. Requires precise temperature and chemical conditions
4. Can be contaminated easily, leading to false results
