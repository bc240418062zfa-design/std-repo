Measurement, Assessment and Evaluation
   - Measurement: The process of quantifying student learning or behavior.
   - Assessment: The process of collecting and interpreting data to determine student
       learning or behavior.
   - Evaluation: The process of making judgments about student learning or behavior
       based on assessment data.
Classroom Assessment OR Types of Assessment
   - Formative: Ongoing, continuous assessment to monitor student progress.
   - Summative: Evaluative assessment to determine student learning at the end of a
       lesson, unit, or course.
   - Diagnostic: Assessment to identify student strengths, weaknesses, and learning
       needs.
   - Self-Assessment: Students evaluate their own learning.
   - Peer Assessment: Students evaluate each other's work.
Formative and Summative Assessment
   - Formative Assessment: Inform instruction, adjust teaching, and provide feedback
       to students.
   - Summative Assessment: Evaluate student learning, grade, and report progress.
Types of Assessment: Methods of Interpreting Results
   - Norm-Referenced: Compare student performance to a larger group.
   - Criterion-Referenced: Evaluate student performance based on specific criteria.
   - Standards-Based: Evaluate student performance based on learning standards.
Role of National Curriculum in Assessment: Provides a framework for assessment and
evaluation. Ensures consistency and standardization.
Connecting all Four Levels in Curriculum
   - Aims: Overall goals of education.
   - Objectives: Specific learning outcomes.
   - Learning Outcomes: Statements of what students will know or be able to do.
   - Assessment: Evaluates student achievement of learning outcomes.

     Bloom‘s Taxonomy
  A framework for categorizing learning objectives into six levels:

     - Remembering (recall knowledge)
     - Understanding (comprehend concepts)
     - Applying (use knowledge in new situations)
     - Analyzing (break down complex information)
     - Evaluating (make judgments based on criteria)
     - Creating (generate new ideas or products)
     Revised version of Bloom‘s Taxonomy:
  Updates the original taxonomy to include:
     - Remembering
     - Understanding
     - Applying
     - Analyzing
     - Evaluating
     - Creating
     Instructional Objectives: Specific statements that outline what students will learn or
     achieve. Should be clear, measurable, and aligned with assessments

   Educational Decisions Making
Assessment data informs decisions about:
       - Student promotion or retention
       - Curriculum development
       - Instructional strategies
       - Resource allocation
   Norm Referenced Assessment (NRT)
       - Compares student performance to a larger group (norm)
       - Purpose: Rank students, determine relative standing
       - Example: Standardized tests like SAT or ACT
   Criterion Referenced Assessment (CRT)
       - Evaluates student performance based on specific criteria or standards
       - Purpose: Determine mastery or achievement of learning objectives
       - Example: Quizzes, unit tests, project evaluations
   Purpose of Testing
       - Evaluate student learning
       - Identify strengths and weaknesses
       - Inform instruction
       - Make educational decisions
   Characteristics of Criterion Referenced Assessment
       - Focus on specific learning objectives
       - Emphasizes mastery or achievement
       - Uses clear criteria and standards
       - Results are reported in terms of mastery or achievement
   Difference Between NRT and CRT
       - NRT: Compares students to a norm, focuses on relative standing
       - CRT: Evaluates student performance based on specific criteria, focuses on mastery
          or achievement
   Formative Assessment
       - Ongoing, continuous assessment during instruction
       - Purpose: Monitor progress, adjust instruction, provide feedback
       - Examples: Quizzes, classwork, self-assessment
   Functions of Formative Assessment:
       - Identify areas of improvement
       - Adjust instruction
       - Provide feedback to students
       - Encourage student self-assessment
   Summative Assessment
       - Evaluative assessment at the end of a lesson, unit, or course

      - Purpose: Determine student learning, grade, or evaluate achievement
      - Examples: Unit tests, final exams, project evaluations
   Functions of Summative Assessment
      - Evaluate student learning
      - Determine grades or promotion
      - Provide feedback to students and parents
      - Evaluate instructional effectiveness
   Table of Specification
      - A tool for constructing assessments
      - Aligns learning objectives with assessment items
      - Ensures coverage of all learning objectives
   Elements and Appropriateness in Table of Specification
      - Elements: Learning objectives, assessment items, cognitive levels
      - Appropriateness: Ensures assessment items align with learning objectives and
          cognitive levels
   Balance Among Learning Objectives and their Weight in Table of Specification with
