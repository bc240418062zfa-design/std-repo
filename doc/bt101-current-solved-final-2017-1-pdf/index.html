Red tide:

The harmful and toxic blooms of red Dinoflagellates causes the colour of sea water to become red. Red
Dinoflagellates (Example: Gonyaulax) undergo such rapid multiplication that they make the sea appear
red

Characters of Phycomycetes:

The mycelium is aseptate and coenocytic. Asexual reproduction takes place by zoospores (motile) or by
aplanospores (nonmotile). These spores are endogenously produced in sporangium.

Examples of Phycomycetes:

       Mucor
       Rhizopus (the bread mould)
       Albugo (the parasitic fungi on mustard).

Eutrophication Too much nutrients causes harmful algal blooms (HABs) which are extremely toxic to
living creatures (fish, shellfish, birds and mammals) It decreases the oxygen content rapidly

Global warming:

The average temperature of earth surface is increasing day by day due to increase in concentration of
greenhouse gases in the atmosphere. Human activities are main cause of it. The earth is gradually
heating up due to rise in temperature. This phenomenon is called Global warming. It is causing serious
problems to the health of plants and animals and also the surrounding environment.

Causes of Global warming:

Burning of fossil fuels Volcanic eruption Industrial effluents Deforestation Transportation Power plants

Effects of Global warming Rise in temperature Rise in Sea Level Climate change Extinction Droughts Loss
of biodiversity

    1. Protozoans:-
All protozoans are heterotrophs and live as predators or parasites. Amoeboid protozoans Flagellated
protozoans Ciliated protozoans Sporozoans

Amoeboid protozoans: These organisms live in fresh water, sea water or moist soil. They move and
capture their prey by putting out pseudopodia (false feet) as in Amoeba. Marine forms have silica shells
on their surface. Some of them such as Entamoeba are parasites.

Flagellated protozoans: Freeliving or parasitic. They have flagella. The parasitic forms cause diastases
such as sleeping sickness.

Ciliated protozoans: Aquatic and movement by cilia. A cavity (gullet)opens to outside of the cell surface.
The coordinated movement of rows of cilia causes the water loaded with food to be driven into the
gullet. Example: Paramoecium.

Sporozoanes: This includes diverse organisms that have an infectious sporelike stage in their life cycle.
The most notorious is Plasmodium (malarial parasite) which causes malaria, a disease which has a
staggering effect on human population.

Growth forms of Lichens :

Crustose :A type of lichen that form a flat crust on the substratum and firmly attached to substratum.

Foliose: This type of lichens has flat leaf like structures and are attached to substratum by thread like
structures .

Fruticose: This type of lichens have erect filamentous like outgrowths and possess vertical pattern of
growth.

Benefits of Lichens Bioindicators Ecosystem sustainability Medicines Animal food Dye for clothes

Common form of symbiosis are Commensalism , Mutualism.

Euglenoids: have a protein rich layer called pellicle which makes their body flexible.

Certain marine algae produce large amounts of volatile DMS (dimethyl sulfide). In the atmosphere, DMS
is converted to sulfur dioxide, some of which in turn is converted to sulfur trioxide gas and to tiny
droplets of sulfuric acid (H2SO4 ).

Neurospora is used extensively in biochemical and genetic work. Many members like morels and truffles
are edible and are considered delicacies.

    3. Steps in nitrogen cycle
Fixation:

Two natural processes convert or fix N2 into compounds useful as nutrients for plants and animals.
Electrical discharges, or lightning, taking place in the atmosphere. Nitrogen fixation by nitrogen fixing
bacteria

Nitrification:

Specialized soil bacteria convert most of the Ammonia (NH3) and Ammonium ions (NH4+) in soil to
nitrate ions (NO3 ) which are easily taken up by the roots of plants.

Assimilation:

The plants then absorb nitrates from the soil into their roots to produce various amino acids, proteins,
nucleic acids, and vitamins.

Ammonification:

When a plant or animal dies, specialized decomposer bacteria convert this detritus into simpler
nitrogencontaining inorganic compounds such as Ammonia (NH3) and watersoluble salts containing
Ammonium ions (NH4+)
