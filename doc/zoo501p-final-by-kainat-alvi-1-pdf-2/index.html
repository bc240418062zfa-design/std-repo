   1. Label the structure of sperm

   2. What happened if sperm lack the centriole?
If a sperm lacks the centriole, it can impact the process of fertilization. The centriole is crucial for the
formation of the microtubules that make up the sperm's tail and are essential for movement. Without
a functional centriole, the sperm may experience difficulty in swimming toward the egg. Additionally,
the centriole plays a key role in the formation of the mitotic spindle during fertilization, aiding in the
proper division of genetic material. Therefore, a lack of centriole may result in reduced fertility or
hinder the sperm's ability to fertilize an egg successfully.
   3. Acrosomal reaction and cortical granules reaction
   ❖ Acrosomal Reaction:
The acrosomal reaction is a process in which a sperm releases enzymes from a structure called the
acrosome. This reaction helps the sperm penetrate the outer layer of the egg, known as the zona
pellucida, allowing fertilization to occur.
   ❖ Cortical Granules Reaction:
The cortical granules reaction is a protective mechanism that occurs in the egg after fertilization. It
involves the release of special vesicles, called cortical granules, which prevent additional sperm from
binding to the egg. This reaction ensures that only one sperm successfully fertilizes the egg,
maintaining the genetic integrity of the resulting embryo.
   4. Differences between the white and yellow yolk of eggs.
   ❖ Yellow yolk
A major source of vitamins, minerals, almost half of the protein, and all of the fat and cholesterol. The
yolk contains less water and more protein than the white, some fat, and most of the vitamins and
minerals of the egg. These include iron, vitamin A, vitamin D, phosphorus, calcium, thiamine, and
riboflavin. The yolk is also a source of lecithin, an effective emulsifier. Yolk color ranges from just a
hint of yellow to a magnificent deep orange, according to the feed and breed of the hen.
   ❖ White yolk
Also known as, the latebra is an area of white yolk located in the center of the yolk. It is lower in fat
and therefore stands out as a bright white area in many Magnetic Resonance Images. The specific
function of the latebra is uncertain but it may act as a central structure around which the additional
layers of the yolk are formed.
   5. Describe the differences between an ovum and a zygote.
   ❖ Ovum
An ovum, also known as an egg cell, is the female reproductive cell. It is produced in the ovaries and
released during ovulation. The ovum is a large cell that contains genetic material and is capable of
being fertilized by a sperm cell.

   ❖ Zygote
On the other hand, a zygote is the result of the fusion of an egg cell (ovum) and a sperm cell. It is the
earliest stage of development after fertilization occurs. The zygote contains a complete set of genetic
material from both the mother and father, and it marks the beginning of a new individual's
development.
   6. What is ovaprim and why it is used in breeding of fishes?
Ovaprim is one of the most popular and effective solutions to stimulate the maturation of male and
female brood fish.
Ovaprim is a hormone-based fish spawning agent that is used to help breed fishes. When injected into
female fishes, Ovaprim helps stimulate the release of eggs (ova) from their ovaries. It makes sure that
the breeding happens at the right time. It also helps in synchronizing the spawning process, making it
easier for fish farmers to control and manage breeding. Fish farmers use Ovaprim to increase the
success rate of breeding and to produce more fish for various purposes, like stocking ponds or
supplying fish for consumption.

   8. How Trichostrongylus infect human
Infections occur via ingestion of infective larvae from contaminated vegetables or water.
Trichostrongylus spp are a type of parasitic worms that can infect humans. The primary mode of
infection is through the ingestion of contaminated food or water. When humans consume food or
water that is contaminated with the larvae of Trichostrongylus spp, the larvae can enter the digestive
system. From there, they migrate into the small intestine, where they attach themselves to the
intestinal lining and start feeding on blood. This can lead to symptoms such as abdominal pain,
diarrhea, and anemia.

   11.        Role of cilia in oviduct?/ Role of cilia in mammalian cleavage.
In Mammalian cleavage, Cilia in the oviduct push the embryo toward the uterus. Cilia in the oviduct
play a crucial role in guiding the egg from the ovary to the uterus. These tiny hair-like structures
create a coordinated wave-like motion, helping to move the egg along the oviduct. Additionally, cilia
assist in transporting nutrients and providing a suitable environment for fertilization to occur.
