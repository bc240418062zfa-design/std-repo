SUBJECTIVE PART

1. What are the three main structures in the head region of a human sperm cell?
Answer: The three main structures in the head region of a human sperm cell are - a haploid nucleus, an acrosome cap, and paired centrioles.

2. What are the two distinct layers surrounding a typical egg cell?
Answer: The two distinct layers surrounding a typical egg cell are - the zone pellucida (jelly coat) and the corona radiate.

3. What is the function of the bloom or cuticle on an eggshell?
Answer: The bloom or cuticle on an eggshell somewhat seals the pores and is useful in reducing moisture losses and in preventing bacterial penetration of the egg shell.

4. During cleavage in a chick embryo, in which plane does the third cleavage furrow run?
Answer: In a chick embryo, the third cleavage furrow runs horizontally but in a plane closer to the animal than to the vegetal pole.

5. What structure is formed when the contents of a chicken egg cool and contract after it is laid?
Answer: When the contents of a laid chicken egg cool and contract, an air cell usually forms between the outer and inner membranes at the egg's larger end.

6. What hormone solution is commonly used to induce breeding and spawning in fish?
Answer: Ovaprim is one of the most popular and effective solutions used to stimulate maturation of male and female broodfish and induce spawning in fish.

7. What are the developmental stages of nematodes found in animal dung?
Answer: The developmental stages of nematodes found in animal dung are egg, larval stages (L1-L3), and adult. The infective stage is the third larval (L3) stage.

8. How can fingerprints be used to identify unknown deceased in disasters?
Answer: If unknown deceased persons in disasters have fingerprints on file, they can be identified by matching their fingerprints from the records to the ones collected from the body.

9. How do investigators use fingerprints collected from crime scenes?
Answer: Investigators can compare fingerprints collected from crime scenes to the known prints of victims, witnesses and suspects to generate leads and identify perpetrators.

10. What information is contained in fingerprint records that help officers make decisions regarding sentences and parole?
Answer: Fingerprint records contain information about a convicted offender's previous arrests, convictions, criminal tendencies and known associates that help officers make decisions regarding sentences, probation, parole or pardon.

11. What is the function of the zona pellucida surrounding the egg cell?
Answer: The zona pellucida surrounding the egg cell acts as a barrier to sperm entry.

12. What causes the air cell to become larger as the egg ages?
Answer: As the egg ages, moisture and carbon dioxide leave through the pores of the shell, air enters to replace them, and so the air cell becomes larger.

13. What is the significance of more prominent chalazae in an egg?
Answer: More prominent chalazae in an egg indicate that the egg is fresher.

14. Why is rotational cleavage unique to mammalian embryos?
Answer: Rotational cleavage, where the second cleavage results in one meridional and one equatorial division, is unique to mammalian embryos and distinguishes mammalian cleavage from that in most other animals.

15. How do fingerprints help identify perpetrators of unsolved crimes?
Answer: Fingerprints from unsolved crimes can sometimes match prints in databases, linking multiple crimes and helping investigators identify perpetrators.

16. What are the two membranes located just inside the eggshell called?
Answer: The two membranes located just inside the eggshell are called the outer shell membrane and inner shell membrane.

17. Which part of the thick egg white contains high levels of riboflavin and protein?
Answer: The inner thick white (chalaziferous layer) of the egg contains high levels of riboflavin and protein.

18. What is the small, circular, white spot on the yolk called where the sperm enters the egg?
Answer: The small, circular, white spot on the surface of the yolk where the sperm enters the egg is called the germinal disk or blastoderm.

19. What happens during the compaction stage in human embryo development?
Answer: During compaction of the human embryo on day 4 at the 10-cell stage, the blastomeres flatten against each other, removing intercellular gaps and increasing cell-cell adhesion.

20. How does the vitelline membrane help prevent egg yolk mottling?
Answer: The strength and integrity of the vitelline membrane prevents the egg yolk surface from becoming covered with pale spots or blotches, known as mottling.

21. What is the recommended Ovaprim dosage range for inducing spawning in female siban fish?
Answer: The recommended Ovaprim dosage range for inducing spawning in female siban fish is 0.5-0.7 ml per kg body weight.

22. What common parasite egg was found in the microscopic analysis of animal dung samples?
Answer: Trichostrongylus retortaeformis eggs were the most commonly found nematode eggs in 50% of the fecal samples analyzed.

23. How do Trichostrongylus infective larvae enter herbivore hosts?
Answer: Trichostrongylus infective larvae are ingested by herbivores along with plant material while grazing on contaminated pastures.

24. When are a perpetrator's fingerprints important evidence in an assault case?
Answer: In an assault case, if the perpetrator left fingerprints on the victim’s skin, it can serve as important evidence.

25. What characteristic of fingerprints enables accurate and definitive identification?
Answer: The characteristic of uniqueness that fingerprints possess enables accurate and definitive identification.

26. How can a thief's fingerprints help link them to the crime?
Answer: A thief may leave fingerprints on surfaces like a broken window pane or a safe, which can be used to identify them as the perpetrator.

27. What is the infective stage for Trichostrongylus species?
Answer: The third larval (L3) stage is the infective stage for Trichostrongylus species.
