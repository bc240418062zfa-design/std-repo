    1. The Resolution was moved by Liaquat Ali Khan.
    2. The then Prime Minister of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan, and approved on March 12, 1949.
    3. The first Constituent Assembly came into existence under Indian Independence Act 1947.
    4. During The Constituent Assembly (1947-54) the elections were held in July 1946 to decide the destiny of the All India Muslim League (AIML).
    5. The members from the districts that became part of Pakistan were declared members of the Constituent Assembly. And they total 69 members.
    6. 69 members were increased to 79 after the 1947 when some states joined Pakistan and then increase in the population.
    7. There were two major parties, Muslim League and Congress in the Assembly at that time.
    8. Allah is the authority which He has delegated to the state of Pakistan through its people for being exercised within the limits prescribed by Him is a sacred trust.
    9. Features of the Objectives Resolution are 10.
    10. The Resolution declared the sovereignty of God as the distinctive political philosophy.
    11. The representatives of the people of Pakistan will manage the affairs under the universal ideology of democracy, freedom, equality, tolerance, and social justice with the spirit of an Islamic framework.
    12. The Objectives Resolution is a basic and primary document of the constitutional history of Pakistan.
    13. The Objectives Resolution is a framework that provides mechanism to achieve goals for a better life of the people of Pakistan.
    14. The AIML leaders were modernist Muslims not in favor of an orthodox religious state.
    15. .The Resolution 1949 remained ‘Preamble of all the constitutions due to its importance.

Lecture 17
    16. Constitution is a set of basic principles and framework for governance and exercise of political power and legal authority
    17. Constitution clarifies the scope of power, relationship among various institutions within the government and society.
    18. The Government of India during the Act (1935) was modified and promulgated in the newly state of Pakistan.
    19. The elected members in the 1946 elections made the first Constituent Assembly that faced grievous circumstances.
    20. The major issues, the first constituent assembly faced, were about 06.
    21. The main was that Pakistan consisted of two territorial parts. East Pakistan (with more population, less territory but administratively one unit) and West Pakistan (administratively 4 units).
    22. Federalism is meant to accommodate such kind of diversity maintaining the unity of the state or country.
    23. The heritage of British rule gave the tradition of a Strong Centre. But the provinces were demanding more Autonomy and Provincial Rights.
    24. In the Interim Constitution and the 1956 Constitution tradition of Strong Centre continued.
    25. Representation at the federal level was another conflicting issue because East Pakistan and West Pakistan were different in population and size.
    26. On the other hand there was diversity in Western part of Pakistan
    27. The provinces of West Pakistan were also different in population and size.
    28. To have a Standard Formula for the representation of units and population the Constituent Assembly (CA) formed a Basic Principle Committee (BPC) on March 12, 1949.
    29. The primary task of Basic Principle Committee (BPC) was to frame a set of basic principles for the future constitution of Pakistan.
    30. According to first report Basic Principle Committee (BPC) two houses of the parliament were proposed lower house and upper house.
    31. The lower house was to be elected on the basis of POPULATION.
    32.  The upper house was to be elected on the basis of equal representation for all the provinces of Pakistan namely East Bengal, West Punjab, Sindh, NWFP and Baluchistan.
    33. No mention of National Language was made and East Bengal opposed this report and Liaqat Ali Khan withdrew it.
    34. Basic Principle Committee BPC presented its final and second report on 22nd December 1952. According to Basic Principle Committee BPC second report two Houses of the Parliament will enjoy the equal status and powers.
    35. The Second report also faced reaction in both the wings of Pakistan.
    36. The principle of parity was not appreciated in both East Pakistan and Punjab.
    37. Muhammad ALI BOGRA immediately after assuming the office of the Prime Minister presented a formula to resolve the deadlock in constitution making.
    38. According to Muhammad ALI BOGRA formula Pakistan would have a bicameral legislature.
    39. In upper house there would be EQUAL representation to each of five units.
    40. In lower house population will be represented.
    41. In this way more representation was given to East Pakistan.
    42. Both wings would have equal strength in joint sessions of the two houses.
    43. Muhammad ALI BOGRA solved the problem of national language by suggesting Urdu and Bengali both as national language.
    44. One Unit of West Pakistan was established on 14th October 1955.
    45. The provinces of Punjab, Sindh, NWFP and Baluchistan would be amalgamated in one unit to establish parity between the two parts of the country.
    46. Separate electorate was adopted on the demand of Muslims in 1909 by the British Government.
    47. Religious elements supported Separate electorate as a part of heritage of British Government.
    48. East is decided for Joint Electorate.
    49. West is decided for Separate electorate.
    50. Joint Electorate was adopted for all Pakistan by the National Assembly in 1957.
    51. In 1948 Jinnah declared that Urdu would be the national language but provinces could use their languages.
    52. Opposition against Urdu was there in East Bengal.
    53. Language Movement started in East Pakistan February, 1952.
    54. There was a complaint about anti Bengali language attitude of the federal government.
    55. Two-language formula was adopted in 1954.
