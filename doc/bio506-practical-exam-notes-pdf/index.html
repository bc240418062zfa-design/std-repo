Here are 15 open-ended questions from the file along with answers that
could be important from an examination point of view:

1. What are the two main types of glassware used for measuring liquid
volumes in a laboratory? Describe each briefly.
Answer: The two main types are bulb/graduated pipettes and burettes.
Bulb/graduated pipettes are used to transport specific amounts of fluids
from one place to another. Burettes are used to dispense exact quantities
of liquid into another vessel.

2. Name the equipment used for anaerobic cultivation of
microorganisms in the laboratory.
Answer: The Anaerobic Jar is the equipment used for anaerobic
cultivation of microorganisms in the laboratory.

3. What is the principle behind the iodine test for detecting
carbohydrates?
Answer: The iodine test detects carbohydrates based on the intense "blue-
black" color that is produced when the triiodide anion complexes with
starch. This complex forms an infinite polyiodide homopolymer that gives
the distinct color change.

4. What observations indicate that fermentation has occurred in
yeast?
Answer: The main observation indicating fermentation by yeast is the
production and release of carbon dioxide gas. This can be detected by the
inflation of a balloon placed over the fermentation vessel.

5. How can amylose and amylopectin be distinguished in an extract?
Answer: Amylose stains dark blue/black when iodine is added while
amylopectin stains reddish brown with iodine solution. This color
difference allows them to be distinguished.

6. Where is glycogen primarily stored in the human body?
Answer: Glycogen is stored primarily in the liver and skeletal muscle
cells in humans.

7. What is the purpose of adding NaCl during glycogen extraction
from liver tissue?
Answer: Adding NaCl helps precipitate the glycogen out of solution so it
can be isolated by centrifugation when extracting from liver tissue.

8. What are polar and non-polar lipids? How would their separation
be achieved?
Answer: Polar lipids have hydrophilic head groups while non-polar lack
these. Their separation can be achieved by phase separation after folch
extraction where the non-polar lipids dissolve in the organic phase while
polar lipids stay in the aqueous phase.

9. Define retention factor (Rf) value in the context of thin layer
chromatography.
Answer: The Rf value is defined as the ratio of the distance moved by a
compound relative to the solvent front in thin layer chromatography. It is
a constant for a given compound with a particular solvent and adsorbent
system.

10. What change in urine could indicate a bacterial urinary tract
infection?
Answer: Cloudy or turbid urine may indicate a bacterial urinary tract
infection due to the presence of white blood cells.

11. What causes urine pH to vary under different conditions?
Answer: Urine pH can vary with diet, certain medications, kidney disease
as well as metabolic diseases like diabetes which can make it more acidic
or alkaline.

12. What is the principle behind using Fehling's reagent to detect
glucose in urine?

Answer: Fehling's reagents react with reducing sugars like glucose to
form a brick red precipitate of cuprous oxide. The formation of this
precipitate indicates the presence of glucose.

13. Why must glassware used for lipid extraction be very clean?
Answer: The high sensitivity of the analytical methods used to measure
extracted lipids means that even small amounts of contaminants can
interfere with the results, hence very clean glassware is essential.

14. What is the purpose of homogenizing tissue samples during lipid
extraction?
Answer: Homogenization helps break down the tissue structure to
facilitate efficient extraction of lipids by the organic solvents.

15. What preanalytical conditions are vital for reliable urinalysis in
the laboratory?
Answer: Key preanalytical factors are proper sample collection technique
(midstream), using first morning urine sample, ensuring no infection or
exercise prior to collection, refrigeration after collection and analysis
within 8 hours if possible.

16. How can a saturated solution be distinguished from an
unsaturated one?

Answer: A saturated solution cannot dissolve any more solute at a given
temperature, while an unsaturated solution can still dissolve more solute.

17. What are the units used to express very dilute concentrations in a
solution?
Answer: Parts per million (ppm) is used to express very dilute
concentrations of a solute in a solution.

18. What does a colony counter consist of and what is its function?
Answer: A colony counter has an illuminated stage with a magnifying
lens attached and is used to facilitate counting of bacterial or fungal
colonies growing on culture medium in a petri dish.

19. What is the purpose of flame sterilizing inoculating needles/loops?
Answer: Inoculating needles and loops are flamed to red hot before and
after use to kill any contaminating microbes through heat sterilization.

20. Name the scale used to indicate the acidity or alkalinity of
solutions.
Answer: The pH scale measures the acidity or alkalinity (basicity) of a
solution based on hydrogen ion concentration.

21. What is the structure of glycogen and how does it differ from
starch?

Answer: Glycogen is a branched polysaccharide while starch has an
unbranched structure. The branching allows more non-covalent bonds in
glycogen allowing it to dissolve more readily.

22. Why must slides be baked before use in thin layer
chromatography of lipids?
Answer: Baking evaporates any moisture on the slide that could interfere
with sample separation during TLC.

23. How does centrifugation help in the microscopic analysis of urine?
Answer: Centrifugation concentrates any cells, casts, crystals etc. in urine
to the bottom so they can be readily visualized under the microscope.
