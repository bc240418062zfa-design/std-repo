   1. Nutrition
Nutrition includes all of those processes by which an animal takes in, digests, absorbs, stores, and
uses food (nutrients) to meet its metabolic needs.
   2. Role of Magnesium (Mg) in Human Body?
Mg is the component of many co enzymes; needed for normal neuron and muscle function as well as
carbohydrates and protein metabolism.
   3. Phosphorus (P)
• Major positive ion in cells; influences muscle contraction and neuron excitability; part of DNA, RNA,
ATP, energy metabolism.
   4. Write example of macronutrient?

   6. Give example of protein

   7. Difference between Intracellular & Extracellular Digestion?
Digestion is of two main types:
   ❖ Intracellular:
In intracellular digestion, breakdown of food occurs within the cells. • First food in taken into the cell
vacuole. • The cytoplasm secretes digestive enzymes into the food vacuole. • Enzymes break larger
molecules into smaller ones, which enter the cytoplasm.
   ❖ Extracellular:
• In extra cellular digestion, the enzymes are secreted outside the cell into the gut cavity or lumen
where digestion occurs. • Nutrients from the food then pass into body cells for energy metabolism or
biosynthesis.
   8. What is fluid feeder?
• Fluid Feeders are the animals which feed on biological fluids of animals and plants. • These fluids
are a rich source of nutrients. • Fluid feeding is the characteristic of some parasites, such as the
intestinal nematodes that bite and rasp off host tissue or suck blood. • External parasites
(ectoparasites), such as leeches, ticks, mites, lampreys, and certain crustaceans, use a wide variety of
mouthparts to feed on body fluids.
For example:
   o Insects have the most highly developed sucking structures for fluid feeding.
   o Butterflies, moths, and aphids have tube-like mouthparts that enable them to suck up plant
     fluids.
   o Blood-sucking mosquitoes have complex mouthparts with piercing stylets.

   9. Contractile Vacuole
Contractile vacuole is a specialized type of vacuole that regulates the quantity of water inside a cell.• A
contractile vacuole is a sub-cellular structure involved in osmoregulation.• The contractile vacuole, as
its name suggests, expels water out of the cell by contracting. Many freshwater species (protozoa,
sponges), have contractile vacuoles that pump out excess water.• Contractile vacuoles are energy-
requiring devices that expel excess water from individual cells exposed to Hypo-osmotic
environments

   10.        Role of Gall Bladder in Digestion
• The gallbladder is a small bag near the liver. • The gallbladder stores the greenish fluid, the bile that
the liver cells continuously produce. • Bile is very alkaline and contains pigments e.g. biliverdin
(green) and bilirubin (Red) • Bile also contain salts that act as detergents to emulsify fats and aid in
fat digestion and absorption.• Bile salts also combine with the end products of fat digestion to form
micelles.
                                                    OR
Gallbladder is a small hollow organ where bile is stored and Concentrated. When we eat, bile is
released into the small intestine to help break down and digest fats. It plays a crucial role in the
digestion and absorption of dietary fats. • The structure and position of the gallbladder can vary
significantly among animal species.
   11.        How Primitive Streak forms in Birds?
• The major structural characteristic of avian, gastrulation is the primitive streak. • This streak is first
visible as a thickening of the epiblast at the posterior region of the embryo.• As these cells migrate
from the lateral region of the posterior epiblast toward the center of the Primitive streak, that
elongates toward the future head region. The streak eventually extends 60 – 75% of the length of the
area pellucida. • The primitive streak defines the axes of the embryo. It extends from posterior to
anterior end.
   12.        What is extra fertilization?
Extra fertilization, also known as polyspermy, occurs when multiple sperm successfully penetrate and
fertilize an egg. In normal fertilization, only one sperm is supposed to enter the egg to prevent genetic
abnormalities. However, in cases of extra fertilization, the presence of multiple sperm can disrupt the
normal development of the embryo. To prevent this, the egg has mechanisms in place to block
additional sperm from entering after the first one. This ensures that only one sperm can fertilize the
egg and maintain the proper balance of genetic material.
   13.        Digestion in grasshopper
 During feeding, the mandibles and maxillae break up the food, which then goes into the mouth and
passes to the crop through the esophagus. Saliva, containing the enzyme amylase, helps with
carbohydrate digestion. The crop stores the food, and the midgut secretes enzymes for further
digestion. Nutrient absorption primarily occurs in the midgut and intestine. Undigested material
moves along the intestine, and water and ions are absorbed in the rectum. Finally, solid fecal pellets
are eliminated through the anus.

primary osmoregulatory structures.
