         MEANING, THOUGHT AND REALITY

Topic 28: Sense and Concepts

What is the role of sense in language and thought?

Sense is a mental representation that links words and the real world.

How does sense relate to the concept of resemblance?

The relationship between sense and the real world is one of resemblance.

How can the word "triangle" have different senses for different speakers?

Different speakers may have various mental images of triangles based on their
experiences.

What modification is made to the image theory regarding the sense of some
words?

What is the role of sense in language and thought?

Sense plays a crucial role in bridging the gap between language and thought. It
represents a level of mental representation that links words to the real world. In
other words, it allows us to understand and conceptualize the meaning of words
by associating them with mental images or representations. This connection
between words and mental representations is fundamental in understanding how
we convey and comprehend meaning through language.

Topic 29: Necessary and Sufficient Conditions

What is the traditional approach for describing concepts?

Necessary and sufficient conditions are used to describe concepts.

What are necessary conditions in the context of concepts?

Necessary conditions are attributes that something must have to be considered
an example of a concept.

What are sufficient conditions in the context of concepts?

Sufficient conditions are the right set of conditions that define a concept fully.

Topic 30: Prototypes

How is the model of concepts as prototypes structured?

Concepts are structured with central, typical members and less typical or
peripheral members.

How do experiments support prototype theory?

Speakers tend to agree more readily on typical members, which come to mind

What is the essence of the prototypical conception of categories?

The prototypical conception emphasizes typical members and the gradient nature
of categories.

How is the model of concepts as prototypes structured?

The model of concepts as prototypes is structured in a way that acknowledges the
existence of central or typical members within a category, such as "BIRD" or
"FURNITURE." In this model, there are central members that are considered
highly typical and representative of a particular category. These central members
gradually transition into less typical or peripheral members, which do not exhibit
all the typical characteristics but still belong to the same category.

Topic 31: Linguistic Relativity

Who are the key figures associated with the notion of linguistic relativity?

Edward Sapir and Benjamin Lee Whorf are associated with linguistic relativity.

What does linguistic relativity propose regarding language and thought?

It suggests that the language we speak conditions our conceptualization and is
influenced by culture.

Topic 32: The Language of Thought Hypothesis

Why do proponents of cognitive science reject linguistic relativity?

Cognitive science rejects linguistic relativity and argues that thinking can occur
without language.

What is the "language of thought hypothesis"?

It posits that cognitive processes use a separate computational system in the
mind, a "language of thought."

How do proponents of the "language of thought" view the relationship between

They believe that we think in a language of thought (Mentalese) and translate it

Lesson 07

                          Lexical Relations - I
Topic 33: What Are Lexical Relations?

What is the branch of semantics that deals with word meanings called?

Lexical semantics.

How would you define lexical relations?

Lexical relations are relationships of the meanings of words to other words.

What types of relations do lexical relations encompass, aside from semantic
relations?

Lexical relations include phonetic relations such as rhyme and alliteration,
morphological relations such as inflectional variations, and morpho-syntactic
relations such as co-membership in a grammatical category.

What does the term "lexical relation" encompass, and how does it differ from
just a semantic relation?

The term "lexical relation" includes phonetic relations like rhyme and alliteration,
morphological relations such as inflectional variations, and morpho-syntactic
relations like co-membership in a grammatical category. It goes beyond just
semantic relations and contributes to the network-like structure of our mental
lexicon.

Topic 34: Words and Lexical Items

What is the focus of interest in semantic relations?

Lexemes or semantic words.

How do words differ in their representation in different linguistic levels (writing,
phonology, syntax)?

Words can be identified in writing by white spaces, in phonology as strings of
sounds, and in syntax, they can have various grammatical variations.

What is a lexeme in semantics?

A lexeme is the same semantic word represented by several grammatically
distinct variations.
