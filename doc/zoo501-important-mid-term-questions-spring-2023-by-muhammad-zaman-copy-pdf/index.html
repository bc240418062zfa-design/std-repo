What happens when egg and sperms fuse?
When the egg and sperm fuse:

   1. The sperm's membrane merges with the egg's, releasing its nucleus into the
      egg.
   2. The genetic material from both gametes combines, forming a zygote with a
      complete set of chromosomes, marking the restoration of diploid genetic
      content.
   3. The egg activates, initiating embryonic development and cellular division.

This fusion is the first step in the creation of a new organism and signifies the
beginning of embryogenesis.

Function of progesterone
In frogs, progesterone plays a crucial role in egg maturation and ovulation:

   1. Stimulating Meiotic Resumption: Progesterone signals the egg to resume
      meiosis, which had been arrested in metaphase I. This allows the egg to
      complete the first meiotic division.
   2. Ovulation Induction: It promotes the release of the mature egg from the
      ovary, preparing it for fertilization.
   3. Reproductive Role: Progesterone ensures the egg's readiness for
      fertilization by coordinating hormonal and cellular processes

Autonomous specifications
Autonomous Specification

It is the process where cells are pre-programmed to develop into specific cell types based on the
cytoplasmic molecules (determinants) they inherit during division.

      Key Features:
          1. Mosaic Development: Each cell in the embryo independently determines its fate,
             similar to a tile in a mosaic.
          2. Invariant Cleavage: The same lineage of cells is produced in every embryo of
             the species.

         3. No Compensation: If a blastomere (early embryonic cell) is removed, the
            organism cannot replace its function, leading to incomplete development.
         4. Characteristic of Invertebrates: Commonly seen in species like mollusks and
            annelids.
         5. Precedes Migration: Cell fate is determined before large-scale embryonic cell
            movements occur.

Difference between Monospermy and Polyspermy
   1. Monospermy:
         o Only one sperm enters the egg during fertilization.
         o The haploid sperm nucleus combines with the haploid egg nucleus,
            forming a diploid zygote.
         o It ensures normal embryonic development by providing the correct
            chromosome number.
   2. Polyspermy:
         o Multiple sperms enter the egg.
         o This causes a triploid or abnormal nucleus, with extra
            chromosomes, leading to non-viable embryos or developmental
            issues.
         o It disrupts normal fertilization and is considered a pathological
            condition

Avoidance of polyspermy
Polyspermy, the entry of multiple sperm into an egg, is prevented by the fast block
to polyspermy:

   1. Fast Block Mechanism:
         o Upon the first sperm’s entry, the egg's plasma membrane
            depolarizes by changing its electric potential.
         o Sodium ions from the external environment flow into the egg, altering
            the membrane potential.
   2. Ionic Concentration:
         o The egg cytoplasm has low sodium and high potassium, unlike
            seawater, which has a high sodium concentration.

         oThis creates a resting membrane potential of approximately -70 mV,
          which is reversed to block additional sperm.
  3. Maintaining the Block:
       o The plasma membrane prevents sodium ion leakage and restricts
          potassium ion loss to sustain the new potential.

Branches of metazoans

Diploblasts
     Animals with two germ layers: ectoderm (outer layer) and endoderm (inner
      layer).
     Lack a true mesoderm (middle layer).
     Examples: Cnidarians (e.g., jellyfish, hydras) and Ctenophores (e.g., comb
      jellies).

Protostomes
     From the Greek term meaning "mouth first."
     During embryonic development, the mouth forms first from the blastopore,
      and the anus develops later.
     Includes phyla like mollusks, arthropods, and annelids (worms).

Deuterostomes
     From the Greek term meaning "mouth second."
     In this group, the anus forms first from the blastopore, followed by the
      mouth.
     Includes phyla like chordates (e.g., vertebrates) and echinoderms (e.g.,
      starfish).

Key Differences:

     Diploblasts have two germ layers, whereas protostomes and
      deuterostomes are triploblastic with three germ layers (ectoderm,
      mesoderm, and endoderm).
     Protostomes form the mouth first, while deuterostomes form the anus first

Mosaic development
Mosaic development refers to a type of embryonic development where each cell's
fate is predetermined and invariant, often dictated by the distribution of
cytoplasmic determinants during early cleavages.
This autonomous specification means that the blastomeres develop independently,
and their fates cannot be altered. If a blastomere is lost, the corresponding part of
the embryo will not form, as seen in organisms exhibiting strict lineage patterns
Hammerling's hypothesis
Hammerling's hypothesis suggests that the nucleus produces stable mRNA, which
remains inactive in the cytoplasm until it is needed for specific development, like
cap formation. This means that the nucleus controls development by
transcription, while the cytoplasm plays a role in translation. In this way,
development in unicellular organisms is managed at both levels.

Note on developmental Hierarchy
Developmental Hierarchy refers to the stepwise decisions that guide cells from a
broad potential to a specific function. These decisions are typically irreversible and
progressively restrict a cell's fate.

During animal development, the embryo divides into three germ layers:

   1. Ectoderm: Forms skin and nervous system.
   2. Mesoderm: Forms muscles, bones, and blood.
   3. Endoderm: Forms internal organs like the gut and lungs.
