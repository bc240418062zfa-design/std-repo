                     ENG523
1. Discourse Analysis

The study of language use beyond the sentence level, focusing on how meaning is constructed in
different contexts and social interactions.

2. Text Grammarians

Experts who analyze written texts to understand relationships between linguistic elements,
focusing on how these elements form coherent structures.

3. Functional Linguistics

An approach that examines language based on its social functions, emphasizing how grammar
interacts with meaning and context.

4. Halliday’s Framework

A model by M.A.K. Halliday that views language as a system of choices influenced by social
functions, focusing on thematic and informational structures.

5. Context and Culture in Language

The idea that language use is shaped by its context and cultural influences, affecting how it is
interpreted and used.

6. Pragmatics

A branch of linguistics that studies how context influences the interpretation of meaning,
including speaker intentions and implied meanings.

7. Speech Acts

Actions performed via language, such as requesting, apologizing, or commanding. Speech acts
analyze what the speaker intends to accomplish.

8. Intonation in Discourse

The rise and fall of pitch in speech, which can change the meaning of a statement and reflect
emotions or emphasis.

9. Cohesion

The linguistic connections within a text that link sentences together, such as pronouns,
conjunctions, or lexical repetition.

10. Coherence

The logical and meaningful connection of ideas in a text, ensuring it "makes sense" to the reader
or listener.

11. Turn-Taking

The structured process of managing conversational turns, ensuring participants know when to
speak or listen.

12. Adjacency Pairs

Paired utterances in conversation where the first part predicts a specific type of response, like a
question-answer or greeting-reply sequence.

13. Initiating Moves

The first action in an interaction, such as asking a question or making a statement, to start a
conversational exchange.

14. Eliciting Exchanges

Interactions where one speaker seeks specific information, often seen in classroom settings, such
as teacher questions and student responses.

15. Boundary Exchanges

Discourse structures that signal transitions or divisions within conversations, such as shifting
topics or concluding segments.

16. Transactional Language

Language used to achieve specific tasks or exchange information, such as ordering food or
asking for directions.

17. Interactional Talk

Language used for social purposes, like building relationships or maintaining communication, as
in casual conversations.

18. Discourse Markers

Words or phrases, such as well, so, or you know, used to organize speech, manage turn-taking, or
signal shifts in topic.

19. Genre vs. Text Type

Genres are culturally recognized categories like novels or essays, while text types (e.g., narrative,
descriptive) focus on structural properties of texts.

20. Problem-Solution Pattern

A common discourse structure presenting a situation, identifying a problem, proposing a
solution, and evaluating the result.

21. Claim-Counterclaim Pattern

A structure where a claim is stated and challenged by a counterclaim, often used in debates and
argumentative texts.

22. General-Specific Pattern

A pattern where a general idea is followed by specific examples or details, commonly found in
academic writing.

23. Cultural Rhetoric

The study of how cultural beliefs and values shape rhetorical practices and language use in
different societies.

24. Comparative Rhetoric

A cross-cultural analysis of rhetorical traditions, highlighting differences and similarities in
communication styles.

25. Schema Theory

A theory stating that readers use their prior knowledge (schemata) to interpret and understand
texts.

26. Illocutionary Acts

The intended function of a speech act, such as asserting, questioning, or commanding.

27. Speech Events

Specific communicative situations, like interviews, lectures, or debates, each with its own norms
and conventions.

28. Discourse Units

Segments of language that form coherent sections of discourse, such as paragraphs, turns, or
entire conversations.

29. Conversational Analysis

A method of studying the structure and patterns of spoken interaction, focusing on turn-taking
and conversational norms.

30. Politeness Theory

A framework analyzing how speakers maintain social harmony by managing face (self-image)
through politeness strategies.

31. Face-Saving Strategies

Techniques used to avoid threatening someone's self-image in conversation, such as softening
criticism.

32. Back-Channeling

Listeners' verbal or non-verbal cues (e.g., mm-hmm, nodding) to show attention and encourage
the speaker.

33. Transactional Model

A communication model where participants exchange messages simultaneously, influencing
each other interactively.

34. Overlapping Talk

When two or more speakers talk at the same time, often signaling enthusiasm or competition for
the floor.

35. Topic Management

The skill of maintaining, shifting, or closing topics in conversation.

36. Foregrounded Information

Highlighted or emphasized information in discourse that the speaker wants to focus on.

37. Backgrounded Information

Information given less emphasis or treated as secondary in a discourse.

38. Intercultural Communication

Communication between individuals from different cultural backgrounds, requiring sensitivity to
cultural norms and values.

39. Narrative Discourse

The structure and function of storytelling, focusing on how events and characters are presented.

40. Textual Patterns

Recognizable structures within texts, like cause-effect or compare-contrast, aiding
comprehension and organization.

41. Explicitness in Written Text

The degree to which a text clearly conveys meaning without relying on shared context or
assumptions.

42. Reader-Text Interaction

How readers engage with and interpret a text, influenced by their background knowledge and
expectations.

43. Critical Discourse Analysis

A method of examining how power and ideology are reflected and constructed in language use.
