1. What is Language Awareness?

Language awareness refers to the explicit knowledge and sensitivity to language forms,
functions, and uses. It supports understanding how language works and helps learners monitor
and develop their language proficiency.

2. What is meant by Second Language?

A second language is any language learned after the first language (mother tongue), used for
communication in daily life or education.

3. Define Fossilization.

Fossilization is a phenomenon where incorrect language forms become a fixed part of a learner’s
second language use, despite exposure to correct forms or further instruction .

4. Define Scaffolding (Activity Theory).

Scaffolding is the temporary support given by a teacher or more capable peer to assist a learner
within their Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD), enabling the learner to perform a task until
they can do it independently .

5. Define Input and Output.

      Input: Language data learners are exposed to (e.g., reading, listening).
      Output: Language produced by learners (e.g., speaking, writing).

6. Define Diglossia.

Diglossia is a sociolinguistic situation where two language varieties coexist in a society: one high
(formal) and one low (informal), used in different contexts .

Private speech is the self-directed speech used by learners (especially children) to plan, guide, or

8. Three Types of Private Speech (Ohta):

   1. Repetition – Echoing another’s utterance.
   2. Expansion – Modifying and adding to a heard sentence.
   3. Substitution – Replacing words to create variations .

9. What is Productive Skill?

Productive skills are language abilities used to produce language, including speaking and
writing.

10. Producing Language

Refers to learners actively using the language in speech or writing to express themselves.

11. Input Enhancement

Input enhancement involves techniques (like bolding or underlining) used to make specific
language features more noticeable to learners .
