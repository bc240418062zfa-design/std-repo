                    BIF101
   CURRENT FINAL TERM SUBJECTIVE
      By (HAFIZA SAMEEN)
                 VU EXPERT ZOOLOGIST

1. File type used in NGS format:
  ● FASTQ
  ● FASTA
  ● SAM
  ● BAM
2. Role of binding sites in translation:
  ● Initiate protein synthesis and regulation
  ● Specific sequences attract ribosomes and tRNA
  ● Ensure accurate translation and gene expression
  ● Regulate protein production and function
3. Role of bioinformatics in biological research:
  ● Analyze and interpret biological data
  ● Develop algorithms and statistical tools
  ● Understand complex biological systems
  ● Enable personalized medicine and genomics

4. Carbon is central atom of Amino acid:
  ● Forms covalent bonds with other atoms
  ● Creates a central scaffold for amino acids
  ● Diverse functional groups attached to carbon
  ● Determine amino acid properties and functions
5. Structural modeling:
  ● Predict 3D structures of biological molecules
  ● Use computational methods and algorithms
  ● Understand protein function and interactions
  ● Enable drug discovery and design
6. Releasing factors in prokaryotes and Eukaryotes:
  ● Prokaryotes: RF1, RF2, and RF3
  ● Eukaryotes: eRF1 and eRF3
7. Cell is a functional unit of life:
  ● Basic structural and functional unit
  ● Carry out essential biological processes
  ● Maintain homeostasis and respond to stimuli
  ● Enable life and support complex organisms
8. Factors studied after sequencing:
  ● Gene expression and regulation
  ● Genetic variation and mutation
  ● Epigenetic modifications and chromatin
  ● Understand biological processes and disease

9. Binding sites of ribosomes:
  ● mRNA binding site:
    Shine-Dalgarno
  ● tRNA binding sites: A, P, and E
  ● Ribosome binding sites: 16S and 23S rRNA
10. Work of ribosomal RNA in translation:
  ● Form ribosome structure and function
  ● Catalyze peptide bond formation
  ● Regulate translation initiation and elongation
  ● Essential for protein synthesis and life
11. Types of symmetry:
  ● Line symmetry
  ● Radial symmetry
  ● Rotational symmetry
  ● Glide symmetry
12. Modeling for Homology AND Ab initio methods:
Homology modeling
of the target structure can be done as follows:
1. Template recognition and initial alignment
2. Alignment correction
3. Backbone generation
4. Loop modeling

5. Sidechain modeling
6. Model optimization
7. Model validation.
Ab initio methods
have Anfinsen’s thermodynamic hypothesis at the
center.These methods attempt to identify the structure
with minimum free energy.
Need for Ab Initio Modelling
• Applicable to any sequence
• Not very accurate biologically
• Accuracy and applicability are
limited by our understanding of the protein folding
problem.
13.I-TASSER
(Iterative Threading ASSEmbly Refinement) is a
bioinformatics method for predicting three-
dimensional structure model of protein molecules from
amino acid sequences.
14.FASTA and gene bank:
FASTA is a sequence Format. DNA sequences are stored
in specified formats. FASTA is the most frequently used
format to present DNA and Protein sequences.

Gene banks are a type of biorepository that preserves
genetic material. ... In an effort to conserve agricultural
biodiversity, gene banks are used to store and conserve
the plant genetic resources of major crop plants and
their crop wild relatives.
15.Difference between the I-tasser and modeler:
I-TASSER
(Iterative Threading ASSEmbly Refinement) is a
bioinformatics method for predicting three-
dimensional structure model of protein molecules from
amino acid sequences.
MODELLER
is used for homology or comparative modeling of
protein three-dimensional structures (1,2).
16.Applications of proteomics:
Proteomics-based technologies are utilized in various
capacities for different research settings such as
detection of various diagnostic markers, candidates for
vaccine production, understanding pathogenicity
mechanisms, alteration of expression patterns in
response to different signals and interpretation of
functional protein pathways in different diseases.
Proteomics is practically intricate because it includes
the analysis and categorization of overall protein

signatures of a genome. Its also applicable in
personalized medicines, biomarker discovery, drug
discovery and development, systems biology,
agriculture, food sciences, poleo proteomics and in
astrobiology.
17.Types of GEO:
1)Sample (GSM) preparation and description of the
samples.
2) Platform (GPL) technology used and the features
detected – microarray or RNASeq.
3) Series (GSE) defines a set of samples and how they
are related.
4) Datasets (GDS) sample data collections assembled by
GEO.
18.What is algorithm: Bioinformatics is the study of
living organisms viewed as information processors. We
study algorithms for sequence alignment, motif finding
and gene finding, construction of phylogenetic trees,
and structure prediction.
19.NCBI: (National Center for Biotechnology
Information) NCBI has two options:
1) BANKIt
  ● For simple sequences and annotations

  ● For submission through web
  ● Do not require advanced tools
2) Sequin
  ● For Complex sequences and annotations
  ● For off-line submissions
  ● Do require advanced tools and graphical reports.
20.Entrez:
is an integrated search engine which allows users to
search and retrieve different data from the NCBI.
Entrez is a molecular biology database system that
provides integrated access to nucleotide and protein
sequence data, gene-centered and genomic mapping
information, 3D structure data, PubMed MEDLINE.
21.Basic modes of protein Localization:
1. Gated transport
2. Transmembrane transport
3. Vesicular transport
22.Folding process:
Protein folding is a process by which a polypeptide
chain folds to become a biologically active protein in its
native 3D structure. The amino acids in the chain
eventually interact with each other to form a

well-defined, folded protein.The amino acid sequence
of a protein determines its 3D structure.
