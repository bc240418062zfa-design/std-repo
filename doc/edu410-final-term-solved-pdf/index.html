Q. Define short vowels

A short vowel is a pure vowel sound (monophthong) that does not allow the vowel
within it to generate its long sound. It does not glide up or down towards a new
position of articulation.

Q. What is a consonant blend?

A consonant blend is a sequence of two or more consonants in a word where each
consonant maintains its sound, such as "bl" in "blue" or "str" in "street".

Q. Explain the 3-2-1 reading strategy:

This strategy is used after reading to review the day's lesson and promote higher-
order thinking. The steps include:

3 things they discovered

2 things they found interesting

1 question they still have.

Q. Why are idioms and collocations difficult for learners?

Idioms and collocations are difficult because:

   •   They have no direct translation.
   •   They do not follow grammatical rules.
   •   They require understanding of context and frequency.
   •   First language interference affects comprehension.

Q. What are the main tenses in English?

There are three main tenses in English: Past, Present, and Future. Each of these has
four aspects: Simple, Progressive, Perfect, and Perfect Progressive.

Q. What difficulties do learners face while pronouncing triphthongs?

Triphthongs involve a glide from one vowel to another and then to a third, all
produced rapidly. This quick tongue movement makes them difficult to perceive and
pronounce.

Q. What are the types of vowel sounds?

   •     Monophthongs (single, fixed vowel sounds)
   •     Diphthongs (a glide between two vowel sounds)
   •     Triphthongs (a glide through three vowel sounds).

Q. What is the difference between independent and dependent clauses?

   •     Independent Clause: Can stand alone as a complete sentence.
   •     Dependent Clause: Cannot stand alone and needs an independent clause.

Q. What is the difference between receptive and productive vocabulary?

   • Receptive vocabulary: Words understood when heard or read.
   • Productive vocabulary: Words actively used in speaking or writing.

Q. Explain bottom-up and top-down strategies in reading:

   •     Bottom-up: Focuses on decoding words and understanding smaller
         linguistic units.
   •     Top-down: Uses background knowledge and context to derive meaning.

Q. What is word study?

Word study involves understanding the structure, meaning, and usage of words to
improve vocabulary and spelling.
