Question # 1                                                                    Marks:4
A university survey asks students to report their:
  1. Major (e.g., Biology, English, Engineering)
  2. Year in school (Freshman, Sophomore, Junior, Senior)
  3. GPA on a 4.0 scale
  4. Number of hours studied per week
For each of the four variables, identify the correct level of measurement:
nominal, ordinal, interval, or ratio. Justify your answer briefly.

Solution:
    In the given university survey, each of the four variables represents a different level of
measurement. The correct classifications and justifications are as follows:

1. Major
       o Level of Measurement: Nominal
       o Justification: The "major" of a student refers to a category or label such as

         Biology, English, or Engineering. These are names without any intrinsic order
         or ranking. Therefore, this variable is measured on the nominal scale.

2. Year in School
       o Level of Measurement: Ordinal
       o Justification: The academic year (Freshman, Sophomore, Junior, Senior) follows

         a logical order, showing progression through university. However, the differences
         between the years are not necessarily equal, so this is an ordinal variable.

3. GPA on a 4.0 Scale
       o Level of Measurement: Interval
       o Justification: GPA is a numerical value where differences between values are

         meaningful (e.g., the difference between 3.0 and 3.5 is the same as between 2.5
         and 3.0). However, it does not have a true zero, as a GPA of 0 does not mean "no
         academic achievement". Thus, GPA is measured on an interval scale.

4. Number of Hours Studied per Week
       o Level of Measurement: Ratio
       o Justification: This variable is quantitative, with a true zero point (0 hours means

         no study at all) and meaningful ratios (studying 10 hours is twice as much as 5
         hours). Hence, this is a ratio-level measurement.

Conclusion:

Question # 2                                                                    Marks:6
A nutritionist claims that the average daily sodium intake of adults in a certain
city is 2,300 mg. A researcher suspects that the actual average is different. To
test this, the researcher collects a random sample of 25 adults and finds that
their average daily sodium intake is 2,450 mg with a standard deviation of 400
mg.
At the 0.05 significance level, is there enough evidence to reject the
nutritionist’s claim?

Solution:
  A nutritionist claims that the average sodium intake = 2,300 mg. A researcher believes
the average is different (not specified whether higher or lower).
