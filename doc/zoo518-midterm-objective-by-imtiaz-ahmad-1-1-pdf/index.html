Midterm Objective:

Zoo518T-Applied entomology and pest management:

Whatsapp: 03196386297

QNo-1: The starfish and sea urchin are often seen at the seashore.

QNo-2: The largest group of all, however, is the Arthropoda.

QNo-3: In arthropods the body is composed of a series of more or less similar
pieces or segments.

QNo-4: beach fly is included in which group of Arthropods? Crustacea

QNo-5: The crustacea are mainly water-inhabiting animals which breathe
either by gills.

QNo-6: Antennae of insects are also known as? feelers

QNo-7: Loving organ of insect is? Antennae or feelers

QNo-8: Small diplopods about the diameter of the lead of a pencil and gray in
color are often found boring into potatoes and roots in the ground in the fall
and are sometimes wrongly called wireworms.

QNo-9: The chilopoda are also land animals.

QNo-10: In the body of the adult insect there are apparently from 7 to 15
segments.

QNo-11: The embryo shows the presence of 6 segments in the head 3 in the
thorax and 11 or 12 in the abdomen.

QNo-12: The cuticula hardens quickly after its production and consists quite
largely at least of a nitrogenous substance called chitin.

QNo-13: The scleritis on the back of each segment are usually together termed
the notum or tergum.

QNo-14: Those on each side called the pleuron and those below called the
sternum.

QNo-15: Heads with the mouth beneath are called hypognathous; those with it
in front are prognathous.

QNo-16: The cuticle of the surface of the body is transparent where it covers
the surface of an eye permitting access of light to the sensory structures
within.

QNo-17: The function of the maxillae appears to be to hold and retain the food
in the mouth while it is being worked upon by the mandibles.

QNo-18: The presence of sense organs on the maxillary palpi suggests that
these are possibly concerned with the sense of smell.

QNo-19: The first abdominal segment, when seemingly more a part of the
thorax than of the abdomen, is called the median segment or propodeum.

QNo-20: The coxa is the segment that articulates with the body, frequently
partly lying in a more or less cup shaped hollow of the latter.

QNo-21: The trochanter is usually small and may not be visible on all sides of
the leg.

QNo-22: At the end of the last a pair of claws is generally found, and between
them a sort of pad or cushion, the pulvillus.

QNo-23: Small openings, usually a pair in each or at least in some of the
segments are the openings of the breathing organs.

QNo-24: At the end of the abdomen in the females of those insects which lay
their eggs within objects is a combination of pieces known as an ovipositor.

QNo-25: The stomach may develop side pouches or gastric caeca.

QNo-26: The trachea frequently enlarges here and there forming so called
air-sacs.

QNo-27: Insects have only an incomplete system of blood vessels.

QNo-28: The organs that eliminate the nitrogenous wastes from the body and
correspond in function to the human kidneys are known as Malpighian tubes.

QNo-29: The nervous system of insects is located along the middle line of the
body quite near its under surface.

QNo-30: Each ganglion is connected to the one in front and the one behind by
one or two bundles of nerve fibers which are called commissures.

QNo-31: The former are for the most part gathered together in clusters which
are called ganglia.

QNo-32: The larger nerves are really bundles of the fibers running side by side
like the wires of a telephone cable.

QNo-33: All the more evident senses possessed by men appear to be present
in insects Except possibly taste.

QNo-34: In the female the eggs are produced in a pair of ovaries located in the
upper front part of the tube and then pass downward.

QNo-35: Those having eggs that remain in the body of the parent for a time
after hatching are termed ovoviviparous.

QNo-36: Eggs of insects are covered by a chitinous shell, the chorion, which
often bears markings in the form of ridges.

QNo-37: The feeding periods between the molts are called instal,s.

QNo-38: A caterpillar is totally different in appearance from the butterfly; it
finally resembles the white grub in the earth.

QNo-39: It is called June bug? May beetle

QNo-40: In some flies the larva shrinks within its larval skin and transforms,
this skin, now called a puparium.
