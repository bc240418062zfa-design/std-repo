                             What are Viruses
 Virology is the study of viruses

 Are Viruses Living or Non-living?
  What are Viruses?
 A virus is a noncellular particle made up of genetic material and protein
  that can invade living cells

 Obligate intracellular parasites

 Too small to be seen by optical microscopes

 Size typically 20-400 nM: rod shaped or spherical

 Replicate inside host cells using the cell’s enzyme systems

                              What are Viruses
 They have nucleic acid genomes, either DNA or RNA

 However, only one type of nucleic acid is found in the virion of any particular
  type of virus

 Single-stranded (ss), double-stranded (ds), or in the hepadnaviruses, partially
  double-stranded

 Coding capacity: ranging from few proteins to >200 proteins

                                 What are Viruses
 Unlike all living organisms, viruses do not 'grow' or undergo division
  but are produced from the assembly of pre-formed newly synthesized
  components within the host cell

 Host range - wide
 Viruses infect all types of living cells - animals, plants & bacteria
 animals
 plants
 microorganisms, including bacteria and archaea

                             What are Viruses
Viruses are smaller than bacteria: common mistake
The largest virus known (currently Pithovirus sibericum) is 1,200 nm long, while the
smallest bacteria (e.g., Mycoplasma) are only 200-300 nm long.

The largest virus genome (Pandoravirus, 2.8 Mbp—million base pairs—
approximately 2,500 genes)
is twenty times as big as
Smallest bacterial genome (Tremblaya princeps, at 139 kbp—thousand base pairs—
and with only 120 protein coding genes)

Although it is still shorter than the smallest eukaryotic genome (parasitic protozoan
Encephalitozoon, 2.3 Mbp)

        General Characteristics of Viruses
 Viruses are found everywhere

 They are acellular, that is, they contain no cytoplasm or
  cellular organelles

 Metabolically inert

 Obligatory intracellular parasite

 They carry out no metabolism on their own and must
  replicate using the host cell's metabolic machinery

        General Characteristics of Viruses
 In other words, viruses don't grow and divide

 New viral components are synthesized and
  assembled within the infected host cell

 Viruses can infect animals, plants, and even other
  microorganisms

 Totally dependent on their host cell for replication,
  they cannot be grown in synthetic culture media

           General Characteristics of Viruses
 Viruses are infectious agents with both living and nonliving
  characteristics

 Living characteristics of viruses include the ability to reproduce – but
  only in living host cells – and the ability to mutate

   Nonliving characteristics include the fact that
   not cells
   have no cytoplasm
   cellular organelles,
   carry out no metabolism
   therefore must replicate using the host cell's metabolic machinery

 They have a nucleic acid core containing DNA or RNA but no both

 Contain a protein coat called the capsid
