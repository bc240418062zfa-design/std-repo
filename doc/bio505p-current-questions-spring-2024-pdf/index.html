1. How is the DNA Pellet Diluted by Organic Method?

The DNA pellet obtained through the organic method is typically diluted by adding an appropriate buffer, such
as TE buffer (10 mM Tris-HCl, 1 mM EDTA), to resuspend the DNA. The organic extraction method involves
phenol-chloroform extraction, which helps remove proteins and other contaminants. After centrifugation, the
aqueous phase containing DNA is collected and precipitated with ethanol or isopropanol. The resulting DNA
pellet is then washed with 70% ethanol, air-dried, and finally diluted in the buffer for subsequent use.

2. Work of Spectrophotometer

A spectrophotometer measures the absorbance or transmittance of light by a sample at specific wavelengths. In
molecular biology, it is commonly used to quantify nucleic acids and proteins by measuring absorbance at 260
nm and 280 nm, respectively. The absorbance ratio (A260/A280) helps assess the purity of DNA or RNA. The
instrument operates by passing a light beam through a sample and measuring the intensity of light reaching a
detector, providing valuable data on the concentration and purity of the sample.

3. Why RNA Pellet is Purified with RNase-Free Water at -40 to -80°C?

RNA pellets are purified with RNase-free water at temperatures ranging from -40 to -80°C to prevent degradation
by RNases. RNases are ubiquitous and highly stable enzymes that can degrade RNA, leading to loss of sample
integrity. The low temperatures help inactivate any residual RNase activity and preserve the RNA's integrity
during storage. Using RNase-free water ensures that no RNase contamination is introduced during the purification
process, maintaining the quality of the RNA sample for downstream applications.

4. Advantages and Disadvantages of Western Blotting

Advantages:

   1. High specificity and sensitivity in detecting target proteins.
   2. Allows quantification of protein expression levels.
   3. Can differentiate between protein isoforms and post-translational modifications.
   4. Provides visual confirmation through antibody binding and detection.

Disadvantages:

   1. Time-consuming and labor-intensive process.
   2. Requires high-quality antibodies, which can be expensive.
   3. Potential for non-specific binding and background noise.
   4. Limited to detecting proteins with available antibodies.

5. What is Colony PCR?

Colony PCR is a technique used to screen bacterial colonies for the presence of a specific DNA sequence. A small
portion of a bacterial colony is picked and directly subjected to PCR amplification without prior DNA extraction.
This method is used to quickly identify colonies that contain recombinant plasmids or desired genetic constructs.
The PCR product is then analyzed by gel electrophoresis to confirm the presence of the target sequence, making
colony PCR a rapid and efficient tool for molecular cloning and genetic engineering.

6. Sources of DNA Extraction

DNA can be extracted from a variety of sources, including:

   1. Blood: Commonly used for clinical and forensic analysis.
   2. Saliva: Non-invasive and convenient for genetic testing.
   3. Tissue samples: Obtained from biopsies or surgical specimens.
   4. Buccal swabs: Easy and non-invasive method for collecting DNA from cheek cells.
   5. Plant material: Leaves, seeds, and roots for botanical studies.
   6. Microorganisms: Bacteria, fungi, and viruses for research and diagnostic purposes.

7. Primer Used for Denaturation Step of PCR

Primers are not used during the denaturation step of PCR. The denaturation step involves heating the DNA sample
to around 94-98°C to separate the double-stranded DNA into single strands. Primers are short, single-stranded
DNA sequences added during the annealing step (around 50-65°C), where they bind to the complementary
sequences on the single-stranded DNA, allowing the DNA polymerase to extend the DNA during the extension
step.

8. Advantages and Disadvantages of PCR

Advantages:

   1. Highly sensitive, capable of detecting small amounts of DNA.
   2. Rapid and efficient, with results available in a few hours.
   3. Specific, with the ability to amplify specific DNA sequences.
   4. Versatile, applicable to various fields including diagnostics, forensics, and research.

Disadvantages:

   1. Susceptible to contamination, leading to false positives.
   2. Requires precise temperature control and high-quality reagents.
   3. Limited by the size of the DNA fragment that can be amplified.
   4. PCR inhibitors in the sample can affect the efficiency and accuracy of the reaction.

9. Composition and Storage of 5M Sodium Chloride

5M Sodium Chloride (NaCl) solution is prepared by dissolving 292.2 grams of NaCl in enough water to make 1
liter of solution. This high concentration solution is typically used in various molecular biology and biochemical

applications. It should be stored at room temperature in a tightly sealed container to prevent evaporation and
contamination. For long-term storage, it can be kept at 4°C.

10. Why We Use Trizol Reagent?

Trizol reagent is used for the extraction of RNA, DNA, and proteins from biological samples. It is a monophasic
solution of phenol and guanidine isothiocyanate, which helps in:

   1. Simultaneous extraction: Allows the isolation of RNA, DNA, and proteins from a single sample.
   2. RNA protection: Inactivates RNases, protecting RNA from degradation during the extraction
      process.
   3. High yield: Provides high-quality and high-quantity RNA suitable for downstream applications like
      qPCR and sequencing.
   4. Versatility: Effective for a wide range of sample types, including tissues, cells, and blood.
