Define physiology?
Ans: It is the study of functions of tissues, organs and organ systems of animals to understand the
mechanisms operating in animals.
Features of Cardiac Muscles. 3
   1.   Cardiac muscle cells (myocyte) are mononucleate.
   2.   Skeletal muscle cells are multinucleate.
   3.   They don’t produce discrete PSP.
   4.   They only increase or decrease the strength of myogenic contractions.
Donnan Equilibrium
“If diffusible solutes are separated by a membrane that is freely permeable to water and
electrolytes but totally impermeable to one species of ion, the diffusible solutes become
unequally distributed between the two compartments”.
Describe the types of feedback system?
1= Negative Feedback:(Negative feedback is a regulatory mechanism in which a 'stimulus'
causes an opposite 'output' in order to maintain an ideal level of whatever is being regulated.)
2= Positive Feedback: Positive feedback is the amplification of a body's response to a
stimulus.
What is meant by muscle twitch?
Ans: A muscle twitch is a single contraction in response to a single action potential.
Three Components of a Single Muscle Twitch: 1= Latent period, or lag phase 2= Contraction
phase 3=Relaxation phase
What is sarcoplasmic Reticulum?
Ans: The sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR) is a membrane-bound structure found within muscle cells
that is similar to the smooth endoplasmic reticulum in other cells. The main function of the SR is
to store calcium ions (Ca2+).

What is Acclimation?
Acclimation refers to a physiological change in an individual stimulated by exposure to a
different, often stressful, environment. As such it represents physiological phenotypic plasticity.

How permeability can be altered?
Hormones and some other molecules can alter the permeability to certain substances. Hormones
react with receptor sites and influence channel size or airier mechanisms.
Acclimatization
•An adaptive change within an individual animal.
Example: 1=An animal migrates to a high altitude area 2= Faces low oxygen partial pressure
Effects: 1=Lung ventilation rate increases initially 2=Ventilation drops to normal in few days
3=Animal has acclimatized to the high-altitude
Acclimation: Changes are induced experimentally in the laboratory
Example: 1=Simulating high-altitude conditions in lab 2=An animal physiologist places the
animal in a hypobaric chamber.
Effects: 1= Breathing rate reacts in the same way. 2= Animal becomes acclimated to the
experimental conditions
Rigor Mortis After death, human and other animal’s bodies gradually become rigid.This
condition is called rigor mortis.
Role of Ca2+ in Cross-Bridge Attachment
1= Ca2+ ions play a crucial role in regulating the contractile activity of muscles.
2= Calcium helps to expose the active sites of actin that bind myosin heads to form cross-
bridges, necessary for contraction.
Neurofibrils: Delicate threads running from cell body into the axon and dendrites.
Neurofibrils Functions: 1=Give support and shape to the neuron. 2=Transport substances
from cell body to the distal cell processes.
Quantal Release of Neurotransmitter 2
•Neurotransmitters are generally released in tiny packets called quanta.
•Each quantum may consist of about 2000 to 10,000 molecules of transmitter molecules.
Conformers and its type 2
Animals that conform to external changes. They Are unable to maintain homeostasis for internal
conditions
Types: 1=Osmoconformers 2=Oxyconformers 3=Thermoconformers
Terminal cisternae of Sarcoplasmic Reticulum 3
Terminal cisternae are larger chambers that are closely associated with T-tubules (12 nm apart).
These are the primary site of calcium release as they have channel proteins that open with AP in
T tubules.
Action potentials: are the type of signals that neurons use to send information over long
distances.
Properties of Hormones:
1= They act only on specific target cells or tissues. 2=Hormonal action depend on the type of
receptor. 3= Hormones are active at very low concentration. 4= The amount of hormone
produced by an endocrine gland is small. 5= It is further diluted in the blood and interstitial fluid.
Phases of AP: 1. Stimulation 2. Rising phase (Depolarization) 3. Peak phase. 4.Falling phase
(Repolarization) 5. Undershoot (after hyperpolarization)
Synthesis of Steroid Hormones: 1=Steroid hormones are not stored in the glandular cells
normally. 2=They are synthesized from cholesterol instantly when a stimulus is received. 3=For
this purpose, cell maintains large stores of cholesterol which can be rapidly mobilized.
T Tubules: 1= T tubules are thin internal extensions of the cell membrane. 2= They are less
than 0.1 μm in diameter. 3= They innervate the cell at the level of Z disk. 4= Make branching
networks around the perimeter of each myofibril.
Role of T Tubules: 1= T tubule system provides the anatomic link between the surface
membrane and the myofibrils deep inside the muscle fiber. 2=When an action potential spreads
over a muscle fiber membrane, a potential change also spreads along the T tubules to the deep
interior of the muscle fiber. 3= In the cell interior, T tubes are linked to SR. 4= SR releases Ca2+
that permit myosin cross-bridges to attach and generate force for contraction.
