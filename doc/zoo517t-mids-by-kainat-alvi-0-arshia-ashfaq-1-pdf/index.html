  4)    Define Digestion & its Types.
Digestion is the chemical and/or mechanical breakdown of complex food by the action
of enzymes into simpler diffusible molecules i.e. particles that individual cells of an
animal can absorb.
        o Starch into glucose.
        o Proteins into amino acids.
        o Fats into fatty acids & glycerol.
Digestion is of two main types:
  1) Intracellular: In intracellular digestion, breakdown of food occurs within the
     cells. • First food in taken into the cell vacuole. • The cytoplasm secretes digestive
     enzymes into the food vacuole. • Enzymes break larger molecules into smaller
     ones, which enter the cytoplasm.
  2) Extracellular: • In extra cellular digestion, the enzymes are secreted outside
     the cell into the gut cavity or lumen where digestion occurs. • Nutrients from the
     food then pass into body cells for energy metabolism or biosynthesis.

  5)    Types of Protein

   o Structural proteins: Support e.g. Collagen, Keratin
   o Storage Proteins: Storage of amino acids e.g. Albumin, Casein
   o Transport proteins: Transport of other proteins e.g. Hemoglobin
   o Hormonal proteins: Coordination of activities e.g. Insulin
   o Contractile Proteins: Movement e.g. Actin and myosin
   o Defensive proteins: Protection against disease e.g. Antibodies
   o Enzymatic: Acceleration of chemical reactions. E.g. Pepsin, trypsin.

   6)    What is fluid feeder?
• Fluid Feeders are the animals which feed on biological fluids of animals and plants.
• These fluids are a rich source of nutrients. • Fluid feeding is the characteristic of
some parasites, such as the intestinal nematodes that bite and rasp off host tissue or
suck blood. • External parasites (ectoparasites), such as leeches, ticks, mites, lampreys,
and certain crustaceans, use a wide variety of mouthparts to feed on body fluids.
For example:
   o Insects have the most highly developed sucking structures for fluid feeding. •
     Butterflies, moths, and aphids have tube-like mouthparts that enable them to
     suck up plant fluids.
   o Blood-sucking mosquitoes have complex mouthparts with piercing stylets.
   7)    Role of Gall Bladder in Digestion
• The gallbladder is a small bag near the liver. • The gallbladder stores the greenish
fluid, the bile that the liver cells continuously produce. • Bile is very alkaline and
contains pigments e.g. biliverdin (green) and bilirubin (Red) • Bile also contain
salts that act as detergents to emulsify fats and aid in fat digestion and absorption.• Bile
salts also combine with the end products of fat digestion to form micelles.
                                            OR
Gallbladder is a small hollow organ where bile is stored and Concentrated. When we
eat, bile is released into the small intestine to help break down and digest fats. It plays
a crucial role in the digestion and absorption of dietary fats. • The structure and
position of the gallbladder can vary significantly among animal species.
   8)    Part of small intestine?
The small intestine has three parts:
   1) Duodenum - It is about 6 m in length and functions primarily in digestion.
   2) Jejunum and Ileum. -Both function in nutrient absorption. There is no clear
      border between the jejunum and the ileum.

   9)    Small Intestine Function in Digestion?
Duodenum:
When chyme passes from the stomach into duodenum, its acidity stimulates the release
of three secretions:
   1) Digestive enzymes : Intestinal enzymes help break down carbohydrates,
      proteins, and fats in the small intestine.
   2) Pancreatic enzymes: Pancreatic enzymes aid in digestion by further breaking
      down these nutrients.
   3) Bile: The liver produces bile to emulsify fats and helps with the absorption of
      digested nutrients.

Absorption of Food: When we eat, the small intestine also absorbs nutrients from
our food. Sugars and amino acids are absorbed into the capillaries, while fatty acids
combine with glycerol to form triglycerides. These triglycerides are then coated with
proteins and enter the lacteals.
   10) Contractile Vacuoles
• The contractile vacuole is a specialized type of vacuole that regulates the quantity of
water inside a cell.• A contractile vacuole is a sub-cellular structure involved in
osmoregulation.• The contractile vacuole, as its name suggests, expels water out of the
cell by contracting. Many freshwater species (protozoa, sponges), have contractile
vacuoles that pump out excess water.• Contractile vacuoles are energy-requiring
devices that expel excess water from individual cells exposed to Hypo-osmotic
environments
   11) How Primitive Streak forms in Birds?
• The major structural characteristic of avian, gastrulation is the primitive streak. • This
streak is first visible as a thickening of the epiblast at the posterior region of the
embryo.• As these cells migrate from the lateral region of the posterior epiblast toward
the center of the Primitive streak, that elongates toward the future head region. The
streak eventually extends 60 – 75% of the length of the area pellucida. • The primitive
streak defines the axes of the embryo. It extends from posterior to anterior end.
