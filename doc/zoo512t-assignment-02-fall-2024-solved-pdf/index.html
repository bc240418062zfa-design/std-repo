Task Assigned as Assignment # 2

Topic: Ecdysis or Molting

1. What is ecdysis? (1 mark)

2. Why it is required in insects? (1 mark)

3. Write four steps involved in the process of Ecdysis. (3 marks)

4. Make a handwritten diagram showing the steps involved in the process of Ecdysis in

an insect. Ensure that your diagram is labeled clearly. (5 marks)

1. What is ecdysis?

Ecdysis, or molting, is a biological process wherein arthropods, including insects, shed their old
exoskeleton to allow growth or morphological changes. The exoskeleton is a rigid, non-living
structure made of chitin and proteins, providing protection, support, and a barrier against water
loss. However, this rigidity prevents the organism from increasing in size as it grows. Ecdysis is
triggered by hormonal changes, particularly the release of ecdysone, which initiates the process.

The process involves enzymatic digestion of the inner layers of the old exoskeleton and the
synthesis of a new, larger cuticle underneath. After shedding the old exoskeleton, the new cuticle
is initially soft and pliable, allowing the organism to expand by absorbing air or water before it
hardens. Ecdysis is a critical and vulnerable period for arthropods, as they lack the protection of
a hardened exoskeleton during this time.

2. Why is it required in insects?

Ecdysis is vital for insects because their exoskeleton is inflexible and does not grow with the
organism. As the insect grows, the exoskeleton becomes restrictive, preventing further
development. To accommodate growth, the old exoskeleton must be shed and replaced with a
larger one. This process is especially important during the juvenile stages of development, such
as in nymphs and larvae.

In addition to facilitating growth, ecdysis is essential for insects undergoing metamorphosis. For
instance, in holometabolous insects, such as butterflies, the transition from larva to pupa and
pupa to adult involves significant structural changes, all requiring ecdysis. Moreover, shedding
the exoskeleton allows the insect to remove any accumulated debris, pathogens, or parasites that
may have adhered to it. Without ecdysis, insects would be unable to grow, reproduce, or survive
long-term.

3. Write four steps involved in the process of ecdysis.

   1. Apolysis: The process begins with the separation of the old exoskeleton from the
      epidermis beneath it. This is initiated by the secretion of ecdysone, which triggers the
      epidermal cells to produce molting fluid. The molting fluid contains enzymes that digest
      the inner layers of the old exoskeleton, starting the degradation process.
   2. Secretion of the New Cuticle: As the molting fluid breaks down the old exoskeleton, the
      epidermis starts synthesizing a new cuticle beneath the old one. This cuticle is soft and
      contains layers of proteins and chitin, which will harden and form the new exoskeleton.
   3. Shedding of the Old Exoskeleton: The insect inflates its body by swallowing air or
      water, creating pressure that causes the old exoskeleton to crack along pre-determined
      lines called ecdysial sutures. The insect then wriggles out of the old exoskeleton,
      exposing the new, soft cuticle underneath.
   4. Hardening and Darkening of the New Exoskeleton: After the old exoskeleton is shed,
      the new cuticle undergoes a process called sclerotization. This involves the cross-linking
      of proteins, which hardens and strengthens the exoskeleton. Pigments are also deposited,
      giving the exoskeleton its characteristic color. This step ensures the insect’s structural
      integrity and protection.

4. Handwritten Diagram of Ecdysis Process

A detailed diagram should include the following stages:

   1. Apolysis: Label the epidermis, old exoskeleton, and molting fluid separating the layers.
   2. New Cuticle Formation: Show the newly formed soft cuticle beneath the degrading old
      exoskeleton.
   3. Shedding: Illustrate the insect emerging from the cracked exoskeleton, emphasizing the
      ecdysial sutures.
   4. Hardening: Depict the insect with its soft, expanded cuticle, and label the new hardened
      exoskeleton after sclerotization.
