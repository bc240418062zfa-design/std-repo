                                           BT513/Bio204

                                          Important mcqs
1. Change in state/ number/ size/ volume or in other sense it is the ratio between birth (B) and
death (D). Growth

2. A spectrophotometer is used to determine turbidity ("cloudiness") by measuring the amount
of light that passed through a suspension ofcells.

3. Growth kinetics refers to the change in number or size of organisms with respect to time.

4. A viable cell is defined as a cell which is able to divide and increase cell numbers.

5. A colony-forming unit (CFU) is a unit used to estimate the number of viable bacteria or
fungal cells in a sample.

6. Batch Culture is a closed system.

7. In batch culture "s" represent the concentration of the substrate.

8. Doubling time (td) is defined as the time required for cell mass to double.

9. number of generation ‘n’ after time 't' are given by: N=t/td

10. Cell density (Xt) after time ‘t’ will be: Xt = Xo2n = Xo2t/td
x ∝t
dX∝dt
change in biomass ∝change in time

11. dX =µ.X.dt (dX/dt =µ.X).......X=concentration of microbial biomass, µ=is the Specific Growth
Rate per unit cell mass

12. Xt = Xoeµt

Xo= original biomassconcentration
Xt= biomass concentration after the time interval t hours
e=base of the natural logarithm

13. If, lnX is taken along Y-axis & T is taken on X-axis, a plot of the natural logarithm of biomass
concentration against time should yield a straight line, the slope of which would equal µ.

14. µ (specific growth) is applied only when cell is unicellular.

15. The Growth Yield (Y) is the increase in cell mass (dX) which results due to consumption of a
small amount of substrate (dS)

16. The maximum specific growth rate represented by µmax.

17. A substrate-specific constant, Ks {µ= µmax/Ks+S (MONOD EQUATION)}

18. Specific growth rate is independent of substrate concentration as long as excess substrate
is present.

19. Accumulation = Growth –Death

20. Tropophase (Refers to exponential phase- stage where primary metabolites are produced).

21. Idiophase (Usually refers to stationary or death phase- where secondary, tertiary or other
metabolites are produced).

22. Borrow et al., (1961) divided the growth of organisms into several phases.

23. Yoshida et al., (1973) introduced the term fed-batch culture.

24. The kinetics of variable volume in fed-batch culture have been developed by Dunn and Mor
(1975) and Pirt (1974, 1975, 1979).

25. As time progresses the dilution rate will decrease as the volume increases and D will be
given the expression: D = F / (Vo +Ft) where Vo is the original volume.

26. According to Monod kinetics, residual substrate should decrease as D decreases resulting in
an increase in the cell concentration.

27. µ is constant in the chemostat but decreases in the fed- batch.

28. Pirt (1979) has expressed the change in product concentration in variable volume fed-batch
culture.

29. Product concentration changes according to the balance between production rate and
dilution by the feed.

30. Fed-batch quasi steady state change over the time of the fermentation.

31. As in the case of variable volume fed-batch the feed profile would be optimized according
to the relationship between qp and µ.

32. Exponential growth in batch culture may be prolonged by the addition of fresh medium to
the vessel.

33. The substrate concentration in the vessel will rise because fewer cells are left in the vessel
to consume it.

34. An alternative type of continuous culture to the chemostat is the turbidostat.

35. Overall growth Yield is related maintenance and growth requirement for limiting substrates
that act as energy sources.

36. The function of the fermenter or bioreactor is to provide a suitable environment in which
an organism can efficiently produce a target product.

37. There is no universal bioreactor.

38. Adequate aeration and agitation should be provided to meet the metabolic requirements of
the micro-organism.

39. European Federation of Biotechnology GILSP = Good Industrial Large Scale Practice.

40. Pathogenicity....The number of organisms required to initiate an infection.

41. On a small scale (1 to 30 L) it is possible to use glass and/or stainless steel.

42. Glass is useful because it gives smooth surfaces, is non-toxic, corrosion proof and it is
usually easy to examine the interior of the vessel.

43. The largest practical diameter for glass fermenters is 60 cm.

44. AISI Grade 316 steels which contain 18% chromium,10% nickel and 2-2.5% molybdenum
are now commonly used in fermenter construction.

45. In a citric acid fermentation where the pH may be 1 to 2 it will be necessary to use a
stainless steel with 3-4% molybdenum (AISI grade 317) to prevent leaching of heavy metals
from the steel which would interfere with the fermentation.

46. AISI Grade 304, which contains 18.5% chromium and 10% nickel, is now used extensively
for brewing equipment.

47. Nitryl or butyl rubbers are normally used for these seals as they will withstand
fermentation process conditions.

48. Number of impellers will depend on the height of the liquid in the reactor.

49. Each impeller will have between 2 and 6 blades.

50. Most microbial fermentations use a Rushton turbine impeller.

51. For large reactors, a 3 phase motor should be used.

52. Radial flow impellers are designed to, generate high shear conditions.

53. The Rushton turbine has 4 or 6 blades which are fixed onto a disk. The diameter of the
Rushton turbine should be 1/3 of the tankdiameter.

54. A Rushton turbine is often referred to as a disk turbine.

55. Axial flow impellers are used for mixing shear sensitive processes such as crystallization
and precipitation reactions.

56. Intermig impeller is a axial flow impeller which is used for microbial fermentations.

57. To make an accurate estimate of heating/cooling requirements for a specific process it is
important to consider the contributing factors.

58. Qexch is the heat which will have to be removed by a cooling system.
