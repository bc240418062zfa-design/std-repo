Q. Purpose of structure of conclusion
The purpose of structuring a conclusion in research writing is to summarize the key
findings and contributions of the study. It serves to reinforce the main argument or
hypothesis, discuss the implications of the results, and suggest avenues for future
research. A well-structured conclusion ensures that the reader understands the
significance of the study and its broader implications.
Q. Function of conclusion
The function of a conclusion in research writing is to provide closure by
summarizing the main points discussed in the paper. It offers a synthesis of the
findings and interpretations, emphasizes the significance of the research, and often
suggests recommendations or implications for further study. It acts as the final
opportunity to leave a lasting impression on the reader and to reinforce the
importance of the work conducted.
Q. Conclusion
In the context of academic writing, a conclusion refers to the final part of a paper or
thesis where the main findings, interpretations, and implications of the research are

summarized. It ties together the various threads of the argument and provides a sense
of closure to the reader.
Q. Generative writing
Generative writing refers to a technique where the focus is on generating ideas and
content without being overly concerned with editing or correctness. It encourages
free-flowing creativity and exploration of ideas, which can be particularly useful
during the initial stages of writing to overcome writer's block or to explore different
angles of a topic.
Q. Concise writing
Concise writing involves expressing ideas clearly and succinctly, using the
minimum number of words necessary to convey the intended meaning. It avoids
unnecessary repetition, wordiness, and ambiguity, making the text more readable
and effective in communicating the author's message.
Q. Writing
Writing encompasses the process of putting thoughts, ideas, and information into
written form. In academic contexts, writing involves researching, drafting, revising,
and editing to produce clear and coherent texts that contribute to knowledge or
understanding within a particular field.
Q. Model verb
A modal verb is a type of auxiliary verb that expresses necessity, possibility,
permission, or ability. Examples include "can," "could," "may," "might," "must,"
"shall," "should," "will," and "would." In academic writing, modal verbs are often
used to convey degrees of certainty, obligation, or speculative interpretation.
Q. Capitalization
Capitalization refers to the use of uppercase letters in writing. In academic writing,
capitalization rules dictate when to capitalize proper nouns (e.g., names of people,
places, and organizations), the first word of a sentence, and specific titles or
headings.
Q. Types of figures
In research writing, figures typically refer to visual representations such as graphs,
charts, diagrams, photographs, or illustrations that are used to present data or
enhance understanding of concepts. Types of figures can include line graphs, bar

charts, scatter plots, and more complex diagrams depending on the nature of the data
being presented.
Q. Use of numbers in words
This refers to the convention of writing numbers as words (e.g., "twenty-five") rather
than numerals (e.g., "25"). In academic writing, numbers are generally written as
numerals for precise data and measurements, but numbers at the beginning of
sentences or those under ten are often written out in words for clarity and style
consistency.
Q. Guidelines for clear and concise writing
These are principles and practices that help writers communicate effectively and
efficiently. They include using simple and precise language, organizing ideas
logically, avoiding jargon or unnecessary technical terms, and ensuring coherence
and flow in the text.
Q. Why figure used in thesis?
Figures are used in a thesis to visually represent data, trends, relationships, or
concepts that are difficult to convey through text alone. They enhance the clarity and
impact of the research findings, making complex information more accessible and
understandable to readers.
Q. Conclusion structure and purpose
The structure of a conclusion in academic writing typically includes a summary of
key findings, a discussion of their implications, and suggestions for future research.
Its purpose is to provide closure to the paper by reinforcing the main arguments,
highlighting the significance of the research, and offering insights into its broader
implications.
Q. Sexiest bias use and distraction
This might refer to the concept of bias in research, where personal or cultural biases
can influence the design, conduct, interpretation, or reporting of research findings.
Bias can undermine the validity and reliability of research results if not properly
addressed or minimized. It's important in academic writing to acknowledge biases
and mitigate their impact to ensure objectivity and credibility.
Q. Why conclusions present in work?

Conclusions are present in academic work to summarize the main findings and
interpretations of the research. They provide closure by highlighting the significance
of the study, discussing its implications, and suggesting avenues for further
exploration or application of the findings.
Q. Types of figures and used briefly
Figures in academic writing can include tables, graphs, charts, diagrams, maps, and
photographs. They are used to present quantitative data, qualitative relationships,
trends, comparisons, or visual explanations that enhance the understanding and
impact of the research presented in the text.
Q. Discuss introduction chapter
The introduction chapter of a thesis or research paper sets the stage by providing
background information on the topic, stating the research problem or question,
outlining the research objectives, and explaining the significance or relevance of the
study. It serves to orient the reader and establish the context for the research that
follows.
