1. (Preparation of Stock and Working Solutions in Laboratory) You are
preparing solutions for a series of biochemical tests, and you need to ensure
the exact concentration of hydrochloric acid (HCl) for each experiment. If you
have a stock solution of 37% HCl and need to prepare 100 ml of an 8.5% HCl
solution, how much of the stock solution would you need?
Let me solve this step by step using the dilution formula.

Given:

- Stock solution concentration (C₁) = 37% HCl

- Required concentration (C₂) = 8.5% HCl

- Required volume (V₂) = 100 ml

- Need to find volume of stock solution (V₁)

Step 1: Using the dilution formula

C₁V₁ = C₂V₂

Step 2: Substituting the known values

37V₁ = 8.5(100)

Step 3: Solving for V₁

37V₁ = 850

V₁ = 850/37

V₁ = 22.97 ml

Step 4: Preparation method:

1. Measure 22.97 ml (rounded to 23.0 ml for practical purposes) of 37% HCl

2. Add it slowly to some distilled water in a suitable container (less than 100 ml)

3. Add more distilled water to make up to final volume of 100 ml

4. Mix gently

Safety Considerations:

- Always add acid to water, never water to acid ("AAW" rule)

- Perform in a fume hood

- Wear appropriate PPE (gloves, goggles, lab coat)

- Use glass measuring equipment

- Allow solution to cool as dilution is exothermic

Therefore, you would need 23.0 ml of 37% HCl stock solution to prepare 100 ml of 8.5% HCl
solution.

2. (Lab Equipment Familiarization: Lyophilizer) In 150 words, explain the
function of a Lyophilizer (freeze-dryer) and why it is commonly used in
biochemical laboratories for sample preservation
A Lyophilizer or freeze-dryer is a sophisticated laboratory instrument used to dehydrate samples
through the process of lyophilization (freeze-drying). Here's how it functions and why it's
valuable:

Function:

- Freezes the sample (usually to -40°C to -80°C)

- Creates a vacuum environment

- Allows frozen water to sublimate directly from solid to vapor state

- Removes water while preserving the sample's structural integrity

Common Applications in Biochemical Labs:

1. Protein Preservation

 - Maintains protein structure and activity

 - Prevents denaturation

 - Extends storage life

2. Sample Storage

 - Reduces sample degradation

 - Allows long-term storage

 - Maintains sample stability

3. Key Advantages:

 - Prevents thermal damage

 - Maintains biological activity

 - Preserves chemical composition

 - Easy sample reconstitution

 - Reduces contamination risk

This method is particularly valuable for preserving sensitive biological materials like proteins,
enzymes, microorganisms, and pharmaceuticals, making it an essential tool in biochemical
research and development.

3. (Iodine Test for Polysaccharides) You need to test starch concentration in
processed foods for quality control purposes. If the iodine solution has a
concentration of 0.5M and you add 3 drops of iodine to a sample, calculate the
total number of moles of iodine used (each drop measures 0.05 ml). Can you
elaborate whether concentration of blue-black colouration is dependent on
concentration of starch.
Let me solve this step by step.

Part 1: Calculating moles of iodine

Given:

- Iodine concentration = 0.5M (moles/liter)

- Number of drops = 3

- Volume per drop = 0.05 ml

Step 1: Calculate total volume in liters

Total volume = 3 drops × 0.05 ml/drop = 0.15 ml

Convert to liters: 0.15 ml × (1 L/1000 ml) = 0.00015 L

Step 2: Calculate moles using M = moles/L formula

Moles = Molarity × Volume (in L)

Moles = 0.5 M × 0.00015 L

Moles = 0.000075 moles or 7.5 × 10⁻⁵ moles of iodine

Part 2: Relationship between starch concentration and color intensity

Yes, the blue-black coloration intensity is dependent on starch concentration:

1. Direct Relationship:

- Higher starch concentration = Darker blue-black color

- Lower starch concentration = Lighter blue color

- Very low concentration = Light brown/yellow color

2. Scientific Explanation:

- Iodine molecules fit into helical structure of amylose

- More starch = More helices available

- More iodine-amylose complexes = Darker color

3. Quantitative Analysis:

- Color intensity can be measured spectrophotometrically

- Beer-Lambert law applies

- Absorbance is proportional to starch concentration

- Useful for quantitative determination

4. Limitations:

- Subjective visual interpretation

- Affected by other factors (temperature, pH)

- Best used with standard curve

- Not all starches react equally

This relationship makes the iodine test useful for both qualitative and semi-quantitative analysis
of starch content.
