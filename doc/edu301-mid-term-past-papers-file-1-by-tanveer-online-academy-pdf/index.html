What is the purpose of lessons planning?

Answer:

The purpose of lesson planning is to provide a roadmap for effective teaching and learning. It

helps teachers organize their thoughts, identify learning objectives, select appropriate

instructional strategies, and assess student progress. Lesson planning ensures that educational

goals are met, promotes student engagement, and allows for adjustments based on student

needs and feedback.

What is observational learning?

Answer:

                                                                                             1

Observational learning is a process in which individuals learn new behaviors or skills by

observing and imitating others. It involves paying attention to the actions of others, retaining

the observed information, and reproducing the behavior when the appropriate opportunity

arises. This form of learning is influenced by factors such as attention, retention, motor

reproduction, and reinforcement.

What is a Lesson plan?

Answer:

A lesson plan is a structured guide outlining the objectives, activities, and assessments for a

single instructional session, helping teachers effectively deliver content and engage students.

What is Social cognitivism?

Answer:

Social cognitivism is a theory of learning that emphasizes the role of social interactions and

observational learning in cognitive development. It posits that individuals learn through

observing others and participating in social activities, with an emphasis on factors such as

Simpson's model?

Answer:

The Simpson's model, also known as the Simpson's paradox, is a statistical phenomenon in

which a trend appears in several different groups of data but disappears or reverses when the

groups are combined. It highlights the importance of considering the nuances and potential

confounding variables when interpreting statistical results, as the overall trend may not

accurately reflect the underlying relationships within subgroups.

Define Modelling?

Answer:

Modelling is the process of representing real-world phenomena using simplified

representations or structures. It involves creating mathematical, conceptual, or physical models

to simulate, understand, and predict the behavior of complex systems or processes, aiding in

problem-solving, decision-making, and analysis.
