Q No.
How Fishes live in extremely cold water?

1. They have “antifreeze” materials in their blood in the form of Polyalcohols (e.g., sorbitol,
glycerol) or water soluble peptides and glycopeptides.
Because these substances lower the freezing point of blood plasma and other body fluids.
2. These fishes also have proteins or protein-sugar compounds that inhibit the growth of ice
crystals that begin to form.
• 3. These adaptations enable these fishes to stay flexible and swim freely in a super-
cooled state (i.e., at a temperature below the normal freezing temperature of a surrounding
water).

QNo. Intracellular and Extracellular digestion?
Digestion is of two main types: Intracellular and Extracellular.
                           S
In intracellular digestion breakdown of food occurs within the cells.
First food is taken into the cell vacuole.
The cytoplasm secretes digestive enzymes into the food vacuole. Enzymes break larger
molecules into smaller ones, which enter the cytoplasm.

• In extracellular digestion,
In it, the enzymes are secreted outside the cell into the gut cavity or lumen where digestion
occurs.

QNo. Gastrointestinal (GI)?

As with any organ, the function of
the gastrointestinal tract is determined by the type of tissues it contains.
• Most of the mammalian gastrointestinal tract has the same anatomical structure
along its entire length.
From the outside inward is a thin layer of connective tissue called the serosa.

The serosa forms a moist epithelial sheet called the peritoneum. This peritoneum lines the
entire abdominal cavity and covers all internal organs.Next are the longitudinal smooth-muscle
layer and circular smooth-muscle layer.
Underneath this muscle layer is the submucosa. The submucosa contains
connective tissue, blood, and lymphatic vessels.
• The mucosa faces the central opening, which is called lumen. The coordinated contractions of
the muscle layers of the
gastrointestinal tract mix the food material with various secretions and move the food
from the oral cavity to the rectum.
The two types of movement involved are peristalsis and segmentation.

Q No. The ruminant stomach:

Ruminant mammals are the animals that “chew their cud,’’ such as cows, sheep,
and deer.
The ruminant stomach provides an opportunity for large numbers of microorganisms to digest
the cellulose walls of grass and other vegetation.
• Cellulose contains a large amount of energy; however, animals generally lack the ability to
produce the enzyme cellulase for digesting cellulose and obtaining its energy. Because gut
microorganisms can produce cellulase, they have made the herbivorous lifestyle more effective.

Define Osmoregulation:
It is defined as the maintenance of constant osmotic pressure in the fluids of an organism by the
control of water and salt concentrations. It means osmoregulation governs water and salt
balance, and excretion of metabolic wastes.

In fishes, reptiles, birds, and mammals, the kidneys are the primary osmoregulatory structure.

The primitive strike The major structural characteristic of avian, reptilian, and mammalian

posterior region of the embryo, just anterior to Koller's sickle.
