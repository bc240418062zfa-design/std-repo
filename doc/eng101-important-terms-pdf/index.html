Reading and Dictionary

Reading involves decoding written symbols (letters and words) and understanding their meaning.
A dictionary is a reference book or digital resource that provides definitions, pronunciations,
and other information about words. When reading, a dictionary can help clarify the meaning of
unfamiliar words, aiding comprehension.

Pronunciation

Pronunciation refers to the way words are spoken, including the correct articulation of sounds,
stress, and intonation patterns. Proper pronunciation is essential for effective communication, as
it ensures that listeners can understand what is being said. In reading, understanding
pronunciation helps in decoding and recognizing words, especially when reading aloud.

Reading for Main Idea

Reading for the main idea involves identifying the central concept or argument of a text. It’s
about understanding the primary point that the author wants to communicate. Often, the main
idea is presented in the introduction or conclusion of the text, but it can also be found in the topic
sentence of each paragraph. Reading for the main idea helps readers focus on what’s important
and ignore less relevant details.

Reading: Topic, Main Idea

   •   Topic refers to the subject or what the text is about.
   •   Main idea is the specific point the author is making about the topic. The main idea
       provides the overall purpose of the text, while the topic is broader and more general.

For example, if the topic of a passage is "global warming," the main idea might be "global
warming is caused by human activities and needs urgent attention."

Reading: References

References in reading refer to words or phrases that direct readers to other parts of the text or
previous knowledge. This can include pronouns (he, she, it) or words like "this," "that," or
"such." References help readers connect ideas and maintain coherence within the text.

Reading Skills

Reading skills include the various techniques and strategies that help individuals understand,
interpret, and analyze texts. These skills include:

   •   Decoding words
   •   Understanding sentence structure
   •   Making inferences
   •   Identifying the main idea
   •   Skimming and scanning
   •   Critical reading (evaluating the text)

Developing these skills enables better comprehension and the ability to engage with different
types of texts.

Reading and Vocabulary

Reading and vocabulary are closely related. Vocabulary refers to the set of words a person
understands and uses. A rich vocabulary aids in comprehension because readers can grasp the
meaning of a text more easily. Reading new texts is a powerful way to expand vocabulary, as it
exposes readers to unfamiliar words in context.

Word Formation - Prefixes

Word formation refers to the process of creating new words through prefixes, suffixes, and
other methods. A prefix is a group of letters added to the beginning of a root word to alter its
meaning. For example:

   •   Un- in "undo" means "not."
   •   Re- in "replay" means "again." Understanding prefixes helps readers understand the
       meaning of unfamiliar words by recognizing common word parts.

Reading: Description, Locating Information

Reading for description involves identifying detailed information in a text. Descriptive
passages provide specific characteristics, features, or qualities about a subject. When locating
information, readers scan the text for facts, dates, names, or other specific data. This type of
reading is focused on finding and understanding particular pieces of information rather than
reading for overall comprehension.

Reading: Word Forms

Word forms refer to the different variations of a word that are created through changes in tense,
number, gender, or case. For example, the word "run" can appear as "running" (present
participle), "ran" (past tense), or "runner" (noun form). Understanding word forms helps readers
understand how words function within a sentence.

Reading: Understanding Reading

Understanding reading involves grasping the meaning of the text and interpreting its content. It
requires comprehension of vocabulary, sentence structure, and the underlying message. It also
involves connecting the new information to what the reader already knows, thus enriching their
understanding.

Reading: Making Inferences

Making inferences is the ability to understand information that is implied but not directly stated
in the text. Readers use clues from the text, their background knowledge, and context to draw
conclusions about things not explicitly mentioned. For example, if a passage says, "It was
pouring rain, and she grabbed her umbrella," the inference might be that the woman dislikes
getting wet or that it rains frequently in her area.

Reading: Assessing the Text

Assessing the text involves evaluating the effectiveness, accuracy, and quality of the reading
material. It includes looking at the author's arguments, the evidence provided, and whether the
text is well-structured and coherent. This is a critical skill for academic reading, as it helps
determine the value and reliability of the information.

Reading: Assessing Texts

This is similar to assessing the text but with a focus on evaluating the content more
comprehensively. It includes assessing:

   •   The clarity of the text
   •   The objectivity and bias of the author
   •   The relevance and quality of supporting evidence
   •   The overall logical flow of ideas

Evaluating Texts: Interpreting Visual Data

Interpreting visual data involves analyzing and understanding charts, graphs, tables, diagrams,
and other visual aids that accompany a text. Visual data can provide additional context and help
readers understand complex information, making it easier to evaluate and draw conclusions.
