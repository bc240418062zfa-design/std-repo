What is the scientific method, and what does it rely on?
Answer: The scientific method relies on reason, logic, and experimental evidence to
distinguish scientific correctness. Scientists test and retest before accepting a
discovery.

Why is it important that scientific discoveries be established repeatedly?
Answer: Repeated establishment in different laboratories and times by various people
is crucial for accepting a discovery as scientifically valid.

Do the laws of physics change from place to place or over time?
Answer: No, the laws of physics are believed to be consistent across locations and
times. Experiments worldwide have consistently yielded the same results.

How can physical quantities be expressed in terms of fundamental dimensions?
Answer: Physical quantities can be expressed in terms of Mass (M), Length (L), and

Is it allowed to add quantities with different dimensions?
Answer: No, you cannot add quantities with different dimensions. They must have the
same dimensions to be combined.

What is the distinction between units and dimensions in measuring physical
quantities?

fundamental properties (M, L, T). Different units can be used to measure the same
dimension.

What is x(t) in kinematics, and what does it represent?
Answer: x(t) is displacement, representing the position of a body at a given time.

Answer: The formula d = vt is most accurate for small time differences when a body's
speed remains relatively constant.

How is instantaneous velocity defined at any time t?
Answer: Instantaneous velocity at time t is defined as the rate of change of
displacement with respect to time, v = Δx/Δt.

What is instantaneous acceleration in physics, and what does negative
acceleration signify?
Answer: Instantaneous acceleration at time t is the rate of change of velocity.
Negative acceleration (deceleration) indicates a decrease in speed with time.

Can a body have a large acceleration and still be stationary at a given time?
Explain.
Answer: Yes, a body can have a large acceleration and be stationary if it's moving in
the opposite direction to its acceleration.

What is the relationship between velocity and time for a body starting from rest
and experiencing constant acceleration?
Answer: Velocity increases linearly with time when a body starts from rest and
experiences constant acceleration.

What formula should be used to calculate the distance traveled by a body with
constant acceleration when the speed is increasing linearly?
Answer: Use the formula x = x0 + vt + (1/2)at^2, where x0 is the initial position, v is
the initial speed, a is acceleration, and t is time.

How can you determine the final speed of a body after it has traveled a distance
x with constant acceleration?
Answer: Use the formula v^2 = v0^2 + 2ax to calculate the final speed.
