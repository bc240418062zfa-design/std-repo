                            Theme 1: Introduction to Marketing
  Topic 1: Understanding Marketing

  Marketing is the process of identifying customer needs and fulfilling those needs. It involves various
  terms and concepts that help us understand the dynamics of marketing.

  Three Further Terms of Marketing:

       Need: A need refers to something that is essential for a person to live a healthy life, such as
        air, water, and food.
        Example: Basic necessities like air, water, and food are considered needs.
       Want: A want is the form that a human need takes as shaped by culture and individual
        personality. It arises when a need is influenced by specific desires or preferences.
        Example: Want for mineral water or fast food, which are specific preferences based on
        personal or cultural factors.
       Demand: Demand refers to the quantity of goods that consumers are willing and able to
        purchase at various prices.
        Example: Having a budget of Rs. 2.5 lakh to purchase a car indicates the demand for a car
        within that price range.

  Exchange: Exchange is the process of obtaining what you want by giving something in return. It
  involves a transaction between two parties where goods, services, or money are transferred from
  one account to another. Example: Going to an eggs shop to purchase eggs for breakfast represents
  an exchange.

  Transaction: A transaction occurs when goods, services, or money are transferred from one account
  to another.

  Marketing Offering: A marketing offering refers to any combination of products, services, or
  information that is provided to the market to satisfy a customer's needs or wants. Example: Virtual
  student support services providing academic services to students worldwide are an example of a
  marketing offering.

  Marketing Mix:

  The marketing mix is a crucial concept in marketing. It consists of four elements, often referred to as
  the "4 Ps":

     1. Product: Refers to the tangible or intangible item or service that is offered to fulfill a
        customer's need or want.
     2. Price: Represents the amount of money customers are willing to pay for a product or service.
     3. Place: Refers to the distribution channels and locations where the product or service is made
        available to customers.
     4. Promotion: Involves the activities and strategies used to communicate and promote the
        product or service to the target market.

  Different Demand Situations:

     1. Negative Demand: Occurs when a product faces biased or negative views from potential
        customers. Example: Society perceiving frozen food as unhealthy compared to fresh food.
     2. No Demand: Refers to a situation where people are not aware of a product's existence.
        Creative marketing is needed to create demand.
     3. Latent Demand: Represents a situation where people may have a demand for a product, but
        it is currently hidden or unexpressed. Example: When insurance policies were initially
        introduced, they faced latent demand as people were not initially interested in them.
     4. Declining Demand: Occurs when the demand for a product starts to decline due to various
        reasons. Marketing strategies, such as re-marketing or product redevelopment, are employed
        to address this situation.

     5. Adequate or Sufficient Demand: Refers to a situation where the demand for a product is
        equal to the production capacity, indicating a regular and satisfactory demand level.
     6. Irregular Demand: Represents fluctuations in the demand for a product based on different
        seasons or specific periods.
     7. Overfull Demand: Occurs when the demand for a product exceeds the production capacity,
        resulting in overwhelming demand. Companies may employ de-marketing strategies to
        manage this situation.
     8. Unwholesome Demand: Refers to the demand for unhealthy products, such as narcotics or
        drugs.

  Marketing Philosophies:

  There are different marketing philosophies that guide the actions of marketers. Let's discuss a few of
  them:

     1. Production Concept: This concept believes that customers will favor products that are
        cheaper and more readily available. The focus is on increasing production to boost sales.
     2. Product Concept: The product concept focuses on understanding and showcasing the best
        qualities and features of a product. Marketers invest time and research to target their
        intended audience effectively.
     3. Selling Concept: The selling concept holds that consumers will not buy enough of a company's
        products unless it undertakes extensive selling and promotional efforts.
     4. Marketing Concept: The marketing concept emphasizes understanding the needs and wants
        of the target market and delivering superior customer value and satisfaction. It focuses on
        building long-lasting customer relationships.
