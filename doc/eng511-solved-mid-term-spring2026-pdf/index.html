Short Questions
1. What is pre-linguistic communication?

Pre-linguistic communication refers to the communication that occurs before children acquire
language. It includes crying, smiling, eye contact, facial expressions, gestures, and babbling used
to convey needs and emotions.

2. Differentiate between a friend and an acquaintance with reference to
communication.

       Friends: Use more informal, implicit, personal, and emotionally rich communication.
       Acquaintances: Use more formal, explicit, and socially distant communication with less
        shared background knowledge.

3. Name five types of idioms.

   1.   Pure idioms
   2.   Binomial idioms
   3.   Partial idioms
   4.   Prepositional idioms
   5.   Proverbs (idiomatic expressions)

4. What are two principles on which each level of representation in memory of
discourse is based?

   1. Meaning-based representation
   2. Structure-based representation

These operate across surface, propositional, and situational levels of discourse memory.

5. Explain in 2–3 lines Deese-Roediger-McDermott (DRM) paradigm.

The DRM paradigm is a memory experiment in which participants study lists of related words.
Later, they often falsely remember a related word that was never presented, demonstrating false
memory and semantic association effects.

6. Semantic network model.

A semantic network model represents knowledge as interconnected nodes (concepts) linked by
relationships. Meaning is stored in a network, and activation spreads from one concept to related
concepts.

7. Two principles of memory discourse in base.

   2. Meaning or propositional representation is retained longer and forms the basis of

8. Syntactic knowledge and why it’s beneficial.

Syntactic knowledge is the knowledge of grammatical categories and rules governing sentence
structure. It helps speakers form grammatical sentences and understand language efficiently.

9. Define reference in conversation between friends.

Reference is the relationship between words and the objects, people, events, or ideas they
represent in the real world. Friends often use shared references that both participants understand.

10. Term parapraxis.

Parapraxis is a slip of the tongue or speech error, commonly called a Freudian slip, where an
unintended word or phrase is produced.

11. What is meant by ‘editing process intervenes between the planning of
utterance and its articulation’?

It means speakers monitor and check their planned utterances before speaking. Errors may be
detected and corrected before actual articulation occurs. This internal editing process helps
ensure accurate speech.

12. What are 3 mental processes involved in use of language?

   1. Language comprehension
   2. Language production
   3. Language acquisition/use of language in communication

Long Questions
1. Explain sign language and enlist any three means of
communication with reference to sign language.
Sign language is a complete and natural language that uses visual-manual symbols instead of
speech sounds. It possesses its own grammar, syntax, and rules and is primarily used by deaf and
hard-of-hearing individuals. Psycholinguistic research has shown that sign languages are true
languages because they exhibit the same complexity and expressive power as spoken languages.

Three means of communication in sign language:

   2. Facial expressions – convey emotions and grammatical information.
   3. Body movements and posture – provide additional meaning and emphasis.

These components work together to create meaningful communication.

2. How can Motor Theory of Speech Perception be applied
in developing a speech therapy program for individuals who
struggle with phonetic sound differentiation?
The Motor Theory of Speech Perception, proposed by Liberman and colleagues, states that
listeners perceive speech by relating sounds to the articulatory movements used to produce them.
According to this theory, speech perception relies on knowledge of how speech sounds are
produced.

Application in Speech Therapy:

   1. Articulatory Training
          o Teach clients how specific sounds are physically produced by the tongue, lips,
             and vocal tract.
   2. Visual and Mirror Exercises
          o Use mirrors and visual demonstrations to show mouth movements for difficult
             sounds.
   3. Silent Articulation Practice
          o Encourage silent production of target sounds before speaking them aloud, as
             suggested by motor theory.
   4. Perception–Production Integration
          o Combine listening exercises with pronunciation practice so perception and
             production develop together.
   5. Feedback-Based Learning
