                                 SHORT QUESTIONS(3)
   1. How Movement is different from Locomotion?
• Movement is the act of changing location from one place to another.      e.g. a trolley, a ship
• Locomotion is the movement of an organism by its natural means. e.g. fish, insect, bird
   2. How odor is perceived in vertebrates?
 Odor molecules physically interact with protein receptors on the plasma membrane. • This
interaction alters membrane permeability and leads to generator potential.
   3. What are the type of neurons?
Types of Neurons:
   1) i Sensory (receptors)
   2) ii Interneurons (CNS)
   3) iii Motor (effectors)
Sensory Neurons act as receptors themselves or activated by receptors.
Sensory neurons are responsible for transmitting sensory information from the body to the central
nervous system (CNS), such as information about touch, temperature, and pain.
Interneurons comprise the integrating centers.
Interneurons are located entirely within the CNS, and are responsible for processing and transmitting
information between sensory and motor neurons.
Motor Neurons send the processed information to effectors (muscles or glands)
Motor neurons are responsible for transmitting motor commands from the CNS to the muscles and
organs of the body, allowing us to move and perform voluntary actions.
   4. Structure of different kinds of neurons I and II.

   ➢ Neuron I
Neurons are composed of:
   1) Cell Body
   2) Dendrites
   3) Axon
Cell Body: Central body that contains Nucleus.
Dendrites: Short thread like branches extending from cell body.
Axon is a long cylindrical process that extends from cell body.
Neurolemmocyte (Schwann Cell): These are the chain of supporting cells that surround the
axon forming insulation layer, the Myelin Sheath.
Neurofibril node (Node of Ranvier): A gap in the myelin sheath between adjacent Schwan Cells.
Synapse: Is the junction where the one neuron communicates with the other neuron or muscle or
gland cell. Also called Neuronal Junction.
   ➢ Neuron II
Glial cells:
They are thus known as the "supporting cells" of the nervous system. Main functions of glial cells are:
   o   To surround neurons and hold them in place.
   o   To supply nutrients and oxygen to neurons,
   o   To insulate one neuron from another, and
   o   To destroy and remove the carcasses of dead neurons (clean up).

   5. What do you know about photoreceptors of euglena? Explain its role.
   o Stigma (mass of bright red granule containing carotenoid).

   o Actual photoreceptor is the swelling at the base of the flagellum.
   o Stigma serves as a shield, which is essential for the receptor to detect light.
   o Thus the photoreceptor plus stigma enable euglena to orient itself so that its receptor is
     exposed to light.
   o This helps the protozoan maintain itself in the region where sufficient light is available.

   6. Benefits of cartilage over bone in vertebrates
Cartilage is a specialized type of connective tissue that provides:
   1) Supports
   2) Helps in movement at joint
   3) Site for muscle attachment.
Histologically it consists of:
   1) Cells (chondrocytes)
   2) Fibers
   3) Cellular matrix
Benefits:
Cartilage has several benefits over bone in vertebrates. First, cartilage is more flexible and elastic than
bone, which allows it to bend and deform without breaking. This is particularly important in areas of
the body that are subjected to a lot of stress or movement, such as the joints.
Second, cartilage is lighter than bone, which allows for greater mobility and agility. This is particularly
important in animals that need to move quickly or swim efficiently.
Finally, cartilage is capable of growing and repairing itself more quickly than bone. This is because
cartilage has a greater blood supply than bone, which allows for faster healing and regeneration.
Overall, cartilage is an important tissue in vertebrates because it provides flexibility, mobility, and
rapid healing.

   8. What are the integumentary system of insects in correct sequence?
The integumentary system of an insect consists of several layers, including the cuticle, epidermis,
basement membrane, and dermis. Here is a possible correct sequence:
1. Cuticle: The outermost layer of the integumentary system, which is made up of chitin and protein
and provides protection against physical and chemical damage.
2. Epidermis: The layer beneath the cuticle, which is responsible for the production of new cuticle
layers and the secretion of wax to prevent water loss.
3. Basement membrane: A thin layer of extracellular matrix that separates the epidermis from the
underlying dermis.
4. Dermis: The innermost layer of the integumentary system, which contains muscles, nerves, and
other structures that help the insect move and sense its environment.
   9. Integumentary system in birds
   o Skin of birds show many reptilian features with no epidermal glands except uropygial or
     preening gland.
   o Epidermis is thin having two to three cell layers.
   o Outer keratinized layer is quite soft.
   o Feathers are the most prominent epidermal derivatives.
   o The dermis of birds is similar to those of reptiles containing blood and lymphatic vessels,
     nerves and sensory bodies.

   o Feathers are important in thermal regulation, flying and behavior.

   10.        Integumentray system in protozoa(invertebrates)
   o Plasma Membrane
   o Pellicle
   o Calcium Carbonate shell
Some Protozoa belonging to Foraminifera have external shells of CaCo3 which secreted by the body.
Foraminifera are exclusively marine protozoans and have mollusk-like shells. These are the only
protozoa which are found fossilized.
