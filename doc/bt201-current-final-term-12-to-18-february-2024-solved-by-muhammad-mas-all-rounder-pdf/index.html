1. Properties of diatom: Diatoms are single-celled algae characterized by their unique cell
walls made of silica. They have a diverse range of shapes and sizes, often featuring intricate
patterns. Diatoms play a crucial role in aquatic ecosystems as primary producers.
2. Work of Robert Hooke: Robert Hooke was an English scientist who, among other things, is
credited with coining the term "cell" after his observations of cork cells under a microscope. He
made significant contributions to the field of microscopy and cell biology.
3. Difference between free-living and parasitic forms of protests: Free-living protests are
independent organisms that can exist and thrive on their own, while parasitic protests rely on a
host organism for nutrients and survival. Parasitic forms often cause diseases in their hosts.
4. Factors influencing decomposition in ecosystems: Decomposition is influenced by factors
such as temperature, moisture, nutrient availability, and the presence of decomposer organisms
like bacteria and fungi.
5. Effect of habitat and global warming: Changes in habitat and global warming can lead to
shifts in species distribution, migration patterns, and alterations in ecosystems. Species may
adapt, migrate, or face challenges in their survival.
6. Factors affecting decomposition rate: Factors include temperature, moisture, nutrient
content, presence of decomposer organisms, and the chemical composition of the organic matter
being decomposed.
7. Enlisted codon: A codon is a sequence of three nucleotides in DNA or RNA that codes for a
specific amino acid or serves as a start or stop signal during protein synthesis.
8. Lamarckism: Lamarckism is a theory of evolution proposing that acquired traits during an
organism's lifetime can be passed on to the next generation. However, this concept is not
supported by modern evolutionary biology.
9. Ancestral shared character: An ancestral shared character is a trait that is present in a
common ancestor and shared by different species within a taxonomic group.
10. Biosphere: The biosphere is the zone of Earth where living organisms exist, including the
land, water, and atmosphere. It encompasses all ecosystems and is crucial for supporting life.
11. Wormlike and algae fossils in 700-900 million-year-old rocks in China: These fossils
provide evidence of early life forms, suggesting the presence of multicellular organisms like
worms and photosynthetic algae during that ancient period.

12. Fitness: Fitness refers to the contribution of an individual's genotype to the next generation
through successful reproduction and survival.
13. Environment fostering clumped distributions: Patchy resources in an environment can
lead to clumped distributions as organisms gather where resources are abundant.
14. Gene pool: The gene pool is the sum of all genetic variation within a population of
interbreeding individuals.
15. Environment limiting population growth: The environment can limit population growth
by influencing birth rates, death rates, or both.
16. Environment limiting population growth by changing birth and death rates:
Environmental changes can affect population growth by altering both birth and death rates.
17. Behavior of Spiny-headed worms as exploitation: Exploitation refers to one organism
benefiting at the expense of another, which can be seen in the behavior of Spiny-headed worms
preying on host organisms.
18. Supporting evidence for endosymbiosis: Endosymbiosis is supported by studies of living
prokaryotic organisms and the similarities between certain organelles (like mitochondria and
chloroplasts) and free-living bacteria.
19. Predation in flour beetles as interference competition: Predation by one flour beetle
species on another represents interference competition as they directly compete for resources.
20. Slime molds reproducing asexually: Slime molds often reproduce asexually through spore
formation.
