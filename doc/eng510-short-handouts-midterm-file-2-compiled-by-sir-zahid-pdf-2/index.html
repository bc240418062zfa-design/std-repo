 Topic 026: Variables and Variants
 What is the example from Bequia that illustrates language variation?
 Cheers and chairs in Bequia pronunciation.
 Define the terms "variable" and "variant" in the context of studying
 language variation.
 Variable is the abstract feature that varies, and variant is the actual instantiation
 of the variable in speech.
 How can a variable be identified in linguistic analysis?
 By convention, variables can be written in parentheses (e.g., (ear)) or referred
 to using key words.
 Topic 027: Types of Variables and Variants

What are "allophones" in phonology, and how are they represented using
the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)?
Allophones are phonetic variants of a phoneme. They are represented as [ph]
for aspirated and [p] for unaspirated using IPA.
Compare the regularity of variation in /p/ in English to the variation in Bequian.
Variation in /p/ in English is regular and predictable, whereas Bequian variation
(NEAR class) is less precise and can vary within the same speaker.
Explain the variation in the pronunciation of the sound /p/ in English. How
is this variation predictable?
The pronunciation of /p/ in English varies depending on its position within a
word. At the start of a word, it is pronounced with clear aspiration (e.g., pinch).
In contrast, at the end of a word (e.g., rap) or following an /s/ at the start of a
word (e.g., speak), it is pronounced without aspiration. This variation is
predictable and depends on the immediate linguistic context.
Topic 028: Synchronic Variation
Define synchronic linguistics and contrast it with diachronic linguistics.
Synchronic linguistics studies a language at a specific moment in time without
considering its history, while diachronic linguistics examines language
changes over time.
What aspects of language does synchronic variation in sociolinguistics
focus on?
Synchronic variation explores grammar, syntax, semantics, and phonology at
a single time point, considering different dialects and their features.
How can a synchronic study of English examine language variations?
By analyzing spelling differences, pronunciation, syntax, and vocabulary
choices at a specific point in time.
What aspects of language are explored in synchronic variation in
sociolinguistics, and how does it investigate dialects?
In synchronic variation in sociolinguistics, researchers explore language
features at a single time point, including grammar, syntax, semantics, and
phonology. This approach investigates various dialects and their features
without referencing earlier or later stages.
Topic 029: Diachronic Variation

What is diachronic linguistics, and how does it differ from synchronic
linguistics?
Diachronic linguistics studies language change over time, while synchronic
linguistics focuses on a language at a specific moment.
Provide examples of diachronic linguistic changes in different linguistic
levels (phonetic, phonemic, syntactic, semantic).
Examples include shifts in sounds, phonemes, syntax, and word meanings over
time.
What is the significance of diachronic linguistics in understanding
language?
Diachronic linguistics provides insights into language changes and patterns
over time that cannot be discerned through synchronic analysis.
Define diachronic linguistics and explain its significance in understanding
language.
Diachronic linguistics studies language change over time, examining shifts and
mutations in language across different historical periods. It is essential for
understanding how languages evolve and for tracing changes at various
linguistic levels (e.g., phonetic, phonemic, morphological, syntactic,
semantic).
Topic 030: Possibility of Predicting Language Change and Variation
Can linguists make accurate predictions about future linguistic
developments? Why or why not?
Linguists generally cannot predict future linguistic developments due to the
complexity of language change, including its random and unpredictable nature.
How does the "Uniformitarian Axiom" relate to predictions about
linguistic change?
The Uniformitarian Axiom suggests that current principles in language will
apply to future stages, but linguistic change often follows an S-shaped growth
curve, not linear patterns.
What challenges exist in predicting language change, even when general
principles are known?
Language change is influenced by internal, social, cognitive, and cultural
factors, and unforeseen events can impact the direction and speed of change.

Explain the "Uniformitarian Axiom" in the context of predicting language
change. How does it relate to predictions?
The Uniformitarian Axiom suggests that what is true in the present was also
true in the past, and this principle is often used for predictions. However,
linguistic change does not follow linear patterns, and the application of this
axiom to the future may not yield accurate predictions. Instead, linguistic
change often follows an S-shaped growth curve, as observed in the historical
separation of modal verbs and the development of the progressive tense.
