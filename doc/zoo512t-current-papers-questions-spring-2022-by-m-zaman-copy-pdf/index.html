Lobopodium [Gk. lobes = lobe; podium = foot] (2 marks)
• It is a short, finger or tongue-like projection which is accompanied by a flow of endoplasm
and ectoplasm. • The pseudopodium is broad with rounded or blunt tips. The ectoplasmic area
is distinctly clear, called the hyaline cap. • It is the characteristic of many amoebas such as
Amoeba, Chaos, Entamoeba, Arcella, Difflugia

Name the Animals which included in class Diplopoda? (2 marks)

Holometabolous Metamorphosis (2 marks)
In holometabolous (Gr. holos, whole) metamorphosis, immature are called larvae because they
are very different from the adult in body form, behavior, and habitat. • The number of larval
instars is species specific, and the last larval molt forms the pupa.
Example: Beetles, butterflies and moths, flies, and wasps.

Paurometabolous metamorphosis?
Paurometabolous (Gr. pauros, small) metamorphosis involves a species-specific number of
molts between egg and adult stages, during which immature gradually take on the adult form.
Immatures are called nymphs.
Paurometabolous is the type of growth that occurs in insects such as cockroaches, which
includes just three stages, i.e. egg, nymph, and adult. It is called incomplete metamorphosis or
paurometabolous since it lacks the pupa stage.
Example: Grasshoppers and chinch bugs

Trepang
The body wall of sea cucumbers, when boiled and dried, is known as trepang in Asian countries.

Phylum Annelida (General Characteristics)
Characteristics of the phylum Annelida include:
   1. Body metameric, bilaterally symmetrical, and wormlike.
   2. Protostome characteristics include spiral cleavage, trochophore larvae (when larvae are
      present), and schizocoelous coelom formation.
   3. Organ Level of organization.
   4. Tube-within-tube body cavity.
   5. Paired, epidermal setae.
   6. Closed circulatory system.
   7. Dorsal suprapharyngeal ganglia and ventral nerve cord(s) with ganglia.

   8. Metanephridia (usually) or protonephridia.

Book lungs
• Some arachnids possess structures, called book lungs, that are assumed to be modifications
of the book gills in the Merostomata. • Book lungs are paired invaginations of the ventral body
wall that fold into a series of leaflike lamellae. • Air enters the book lung through a slitlike
opening and circulates between lamellae.

Synchronous Flight Mechanism of Insects
• Direct or synchronous flight is a mode of transportation that is fueled by wing muscles that
insert directly into the wing base. • The insertion point of the wing is hinged which enables the
muscles downward movements to lift the wing portion upward and upward movements pull
the wing portion downward.
Butterflies, dragonflies, and grasshoppers are examples of insects with a synchronous flight
mechanism.

Circulation or Circulatory system in Crayfish (5 marks)
• Circulation in crayfish is similar to that of most arthropods. • Dorsal, anterior, and posterior
arteries lead away from a muscular heart. • Branches of these vessels empty into sinuses of the
hemocoel.
 • Blood returning to the heart collects in a ventral sinus and enters the gills before returning to
the pericardial sinus, which surrounds the heart.
• Crustaceans have an open circulatory system meaning that all their blood is not contained
within vessels, instead, blood is drawn in to the heart through holes called ostia, then pumped
out again to circulate through the tissues and return again to the heart.

Defined Flukes (2 marks)
The approximately eight thousand species of parasitic flatworms in the class Trematoda (Gr.
trematodes, perforated form) are collectively called flukes, which describes their wide, flat
shape. • Almost all adult flukes are parasites of vertebrates, whereas immature stages may be
found in vertebrates or invertebrates, or encysted on plants.

Compound eyes (2 Marks)
• Compound eyes are well developed in most adult insects. • They are similar in structure and function
to those of other arthropods, although zoologists debate their possible homology (common ancestry)
with those of crustaceans, horseshoe crabs, and trilobites.

• Compound eyes consist of a few to 28,000 receptors, called ommatidia that fuse into a multifaceted
eye.• The compound eye is better suited for detecting movement.
