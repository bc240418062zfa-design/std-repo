1. Difference between Active and Passive Sentence

      Active voice: The subject performs the action.
       Example: "The teacher explained the topic."
      Passive voice: The subject receives the action.
       Example: "The topic was explained by the teacher."

2. Types of Sentences

      Simple: One independent clause.
      Compound: Two or more independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction.
      Complex: One independent clause + one or more dependent clauses.
      Compound-Complex: Two or more independent clauses + at least one dependent clause.

3. Active/Passive – Identification

      Active: "Ali wrote a letter."
      Passive: "A letter was written by Ali."

4. Identify Simple, Complex, Compound Sentences

      Simple: “She sings.”
      Compound: “She sings and he plays guitar.”
      Complex: “She sings because she is happy.”

5. Which Sentence Type is Dependent on Interpretation?

      Complex sentences can vary in interpretation due to the meaning of the subordinate
       clause.

6. Exclamatory Sentence

      Expresses strong emotion.
       Example: “What a beautiful painting!”

7. Standard Form likhni thi

      Likely refers to writing sentences in standard (SVO) form:
       Subject + Verb + Object
       Example: “He (S) eats (V) apples (O).”

8. Relative Clauses – Types

      Defining (restrictive): Gives essential info.
      Non-defining (non-restrictive): Adds extra info, set off with commas.
       Example: “The man who called you is my brother.” (Defining)

9. Prepositional Phrase

      A group of words beginning with a preposition and ending with a noun/pronoun (object
       of preposition).
       Example: “On the table.”

10. Phrase – Prepositional Verb

      A prepositional verb consists of a verb + preposition, forming a fixed expression.
       Example: “Look at,” “depend on”

11. Adjective Phrase (with Examples)

      A phrase that functions as an adjective.
       Example: “Full of joy,” “Afraid of dogs”

13. CP, DP, PP Stands For

       CP: Complementizer Phrase
       DP: Determiner Phrase
       PP: Prepositional Phrase

14–16. Types of Phrases / Three Types of Phrases

       Noun Phrase (NP)
       Verb Phrase (VP)
       Prepositional Phrase (PP)
       Others: Adjective Phrase (AdjP), Adverb Phrase (AdvP)

17. Headed Movement in Syntax (3 Marks)

       Head movement involves moving the head of a phrase (e.g., verb or auxiliary) to another
        position in the sentence structure, usually to T (tense) or C (complementizer).

18. Embedded Sentence

       A clause placed within another clause.
        Example: “I believe that she is honest.”

19. Five Types of Clefting

   1.   It-cleft
   2.   Wh-cleft (Pseudo-cleft)
   3.   Reversed wh-cleft
   4.   All-cleft
   5.   Inferential cleft

20. Adjuncts – Related Question

       Adjuncts are optional elements that provide extra information (e.g., time, place).
        Example: “He left after lunch.”

21. Projection of N and Highest N

      The head noun (N) projects into N′ (N-bar) and then into NP (Noun Phrase).
          o Highest level: NP
          o Intermediate level: N′
          o Lowest level: N

22. Except Head N, Two Parts of Prepositional Phrase

      Preposition (Head)
      Complement (usually a noun phrase)

23. Identify Verb Phrase and Complement of Preposition

      Verb phrase: “has been singing”
      Complement of preposition: In “on the table,” “the table” is the complement.

24. Phrase Structure – Prepositional Verb

      A prepositional verb includes the verb and its preposition as a unit, followed by a
       complement.
       Example: “Look at the stars.”

25. N-bar Explanation + Example

      N-bar (N′) is an intermediate projection in X-bar syntax theory.
       Example: In “the beautiful garden,” “beautiful garden” = N′.

26. Do-Support with Examples

      Used in negatives, questions, emphasis:
          1. “Do you like apples?”
          2. “I do want to help.”
