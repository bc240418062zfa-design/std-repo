Question 1: You're part of a team studying a new gene related to a rare genetic condition. You want to see if similar genes exist in other animals to understand how important this gene is across species. How can the NCBI website help you find these similar genes?
 Using BLAST on NCBI to Find Similar Genes
1.Go to the NCBI homepage (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/)
Click on “BLAST” in the top menu
Choose the appropriate BLAST program:
For nucleotide sequences, use “nucleotide blast”
For protein sequences, use “protein blast”
4.In the “Enter Query Sequence” box, paste your gene sequence
5. Under “Choose Search Set”:
Select “Nucleotide collection (nr/nt)” or “Non-redundant protein sequences (nr)” depending on your sequence type
You can limit the search to specific organisms or groups if desired 6. Click the “BLAST” button to run the search
Review the results:
Look at the E-values (lower is better)
Check the percent identity and query coverage
Examine the list of species with similar sequences
Click on promising results to see more details about the matching sequences and their associated genes
Use the “Taxonomy” view to see how the matches are distributed across different species

 Using UniProt to Gather Protein Information
Go to the UniProt homepage (https://www.uniprot.org/)
In the search bar at the top of the page, enter the name of your protein(Ketatin) or its accession number if you know it.
Press Enter or click the search button.
From the search results, click on the entry that matches your protein of interest.
You’ll now be on the main page for your protein. This page is divided into several sections
