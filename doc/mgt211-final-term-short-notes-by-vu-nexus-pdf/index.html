                                                   Theme 7
Business Finance and Financial Systems
1. Role of Finance in Business
Finance is the lifeblood of a business, ensuring:
   •   Daily operations (paying salaries, utility bills)
   •   Growth & expansion (purchasing machinery, opening branches)
   •   Marketing & strategy (advertising, campaigns)
   •   Maintaining cash flow
   •   Attracting investors
Example: A Lahore clothing brand uses finance for sewing machines (CapEx), salaries
(OpEx), and Eid campaigns.
Poor financial management → business failure, even if product is good.

2. Sources and Uses of Funds Uses of Funds
   1. Capital Expenditure (CapEx) – Long-term assets Machinery, building,
       equipment
   2. Operating Expenses (OpEx) – Daily running costs
       Salaries, utilities, rent

Sources of Funds
1. Equity Financing
   •   Selling ownership shares (personal savings, investors, venture capital)
   •   Pros: No repayment
   •   Cons: Ownership diluted
2. Debt Financing
   •   Loans, bank financing, bonds
   •   Pros: Full control retained
   •   Cons: Must repay with interest (risk)
3. Internal Financing
   •   Reinvesting profits or selling old assets
   •   Pros: No debt, no dilution
   •   Cons: Limited funds
4. Grants/Subsidies

   •   Government or NGO financial support
   •   Pros: No repayment
   •   Cons: Hard to qualify and restrictive

3. Short-Term vs Long-Term Financing
Short-Term Financing (< 1 year)
Used for immediate needs like cash shortages or inventory.
       •      Trade Credit: Supplier gives goods on credit (30 days).
       •      Bank Overdraft: Withdraw more than balance; high interest.
       •      Factoring: Selling receivables for quick cash.
Long-Term Financing (> 1 year)
Used for expansion and large assets.
       •      Equity (IPOs) – Good for scaling but reduces ownership
       •      Debentures/Bonds – Fixed-interest debt
       •      Venture Capital – High-growth startups give equity for funding Example:
Tech startup → venture capital
Manufacturer → long-term bank loan for equipment

4. Securities Markets
Financial markets where stocks, bonds, derivatives are traded.
Primary Market
       •      New securities issued (e.g., IPOs)
Secondary Market
       •      Trading of already issued securities
       •      Pakistan: PSX (Pakistan Stock Exchange)
       •      Regulated by SECP
       •      Investors need:
       → Brokerage account
       → CDC account
Challenges: Low public participation, economic instability.

5. Trends in Financial Management
   1. Digital Transformation o       AI forecasting, cloud
       accounting tools (QuickBooks)
   2. ESG Investing o        Focus on environmental & social
       governance o Green bonds

   3. Fintech Growth o           Mobile wallets: JazzCash, Easypaisa
       o        Digital banks: NayaPay o       Blockchain
       applications
   4. Islamic Finance Growth o Popular due to ethical and
       Shariah compliance o Instruments like Sukuk (Islamic
       bonds)

6. Blockchain & Cryptocurrency
       •        Blockchain: Decentralized, tamper-proof digital ledger
       •        Underlies cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin
       •        In Pakistan:
       → Crypto is not legal tender, but informally traded
       •        Risks: Volatility, scams, lack of regulation
       •        Benefits: Transparency, secure record-keeping
       (e.g., supply chain tracking)

7. Islamic Finance Principles
Islamic finance prohibits interest (riba) and promotes ethical, asset-based transactions.
Key Features:
       •        Risk-sharing: Profits/loss shared
       •        Ethical investing: No alcohol, gambling, etc.
Common Islamic Products:
       •        Murabaha: Cost-plus financing
       •        Mudarabah: Profit-sharing
       •        Musharakah: Joint venture
       •        Ijara: Leasing
Pakistan’s major Islamic bank: Meezan Bank

8. Accounting: The Language of Business
Accounting records and reports all financial activities.
Main Financial Statements:
       •        Balance Sheet – Assets, liabilities, equity
       •        Income Statement – Profit or loss
       •        Cash Flow Statement – Cash inflow/outflow
Accounting helps with:
       •        Investor confidence

       •         Fraud detection
       •         Compliance with laws
       •         Decision-making using data
Tools: SAP, Tally, QuickBooks

Key Takeaways (Summary)
       •         Finance enables operations, growth, and investment.
       •         Businesses use different funding sources: equity, debt, internal, grants.
       •         Financial markets (PSX) connect investors and companies.
       •         New trends: fintech, blockchain, digital accounting, ESG.
       •         Islamic finance combines profitability + ethics.
       •         Accounting provides transparency and decision support.

                                           Theme 8

Global Business Competition: Strategies and Challenges
Introduction to Global Trade
Global Trade means the exchange of goods and services across international
borders.
           It allows countries to specialize in products where they have a competitive
           advantage (competitive advantage = ability to produce at lower cost or better
           quality).
Example (Pakistan):
           Pakistan exports textiles and rice and imports oil and machinery.
   •   Exports: Goods sold to other countries
   •   Imports: Goods bought from other countries
Importance of Global Trade:
   •   Expands consumer choices
   •   Reduces costs through economies of scale
       (economies of scale = lower cost due to large-scale production)
   •   Promotes technology and cultural exchange
   •   Improves economic growth

Theories of International Trade
1. Mercantilism (16th–18th Century)
   •   Focus on trade surplus
