Module# 110 Random Sampling
• If every individual of target population has an equal chance of selection then it will be called
random sampling. Simple random sampling is the easiest form of probability sampling.
• No biasness and expert opinion is required in this method.

Random Sampling
• All the researcher needs to do is assure that all the members of the population are included in
the list and then randomly select the desired number of subjects.
Examples:
• Selection of plants from rice fields
• Selection of students from class
• When carrying out a survey, it would be impractical to study a whole population.

Module# 113 Cluster Sampling
• It is a sampling technique where the entire population is divided into groups, or clusters and a
random sample of these clusters are selected.
• Members of one cluster have similarity with each other and also with other clusters; minor
differences can be there between different clusters.
• Individual selection from each cluster is random.
Cluster Sampling
• Cluster of plants for study
• Cluster of animals

Module# 114 Multistage Sampling
• It is complicated form of cluster sampling. In this type of sampling we divide our population
into groups (cluster) and each group is further divided into sub- groups in such a way that each
lower group is adjusted into higher group.
Multistage Sampling
• For example in a survey of tuberculosis prevalence in world, if we divide whole world into
continents, then select 10countries from each continent randomly, then 10districts from each
country, 10 cities from each district, 10 villages from each city and finally 10 families from each
villages randomly. This type of END sampling is multistage sampling.

Module# 115 Principles of Systematics
Non-Probability Sampling
• Quota sampling: In this type of sampling we divide the population intro different groups or
strata based on some specific criterion but selection of individual from each group is not
random.
