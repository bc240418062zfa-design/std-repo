1. Biophysics is an interdisciplinary science that applies the approaches and methods of physics to study biological
systems.

2. Biophysics discovers how atoms are arranged to work in DNA and proteins.

4. Biophysics is a molecular science that seeks to explain biological function in terms of the molecular structures and
properties of specific molecules.

5. Who used the X-ray crystallography, which revealed the physical patterns of DNA?       Francis Crick

6. James & Watson biophysicists discovered the DNA double helix in 1953.

7. The fluid mosaic model describes the structure of the plasma membrane as a mosaic of components —including
phospholipids, cholesterol, proteins, and carbohydrates—that gives the membrane a fluid character.

8. Membrane biophysics is the study of the physical principles governing biological membranes, including lipid-raft
formation and protein–lipid coupling, as well as their mechanical characteristics.

9. _____ first developed hypotheses about the physical basis for consciousness and perception.     Greeks and Romans

10. The term biophysics was originally introduced by Karl Pearson in 1892.

11. The American Biophysics Society was founded in 1957.

12. The first National Biophysics Congress was held from 3-5 June, 1963 in Italy.

13. Electrophysiology is the study of the electrical nature of tissues and cells.

14. _____ is study abnormal heart rhythms (arrhythmia). Electrophysiology study (EP Test)

15. The scientific study of the motion of fluids, especially non-compressible liquids, under the influence of internal and
external forces is called ______. Hydrodynamics

16. _____ used measure the mechanical properties of living material (such as tissue or cells) Atomic Force
Microscopy.

17. MRI stands for _____ Magnetic Resonance Imaging

18. _____ used in radiology to form pictures of the anatomy and the physiological processes of the body in both health
and disease. Magnetic Resonance Imaging

19. ____ Study of protein conformation and orientation. Fluorescence

20. Intramolecular forces are the forces that hold atoms together within a molecule.

21. Intermolecular forces are also known as van der Waals forces, named after Johannes van der Waals who first
postulated them.

22. The average bond-energy for O-H bonds in water is 463 kJ/mol

23. Ionic bonding is a type of chemical bond that involves the electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions.

24. A covalent bond, also called a molecular bond, is a chemical bond that involves the sharing of electron pairs between atoms.

25. Metallic bonding is the force of attraction between valence electrons and the metal atoms.

26. An ion-induced dipole attraction is a weak attraction that results when the approach of an ion induces a dipole in an
atom or in a nonpolar molecule by disturbing the arrangement of electrons in the nonpolar species.

27. The force of attraction between a non-polar molecule and an ion that may lie in its vicinity is called ion-induced
dipole force.

28. An ion–dipole force is an attractive force that results from the electrostatic attraction between an ion and a neutral
molecule that has a dipole

29. The force of attraction between a polar molecule and an ion that may lie in its vicinity is called an ion-dipole force.

30. Without hydrogen bonding, earth would have no water cycle, no ocean, no floating ice, and no life.

31. Hydrogen bonding can create a long chain of water molecules which can overcome the force of gravity and travel
up to the high altitudes of leaves.

32. The London dispersion force is the weakest intermolecular force.

33. _______ is sometimes called an induced dipole-induced dipole attraction.           London dispersion force

34. The LDF (London dispersion force) is named after the German-American physicist Fritz London.

35. Which of the following are found in liquid state at room temperature?          Bromine

36. Nonpolar covalent compounds—like methane CH4, and nitrogen gas N2- only have London dispersion forces
between molecules.

37. Viscosity is a measure of how well substances flow.

38. Surface tension is a measure of the toughness of the surface of a liquid.

39. Nucleic acids are molecules that allow organisms to transfer genetic information from one generation to the next.

40. Nucleic acids are composed of nucleotide monomers linked together. Nucleotides contain three parts.

41. The five-carbon sugar in DNA is called deoxyribose, while in RNA, the sugar is ribose.

42. Which one of the following is the most important of all biomolecules?            Nucleic acid
