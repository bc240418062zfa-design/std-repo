WHAT IS LANGUAGE?: Language is a system of communication that consists of a set
of rules and symbols used to convey meaning and express thoughts, ideas, and experiences.
It is a fundamental aspect of human society, enabling us to interact, share knowledge, and
build relationships.
Language: A Purely Human and Non-Instinctive: Language is a unique and exclusive
attribute of humans, distinguishing us from animals. It is a learned skill, not an innate
ability, and is shaped by culture, history, and social context. Human language is complex,
creative, and symbolic, setting it apart from other forms of communication.
Communication: Language is a unique characteristic of humans, unlike animal
communication, which is largely instinctive. Human language is learned, not innate, and is
shaped by culture, environment, and social context.
Language: A Symbol System: Language uses symbols, such as words, sounds, and
gestures, to represent concepts, objects, and ideas. These symbols are arbitrary, meaning
their meaning is not inherent but agreed upon by the language community.
Behavioristic View of Language: The behavioristic view sees language as a behavior
shaped by environment, conditioning, and reinforcement. It emphasizes the role of
imitation, habit, and association in language learning and use.
Structural View of Language: The structural view, developed by linguist Ferdinand de
Saussure, sees language as a system of signs with underlying rules and structures. It
analyzes language as a network of relationships between signs and their meanings.
Miscellaneous Definitions of Language: Language has been defined in various ways,
such as a tool for communication, a means of expression, a system of signs, a social
construct, and a reflection of culture and thought.
HISTORY OF LANGUAGE: The history of language is long and complex, spanning
millions of years. It is believed to have originated from gestures, sounds, and facial
expressions, evolving into complex systems of communication.
Animal Communication and Language: Animals communicate using various signals,
such as sounds, gestures, and body language. While they do not possess language in the
human sense, their communication systems share some similarities with human language.

       Talking Apes: Some primates, like chimpanzees and gorillas, have been taught human
       sign language, demonstrating an ability to learn and use language symbols.
       Written Language: Written language developed around 5,000 years ago, allowing for the
       recording and transmission of language across time and space.
       First Families: The first language families, such as Indo-European and Sino-Tibetan,
       emerged around 6,000-8,000 years ago, spreading across continents and giving rise to
       many modern languages.
       Towards a Science of Language: The study of language has evolved into a scientific
       discipline, incorporating insights from linguistics, psychology, anthropology, and
       computer science to understand the nature, structure, and use of language.
       Future Indicatives: The future of language is shaped by technological advancements,
       globalization, and evolving human communication needs, likely leading to new forms of
       language, such as digital and virtual communication systems.
       The Divine Source: language as a gift from God or gods
       The Natural Sound Source: language emerging from natural sounds and gestures
       The Social Interaction Source: language developing through social interaction and
       communication needs
       The Physical Adaptation Source:
Language may have originated from physical adaptations such as:
                Gestures and body language
                Facial expressions and emotions
                Vocalizations and sound-making abilities
       The Tool-Making Source
Language may have emerged as a result of tool-making and:

                 Cognitive abilities to create and use tools
                 Need for communication to coordinate tool-making activities
                 Development of symbolic thinking and representation
       The Genetic Source:
                 Inherent abilities for language acquisition and use
                 Evolutionary advantages for language development
                 Genetic predispositions for language structure and organization
       Characteristics of Human Language
                Displacement
                Arbitrariness
                Productivity
                Cultural Transmission
                Duality
       Displacement: language allows us to communicate about things not present

Arbitrariness: language symbols have no inherent meaning
Productivity: language enables us to create new expressions and meanings
Cultural Transmission: language is passed down through culture and society
Duality: language has both a tangible (sound, writing) and intangible (meaning, thought)
aspect
Functions of Language
         Language is Expressive: Language allows us to express thoughts, feelings, and
            ideas (e.g., "I'm so happy to see you!").
         Language is Powerful: Language can influence others, shape opinions, and
            assert control (e.g., political speeches).
         Language is Performative: Language can perform actions, make promises, and
            create commitments (e.g., "I promise to meet you at 5 pm").
NONVERBAL COMMUNICATION AND LINGUISTICS
        Aspects of Nonverbal Communication: (Facial expressions, body language, tone
           of voice, and eye contact convey meaning beyond spoken language.)
        Principles of Nonverbal Communication: Nonverbal cues can reinforce,
           contradict, or complement verbal messages (e.g., a smile can reinforce a friendly
           verbal message).
        Conveyance of Interpersonal and Emotional Messages: Nonverbal
           communication conveys emotions, attitudes, and relationships (e.g., a hug can
           convey empathy and support).
