             Listening: An Introduction

Module 01: Parts and Goals of a Listening and Speaking Course
What are the four strands of a well-balanced language course? (Topic 1)
The four strands are meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output, language-
focused learning, and fluency development.
What is meaning-focused input, and what are typical activities in this strand?
(Topic 2)
Meaning-focused input involves learning through listening and reading with a
focus on understanding and gaining knowledge or enjoyment. Activities include
extensive reading, shared reading, listening to stories, watching TV or films, and
being a listener in a conversation.
What is meaning-focused output, and what are some typical activities in this
strand? (Topic 2)

Meaning-focused output involves learning through speaking and writing. Typical
activities include conversations, giving speeches, writing letters, telling stories,
and instructing someone on how to do something.
What is language-focused learning, and what are some typical activities in this
strand? (Topic 2)
Language-focused learning involves the deliberate learning of language features
such as pronunciation, spelling, vocabulary, grammar, and discourse. Activities
include pronunciation practice, substitution tables, drills, and vocabulary learning.
What is the fluency development strand, and what are some typical activities in
this strand? (Topic 2)
The fluency development strand involves developing fluency in listening,
speaking, reading, and writing. Activities include speed reading, skimming,
scanning, repeated reading, repeated retelling, ten-minute writing, and listening
to easy stories.
How should the four strands be balanced in a language course? (Topic 2)
Each strand should receive roughly the same amount of time in a well-balanced
course, covering both receptive and productive skills.
What principles guide the teaching of listening and speaking in a course? (Topic
2)
Principles include providing comprehensible input, boosting learning through
comprehensible input, supporting and pushing learners to produce spoken and
written output, providing opportunities for cooperative interaction, helping
learners deliberately learn language items, training learners in language learning
strategies, providing fluency development activities, and using analysis,
monitoring, and assessment to address learners' needs.
Module 02: Beginning to Listen and Speak in Another Language
What are the aims of a beginners course in listening and speaking? (Topic 3)

 COMPILED BY SIR ZAHID                                                                 2

The aims are to help learners cope with meaning-focused input and output,
motivate them in their language study through successful listening and speaking,
and make early learning relevant to their language use needs.
What are some key principles for teaching beginners? (Topic 3)
Key principles include focusing on meaningful and relevant content, maintaining
interest through various activities, avoiding overloading learners with new
language, providing comprehensible input, and creating a friendly, safe,
cooperative classroom environment.
Why is memorizing useful phrases and sentences beneficial for beginners?
(Topic 3)
Memorizing useful phrases and sentences helps learners gain early fluency,
allowing them to engage in simple communication and take control of
conversations without needing to know complex grammar.
What is the "What is it?" technique, and how does it work? (Topic 3)
The "What is it?" technique involves the teacher writing sentences describing
something on the blackboard. Learners learn to change the sentences to talk
about different things, promoting speaking and listening skills.
What are some techniques for early meaning-focused speaking? (Topic 3)
Techniques include descriptions based on pictures, the "ask and move" activity,
"twenty questions," and more.
Module 03: Defining Listening
Why is listening important in language learning? (Topic 4)
Listening is essential in language learning as it provides the basis for building the
knowledge necessary to use a language, and it allows learners to receive
information and develop their language skills.
What are some cognitive factors influencing listening success? (Topic 4)
Cognitive factors include vocabulary knowledge, syntactic knowledge, discourse
knowledge, pragmatic knowledge, metacognition, and prior knowledge.

How do affective factors influence listening success? (Topic 4)
Affective factors include anxiety, self-efficacy, and motivation. They can either
facilitate or hinder listening success.
What are the two broad types of listening, and how do they differ? (Topic 4)
The two types are one-way listening (transactional listening) and two-way
listening (interactional listening). One-way listening involves information transfer,
while two-way listening is about maintaining social relations.
What are bottom-up processes in listening? (Topic 4)
Bottom-up processes involve assembling the message piece by piece, starting
with auditory-phonetic, phonemic, and moving up to the semantic and pragmatic
levels.
What are top-down processes in listening? (Topic 4)
Top-down processes involve using prior knowledge and context to predict the
message and make inferences. Inference is a key process in top-down listening.
What are listening strategies, and how can they be classified? (Topic 4)
Listening strategies are techniques for comprehension and recall. They can be
classified as top-down or bottom-up strategies, depending on whether they rely
on context and background knowledge or language itself.
