Key Theories / Concepts
1. Universal Grammar (UG) Theory

      Proposed by Noam Chomsky.
      Children are born with an innate Language Acquisition Device (LAD).
      All human languages share universal principles.
      Language learning is biologically determined.

2. Krashen’s Theory (Monitor Model)

      Distinguishes acquisition (subconscious) from learning (conscious).
      Comprehensible input is necessary for acquisition.
      Includes five hypotheses: Acquisition-Learning, Natural Order, Input, Monitor, and
       Affective Filter.

3. Vygotsky's Mediation Theory / Sociocultural Theory

      Learning occurs through social interaction.
      Knowledge is first developed socially and then internalized.
      Introduced Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD).
      Society and culture shape learning.

4. Pavlovian Conditioning

      Proposed by Ivan Pavlov.
      Learning occurs through stimulus-response association.
      Based on conditioned and unconditioned responses.

5. Jerome Bruner’s Theory

      Introduced Scaffolding and Discovery Learning.
      Learning should focus on "learning how to learn."
      Three stages: Enactive, Iconic, and Symbolic representation.

6. Usage-Based Theory of Language Acquisition

      Language develops from actual language use.
      Learning occurs through exposure and experience.
      Focuses on patterns, frequency, and interaction.

7. Grammar Translation Method (GTM)

      Focuses on grammar rules and translation.
      Emphasizes reading and writing.

10. Functional vs Structural Principle

                Functional Principle                             Structural Principle

11. Positive & Negative Reinforcement

      Positive Reinforcement: Adding a reward to increase desired behavior.
      Negative Reinforcement: Removing an unpleasant condition to increase desired
       behavior.

6. Differentiate Between Functional and Structural Principle

Functional Principle: Language is used to perform communicative functions and convey
meaning.

Structural Principle: Language is viewed as a system of grammatical structures and rules.
