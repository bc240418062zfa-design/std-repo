Q1. Define Survivorship curve.                           2 Marks

Survivorship curve is one tool. It measures the proportion of individuals in a given species that are alive at
different ages.

Q2. When Adaptation occurs?                         2 Marks

Adaptation occurs when a change in a phenotype increases an animal’s chance of successful reproduction.

Change in environment is the force behind adaptations.

It may result in the evolution of multiple new groups.

Q3. Function of Amnion in amniotic egg?                          2 Marks

First vertebrates to possess amniotic eggs. Amnion protects embryo from desiccation, cushion the embryo,
promote gas transfer, and store waste materials.

Q5. Explain Fitness with examples.                       3 Marks

Adaptedness or fitness: is a measure of the capacity for successful reproduction in a given environment.
Not every characteristic is an adaptation to some kind of environmental situation. Neither adaptation leads
to perfection. Genes of all traits are present in a population in form of alleles.

Examples:

Two phenotypes of toads

1. Green

2. Brown • Green leave average 4 offspring:

3. Brown leave average 2.5 offsprings.

• By custom, the most fit phenotype is assigned a fitness value of 1.0.

Q9. Structure and Function of Golgi Apparatus.                           5 Marks

Structure

• Located near the end of the ER close to the nucleus • Composed of several layers of cisternae (fluid-filled
sacs) • Has three different parts: 1. cis-Golgi 2. medial-Golgi 3. trans-Golgi

Functions

• cis is first cisternae. • trans is final cisternae. • From trans, proteins are packaged into vesicles destined to
lysosomes, secretory vesicles, or the cell surface. Transport vesicles are used to move back and forth
between the ER and Golgi bodies • One side of the Golgi body receives transport vesicles produced by the
ER. • This side is called cis face. • Opposite side releasing vesicle is trans face.

Effort By Amaan Khan

Q10. Composition of Atmosphere?                               10 Marks

Comprised of a mixture of:

1. Invisible permanent gases

2. Variable gases

3. Suspended microscopic particles

1. Permanent Gases Form a constant proportion of the total atmospheric mass

2. Variable Gases Distribution and concentration varies in space and time

3. Aerosols Suspended particles and liquid droplets (excluding cloud droplets)

1. Permanent Gases

78% Nitrogen (N2) 21% Oxygen (O2) Relative percentages of the permanent gases remain constant up to
80 - 100km high. Turbulent mixing causes atmospheric composition to be fairly homogenous. • Called as
Homosphere

2. Variable Gases

• Water vapor (H2O): 0 to 4% • Carbon Dioxide (CO2): 0.038% • Methane (CH4): 0.00017% • Ozone
(O3): 0.000004%
