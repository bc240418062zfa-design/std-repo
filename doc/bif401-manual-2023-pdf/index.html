LAB 01 | BIF401P

The genetic information of an organism is stored in DNA molecules. As you know that, the DNA sequence
contains A, T, G & C nucleotide bases. In-case of RNA, T is replaced with U. The sequence of these four bases
can provide all the instructions needed to build any living organism. For example, the human genome consists
of around 3.2 – billion base pairs divided-up between 23 paired Chromosomes.

Note: Protein sequence contain A, R, N, D, C, E, Q, G, H, I, L, K, M, F, P, S, T, W, Y & P these are actually 20
different amino acids in nature which compose a protein.

So, Question arise, how and where to store these biological sequences?

Following databases stores DNA/Protein data:

          For DNA & RNA: The public database is NCBI - GenBank (By NIH).
          For Proteins: The public database is UniProt (By UniProt Consortium)

Both GenBank and UniProt are online databases, which provide free access to get DNA or Protein data.

NCBI: National Centre for Biotechnology Information

The NCBI (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) established in 1988 as the government’s response to the need
for more and better information processing methods to deals with huge amount of data generated by
biological experiments. It is developed by National Library of Medicine (NLM) at the National Institute
of Health (NIH). NCBI provides a wealth of information in the fields of medicine and biological
sciences.

GenBank

GenBank ® is the NIH genetic sequence database, an annotated collection of all publicly available nucleotide
sequences and their protein translations, where researcher can get access to the sequences of DNA and RNA.
NCBI builds GenBank primarily from the submission of sequence data from authors and from the bulk

LAB 02 | BIF401P

UniProt – Universal Protein Resource https://www.uniprot.org/

What is UniProt?

           UniProt is produced by the UniProt Consortium, a collaboration between EBI, SIB and
           PIR. UniProt is the world’s most comprehensive catalogue of information on proteins. It is
           the central repository of proteins sequences and functions.

UniProt Consortium comprises four (4) components:

  I.       The UniProt Knowledgebase (UniProtKB)
 II.       UniProt Reference Clusters (UniRef)
III.       UniProt Archive (UniParc)
IV.        UniProt Metagenomic and Environmental Sequences (UniMES)

The UniProt Knowledgebase (UniProtKB) is the central access point for the extensive curated
protein information, including functions, classifications, and cross reference. The database is
divided into two section UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot which is manually curated and
UniProtKB/TrEMBL which is automatically maintained.

UniProt Reference Clusters (UniRef): Three UniRef databases – UniRef100, UniRef90 and
UniRef50. UniRef100 is based on all UniProtKB records. It also contains selected UniParc recods,
including Ensembl protein translations from human, chicken, cow, dog, fly, mouse, rat, zebrafish.

UniProt Archive (UniParc) is designed to capture all publicly available protein sequence data
and contains all the protein sequences from the main publicly available protein sequence databases.
This makes UniParc the most comprehensive publicly accessible non-redundant protein sequence
database.

UniProt Metagenomic and Environmental Sequences (UniMES), to stores sequences which
are recovered directly from environmental samples.

About the UniProt Consortium

The UniProt Consortium comprises the Swiss Institute of Bioinformatics (SIB), European Bioinformatics Institute (EBI)
and the Protein Information Resource (PIR). The EBI maintains a vast collection of bioinformatics databases and
services. The goal of UniProt is to furnish the scientific community with a comprehensive, high-quality, and freely
accessible collection of protein and functional information.

LAB 03 | BIF401P

Alignments are a powerful method of comparing related DNA or Protein sequences. By aligning
sequences, it is possible to identify similarities and differences between them, which can provide
information about evolutionary relationship, functional domains and areas that might be affected
by mutations or drug binding. In general, a sequence alignment is an optimization problem which
determines the best transcript of how one sequence was derived from the other. In other words, we
can say that a sequence alignment is a way of arranging the sequences of DNA/RNA/Protein to
identify the regions of similarity that may be a consequence of functional, structural or
evolutionary relationships between the sequences.

HINT:- DNA is made up of chains of nucleotides while proteins are made up of chains of amino
acids. The specific sequences of nucleotides or amino acids within these chains is referred as DNA
and Protein sequences respectively. One of the most important functions of DNA sequences is to
provide the code for protein sequences
