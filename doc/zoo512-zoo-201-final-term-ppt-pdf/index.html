Nervous System
       • Crustacean nervous
         systems show trends
         similar to those in
         annelids and arachnids.
       • Primitively, the ventral
         nervous system is
         ladderlike.
       • Higher crustaceans show
         a tendency toward
         centralization and
         cephalization.

Nervous System
       • Crayfish have
         supraesophageal and
         subesophageal ganglia
         that receive sensory
         input from receptors in
         the head and control the
         head appendages.
       • The ventral nerves and
         segmental ganglia fuse,
         and giant neurons in the
         ventral nerve cord
         function in escape
         responses.

Nervous System
       • When nerve impulses
         are conducted posteriorly
         along giant nerve fibers
         of a crayfish, powerful
         abdominal flexor
         muscles of the abdomen
         contract alternately with
         weaker extensor
         muscles, causing the
         abdomen to flex (the
         propulsive stroke) and
         then extend (the
         recovery stroke).

Nervous System
       • The telson and uropods
         form a paddle like “tail”
         that propels the crayfish
         posteriorly.

Sensory Structures: Statocyst
               • In addition to antennae,
                 the sensory structures of
                 crayfish include
                 compound eyes, simple
                 eyes, statocysts,
                 chemoreceptors,
                 proprioceptors, and
                 tactile setae.
               • Chemical receptors are
                 widely distributed over
                 the appendages and the
                 head.

Sensory Structures: Statocyst
               • Many of the setae
                 covering the mouthparts
                 and antennae are
                 chemoreceptors used in
                 sampling food and
                 detecting pheromones.
               • A single pair of
                 statocysts is at the bases
                 of the first antennae.

Sensory Structures: Statocyst
               • A statocyst is a pit like
                 invagination of the
                 exoskeleton that
                 contains setae and a
                 group of cemented sand
                 grains called a statolith.
               • Crayfish movements
                 move the statolith and
                 displace setae.

Sensory Structures: Statocyst
               • Statocysts provide
                 information regarding
                 movement, orientation
                 with respect to the pull of
                 gravity, and vibrations of
                 the substrate.
               • Because the statocyst is
                 cuticular, it is replaced
                 with each molt.
               • Sand is incorporated into
                 the statocyst when the
                 crustacean is buried in
                 sand.

Sensory Structures: Statocyst
               • Other receptors involved
                 with equilibrium, balance,
                 and position senses are
                 tactile receptors on the
                 appendages and at
                 joints.
               • When a crustacean is
                 crawling or resting,
                 stretch receptors at the
                 joints are stimulated.

Sensory Structures: Statocyst
               • Crustaceans detect tilting
                 from changing patterns
                 of stimulation.
               • These widely distributed
                 receptors are important
                 to crustaceans that lack
                 statocysts.

Compound Eyes and Ocelli
            • Crayfish have compound
              eyes mounted on
              movable eyestalks.
            • The lens system consists
              of 25 to 14,000 individual
              receptors called
              ommatidia.
            • Compound eyes also
              occur in insects.

Compound Eyes and Ocelli
            • Larval crustaceans have
              a single, median
              photoreceptor consisting
              of a few sensilla.
            • These simple eyes,
              called ocelli, allow larval
              crustaceans to orient
              toward or away from the
              light, but do not form
              images.

Compound Eyes and Ocelli
            • Many larvae are
              planktonic and use their
              ocelli to orient toward
              surface waters.
