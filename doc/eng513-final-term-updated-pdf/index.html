                               (03 Marks Questions)
Q. Expository Studies
Expository studies focus on explaining or describing phenomena in detail to enhance
understanding. This can include elaborating claims or presenting counter-arguments using
structured discourse.
Q. Vocabulary Learning Rate
L1 learners acquire vocabulary incidentally due to immense input, whereas L2 learners face
limited input and need structured teaching. Teachers significantly influence vocabulary learning
in L2 settings by carefully planning and providing appropriate resources.
Q. Three Strategies for Teaching Pronunciation
   ❖       Phonetic practice: Engaging learners in exercises focusing on sound articulation.
   ❖       Listening discrimination: Using audio materials to identify correct pronunciation.
   ❖       Integration with vocabulary: Combining pronunciation with vocabulary lessons to
           enhance retention.
Q. Teaching and Learning Pronunciation: Three Points
   1.      Stress and intonation are critical for meaning comprehension.
   2.      Learners should focus on naturalistic speech contexts.
   3.      Use of technology, like audio recording, can help practice.
Q. Main Factors on Which Content-Based Instruction Depends
Content-based instruction depends on:
   ❖       Relevance of content to learners’ needs.
   ❖       Integration of language skills with subject matter.
   ❖       Availability of authentic and pedagogically sound materials.
Q. Lexical Variety and Its Significance
Lexical variety ensures richness in language use, impacting reading comprehension and
communication fluency. It involves exposure to diverse vocabulary contexts.
Q. Learner Autonomy
Learner autonomy refers to the ability of learners to take responsibility for their own learning
through self-directed activities, critical reflection, and decision-making.
Q. Techniques to Overcome Communication Barriers and Ensure Clarity
   1.      Using simple and clear language.
   2.      Encouraging active listening.
   3.      Incorporating visual aids.

Q. Metacognitive Strategies
Metacognitive strategies include planning, monitoring, and evaluating one’s learning processes,
particularly effective in enhancing comprehension and self-regulation.
Q. Three Theory-Based Listening Types
   1.      Interactive listening: Listening in real-time conversations.
   2.      Intensive listening: Focused on details and specific inputs.
   3.      Extensive listening: Listening for general understanding and context.
Q. Listening Comprehension
Listening comprehension involves understanding spoken language by identifying main ideas,
details, and implications using various cognitive and linguistic skills.
Q. Characteristics of Listening (as per a specific researcher)
Skehan emphasizes that listening tasks focus on meaning, are interactive, and encourage
comprehension beyond surface-level interpretations.
Q. Define the Term Curriculum
Curriculum refers to the structured plan of educational content, objectives, and methods designed
to achieve specific learning outcomes.
Q. Difference Between Synthetic and Analytic Syllabus
           ❖       Synthetic syllabus focuses on pre-determined language components taught
                   sequentially.
           ❖       Analytic syllabus revolves around tasks or content where learners infer rules
                   through exposure.
Q. Functions of a Lesson Plan
A lesson plan serves to organize content, set learning objectives, guide instructional methods, and
evaluate outcomes.
Q. Lesson Plan Approaches Suggested by Different Scholars
Scholars propose integrating pre-task, task, and post-task phases to enhance clarity and learner
engagement.
Q. Teacher-Designed Practice Assessments
These assessments are tailored by teachers to match students' needs, focusing on specific learning
goals and feedback mechanisms.
Q. Conference Assessments
These involve one-on-one discussions between teachers and students, focusing on progress,
challenges, and individualized feedback.

   1.      Orientation: Distributing essential information.

Q. Definition of "Formulator" (3-5 lines)
In Levelt’s Model, the formulator processes conceptual thoughts into linguistic structures, ensuring
grammatically correct and contextually appropriate sentences.
Q. Two Names in Levelt’s Model
   1.      Conceptualizer.
   2.      Formulator.
