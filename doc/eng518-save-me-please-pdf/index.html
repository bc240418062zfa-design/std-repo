What is research?
  - Research is a systematic process of investigating a topic or problem.
  - It aims to discover new knowledge, validate existing ideas, or solve
     practical issues.
  - Researchers gather data, analyze it, and draw conclusions.
General characteristics of research
  - Rigorous: Research follows a structured approach with clear steps.
  - Objective: It seeks unbiased information.
  - Replicable: Others can repeat the study to verify results.
Functions of research
  - Advancing knowledge: Research contributes to our understanding of
     ELT.
  - Improving practice: It informs teaching methods and materials.
  - Solving problems: Research addresses real-world challenges.
Specific characteristics of ELT research
  - Focuses on language teaching and learning.
  - Investigates language acquisition, classroom strategies, and learner
     needs.
Research in ELT (AL)
  - ELT research explores language teaching theories and practices.
  - It examines language skills, assessment, and teacher development.
What is ELT research about?/Why is research in ELT important?
  - Researchers identify gaps and areas for investigation.
  - They ask relevant questions to guide their studies.
Demythologizing research

  - Demystifies complex research concepts.
  - Makes research accessible to educators.
Identifying important questions in ELT
  - Researchers identify gaps and areas for investigation.
  - They ask relevant questions to guide their studies.
Classification of research
  - Descriptive: Describes phenomena (e.g., language use in classrooms).
  - Experimental: Tests hypotheses (e.g., effectiveness of a teaching
     method).
Major types of research
  - Qualitative: Focuses on understanding context and meaning.
  - Quantitative: Involves numerical data and statistical analysis.
Educational research
  - ELT research falls under educational research.
  - It informs educational policies and practices.
Scientific ways to solve problems
  - Scientists use the scientific method to solve problems.
  - It involves steps like making observations, asking questions, forming
     hypotheses, testing predictions, and iterating.
  - The goal is to find evidence-based solutions.
Characteristics of an investigator
  - Good investigators possess traits like curiosity, analytical thinking, and
     integrity.
  - They ask the right questions, avoid groupthink, and cope effectively
     with challenges.
Assortment of a problem
  - Assortment optimization is a common challenge in fields like retail,
     airlines, and healthcare.
  - It involves selecting the right mix of items to offer (e.g., products in a
     store) to maximize an objective (like revenue).
Identification of a problem
  - Problem identification is crucial.
  - It pinpoints the root cause of a dilemma and helps create a clear problem
     statement.

      - Researchers use it to focus their investigations.

   Criteria for the selection of a problem
     - A good research problem should be novel, significant, and well-
         defined.
     - It must address a real obstacle and contribute original insights.
     - Researchers should ask the right questions to tackle relevant problems.
   Defining a Problem and Formulating a Hypothesis:
     - A research problem sets the stage for investigation.
     - A hypothesis is a testable explanation related to the problem.
     - It guides research design and data collection.
   Kinds of a hypothesis and Characteristics of a good hypothesis
     - Hypotheses can be null (no effect) or alternative (predicting an effect).
     - A good hypothesis is specific, testable, and based on existing
         knowledge.
   Variables in a hypothesis
     - Variables are factors that change in an experiment.
     - Independent variables are manipulated, and dependent variables are
         measured.
   How to locate research?
Researchers use various sources:
     - Preliminary sources: Gather background information.
     - Secondary sources: Explore existing research.
   Locating primary research: exploring databases
     - Primary research involves collecting original data for a specific study.
     - To find primary sources, explore databases (like Google Scholar,
        PubMed, or Scopus).
     - Look for research articles, interviews, surveys, or experiments
        conducted by researchers themselves.
   LPR: Position papers vs primary research
     - Position papers express opinions or stances on a topic.
     - Primary research provides direct evidence (e.g., interviews, data) for
        analysis.

   - Position papers are secondary sources, while primary research is the
      raw material.
LPR: Tables of references and bibliographies
   - A reference list (bibliography) appears at the end of a research paper.
   - It includes all cited sources (primary and secondary).
   - Tables of references organize these sources for easy access.
LPR: Difference between primary & secondary sources
   - Primary sources offer firsthand information (e.g., original documents,
      data).
   - Secondary sources interpret or summarize primary material (e.g.,
      reviews, articles).
   - Both are essential for robust research.
Obtaining ELT related research articles
   - Explore ELT (English Language Teaching) journals, databases, and
      repositories.
   - Look for articles on language acquisition, teaching methods, and
      learner needs.
Journals related to ELT research
   - ELT journals publish research findings.
   - Examples: "TESOL Quarterly," "Applied Linguistics," and "Language
      Learning."
Exploring ELT research journals
   - Dive into journals to find primary research articles.
   - Learn from experts and stay updated on language teaching trends.
Sampling terminology
   - Sampling involves selecting a subset from a larger population.
   - Terms: sample, population, random sampling, and stratified sampling.
Sampling paradigms
   - Information-Rich Paradigm: Focuses on detailed data from a few
      cases.
   - Representative Sampling Paradigm: Aims for a broader, balanced
      sample.
