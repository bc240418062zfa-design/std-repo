Module 13: Principles of CALL Evaluation
Topic 26 - Principles for CALL Evaluation
Why has CALL been viewed as an experiment requiring scrutiny beyond other
classroom activities?
CALL has been viewed as an experiment due to its need for continuous evaluation
and justification, even when the results remain inconclusive.
What three needs must be addressed to improve CALL evaluation?
The three needs for improving CALL evaluation are:
   1. Incorporating findings and theory-based speculation about ideal conditions
      for SLA.

   2. Providing guidance on how evaluation criteria should be used.
   3. Applying criteria and theory to both software and the tasks teachers plan.
What are the five principles of evaluation for CALL discussed in the text?

The five principles are:
   1. Evaluation of CALL is a situation-specific argument.
   2. CALL should be evaluated through two perspectives: judgmental analysis of
      software and planned tasks, and empirical analysis of learners'
      performance.
   3. Criteria for CALL task quality should come from theory and research on
      instructed SLA.
   4. Criteria should be applied in view of the purpose of the task.
   5. Language learning potential should be the central criterion in the
      evaluation of CALL.
Topic 27 - Criteria from Theory and Research on SLA
What is the concept of "language learning potential" in the context of CALL
evaluation?
Language learning potential refers to the extent to which a CALL activity promotes
beneficial focus on form and encourages language learning, rather than just
language use.
How does "learner fit" relate to CALL evaluation criteria?
"Learner fit" considers whether the difficulty level of the targeted linguistic forms
is appropriate for the learners and whether the task suits the characteristics of
the intended learners.
What does "authenticity" mean in the context of CALL evaluation?
"Authenticity" refers to the degree of correspondence between a CALL task and
real-world language tasks that learners may encounter outside the classroom.
Why is "positive impact" an important criterion in CALL evaluation?

"Positive impact" assesses the effects of a CALL task beyond language learning
potential, including its effects on learners, teachers, and the educational system
as a whole.
What does "practicality" refer to in CALL evaluation?
"Practicality" evaluates how easy it is for learners and teachers to implement a
CALL task within the constraints of their class or language program, including
hardware, software, and personnel resources.
Module 14: Judgmental Evaluation of CALL
Topic 28 - Judgmental Evaluation of CALL
What are the two main aspects that judgmental evaluation criteria for CALL
focus on?
Judgmental evaluation criteria for CALL focus on both the aspects of the task
designed by the software and those designed by the teacher.
What is a key advantage of computer-assisted classroom discussion in CALL?
Computer-assisted classroom discussion increases participation from all members
of the class and allows for more interaction and engagement.
In a micro-world activity, how do learners interact with the computer?
In a micro-world activity, learners interact with the computer through oral
conversation to determine what, when, and how they will input words into the
game, usually as part of a guessing game.
What is the main focus of a text analysis CALL activity?
The main focus of a text analysis CALL activity is on grammatical form and the use
of a grammar checker to revise written work.
What does the term "storyboard" refer to in CALL?
A storyboard in CALL refers to a guessing game activity where learners work
collaboratively through oral conversation to input words and complete a task with
limited textual information.

Topic 29: Empirical Evaluation of CALL; Evidence for CALL Qualities; Language
Learning Potential; Focus on Form; Modified interaction; Modified output

What is empirical evaluation, and why is it important in CALL?
Empirical evaluation is a systematic method for testing hypotheses about the
benefits of CALL tasks. It's essential because it provides empirical data to support
or refute the claims about CALL qualities and their impact on language learning.
What are the qualities that need to be evaluated in CALL tasks, and why?
The qualities to be evaluated in CALL tasks include language learning potential,
learner fit, meaning focus, authenticity, impact, and practicality. Evaluating these
qualities helps understand the effectiveness of CALL tasks in promoting language
learning.
How can evidence for the language learning potential of a CALL activity be
demonstrated?
Evidence for language learning potential can be demonstrated by showing that
learners have improved in their control of the target language aspects focused on
in the activity and that they focused on the form during the CALL task.
Why is "focus on form" important in CALL, and what evidence supports its
effectiveness?
"Focus on form" is important because it draws learners' attention to specific
linguistic forms. Research suggests that CALL materials with carefully selected and
highlighted target forms can offer superior language learning potential.
