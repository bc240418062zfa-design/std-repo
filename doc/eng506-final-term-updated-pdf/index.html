Q. What is Englishization?

Englishization refers to the process where English becomes increasingly used in various domains,
often influencing local languages and cultures.

Q. Describe the status of Japanese English.

English in Japan lacks an internal reason for promotion. Japanese is the medium of instruction and
government business. There is a cline of proficiency, with a small segment fluent in English. There
is also significant nativization, with many English borrowings.

Q. What are three factors that contribute to language policy?

   ❖ Population,
   ❖ prestige, and
   ❖ functionality.

Q. Define "death of a minority language."

The disappearance of a language due to factors like cultural assimilation, demographic
submersion, and globalization.

Q. What is the role of English in Europe?

English is the biggest de facto lingua franca in Europe, widely used in formal education, business,
and the EU apparatus.

Q. Describe the status of English in Southeast Asia.

English is secure in Singapore, in demand in the Philippines, undergoing revival in Malaysia, and
remains the dominant foreign language in other Southeast Asian countries.

Q. What is the current status of English in the Philippines?

English is widely used in education and governance. There was a shift toward Filipino but a return
to English due to globalization.

Q. How is Chinese developing as an emerging global language?

Chinese is growing due to economic development, globalization, and policies like the Belt and
Road Initiative.

Q. What determines the place of a language in language policy?

   ❖ prestige,

Q. Explain the difference between top-down and bottom-up language policies.

Top-down policies are imposed by governments, while bottom-up policies arise from community
needs and practices.

Q. What are the multilingual features of inner, outer, and expanding circles?

   ❖ Inner: English as the native language;
   ❖ Outer: English coexists with local languages;
   ❖ Expanding: English as a foreign language.

Q. Name three components of the outer and expanding circle context.

   ❖ Use in education,
   ❖ government, and
   ❖ international communication.

Q. How can English be maintained as an international language?

   ❖ Through continued global usage,
   ❖ adaptation to local needs, and
   ❖ maintaining its role in education and commerce.

Q. What challenges arise when compiling a dictionary?

   ❖ Standardization,
   ❖ inclusion of new words, and
   ❖ balancing regional variations.

Q. What is the significance of corpus sources in language studies?

Corpus sources provide empirical data for linguistic analysis, helping to track language evolution.

Q. What is the orthodox view of SLA?

SLA is seen as a cognitive process involving stages of acquisition.

Q. Who are the participants of SLA research?

   ❖ Learners,
   ❖ teachers, and
   ❖ researchers.

Q. Define communicative competence.

The ability to use language effectively in different social contexts.

Q. What are the two purposes of competence analysis?

Understanding language proficiency and identifying learning gaps.

Q. What is the myth of the native speaker as the ideal teacher?

The belief that native speakers are inherently better teachers, ignoring pedagogical training.

Q. How do non-native speakers perceive World English?

As a tool for communication rather than as a single standard variety.

Q. What problems do learners face while learning English? Mention three.

   ❖ Pronunciation difficulties,
   ❖ lack of exposure, and
   ❖ grammatical complexities.

Q. What is testing in ELT?

Assessment of language proficiency using standardized tests.

Q. What are two characteristics of language in Japanese English?

Insertion of vowels in consonant clusters and semantic shifts in vocabulary.

Q. How is English taught and learned in Singapore?

Through a pro-English policy, serving as a unifying language.

Q. What are two factors that influence ELT practices?

Economic demands and government policies.

Q. Why do bilingual women choose to speak English? Mention two reasons.

   ❖ Economic opportunities and
   ❖ social mobility.

Q. How do women adopt the dominant language?

Through education and workplace interactions.
