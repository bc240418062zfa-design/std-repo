1. Capsid Symmetry Types
  •   Virus A
      This is a complex-symmetry virus (e.g. Poxvirus family). Its particle shows an
      ovoid/brick shape with an outer membrane, inner membrane, core wall, lateral bodies,
      and surface tubules. It does not conform to purely icosahedral or helical symmetry.
  •   Virus B
      This is a helical-symmetry virus. Its capsid proteins assemble in a repeating helical array
      around the viral genome to form a rigid, rod-shaped particle (as seen in many plant
      viruses or some animal viruses with helical nucleocapsids).

3. Benefits of Being Enclosed in a Capsid Coating
   1. Protection of the Genome
          o Shields viral nucleic acid from nucleases and physical damage in the extracellular
              environment.
   2. Efficient Self-Assembly
          o Capsid proteins often self-assemble in a highly regulated manner, ensuring every
              virion is correctly packaged.
   3. Facilitated Host-Cell Entry
          o Capsid (and envelope glycoproteins, if present) mediates attachment to specific
              cellular receptors and promotes membrane fusion or endocytosis.
   4. Stability & Transmission
          o A robust capsid/envelope structure enhances survival outside the host (e.g., in
              bodily fluids or on surfaces), increasing infectivity.
   5. Enzyme & Factor Delivery
          o In complex viruses, lateral bodies or core-associated enzymes (polymerases,
              immune antagonists) are delivered directly into the cytoplasm to jump-start
              infection.
   6. Antigenic Modulation
          o Capsid and envelope proteins evolve under immune pressure, helping viruses
              evade host defenses via antigenic variation.
   7. Genome Length Regulation
          o In helical viruses, capsid length automatically adjusts to genome size, ensuring no
              naked nucleic acid is exposed.
