                       LECTURE 1: COMMUNICATION

■ Communication

Communication comes from the Latin word 'Communico' meaning 'to share'. It is the continuous process of
speaking, listening, and understanding by which information (facts, ideas, feelings) is shared between
people so that it is understood.

Why is Communication Important?
We spend about 70% of our waking hours communicating — writing, reading, speaking, and listening.
Good communication skills are required in nearly every job. The ability to communicate effectively
determines success in personal and professional life.

Real Life Example: Think about a job advertisement. Whether you are an engineer, doctor, manager, or
teacher — communication skills are always listed as essential requirements.

The Communication Process / Cycle
Every act of communication involves these 5 steps:

Step                                                  What Happens

1. IDEA (Sender)                                      Information/thought exists in the sender's mind.

2. ENCODING                                           Sender converts the idea into words or symbols (the
                                                      message).

3. CHANNEL                                            The medium used to send the message (phone, letter,
                                                      face-to-face).

4. DECODING                                           Receiver translates words/symbols back into meaning.

5. FEEDBACK                                           Receiver's response that confirms the message was
                                                      understood.

NOISE                                                 Anything that disturbs or distorts the message at any
                                                      step.

Real Life Example: You text your friend 'Meet me at 5' (IDEA + ENCODE). The message travels via phone
(CHANNEL). Your friend reads it (DECODE). They reply 'OK!' (FEEDBACK). But if the phone signal is weak
and the message cuts off, that's NOISE.

Channel Selection Factors:
  • Need for immediate transmission → Use Fax instead of letter
  • Need for immediate feedback → Use Phone instead of Fax
  • Need for a permanent record → Use written communication
  • Need for negotiation/persuasion → Use face-to-face meeting

5 Categories of Feedback:
  • Evaluation – Judging the worth/appropriateness of sender's message
  • Interpretation – Paraphrasing / re-stating what sender meant
  • Support – Helping or encouraging the sender
  • Probing – Asking questions to get more info
  • Understanding – Trying to completely understand sender's meaning

■ Key Point: A message is NEVER communicated unless it is UNDERSTOOD by the receiver.

              LECTURE 2: LEVELS OF COMMUNICATION

There are 5 Levels of Communication:
Level                                                   Description

1. Intrapersonal                                        Communication within yourself — thinking, self-talk,
                                                        daydreaming. You are both sender and receiver.

2. Interpersonal                                        Communication between two or a few people
                                                        face-to-face — conversations, dialogues, small group
                                                        discussions.

3. Mediated                                             Communication using a device/medium — telephone,
                                                        email, letters, radio. No direct face-to-face contact.

4. Person-to-Group                                      One speaker addressing an audience. E.g., a teacher
                                                        in a classroom, a politician giving a speech.

5. Mass Communication                                   Messages sent to large, anonymous audiences using
                                                        print (newspaper, books) or electronic media (TV,
                                                        radio, internet).

Real Life Example: Intrapersonal: You decide in your mind what to wear in the morning. Interpersonal: You
chat with a friend. Mediated: You call your family. Person-to-Group: A professor delivers a lecture. Mass
Communication: You watch news on TV.

Key Concepts in Intrapersonal Communication:
■ Self-Concept

Your total picture of who you are — psychological, physical, spiritual, social, and intellectual self. It is
formed through interactions with others and keeps changing throughout life.
