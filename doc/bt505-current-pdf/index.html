 1-DNA Biosensor: A
1-DNA biosensor is a tool used to detect and analyze DNA. It works by attaching a DNA molecule to a
surface and then measuring changes in the DNA's properties when it interacts with other molecules.
This can help scientists study genetic material and diagnose diseases.

Strain of E. coli that causes infection:
Certain strains of E. coli bacteria can cause infections. These infections are usually related to the
digestive system and can cause symptoms like diarrhea, stomach cramps, and fever. It's important to be
aware of these strains and take steps to prevent infection, such as practicing good hygiene and food
safety.

Components of the Biorecognition Layer:
The biorecognition layer of a biosensor is made up of molecules that can specifically bind to the target
molecule of interest. These components include receptors (such as antibodies or enzymes) that can
recognize and bind to the target molecule, as well as transducers that can convert the binding event into
a detectable signal.

Features of Lab-on-Chip Technology:
Lab-on-chip technology allows for the miniaturization and integration of laboratory functions onto a
single chip. This technology has several key features, including the ability to perform multiple analyses
simultaneously, reduced sample and reagent volumes, and rapid analysis times.

Importance of Lab-on-Chip Technology:
 Lab-on-chip technology is important because it enables faster, more efficient, and more cost-effective
laboratory analyses. It can be used in a variety of applications, including medical diagnostics,
environmental monitoring, and food safety testing.

 Features in designing diagnostic micropads:
Diagnostic micropads are small devices used for diagnostic testing. When designing them, important
features to consider include the type of sample they will be used with, the sensitivity and specificity of
the detection method, the ease of use, and the cost-effectiveness of the device.

Role of hormones of endocrine glands:
 Hormones produced by endocrine glands play important roles in regulating various functions in the
body. For example, insulin, produced by the pancreas, regulates blood sugar levels, while thyroid
hormones, produced by the thyroid gland, regulate metabolism. These hormones act as chemical
messengers, traveling through the bloodstream to target organs where they exert their effects.

How magnetic nanoparticles act as a carrier?

Magnetic nanoparticles can act as carriers by attaching to other molecules, such as drugs or
biomolecules, and transporting them to specific targets in the body. Their magnetic properties allow
them to be guided to the desired location using an external magnetic field, making them useful in
targeted drug delivery and imaging applications.

Three damages caused by heavy metals?
Heavy metals can cause various damages to the body, including:

Neurological damage: Heavy metals like lead and mercury can affect the nervous system, leading to
symptoms like tremors, memory loss, and cognitive impairment.
Kidney damage: Heavy metals such as cadmium can accumulate in the kidneys and cause damage,
leading to kidney failure.
Cardiovascular damage: Heavy metals like arsenic and lead have been linked to cardiovascular
diseases such as hypertension and heart attacks.

Which strain of E. coli causes infections? Explain its pathogenesis.

The strain of E. coli that commonly causes infections in humans is called Enteropathogenic E. coli
(EPEC). Pathogenesis refers to how a bacterium causes disease. EPEC typically infects the intestinal
lining, attaching to and effacing the cells, which leads to diarrhea. It produces proteins called intimin
and Tir, which help it attach to the intestinal cells and form characteristic lesions called attaching and
effacing lesions. These lesions disrupt the normal function of the intestine, leading to diarrhea and
sometimes dehydration.

Electronic nose and electronic tongue.
Electronic nose and electronic tongue are analytical devices designed to mimic the human senses of
smell and taste, respectively. They use arrays of sensors that detect and analyze volatile compounds (in
the case of electronic nose) or taste molecules (in the case of electronic tongue) to identify and
characterize odors or flavors in samples. These devices are used in various applications such as food
and beverage quality control, environmental monitoring, and medical diagnostics.

Why does ferrocene fit all criteria of a good mediator?
Ferrocene fits all criteria of a good mediator because it is stable, reversible, and has well-defined redox
properties. It can easily accept and donate electrons, making it an excellent mediator in electrochemical
reactions. Additionally, ferrocene is non-toxic and relatively inexpensive, making it suitable for use in
various biological and analytical applications.

Structure of molecular beacon?
Molecular beacon is a type of nucleic acid probe used in molecular biology and diagnostics. It consists
of a single-stranded DNA or RNA molecule with a stem-loop structure. The stem is formed by
complementary base pairing within the molecule, while the loop contains a sequence complementary to
the target molecule. At one end of the molecule, there is a fluorophore (a molecule that emits light) and
at the other end, there is a quencher (a molecule that absorbs light). In the absence of the target
molecule, the fluorophore and quencher are close together, resulting in quenching of the fluorescence.
When the target molecule binds to the loop region, it causes the stem to unfold, separating the
fluorophore and quencher, and leading to the emission of fluorescence, which can be detected and
measured.
