   2. Why we classify Fishes?
We classify fishes to identify and distinguish between species, organize them logically, and facilitate
communication among scientists, researchers, and fishermen. Classifying fishes helps in
understanding their biodiversity, behavior, evolutionary relationships, and ecological roles, which is
essential for effective conservation and management of aquatic ecosystems. It aids in conservation,
fisheries management, and scientific research. For example, classifying sharks into different species
helps identify which are endangered and need protection measures.
   3. Write down the measurements of fishes related to taxonomy
   1) Determine family using a key
   2) Identify to the lowest taxonomic unit
   3) Verify with illustrations
   4) Match with previously identified specimens
   5) Confirm geographical range
   6) Compare with standard texts (FAO, Smith’s, Catalog of Fishes, Fishbase)

   4. Methods of Measurement of Fish
Fish measurement involves various methods to ensure accurate data collection for scientific and
commercial purposes. Common methods include:
   1) Total Length (TL): Measurement from the tip of the snout to the end of the longest caudal
      fin ray
   2) Standard Length (SL): Measurement from the tip of the snout to the base of the caudal fin,
      excluding the fin rays
   3) Fork Length (FL): Measurement from the tip of the snout to the fork in the tail
   4) Weight: Using a balance or scale to measure the fish’s weight

   5. Descriptive Measures of Fish Identification
Descriptive measures of fish identification involve morphological and meristic characteristics:
Morphological: Morphological characteristics provide information about a fish’s overall
appearance. Body shape, fin placement, scale patterns, and coloration etc.
Meristic: Meristic characteristics involve counting specific features to distinguish between species.
Countable features like fin rays, scales along the lateral line, and gill rakers.
   6. Meristic Count Related to Taxonomy
Meristic count is a taxonomic tool used to identify and classify fish species based on countable
characteristics such as fin rays, scales, and gill rakers. It involves counting the number of features like
dorsal fin rays, anal fin rays, pectoral fin rays, scales along the lateral line, and gill rakers to create a
unique numerical profile for each species. This profile helps taxonomists distinguish between similar-
looking species, understand evolutionary relationships, and group species into genera and families.
Meristic count is a crucial aspect of ichthyological research, providing valuable information for fish
classification, systematics, and biodiversity studies.

   8. Descriptive Measures of Identification of Labeo rohita?/ Parameters
      of Labeo rohita (Rohu fish)
   o Morphology/Body Shape: Elongated and slightly compressed body with a depth contour.
   o Size: Up to 1 m (3.3 ft) in length, 45 kg (99 Ibs) in weight
   o Coloration: Silver body with a bluish tinge on the dorsal side, with a distinctive pattern of
     spots or markings.
   o Fins: Dorsal fin with 12-16 rays, pectoral fins with 16-18 rays, and a forked caudal fin.
   o Scales: Large cycloid scales covering the body.
   o Snout: Short, pointed
   o Mouth: Inferior,
   o Pharyngeal teeth: 3 rows, 5-6 teeth each
   o Reproductive System: Ovoviviparous, with a pair of gonads, and a distinctive structure of
     reproductive organs in males and females
   o Digestive System: A long alimentary canal with a modified stomach and intestine, with a
     length ratio of approximately 1:3.

   9. Fixation Process
  Specimen should be fixed immediately after collection.
  To fix the specimen wide mouthed glass filled with fixative solution is can be used.
  Tight lids must be used for fixation.
  The specimen must be preserved in as natural as possible.
   The fixative should be injected to the body cavity to facilitate penetration and preservation of
internal organs.
   10.        Why we choose right side of abdomen for operation in fishes ?
Make a small incision on the right side of the abdomen to facilitate penetration of the preservative.
The right side is chosen for this and similar operations like the removal of scale samples because the
left side is used for getting morphometric data
   11.        Physical Parameters of Water Quality
Physical parameters of water quality are those that are determined by the senses of sight, smell, taste,
and touch. These physical parameters include temperature, color, taste and odor, turbidity, and
content of dissolved solids.
   12.        Taxonomic Importance of Fish in Museum
Taxonomy and Classification:
 Fish museums are crucial for the study of fish taxonomy and classification. They help in the
identification and description of new species and in clarifying the evolutionary relationships among
different fish groups. Accurate taxonomy is fundamental to understanding biodiversity and
conservation.
   13.        Sensory regulation in fish related to eyes and lateral lines.

color, and polarized light, guiding navigation, foraging, and communication. The lateral line system,
