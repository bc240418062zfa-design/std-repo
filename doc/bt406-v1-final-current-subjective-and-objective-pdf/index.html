What is the difference between a scale and index? 3
Scale:
   1. Measures intensity: Scales quantify how strongly respondents feel or experience something, like pain
       levels.
   2. Organizes on continuum: Responses are placed along a spectrum, like a Likert scale ranging from
       "strongly agree" to "strongly disagree."
   3. Single or multiple indicators: Can use one question or multiple questions to assess a concept, such as a
       happiness scale.
   4. Ordinal level: Data categorized into ordered ranks but lacks precise measurement, like ranking
       satisfaction as "low," "medium," or "high."
Index:
   1. Combines indicators: Researchers merge various measures into one score, like the Human Development
       Index.
   2. Composite score: Results in a single value derived from multiple factors, such as the Consumer Price
       Index.
   3. Reflects multiple dimensions: Represents diverse aspects of a concept, like the Poverty Index combining
       income, education, and health data.
   4. Provides holistic view: Offers a comprehensive assessment of a construct, like the Quality of Life Index.

What is scale and explain its properties 5
  1. Uniquely classifies: Each response represents a distinct level, like categorizing pain as mild, moderate, or
      severe.
  2. Preserves order: Maintains the sequence of responses, such as ranking satisfaction from lowest to highest.
  3. Equal intervals: Spaces between response options are consistent, like temperature measured in Celsius or
      Fahrenheit.
  4. Natural zero: Possesses a true absence or starting point, as in age or income.
  5. Exhaustive coverage: Encompasses all possible responses within the defined range, like rating scales from
      1 to 10.

Difference semi structure and unstructured interview
Structured interviews involve asking predetermined questions in a fixed order, ensuring consistency and
comparability. For example, a survey about customer satisfaction might include questions like "On a scale of 1 to
10, how satisfied are you with our product?" These interviews are ideal for quantitative analysis.
In contrast, semi-structured interviews blend predefined questions with open-ended ones, allowing flexibility
and deeper exploration. For instance, in a study about job satisfaction, researchers might ask about specific

aspects of the job but also encourage participants to elaborate on their experiences. This approach suits
qualitative research, enabling nuanced insights and context-rich data.
Both interview types offer distinct advantages: Structured interviews ensure uniformity in responses, aiding in
statistical analysis, while semi-structured interviews foster in-depth understanding by allowing participants to
express themselves freely. Choosing between them depends on research goals, the depth of inquiry required, and
the level of flexibility desired in engaging with participants.

Briefly Explain Qualitative Research? / Write Short Note On Quantitative Research/ Different Qualitative
And Quantitative Research With Example?
Qualitative Research: Qualitative research aims to understand social phenomena deeply, often through methods
like interviews or textual analysis. For example, a study on the experiences of cancer survivors might use
interviews to explore their emotions and coping strategies.
Quantitative Research: Quantitative research focuses on descriptive analysis and examining relationships
between variables. For instance, a survey investigating the correlation between exercise frequency and mental
health scores uses statistical analysis to quantify this relationship.

Qualitative vs Quantitative Research:
Qualitative Research:
  1. Explores social phenomena deeply through interviews, textual analysis, etc.
  2. Focuses on understanding "how" and "why" questions about phenomena.
  3. Aims for discovery and comprehension of complex social dynamics.
Quantitative Research:
  1. Analyzes variables and relationships using statistical methods.
  2. Addresses questions about the relationships between variables.
  3. Seeks verification or disconfirmation of hypotheses through deductive reasoning.

Enlist five qualitative research method. 5
Five qualitative research methods are discussed in this subject as discussed by John W. Creswell in his Book.
Following are the five qualitative research methods:

Enlist five qualitative research methods:
   1. Narrative Research: Focuses on gathering and interpreting stories or accounts to understand experiences
        deeply.
   2. Phenomenology: Explores individuals' lived experiences to uncover underlying meanings and essences.
   3. Grounded Theory: Develops theories by systematically analyzing data to uncover patterns and themes.
   4. Ethnography: Immerses researchers in a cultural context to understand social phenomena within their
        natural settings.
   5. Case Study: In-depth examination of a specific instance or situation to explore complex phenomena
        within its context.

***How many type of researches are there? 5
   • Quantitative Research: Utilizes numerical data and statistical analysis to quantify relationships and
     patterns.
   • Qualitative Research: Explores phenomena through non-numerical data to understand meanings and
     interpretations.

   •   Experimental Research: Manipulates variables to establish cause-and-effect relationships in controlled
       settings.
   •   Survey Research: Gathers data from a sample population through questionnaires or interviews.
   •   Case Study Research: In-depth analysis of a specific individual, group, or phenomenon.
   •   Observational Research: Systematic observation and recording of behavior in natural settings.
