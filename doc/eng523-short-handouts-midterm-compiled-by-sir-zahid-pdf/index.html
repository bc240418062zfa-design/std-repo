   Lecture-01
                  Introduction to Discourse Analysis
Topic: Introduction to Discourse Analysis
What is discourse analysis?
Discourse analysis is the study of the relationship between language and the contexts
in which it is used.
Who are the key figures in British discourse analysis, and what were their
contributions?
M. A. K. Halliday emphasized the social functions of language and thematic
structure.
Sinclair and Coulthard developed a model for analyzing teacher-pupil talk.
British discourse analysis focused on structural-linguistic criteria and units.
What were the main contributions of British discourse analysis to the field?
British Discourse Analysis: British discourse analysis was influenced by M. A. K.
Halliday's functional approach, emphasizing social functions of language and
thematic structures. Additionally, work by Sinclair and Coulthard focused on the
analysis of teacher-pupil talk and hierarchy of discourse units.
How does American discourse analysis differ from the British tradition?

American Discourse Analysis: American discourse analysis, particularly
ethnomethodological tradition, involves close observation of communication in
natural settings. It focuses on various speech events, including storytelling and
greeting rituals. This approach emphasizes the social nature of communication and
conversational norms.
What is the distinction between form and function in discourse analysis?
Form and Function: Discourse analysis involves understanding not only the
structural aspects but also the functions of language. Form refers to the surface-level
characteristics, while function focuses on the specific purpose and meaning behind
language use.
What characterizes American discourse analysis, and which traditions
influence it?
American discourse analysis is influenced by ethnomethodological tradition.
It emphasizes close observation of people communicating in natural settings.
What are some types of speech events often analyzed in American discourse
analysis?
Storytelling, greeting rituals, verbal duels, and various cultural and social settings.
What is the role of cohesion in discourse analysis?
Cohesion involves the use of linguistic cues to create links and relationships between
different parts of a text.
What is the distinction between text, discourse, and context in analyzing
discourse?
Text refers to the verbal and non-verbal elements used in communication.
Discourse involves hierarchically structured acts carried out to achieve a
communicative goal.
Context is the background and situational factors that affect communication.
How does cohesion contribute to text coherence?
Cohesion provides cues for the reader to create coherence but does not guarantee it.
How can larger patterns in text, such as problem-solution structures, be
identified and analyzed in discourse?
Larger Text Patterns: Larger patterns in text, like problem-solution structures, are
common and are identified through grammatical and lexical devices. Recognizing
these patterns is important for text interpretation and composition.

What is the essence of discourse analysis, and why has it gained importance in
linguistics and other disciplines?
Essence of Discourse Analysis: Discourse analysis focuses on the study of language
in context, emphasizing how language is used and its relationship with broader
contexts. It has become a critical area of study in linguistics and various other
disciplines.
Who were some influential figures in the development of discourse analysis?
Zellig Harris, M. A. K. Halliday, Sinclair and Coulthard, and Michael Hoey
contributed to the development of discourse analysis.
What is a common pattern found in many texts and what is it called?
The problem-solution pattern, where a situation is presented, followed by a
complication or problem, a response to the problem, and a positive evaluation of the
response.
How do grammatical and lexical devices play a role in signaling larger patterns
in text?
Conjunctions, backward lexical references, and forward references are used to signal
and structure larger patterns in text.

Lecture-02
                  Two Strands of Discourse Analysis
Topic: Spoken Language-I
What are some different types of speech in people's everyday lives?
Telephone calls, interviews, classroom discussions, rituals, monologues, casual
conversation, organizing and directing people.
What are adjacency pairs in conversation?
Adjacency pairs are units of conversation where two speakers exchange one turn
each, and the first turn requires a specific type of response.
Give an example of an adjacency pair.
Answer: Greeting (hello) - Response (hello).
What is the significance of softeners in conversation?
Softeners like "I'm afraid" are used to preface disagreement second pair-parts in
English, especially in friendly situations.
How is turn-taking managed in conversation?

Turn-taking is managed through various means, including formal methods,
adjacency pairs, intonation, gestures, and changes in gaze direction.
Topic: Spoken Language-II
What is the difference between transactional and interactional talk?
Transactional talk focuses on accomplishing tasks, while interactional talk focuses
on building and maintaining relationships.
Give examples of talk as transaction.
Classroom group discussions, ordering food in a restaurant, making a phone call for
information, etc.
What is the purpose of small talk in interactional talk?
Small talk is used to create a positive atmosphere and establish comfort between
people, often in social situations.
