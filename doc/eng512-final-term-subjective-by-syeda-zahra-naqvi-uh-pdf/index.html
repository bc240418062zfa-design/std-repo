1:Three characteristics of linguicism?
1. Discrimination: Favouring one language over others, leading to unfair treatment of people
who speak different languages.
2. Language Hierarchy: Believing that some languages are superior and others are inferior.
3. Restricted Opportunities: Limiting access to education, jobs, or social services based on the
language someone speaks.

2:Difference between adults and children in learning bilingual language:
Children and adults learn a bilingual language differently. Children learn languages quickly
and naturally through play and talking with others. They tend to pick up native-like
pronunciation easily. On the other hand, adults take more time to learn a new language and
usually do so through formal classes and practice. They often retain an accent from their first
language. While children learn because of their environment or school, adults are usually
motivated by specific reasons like work or travel.

3:Flexible convergent arrangement:
In flexible convergent language use, aimed at encouraging a shift from one language to
another, there are two common strategies:
1. Random Code-Switching: Frequently mixing two languages in conversation, which
gradually leads to the dominance of the target language.
2. Monoliterate Bilingualism: Developing literacy (reading and writing) skills in only one
language, encouraging its use over the other.

4. Need for Bilingual Languages in the 21st Century:

1. Global Communication: Helps in connecting with people from different countries.
2. Career Opportunities: Opens up more job options and can lead to better salaries.
3. Cultural Understanding: Promotes understanding and appreciation of different cultures.
4. Cognitive Benefits: Improves brain function, problem-solving skills, and multitasking.

5. Key Points for Scholars Preparing Bilingual Education Material:

1. Cultural Relevance: Ensure the material respects and reflects both cultures.
2. Language Balance: Provide equal focus on both languages to promote proficiency in each.
3. Engaging Content: Use interactive and interesting resources to keep students engaged.
4. Age Appropriateness: Tailor the material to be suitable for the age and developmental level
of the learners.
5. Clear Instructions: Offer clear and concise explanations in both languages.
6. Support Resources: Include tools like glossaries and translations to aid understanding.
7. Inclusivity: Make sure the material is accessible to all students, regardless of their language
background.

6. Two examples of "co-language" in a classroom setting:
1. Bilingual Presentations: Teachers use PowerPoint slides with text in both languages, such
as English and Spanish. Each language is in a different colour, helping all students understand
the material.
2. Subtitled Videos: A history teacher shows a documentary in English with subtitles in
Spanish. This helps students who speak either language understand the content.

7. Characteristicsof Co-languaging:
Using both languages at the same time, known as co-language, is increasingly common in
education, especially in Africa. For instance, universities are integrating students who speak
different languages by presenting curriculum materials in both Afrikaans and English on
PowerPoint slides, using different colours for each language. This approach ensures inclusion
of all students while respecting Afrikaans traditions. Co-language also occurs when teachers
show videos in one language with subtitles in another, or when students use computer
programs that support learning in different languages simultaneously. In primary classrooms,
students may listen to books in two languages, sometimes switching between them as they
learn.

8. Responsible Code-Switching:
Responsible code-switching means carefully managing language switching in the classroom
to support the lesson, ensuring it is meaningful and not random. Most instruction should be in
the main language being developed.

9. Time Allocation in bilingualism:
In bilingualism, time allocation refers to the distribution of time spent using and learning
each language to ensure balanced development and proficiency in both languages.

10. Semilingualism:
Semilingualism in bilingualism refers to the idea that some bilingual individuals have limited
proficiency in both of their languages, compared to monolingual speakers. This concept
suggests that these individuals may struggle with language skills in both languages, rather

Subtractive bilingualism occurs when learning a second language leads to the loss or
