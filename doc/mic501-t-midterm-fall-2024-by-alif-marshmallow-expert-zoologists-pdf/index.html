   Magnetosomes are inclusions of iron oxide surrounded by invaginations of the plasma
membrane. Typically found in Gram-negative bacteria, they function as magnets, enabling
bacteria to adhere to iron-containing rocks for nutrition. Additionally, magnetosomes assist in
decomposing hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), a toxic substance for cells.

Gram Staining:
      Differential stains react differently with different kinds of bacteria. Most common is
Gram staining.
   ➢ 1884, by Hans Christian Gram
   ➢ Crystal violet is added– Primary stain
   ➢ Crystal violet is washed off.
   ➢ Iodine is added to enhance binding– Mordant
   ➢ The slide is washed off with alcohol– Decolorizing agent

   ➢   Gram positive bacteria retain crystal violet
   ➢   Gram negative bacteria appear colorless.
   ➢   Crystal violet and iodine make a complex in the cytoplasm
   ➢   Peptidoglycan layer is thicker in Gram positive bacteria and CV-I is retained.
   ➢   Alcohol is rinsed off and stained with safranin– Counterstain
   ➢   The smear is washed again.
   ➢   Blotted dry and examined microscopically.

OR

Step-by-Step Procedure of Gram Staining

   1. Smear Preparation:
         o Spread a thin bacterial sample on a slide and allow it to air dry.
         o Heat-fix the smear by passing it through a flame.
   2. Primary Staining:
         o Flood the slide with crystal violet for 1 minute.
         o Rinse gently with water.
   3. Mordant Application:
         o Apply iodine solution for 1 minute to form a crystal violet-iodine complex.
         o Rinse with water.
   4. Decolorization:
         o Wash the slide with alcohol or acetone for a few seconds to remove the stain
            from Gram-negative bacteria.
         o Rinse immediately with water to prevent over-decolorization.
   5. Counterstaining:
         o Apply safranin for 30–60 seconds to stain Gram-negative bacteria.
         o Rinse with water and blot dry.
   6. Microscopic Examination:
         o Observe under an oil immersion microscope.
         o Gram-positive bacteria appear purple, while Gram-negative bacteria
            appear pink/red.

Characteristics of Living Organisms for Classification:

   1. Cellular Organization: All living organisms are made up of one or more cells.

   2. Metabolism: They carry out biochemical reactions to obtain energy and sustain life.
   3. Growth and Development: Organisms increase in size and undergo changes over
      time.
   4. Reproduction: They reproduce to pass genetic material to the next generation.
   5. Response to Stimuli: Living beings react to environmental changes.
   6. Homeostasis: They maintain internal stability despite external changes.
   7. Adaptation and Evolution: Organisms evolve over generations to survive in
      changing environments.

Three Chemicals That Disrupt the Plasma Membrane:

   1. Polymyxins – Disrupt phospholipids, increasing membrane permeability, leading to
      cell leakage.
   2. Ethanol (Alcohols) – Dissolves membrane lipids and denatures proteins, causing cell
      lysis.
   3. Chlorhexidine – Disrupts membrane integrity, leading to leakage of cellular contents.
