                             SHORT QUESTIONS(3)

   1) Types of Ecosystem
1. Natural ecosystem which has further types discussed below
   • Terrestrial e.g. Grassland (Savana),Forest, Desert ecosystems
   • Aquatic
     a.Lentic (Stagnant water) like lake, ponds etc.
     b.Lotic (Flowing water) like river, ocean, sea, etc.
2. Artificial ecosystem: A crop land, garden, aquarium, park, kitchen garden.
   2) Types of Biogeochemical cycles
a) Gaseous type-in which reservoir is in atmosphere or the hydrosphere (Ocean).
Examples. Nitrogen Cycle, Carbon Cycle, Water Cycle
b) Sedimentary type-in which reservoir is in the earth Crust. Examples.
Phosphorus Cycle, Sulphur Cycle
   3) Scope Of Ecology

   1)    all organisms living on Earth
   2)    all physical and chemical surroundings.
   3)     for this reason, the field is usually divided into different levels of study
         including: organismal ecology, population ecology, community ecology and
         ecosystem ecology.

   4)    Write the factors affecting the composition of communities
         and ecosystem?
The composition of communities and ecosystems changes due to these factors
   1) Continents movement
   2) Climate changes
   3) Natural disturbances
   4) Succession

   5) Important Steps in Carbon cycle
   1) Photosynthesis
   2) Respiration
   3) Decomposition
   4) Combustion

   6)Write the factors influence the carrying capacity?
These factors include food supply, water supply, habitat space, competition
(intraspecific and interspecific), physical factors (e.g. extreme heat, drought, etc.),
chemical factors (e.g. pH, mineral deficiency, etc.), and anthropogenic factors.

   7) Tundra Climate
A permanently frozen subsoil called permafrost is found in the arctic and antarctic
tundra. The summer temperature may be around 15°C and in winter, it may be as low
as –57°C in arctic tundra .A very low precipitation of less than 400 mm per year

   9) Distribution of Tropical Rain Forest
Tropical Rain (Evergreen) Forest : These are in the tropical region of very high
rain fall. Such forests are well developed over the western coast of India and North
eastern Himalayas and scattered in south east Asia, west Africa and north cost of South
America.
   10)      Define Resource?
A resource is something that is useful or valuable to humans. It can be a material or
an energy source that we rely on for various purposes. For example, water is a vital
resource that we use for drinking, agriculture, and industrial processes.
   11)      Ammonification
When plants or animal die, the nitrogen present in the organic matter is released back
into the soil. The decomposers, namely bacteria or fungi present in the soil convert the
organic matter back into ammonium. This process of decomposition produces
ammonia which is further used for other biological processes.
   12)      Interference competition:
Occurs when an individual of one species directly interferes with an individual of
another species. E.g: a plant releasing allelopathic chemicals to impede the growth of
a competing species.
   13)      Significance of Symbiosis Relationship
Symbiosis: Interspecific interactions in which two species live together in a long-
term, intimate association
   • Symbiotic relationships keep a delicate balance
   • can help individual species to evolve or change and even thrive
   • Provide people with food, populate the planet with trees and plants, and keep
     animal and plant populations in balance
   • Without symbiotic relationships, no coral reefs, trees might not proliferate as far
     and wide, aided by birds and insects that transport seeds afar
   • Adaptations arising through coevolution come from two different species living
     showing various relationships or interactions
   14)      Amensalism: (-/0,Interaction) with 2 examples.
It describes an interaction in which the presence of one species has a negative effect on
another, but the first species is unaffected.
Example:
   1) a herd of elephants walking across a landscape may crush fragile plants
   2) A tree casting a dense shadow that prevents other plants from receiving sunlight.
   3) An antibiotic-producing fungus inhibiting the growth of nearby bacteria.
   15)      Herbivory: (+ −) interaction

• When Predation involves an animal or insect consuming part of a plant, known as
herbivory. • Animals that feed on plants by cropping portions of the plant, but usually
not killing the plant, are herbivores • Plants have spines and toxins-defensive
  16)      Population ecology & Types
Population ecology:
 Any group of organisms of the same species occupying a particular place and
functioning part of biotic community, which in turn, is defined as assembly of
populations functioning as unit, showing interactions due to coevolved metabolic
transformations in particular physical habitat.
Population ecology Types:
• Population Natality: Ability of population to increase by reproduction-birth rate.
• Population Mortality: Death rate of individuals in the population.
  17)      Example of directly and indirectly available resources.
Directly available resources are those that can be readily accessed and utilized by
organisms without the need for any additional processing or conversion. Examples
include sunlight, air, water, and food sources like fruits or nuts.
Indirectly available resources, on the other hand, require some form of
processing or conversion before they can be used by organisms. Examples include
wood that needs to be converted into fuel or building materials, minerals that need to
be extracted and refined, or agricultural crops that need to be harvested and processed
before they can be consumed.
