1. Phase Contrast Microscopy
Phase contrast microscopy is used to observe transparent specimens like live cells without staining. It converts small differences in light passing through the sample into visible contrast. This helps in studying cell structures and their behavior. It’s commonly used in biology labs for live samples.
2. Limitations of Electron Microscope
Electron microscopes cannot observe live specimens because they require a vacuum. They are expensive, need skilled operators, and involve lengthy sample preparation. They also only produce black-and-white images. Maintenance is complex and costly.
3. Electron Microscope
An electron microscope uses a beam of electrons to produce detailed images of tiny structures. It has much higher magnification and resolution than light microscopes. TEM shows internal structures, while SEM provides surface details. It is used in biology and materials research.
4. Factors Affecting Resolution of Microscope
Resolution depends on the wavelength of light or electrons; shorter wavelengths provide better resolution. Lens quality and numerical aperture (light-gathering ability) are also important. Proper alignment of optical components improves clarity. The medium between the lens and sample, like air or oil, also matters.
5. Simple and Compound Light Microscope
A simple microscope has one lens and is used for basic magnification. A compound microscope uses multiple lenses for higher magnification and better detail. It’s used to study cells, tissues, and microorganisms. Compound microscopes are common in schools and labs.
6. How Phase Contrast Microscope Works
A phase contrast microscope enhances light passing through the specimen, making small differences in light waves visible. This highlights internal structures without staining. It is ideal for studying live cells and their behavior. This method preserves the natural state of the specimen.
7. Common Fixatives in Microscopy
Common fixatives include formaldehyde, glutaraldehyde, ethanol, and methanol. Formaldehyde preserves tissue structure by crosslinking proteins. Glutaraldehyde is ideal for detailed preservation in electron microscopy. Alcohol-based fixatives dehydrate and stabilize the sample.
8. Mechanism of Fixation in Microscopy
Fixation preserves tissues by stabilizing proteins and preventing decay. It works by crosslinking molecules to maintain the natural structure. This ensures that cells and tissues stay intact for microscopic analysis. Fixation prevents enzymatic or bacterial breakdown of the sample.
9. Hot and Cold Filtration
Hot filtration removes impurities from solutions at high temperatures, preventing crystals from forming. Cold filtration is used to separate solid impurities from cooled solutions. Both methods improve purity in chemical processes. Each method is selected based on the substance being filtered.
10. Purpose of Multilayer Filtration
Multilayer filtration removes particles of different sizes using multiple filter layers. It improves the purity of solutions by capturing both large and small impurities. This method is used in water treatment and chemical processes. It ensures thorough filtration.
11. Why Electron-Dense Dye is Used in DNA Visualization
Electron-dense dyes, like uranyl acetate, bind to DNA and increase contrast in electron microscopy. These dyes scatter electrons, making DNA visible. They are essential for studying DNA structure under high magnification. This technique helps observe detailed molecular features.
12. How DNA is Visualized in Gel Electrophoresis
DNA is visualized using fluorescent dyes like ethidium bromide or SYBR Green. These dyes bind to DNA and glow under UV light. The glowing bands represent DNA fragments of different sizes. This method helps analyze DNA in a gel.
13. Factors Affecting DNA Migration in Gel Electrophoresis
The size of DNA fragments affects how fast they move; smaller fragments move faster. The gel concentration determines how easily fragments migrate. Voltage and buffer composition also influence movement. Larger fragments face more resistance.
14. Fluorescence & Light Microscope
A light microscope uses visible light to observe specimens and is suitable for basic imaging. A fluorescence microscope uses fluorescent dyes to highlight specific structures, like proteins or DNA. It provides high contrast and is widely used in biological research.
15. Importance of Gel Concentration in Electrophoresis
Gel concentration determines how easily DNA fragments move through it. Higher concentrations are better for separating small fragments, while lower concentrations are used for larger fragments. This affects the clarity and separation of bands.
16. Vibratome Preferred Over Microtome in Some Cases
A vibratome is preferred for cutting soft tissues or live samples without freezing or embedding. It preserves tissue hydration and reduces structural damage. It’s ideal for neuroscience and live-cell research. Vibratomes avoid the complexities of freezing.
17. Process of Band Formation in Gel During Electrophoresis
DNA fragments move through the gel based on size, with smaller fragments traveling faster. As they migrate, they form distinct bands. These bands are visualized using fluorescent dyes under UV light. The position of the bands indicates fragment size.
18. Manual and Automation in Microscopy
Manual microscopy involves direct operation by the user, which can be time-consuming. Automated microscopy uses software to capture and analyze images quickly. Automation provides consistent results and saves time. It is widely used in advanced research labs.
19. Fluorescence Microscopy & Application
Fluorescence microscopy uses fluorescent dyes to visualize specific structures within cells. It provides high contrast and helps in studying proteins, DNA, and organelles. Applications include medical diagnostics and molecular biology. It’s widely used in cancer research.
