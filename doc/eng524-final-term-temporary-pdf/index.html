Q. Political discourse analysis
Political discourse analysis (PDA) involves examining the language used
in political contexts to understand how power, ideology, and political
agendas are constructed and conveyed. This type of analysis often focuses
on speeches, debates, policies, and other forms of communication used by
politicians and political institutions.
Q. Social media CDA
Social media CDA involves analyzing the content and interactions on
social media platforms to understand how discourse is shaped and how it
influences social and political opinions. It includes studying hashtags,
posts, comments, and the network of interactions among users to identify
patterns and implications of online communication.
Q. What is terrorism
Terrorism refers to the use of violence and intimidation, especially against
civilians, in the pursuit of political aims. It is characterized by its
deliberate targeting of non-combatants and its intent to create fear and
coerce governments or societies to achieve specific ideological goals.
Q. Class talk by different linguists
Class talk refers to the way language is used in classroom settings, often
analyzed by different linguists to understand how teachers and students
interact, how knowledge is constructed, and how power dynamics play
out in educational environments.

When the project is media CDA, the three strands that are often similar

     Analysis of representation: How different groups, events, and issues
     are portrayed in the media.
     Analysis of media production: How media content is created,

     Analysis of audience reception: How media messages are
     interpreted and understood by different audiences.
Q. 5 discourse historical strategies by Wodak
The five discourse historical strategies by Wodak include:
     Nomination: How actors, objects, and events are named and referred
     to.
     Predication: Attributing qualities, characteristics, or features to
     social actors, objects, and events.
     Argumentation: The construction and justification of arguments.
     Perspectivization: Positioning the speaker's or writer's point of view.
     Intensification and mitigation: Modifying the intensity or tone of
     statements.

Q. Discursive strategies
Discursive strategies are the methods used in communication to achieve
specific goals, such as persuading, informing, or entertaining. They
include techniques like framing, metaphor, narrative construction, and
argumentation.
Q. How Labor Party different from Conservative Party?
The Labor Party and Conservative Party differ in their political ideologies
and policies. Generally, the Labor Party is associated with social
democracy, advocating for social justice, welfare state, and public
ownership, while the Conservative Party tends to support free-market
policies, limited government intervention, and individual responsibility.

Q. Write note on covert and overt racism
Covert racism refers to subtle, often hidden, discriminatory practices and
attitudes, while overt racism is explicit and openly displayed. Covert
racism can be harder to detect and challenge, whereas overt racism is more
visible and direct.
Q. Jessop has outlined 5 approaches examining neoliberalism
Jessop's five approaches to examining neoliberalism include:
     Economic restructuring.
     Political reorganization.
     Social policy changes.
     Ideological shifts.
     Globalization impacts.
Q. What is Perpetrator mitigation?
Perpetrator mitigation involves strategies and discourse used to downplay
or justify the actions of those who commit offenses or harmful acts. It
seeks to lessen the perceived severity of the perpetrator's actions.
