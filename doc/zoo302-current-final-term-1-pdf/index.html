• Ruminant mammals are the animals that “chew their cud,’’ such
as cows, sheep, and deer show some of the most unusual
modifications of the stomach, but can chew the food at a more
comfortable or safer location.
• The ruminant stomach provides an opportunity for large
numbers of microorganisms to digest the cellulase walls of grass
and other vegetation.
• Cellulose contains a large amount of energy; however, animals
generally lack the ability to produce the enzyme cellulose for
digesting cellulose and obtaining its energy.
• In ruminants, the upper portion of the stomach expands to form
a large pouch, the rumen, and a smaller reticulum.
• The lower portion of the stomach consists of a small
antechamber.
Q.2 Digestion in grasshopper? r
• The grasshopper is a representative insect with a complete digestive tract.
• In grasshopper (or cockroach) the digestion of food starts in the
mouth. The saliva contains some enzymes. Saliva also helps in
lubrication and softening of food. This partially digested food
passes through the esophagus into crop where it is stored
temporarily. Then, the food passes into gizzard. From gizzard, the
food goes into stomach. In the stomach, the enzymes secreted by

elongated glands called hepatic caeca get mixed with the food to
digest it completely.

Q.3 Osmoregulation defines?           r

in the fluids of an organism by the control of water and salt
concentrations.
• It means osmoregulation governs water and salt balance, and
excretion of metabolic wastes.
• In fishes, reptiles, birds, and mammals, the kidneys are the
primary osmoregulatory structures.
Q.4 what is digestion?
Digestion is the chemical and/or mechanical breakdown of
complex food into diffusible molecules i.e. particles that individual
cells of an animal can absorb.
• Starch into glucose.
• Proteins into amino acids.
• Fats into fatty acids & glycerol.

Predation is one of the most sophisticated feeding strategies,
since it requires the capture of live prey.
It is an interaction in which one organism kills another for food.
The organisms that does the killing for food is the predator and
the organisms that is killed for food is the prey.

Examples:

 Wolves hunting a deer or moose, an owl hunting the mice

Q.7 Gallbladder function?
• The gallbladder is a small bag near the liver.
• The gallbladder stores the greenish fluid.
Its main function is to store bile. Bile helps your digestive
system break down fats.
Gallbladder is a small hollow organ where bile is stored and
concentrated before it is released into the small intestine.
• The structure and position of the gallbladder can vary
significantly among animal species. Because of the importance of
bile in fat digestion, the gallbladder is relatively large in
carnivores and vertebrates.
Q.8 Temperature Regulations in Fishes?              Long r
The temperature of the surrounding water determines the
body temperature of most fishes.
How Fishes live in extremely cold water?

1. They have “antifreeze” materials in their blood in the form of
Polyalcohols (e.g., sorbitol, glycerol) or water soluble peptides and
glycopeptides.
• Because these substances lower the freezing point of blood
plasma and other body fluids.

2. These fishes also have proteins or protein-sugar compounds
that inhibit the growth of ice crystals that begin to form.
3. These adaptations enable these fishes to stay flexible and
swim freely in a supercooled state (i.e., at a temperature below
the normal freezing temperature of a surrounding water).
4. These fishes have major blood vessels just under the skin.
• Branches deliver blood to the deeper, powerful, red swimming
muscles, where smaller vessels are arranged in a countercurrent
heat exchanger called the rete mirabile.
5• The heat generated by red muscles is not lost but it is
transferred in the rete mirabile from venous warm blood to cold
arterial blood.
• Some active fishes maintain a core temperature significantly
above the temperature of the water.
For example: Bluefin tuna and the great white shark.
6 This arrangement of blood vessels enhances vigorous activity
by keeping the swimming muscles several degrees warmer than
the tissue near the surface of the fish. • This system has been
adaptive for these fishes.
