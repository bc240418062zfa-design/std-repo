Methods to Count Population Abundance

Population abundance refers to the total number of individuals of a species in a given area. Counting population
abundance is essential for ecological research, conservation efforts, and managing species. The methods can be
divided into direct and indirect approaches:

1. Direct Methods

   •   Quadrat Sampling:
       Small, square plots are marked in the habitat, and the individuals within are counted. This is effective for
       stationary or slow-moving organisms like plants or coral.
           o Example: Counting plants in a grassland using 1m² quadrats.
   •   Transect Sampling:
       A line is drawn across the habitat, and organisms within a fixed distance are counted. This is used for
       organisms distributed along a gradient (e.g., vegetation along a river).
   •   Capture-Recapture (Mark-Recapture):
       Animals are captured, marked, and released. After a certain time, the area is resampled, and the ratio of
       marked to unmarked animals helps estimate population size.
           o Formula: N=M×CRN = \frac{M \times C}{R}N=RM×C, where NNN = population size, MMM
               = marked individuals, CCC = total captured, RRR = recaptured.
   •   Census Counts:
       Direct counting of all individuals in a small area or an entire population. This method is practical for
       populations in small, confined habitats.

2. Indirect Methods

   •   Sign Counts:
       Estimation based on evidence such as tracks, nests, droppings, or feeding marks.
           o Example: Counting bird nests to estimate bird abundance.
   •   Remote Sensing:
       Aerial surveys using drones, satellites, or cameras to count large populations or those in inaccessible
       areas, such as herds of wildebeest.
   •   Acoustic Surveys:
       Uses sound recordings to estimate populations, especially for vocal species like frogs or birds.
   •   Environmental DNA (eDNA):
       DNA fragments from water, soil, or air are analyzed to detect species presence and abundance.

Field Visit Report
Objective:

To analyze the abundance and diversity of aquatic species in the local ecosystem and apply population
estimation techniques for research and conservation purposes.

Location:

The field visit was conducted at Chashma Barrage Wetland, located in Punjab, Pakistan. This site is a rich
ecological zone, known for its diverse aquatic and semi-aquatic species, including fish, amphibians, and birds.

Date and Time:

The visit took place on 22nd December 2024, from 9:00 AM to 3:00 PM. This timing was chosen to cover
both morning and afternoon activity periods of aquatic organisms.

Participants:

A team of 10 students and 2 instructors from the ZOO511P course participated in this activity. All participants
were divided into groups to efficiently utilize the time and resources available.

Methods Applied:

   1. Transect Sampling:
         o A 100-meter transect line was laid along the wetland's edge. Observers counted all fish and
            amphibians visible within 2 meters on either side of the line.
         o The method allowed systematic sampling of aquatic organisms distributed along environmental
            gradients, such as vegetation density or water depth.
   2. Mark-Recapture Method:
         o Small fish were carefully captured using hand nets. Each captured individual was marked with
            a non-toxic dye on its dorsal fin and released back into the water.
         o After 24 hours, the marked area was revisited, and recaptured fish were recorded. This provided
            data for estimating population size using the Lincoln-Petersen Index formula:

                N=M*C/R Where:
                NN = Total population size,
                MM = Number of marked individuals,
                CC = Total individuals captured in the second sample,
                RR = Number of recaptured marked individuals.

   3. Environmental DNA (eDNA) Sampling:

             were filtered and sent to the lab for DNA analysis to detect species that might not be visible

   4. Species Identification and Recording:
         o Observers used field guides and pre-prepared keys to identify species based on physical
