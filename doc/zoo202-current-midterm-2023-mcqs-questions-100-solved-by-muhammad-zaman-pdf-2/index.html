                          Short Questions:
                (These are Most Repeated Questions)

   1. Reproduction and development of cephalopods
       (Cephalochordates) (Short & LONG)

Reproduction:
• Cephalochordates are dioecious.

• Gonads bulge into the atrium from the lateral body wall.

• Gametes are shed into the atrium and leave the body through the atriopore.

Development:
• External fertilization leads to a bilaterally symmetrical larva.

• Larvae are free-swimming

• But they eventually settle to the substrate before metamorphosing into adults

   2. Lateral line system (3)
• Fishes has a lateral-line system.

• The lateral-line system consists of sensory pits in the epidermis In these pits are receptors that
are stimulated by water

            Team Zoology Legends

• Lateral lines are used to detect either water currents, or a predator

• Fishes may also detect low-frequency sounds with these receptors

    3. Feeding In Enteropneusta (Acorn worms)
• Cilia and mucus assist acorn worms in feeding.

• Detritus and other particles adhere to the mucus-covered proboscis. Tracts of cilia transport
food and mucus posteriorly and ventrally.

• Ciliary tracts converge near the mouth and form a mucoid string that enters the mouth. Acorn
worms may reject some substances trapped in the mucoid string by pulling the proboscis against
the collar.
