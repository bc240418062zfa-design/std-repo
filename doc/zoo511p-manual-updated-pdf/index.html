Practical # 1
                                  Phylum Porifera
INTRODUCTION
The poriferans are commonly known as sponges. They play a crucial role in aquatic
ecosystem.

DISTINGUISHING CHARACTERS
      Simplest and primitive organisms
      First multicellular animals
      Porous body structure
      Cellular level of body organization
      Sessile and benthic organisms

GENERAL CHARACTERISTICS
1. Body Structure:

Porifera are the simplest type of multicellular animals. They are sessile and lack true
tissues and organs.

2. Porous structure:

They consist of a porous body covered in tiny openings called ostia, through which water
is drawn.

3. Water Canal System:

Water flows through the sponge's body, facilitated by specialized cells called
choanocytes which flow out through large opening called osculum.

4. Skeleton:

Skeleton consists of spongin fibres and siliceous or calcareous spicules.

5. Nutrition:

They show holozoic nutrition which involves absorbing or ingesting solid organic food
particles from water.

6. Reproduction:

                                             2

They reproduce either sexually by releasing male and female gametes into the water and
asexually by budding. They have also capability of regeneration

7. Classification:

This phylum contains three main classes;

a. Class Demospongiae
b. Class Calcarea
c. Class Hexactinellida

Examples: Spongia, Sycon and Grantia.

                                Spongia officinalis
CLASSIFICATION
Phylum: Porifera
 Class: Demospongiae
   Order: Dictyoceratida
     Family: Spongiidae

GENERAL CHARACTERISTICS
1. Spongia is commonly called as “Bath Sponge”.
2. It is light grey to black in color.
3. It grows in massive, globular lobes with fine openings which are slightly elevated and
   have cone-shaped voids (conules).
4. Oscula can either be scattered or at the tip of the lobes.
5. It has a network of canals and chambers.
6. It consists of ectosomal skeleton and choanosomal skeleton.
7. The choanocytes lining the canals create water currents that bring in food particles,
   which are then captured and digested.
8. Body is composed of spongin fibres and inclusions such as sand grains and spicules.

HABIT AND HABITAT
Spongia officinalis is typically found in shallow marine environments, attached to rocks
or other substrates. It thrives in regions with moderate water currents, allowing it to
efficiently filter water for nutrients.

                                Phylum Cnidaria
INTRODUCTION
Phylum Cnidaria (previously Phylum Coelenterata means hollow cavity) are named due
to presence of cnidocytes in their body.

DISTINGUISHING CHARACTERS
      Radially symmetrical
      Acoelomate (Absence of body cavity)
      Cnidocytes / nematocyst or stinging cells are present
      Diploblastic and tissue level of body organization
      Polymorphic; one is polyp form and other is medusa

GENERAL CHARACTERISTICS
1. Body structure:

Cnidarians are characterized by radial symmetry, which means their bodies are organized
around a central point. Some are bilateral symmetrical.

2. Body organization:

They are diploblastic and exhibit a simple body plan, with a sac-like structure known as
the gastrovascular cavity in which a jelly-like mesoglea is present.

3. Predation and Defense:

They possess specialized stinging cells called cnidocytes or nematocyst, used for both
predation and defense.

4. Polymorphism:

They are polymorphic, one is polyp form and other is medusa. Most of the species
possess alternation of generation (means change from one form to another throughout
their life cycle).

5. Nervous system:

Nervous system consists of a network of nerve cells throughout the body.

6. Reproduction:

                                            5

Reproduction occurs by both asexually and sexually.

7. Habit and Habitat:

They are either solitary or colonial lives in shallow water or in benthic region.

8. Classification:

They are classified into three classes;

       a. Class Hydrozoa
       b. Class Scyphozoa
       c. Class Anthozoa

Examples: Hydra, Physalia, Alcyonium, Aurelia, Fungia and Porpita.

                              Chrysaora fuscescens
CLASSIFICATION
Phylum: Cnidaria
 Class: Scyphozoa
   Order: Semaeostomeae
     Family: Pelagiidae

GENERAL CHARACTERISTICS
1. Chrysaora fuscescens is commonly called as “Pacific Sea Nettle”.
2. The Pacific Sea Nettle boasts a bell-shaped body with long, flowing tentacles.
3. Medusa is plate-like structure in which eight (8) lobes are present. Each lobe consists
   of a tentaculocyst.
4. Mouth is present in the center which opens into gullet.
5. The tentacles are lined with stinging cells.
6. The stinging cells, or cnidocytes are equipped with tiny harpoon-like structures that
   inject venom into prey or potential threats.

HABIT AND HABITAT
Chrysaora fuscescens is often found in the Pacific Ocean, particularly along the western
coast of North America. It inhabits both coastal and open waters, displaying its elegant
tentacles and bell.
