Lesson-01
        LANGUAGE: SOME THEORETICAL CONCEPTS

Topic-001: Defining Language
What is the significance of language in human life?
Language is essential to human existence, permeating every aspect of our
experiences and shaping our perception of the world. It is an integral part of
human life.
Who made the observation that "A definition of language is always, implicitly or
explicitly, a definition of human beings in the world"?
This observation was made by the British cultural critic, Raymond Williams.

Why is language often considered an "unknown object" even by experts in the
field?
Language is considered an "unknown object" because, despite extensive study, its
complexity and true nature remain elusive and not fully understood.
Topic-002: Some Theoretical Concepts: Language as a System
How can language be broadly categorized for understanding its relevance?
Language can be categorized into three broad conceptual vantage points:
language as a system, language as discourse, and language as ideology.
What are the core components of language as a system?
The core components of language as a system include the phonological system,
the semantic system, and the syntactic system.
What is the term for the distinct sound units that can change the meaning of a
word in a language?
The distinct sound units that can change the meaning of a word in a language are
called phonemes.
How does language as a system enable the formation of sentences and texts?
Language as a system enables language users to combine phonemes to form
words, words to form phrases, phrases to form sentences, and sentences to form
spoken or written texts following specific rules.
What concept did Chomsky introduce to explain the ability of humans to
produce an infinite number of sentences using a finite set of rules?
Chomsky introduced the concept of "Universal Grammar," which is a set of
abstract principles and parameters governing the grammatical structure of all
human languages.
What is the difference between linguistic competence and linguistic
performance, according to Chomsky?

 COMPILED BY SIR ZAHID                                                           2

Linguistic competence refers to a speaker-hearer's knowledge of their language,
while linguistic performance relates to the actual use of language in specific
situations, including the effects of memory, attention, and other factors.
Topic-003: Some Theoretical Concepts: Language as Discourse
What is the definition of discourse in linguistics?
In linguistics, discourse refers to an instance of spoken or written language that
has describable internal relationships of form and meaning, which relate
coherently to an external communicative function and audience.
Who introduced the concept of communicative competence?
Dell Hymes introduced the concept of communicative competence, which
encompasses both knowledge and ability for using language in real-life situations.
What are the components of Hymes' SPEAKING model for communicative
competence?
The components of the SPEAKING model include Setting, Participants, Ends, Act
sequence, Key, Instrumentalities, Norms, and Genre.
How does Austin define a speech act?
A speech act is defined by Austin as a combination of three components: locution
(the propositional statement), illocution (the intended meaning), and perlocution
(the expected response).
Why is context important in understanding illocutionary force?
Context is crucial in understanding illocutionary force because it determines the
meaning of a speech act. The same utterance can have different illocutionary
forces based on the situational context.
What is the main focus of discourse analysis as a field of study?
Discourse analysis focuses on studying language use in context, examining the
relationships between form and meaning, and how language is used in
communicative situations.
Topic 004: Language as an Ideology

 COMPILED BY SIR ZAHID                                                               3

What is ideology, as defined in the context of language?
Ideology is a systematic body of ideas organized from a particular point of view,
closely connected to power and domination.
How did Thompson define ideology in "Ideology and Modern Culture"?
Thompson defined ideology as "meaning in the service of power" and emphasized
that studying ideology involves understanding how meaning serves to establish
and sustain relations of domination.
According to Kroskrity, what are the dimensions of language ideologies?
Kroskrity's dimensions of language ideologies include the perception of language
and discourse tied to specific social or cultural groups, the multiplicity of
perspectives within sociocultural groups, varying degrees of awareness of local
language ideologies, and the mediation between social structures and forms of
talk.
How does Michel Foucault conceptualize discourse?
Michel Foucault sees discourse as the entire conceptual territory on which
knowledge is produced and reproduced, encompassing language and determining
what can be said, heard, silenced, accepted, or tabooed.
Topic 005: Language Teaching and Theoretical Concepts about Language
How do theories of language relate to language teaching?
Theories of language provide the foundation for understanding language and its
acquisition, which directly influences teaching methods and approaches.
What role do language acquisition theories play in language teaching?
Language acquisition theories, such as behaviorism, nativism, and social
interactionist theories, guide teachers in choosing effective teaching methods
based on how learners acquire a second language.
