                                    SHORT QUESTIONS(3)

   1. Which glassware is used to mix solutions.
Beakers and flasks are commonly used to mix solutions. Beakers are used for mixing small to
medium volumes of solutions. Flasks (especially Erlenmeyer flasks) are used for mixing larger
volumes of solutions or for heating and mixing solutions.
   2. pH of Water
The pH of pure water is 7, which is neutral. However, the pH of tap water or natural water sources can
vary slightly depending on the presence of dissolved minerals and gases.
In general, the pH of water can range from:
   -   6.5 to 8.5 for tap water
   -   6.0 to 7.0 for river water
   -   7.9 to 8.4 for seawater

   3. Explain the difference between saturated, supersaturated and
      unsaturated solution
   ❖ Unsaturated solution:
A solution that is capable of dissolving more solute at a given temperature than it already contains, is
known as unsaturated solution.
   ❖ Saturated solution:
 A saturated solution is the solution which can dissolve no more amount of the solute, at a given
temperature.
   ❖ Super saturated solution:
A solution that contains more dissolved solute than a saturated solution is called super saturated
solution.
   4. Write the difference between molarity and molality?
   ❖ Molarity (M):
Molarity or the molar concentration is the number of moles of solute dissolved per dm³ of solution.
(1 dm³ is equal to 1Liter)
 Molarity = Number of moles of solute / Volume of solution in liters
   ❖ Molality (m):
Molality is the number of moles of solute dissolved per kilogram of solvent.
   5. Define iodine test and observation at high temperature and solvents
      ?/Write observation of iodine test and what happens to the intensity
      of color when the temperature increases and and some solvent used.
   ❖ Iodine Test:
 The iodine test is mainly performed to check the presence of carbohydrates among which starch and
sugar are the main carbohydrates found in our food products.
   ❖ Observation at High Temperature:
When iodine solution is added to a sample containing starch, a blue-black color appears. The intensity
of the blue-black color produced in the iodine test decreases with increasing temperature.
   ❖ Observation with Solvents:

 The intensity of the color also decreases in the presence of water-miscible organic solvents such as
ethanol. The color may also change to a reddish-brown hue in the presence of certain solvents.
Additionally, the test cannot be performed in non-polar solvents and is affected by the presence of
solvents in general.
   6. Iodine test principle and results
   ❖ Principles:
To produce the triiodide anion (I3−), elemental iodine is dissolved in an aqueous solution of
potassium iodide. The resulting complex with starch produces an intense “blue-black” colour. The
intensity of the colour decreases with increasing temperature and with the presence of water-miscible
organic solvents such as ethanol. The test cannot be performed at very low pH due to the hydrolysis of
the starch under these conditions. It is now thought that the iodineiodide mixture combines with the
starch to form an infinite polyiodide homopolymer. This was rationalized through single crystal x-ray
crystallography and comparative Raman spectroscopy.
   ❖ Result:
Turned to blue-black on adding the iodine solution. This proves the presence of starch in the given
sample.
   7. What is the role of yeast in decomposition of ripe fruit?
Yeast plays a significant role in the decomposition of ripe fruit. As the fruit ripens, yeast feeds on the
sugars present, fermenting them into ethanol and carbon dioxide. This process breaks down the
complex organic molecules into simpler compounds, contributing to the spoilage and softening of the
fruit. As a result, the fruit becomes mushy, develops an unpleasant odor, and eventually rots. Yeast’s
role in decomposition is a natural process that recycles nutrients, returning them to the environment.
   8. Difference between Amylose & Amylopectin/What colour amylose
      give ?
Amylose is an un-branched chain polymer of D-glucose units. It gives a dark blue/black color when
iodine solution is added.
Amylopectin is a branched chain polymer of D-glucose units. It gives a reddish brown color when
iodine solution is added.
   9. Amylose structure
Amylose is a type of polysaccharide with a linear, helical structure composed of α-1,4-glycosidic
bonds. It has a high molecular weight ranging from 10^5 to 10^6 Da, making it relatively rigid and
resistant to digestion. Amylose is insoluble in water, which contributes to its functional properties in
food and industrial applications.
   10.        Structure properties of amylopectin
Amylopectin, on the other hand, is a branched, bush-like polysaccharide with both α-1,4-glycosidic
bonds and α-1,6-glycosidic branch points. Its high molecular weight ranges from 100,000 to 400,000
Da. Unlike amylose, amylopectin is soluble in water and forms a helical structure in solution. It is also
hydrolyzed by enzymes like α-amylase and β-amylase, making it an important component of starch in
plant-based foods.
   11.        What is energy storage form of glucose in body?
Glycogen is a multibranched polysaccharide of glucose that serves as a form of energy storage in
animals, fungi, and bacteria. The polysaccharide structure represents the main storage form of
glucose in the body.
   12.        Role of lipids
Lipids play a crucial role in various cellular and physiological functions. They serve as energy storage
molecules, act as structural components of cell membranes, and regulate hormone signaling.
Additionally, lipids facilitate the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins and maintain healthy skin, hair,
and brain function. Overall, lipids are essential for maintaining various cellular and physiological
processes in the body.
