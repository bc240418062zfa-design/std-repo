Lecture 1                What is ecosystem
        Ecosystem
   •    Community of living organisms in conjunction of its non-living components
   •    Non-living components
   •    Interaction as a system between both components

Population is group of individuals belonging to same species occupying same space in particular
time.

Community is group of populations occupying same space in particular time.
Control of Ecosystem
   •    External
                   Climate, topography
   •    Internal
Types of species, population size, disease, gene pool, reproduction rate. External factors are
independent of internal factors.

Biome
   •    Distinct biological communities
   •    Formed in response to specific external factors in that environment
   •    Internal factors are controlled by feedback loop

Example:
Population size is controlled by availability of resources.
   •    Ecosystem – a dynamic entity
   •    Subjected to disturbances and recovery (succession)

       Lecture 2             • Living organisms can use many forms of energy, but not heat

                                    Flow of Energy in Ecosystem

Learning Objectives
                                                                              MUHAMMAD IMRAN     1

   •   Introduction of energy dynamics in ecosystem. Use of energy by organisms. Type of
       useable energy

Energy
Energy exists in several different forms
       1. Light
       2. Chemical-bond energy
       3. Motion
       4. Heat
All organisms need energy to function

Principles
 Energy is neither created nor destroyed in the biosphere. Organisms cannot convert heat to any of
the other forms of energy. They only convert some chemical-bond or light energy to heat. Earth is an
open system having constant and equal exposure to light. Plants use light energy in photosynthesis.
They convert light energy into chemical bond energy. Animals use chemical bond energy to make
organic molecules. Light and chemical-bond energy are partially converted to heat at every step. Heat
is radiated into outer space at invisible infrared wavelengths. Earth’s incoming and outgoing flows of
radiant energy must be equal for global temperature to stay constant.

       Lecture 3             • Producers
Learning Objectives
   •    Concept of producers. Types of producers. Photosynthesis. Chemosynthesis

Autotrophs
Synthesis of the organic compounds of their bodies from inorganic precursors such as:
       - CO2, H2O, NO3
using energy from an abiotic source
          - Light
         - inorganic oxidation
           reactions

Types of Autotrophs
1. Photoautotrophs
   •    Photosynthetic organisms
   •    Examples: plants, algae, and cyanobacteria
   •    Photosynthesis
6CO2 + 6H2O —> C6H12O6 + 6O2
   •    Main input of free energy into the biosphere
2. Chemoautotrophs
   •    Occurs in dark habitats. They obtain energy by means of inorganic oxidation reactions.
        Convert toxic molicules into glucose
   Example: Microbes, mussels

       Lecture 4             • Consumers
Learning Objectives
   •    Concept of consumers. Types of consumers

Heterotrophs

                                                                            MUHAMMAD IMRAN       3

   •    Cannot synthesize organic compounds from inorganic precursors.Eat organisms from a
        different population for energy. Liberating chemical-bond energy for metabolic use in
        glycolysis, Krebs’s cycle and electron transport chain
Example: Animals

Types of Consumers
        1. Herbivore
        2. Carnivore
        3. Omnivore

   1. Herbivore
   •    Primary food source is plant-based.
   Examples: vertebrates as well as invertebrates
Digestive systems capable of handling large amounts of plant material. There are eight types of
herbivory depending upon different feeding habits.

2. Carnivore
Primary food source is animal-based.
Examples: vertebrates as well as invertebrates
   •    Digestive systems are more efficient in protein digestion.

2. Omnivore
Primary food source is both plant and animal-based.
Examples: human, bear, cockroach, chicken
   •    Digest carbohydrates, protein, fat, and fiber, and metabolize the nutrients

Types
        -   Frugivores: wolves and orangutans
        -   Insectivores: swallows and pink fairy armadillos
        -   Granivores: large ground finches and mice

Heterotrophs
Break down dead or decaying organisms. Absorb nutrients directly through chemical and biological
processes without ingestion.
Types
                               ✓ Fungi
                               ✓ Bacteria

Fungi
   •    Primary decomposer . Release digestive enzymes by their hyphae. Absorb organic matter and
        release CO2

Decomposition
   1. Autolysis
Breaking down of tissues by the body's own internal chemicals and enzymes
2. Putrefaction
   •    Breakdown of tissues by bacteria. release compounds such as cadaverine and putrescine
        causing decaying odor. Proteolysis releases nitrogen and phosphorous
   •    Carbohydrate breakdown

   Anerobic degradation of lipids. Nucleic acids and bones degradation

   •    Plays important role in nitrogen and carbon cycles
