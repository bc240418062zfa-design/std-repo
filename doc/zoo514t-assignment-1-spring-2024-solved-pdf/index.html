“DESCRIBE THE ROLE OF CARBOHYDRATES IN
THERAPEUTICS”
1. Introduction

Carbohydrates are traditionally recognized as energy sources and structural components in living
systems. Over the past decades, however, their role in therapeutics has expanded dramatically.
This “glycoscience” revolution leverages the structural diversity, biocompatibility, and unique
bioactivity of carbohydrates to design drugs, vaccines, diagnostics, and delivery platforms.
Below, we explore in detail the major categories and applications of carbohydrates in modern
therapeutics.

2. Small-Molecule Glycosides

   •   Cardiac Glycosides:
          o Examples: Digoxin, digitoxin, ouabain
          o Mechanism: Inhibit Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase in cardiac myocytes, leading to increased
              intracellular Ca²⁺ and enhanced contractility.
          o Therapeutic use: Heart failure, certain arrhythmias.
   •   Steroid Glycosides (Saponins):
          o Examples: Diosgenin derivatives, ginsenosides
          o Mechanism: Diverse—membrane cholesterol complexation, modulation of
              immune responses.
          o Therapeutic use: Anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory, anticancer adjuncts.

3. Anticoagulants and Antithrombotics

   •   Heparin and Low-Molecular-Weight Heparins (LMWHs):
          o Structure: Highly sulfated glycosaminoglycan polymers.
          o Mechanism: Potentiates antithrombin III, inactivating thrombin and factor Xa.
          o Use: Prevention and treatment of venous thromboembolism, acute coronary
             syndromes.
   •   Fondaparinux:
          o Structure: Synthetic pentasaccharide
          o Advantage: High bioavailability, predictable pharmacokinetics, reduced risk of
             heparin-induced thrombocytopenia.

4. Antibiotics and Antimicrobials

   •   Aminoglycosides:
          o Examples: Gentamicin, streptomycin, neomycin

          o Mechanism: Bind D-Ala-D-Ala termini of peptidoglycan precursors, blocking cell

          o Use: Resistant Gram-positive infections (e.g., MRSA).

   •   Glycoconjugate Vaccines:

             type b, Streptococcus pneumoniae) are conjugated to carrier proteins.

6. Drug Delivery and Controlled Release

   •   Polysaccharide Carriers:
          o Materials: Chitosan, alginate, hyaluronic acid
          o Advantages: Biodegradability, mucoadhesion, modifiable functional groups.
          o Applications: Oral insulin delivery, mucosal vaccines, targeted cancer
              therapeutics.
   •   Glycodendrimers and Glycopolymers:
          o Design: Highly branched polymers with pendant sugars
          o Use: Multivalent binding to lectins on target cells (e.g., tumor endothelia),
              enhancing uptake and specificity.

7. Glycoengineering in Biologic Drugs

   •   Therapeutic Proteins:
          o Role of Glycosylation: Modulates stability, solubility, immunogenicity, and half-
             life (e.g., erythropoietin, monoclonal antibodies).
          o Strategies: Engineered cell lines or in vitro enzymatic remodeling to achieve
             optimal glycan patterns.
   •   Biosimilars and Biobetters:
          o Objective: Match or improve glycosylation profiles of originator biologics to
             preserve efficacy and reduce adverse reactions.

8. Diagnostics and Molecular Probes

   •   Lectin-Based Assays:
          o Principle: Lectins bind specific carbohydrate motifs on cells or glycoproteins.
          o Applications: Cancer biomarker detection (e.g., altered sialylation patterns),
              pathogen identification.
   •   Glycan Microarrays:
          o Utility: High-throughput screening of glycan–protein interactions, guiding drug
              discovery and vaccine design.

9. Challenges and Future Directions

   •   Complexity of Glycan Structures: Unlike nucleic acids or proteins, carbohydrates
       exhibit branching and stereochemical diversity, complicating synthesis and analysis.
   •   Analytical Tools: Advances in mass spectrometry and bioinformatics are essential to
       decode glycomes and identify therapeutic targets.
   •   Synthetic Accessibility: Automated oligosaccharide synthesizers and novel
       chemoenzymatic methods are lowering barriers to tailor-made glycomolecules.
   •   Precision Medicine: Personalized glycomic profiling may enable patient-specific
       therapies, particularly in oncology and autoimmunity.
