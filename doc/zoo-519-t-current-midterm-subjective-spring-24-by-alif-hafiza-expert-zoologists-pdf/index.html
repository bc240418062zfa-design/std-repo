1-Cell membrane permeability (2 times)
Selective Permeability
• Structural features of plasma membrane (PM) confer it with selective permeability.
• PM regulates movement of substances across it.
• Fundamental to the functioning of living cells and maintenance of intracellular physiological
conditions.
Membrane Permeability For Various Substances
• Varies greatly with type and size of molecules.
Non-Polar, Lipid-Soluble Substances
• Cross passively by dissolving in the lipid bilayer of membrane.
• Rate of penetration depends on lipid solubility and size of molecules.
Polar Substances & Ions
• Have difficulty in passing through because of the hydrophobic interior of lipid bilayer.
Macromolecules
• Cannot cross plasma membrane due to size.

2-Membrane flux
• “The amount of solute that passes through a unit area of membrane every second in one
direction”.
• If a solute occurs on both sides of a membrane, it exhibits a flux in each direction.
• Flux in one direction is independent of the flux in opposite direction.
• If influx and efflux are equal, net flux is zero.
• If flux is greater in one direction, then net flux is the difference between the two fluxes.
3-What are steroid hormones? How are these prepared? (7 times)
• Steroid hormones are synthesized from cholesterol.
• They consist of four fused carbon rings including three cyclohexyl rings and one cyclopentyl
ring combined into a single structure.
• Examples: hormones of adrenal cortex (cortisol and aldosterone) hormones of ovaries
(estrogen and progesterone) and hormone of testes (testosterone).
4-Differentiate between protein/peptide and amine hormones. (6 times)
Peptide and Protein Hormones
• Composed of amino acids.
• They include very small peptides as well as large protein molecules.
• e.g. thyrotropin-releasing hormone, TRH, has only 3 amino acids and GH and prolactin with
almost 200 amino acids.
Amine Hormones
• They are small molecules synthesized from a single amino acid tyrosine.
• They include:
• Epinephrine
• Norepinephrine
• Thyroxine
• Triiodothyronine
5-What is sarcomere?
• The arrangement of thick and thin filaments creates a pattern of repeating light and dark
bands.
• This pattern gives a striped appearance to the muscle cell.

• Each repeating unit is called a sarcomere and is the basic functional contractile unit of the
muscle.
• Each dark band in the sarcomere is called A band.
• It is anisotropic i.e. it polarizes visible light.
• It has a lighter stripe in its center called H-zone.
• H-zone is bisected by a dark line called M-line.
6- Properties of hormones (12 times)
• They act only on specific target cells or tissues.
• Hormonal action depend on the type of receptor.
• A hormone comes into contact with all tissues in the body during transport through blood,
however, only the cells containing specific receptors are affected by it.
• The action of a hormone depends on the nature of enzyme cascade linked to the receptor.
• It also depends on the effector molecules expressed in a tissue.
• For these reasons, a hormone can act on two or more different types of tissues and generate
different types of responses.
• Hormones are active at very low concentration.
• The amount of hormone produced by an endocrine gland is small.
• It is further diluted in the blood and interstitial fluid.
• The available conc. for the target cell lies between 10-8 to 10-12 M and the hormone is still
effective.
7- Nissl's bodies (2 times)
• Consist of a group of ribosomes and rough endoplasmic reticulum
• Are associated with protein synthesis
8-Cardiac muscles
• Found only in the heart.
• Striated muscles
• Share many characteristics with
skeletal muscle but differ in several important ways.
• Cardiac muscle cells (myocyte) are mononucleate.
• Skeletal muscle cells are multinucleate.
• Cardiac fibers innervated only diffusely by both:

• Excitatory sympathetic neurons
• Inhibitory parasympathetic neurons
• Skeletal muscle fibers are individually innervated by an excitatory motor axon.
• Cardiac neural innervations are modulatory only.
• Contractions are induced by electrical activity of pacemaker.
• They don’t produce discrete PSP.
• They only increase or decrease the strength of myogenic contractions.
• Cardiac muscle cells are connected electrically by intercalated disks.
• AP initiated in the pacemaker region spreads rapidly from cell to cell, through fast-conducting
pathways to all muscle cells.
9- Release of neurotransmitters
• APs in presynaptic neuron cause release of neurotrans-mitter molecules at axon terminals.
• Release occurs by exocytosis into the synaptic cleft through specialized sites on the
membrane.
10- Neurotransmitters and its types (3 times)
Amino Acids:Simple organic compounds that act as neurotransmitters.
Examples:
   - Glutamate:Primary excitatory neurotransmitter.
   - GABA:Primary inhibitory neurotransmitter.
Monoamines:Derived from amino acids, affecting mood, arousal, and emotion.
Examples:
   - Dopamine: Involved in motivation and reward.
   - Serotonin: Regulates mood, appetite, and sleep.
Acetylcholine: Involved in muscle activation, memory, and learning.
Examples:
   - Acetylcholine: Role in muscle contraction.
   - Nicotine: Binds to acetylcholine receptors.
Neuropeptides:Larger molecules that act as neurotransmitters or neuromodulators.
Examples:
   - Substance P: Involved in pain perception.

   - Endorphins: Natural painkillers and mood enhancers.
Purines: Neurotransmitters derived from ATP.
Examples:
   -Adenosine: Regulates sleep and arousal.
   - ATP: Acts as a neurotransmitter in some pathways.
