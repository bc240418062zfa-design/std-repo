Q. Define Context

Context refers to the circumstances surrounding a discourse, including the linguistic, situational,
social, and cultural background that influences meaning and interpretation.

Q. Definition of ideology by Althusser

Althusser defined ideology as “representing the imaginary relationship of individuals to their real
conditions of existence” which “has a material existence”.

Q. Define Conventional Implicature and Particularized Implicature

      Conventional Implicature: These do not require specific context and are derived from
       the conventional meaning of words. Examples include ‘but’ (contrast), ‘even’
       (unexpectedness), and ‘yet’ (future change).
      Particularized Implicature: These depend on the context of the conversation. The
       meaning is inferred based on situational factors and relevance.

Q. Objectives of English-Urdu Newspapers

      To explore the significance of headlines in discourse and society.
      To investigate how headlines represent the same event differently.
      To analyze how headlines influence ideological construction.
      To identify linguistic similarities and differences among various newspapers.

Q. Three Important Areas of Media CDA Under Research

      Structural conventions of media texts and language
      Representation and normalization of ideologies in media
      Sociological implications of media discourses, including capitalism, neoliberalism, and
       racism.

Q. How Ethnographic Discourse Analysis Contributes to CDA

Ethnographic discourse analysis provides a contextualized understanding of discourse by
analyzing how language is used in specific social and cultural contexts. It helps in revealing
power dynamics and ideological structures in discourse.

Q. Foucault’s Theory of Power (in 4-5 lines)

Foucault’s theory of power suggests that power is not only centralized but also widely dispersed
across social institutions and practices. It operates through discourse and knowledge, shaping
individuals' behavior. Power is both restrictive and productive, influencing norms and self-
regulation. Resistance and counter-resistance are inherent in power relations.

Q. Cook’s View on Advertising and CDA

Cook views advertising as a persuasive discourse that shapes consumer behavior and ideologies.
CDA examines how advertisements use linguistic and visual strategies to create implicit
meanings and influence public perceptions.

Q. Three Characteristics of Corpus Analysis

      It involves analyzing large text corpora.
      It uses computational tools to examine linguistic patterns.
      It helps in identifying discourse trends over time.

Q. Four Analytical Types of Media Studies in CDA

      Structural analysis of media language
      Representation of social identities
      Ideological functions of media discourse
      Sociological implications of media messages

Q. Key Topics in Television and Discourse Studies

      Representation of gender, race, and class
      Ideological construction in news and entertainment
      Media framing and audience interpretation

Q. Positioning and Subjectivity in Media Studies and CDA

Positioning refers to how individuals are placed within discourse, influencing their identity and
perspectives. Subjectivity is the way media constructs individual identities through discourse,
shaping public perceptions and ideological stances

of political aims. It is characterized by its deliberate targeting of non-combatants and its intent to
create fear and coerce governments or societies to achieve specific ideological goals.
