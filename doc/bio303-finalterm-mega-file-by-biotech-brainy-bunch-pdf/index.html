1. Glycolysis?
Glycolysis is a cytoplasmic pathway which breaks down glucose into two three-carbon
compounds and generates energy. Glucose is trapped by phosphorylation, with the help of the
enzyme hexokinase. Adenosine triphosphate (ATP) is used in this reaction and the product,
glucose-6-P, inhibits hexokinase.

4. Source of fatty acids in cells?
To utilize acetate from fat for biosynthesis of carbohydrates, the glyoxylate cycle, whose initial
reactions are identical to the TCA cycle, is used. This pathway thus allows cells to obtain energy
from fat. The oxaloacetate can be converted into Phosphoenolpyruvate, which is the product of
phosphoenol pyruvate carboxykinase, the first enzyme in gluconeogenesis.

5. Advantages of using triacylglycerol as storage source?
1. Fats are highly reduced hydrocarbons with a large energy of complete oxidation (38 kJ/g)
more than twice that for the same weight of carbohydrate or protein.
2. Fats are chemically inert (triglycerides or neutral fats)
that make them especially suitable as storage fuels so they can be stored without fear of
unfavorable reactions.
3. Fats are insoluble molecules that aggregate into droplets.

8. How malate synthase throw light on evolutionary history of glyoxylate?
In recent years, the detection of malate synthase (MS) and isocitrate lyase (ICL), key enzymes
involved in the glyoxylate cycle, in some animal tissue has raised questions regarding the
evolutionary relationship of enzymes in bacteria and animals and suggests that animals encode
alternative enzymes of the cycle that differ in function from known MS and ICL in non-
metazoan species. Isocitrate lyase and malate synthase are unique to the glyoxylate cycle. Notice
that two acetyl groups (light red) enter the cycle and four carbons leave as succinate (blue).
Glyoxylate is subsequently combined with acetyl-CoA to produce malate, catalyzed by malate
synthase. Malate is also formed in parallel from succinate by the action of succinate
dehydrogenase and fumarase.

9. Enzyme kinetics when enzyme concentration is less than substrate?
A numerically large (high) Km reflects a low affinity of enzyme for substrate because a high
concentration of substrate is needed to half-saturate the enzyme.

10. Define pH? Derived its equation. Explain in terms of water pH at 25℃?
pH: The pH of a solution is defined as the logarithm to the base 10 of the reciprocal of the [H+],
i. e, the negative logarithm of the [H+].
Equation:
Designates the H+ and OH- Concentrations
pH = log 1/[H+]
pH = -log[H+]
For each pH unit less than 7.0, the [H+] is increased tenfold.
for each pH unit above 7.0, it is decreased tenfold.
Water pH at 25℃:
The pH of water at 25°C, in which H+ and OH– ions are present in equal numbers, is 7.0
pH = -log[1 x 10-7]= 7
