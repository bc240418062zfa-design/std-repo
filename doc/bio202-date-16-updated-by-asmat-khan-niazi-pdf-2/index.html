Q.1 What is Bohr effect?
 Bohr effect is a phenomenon first described in 1904 by the
Danish physiologist Christian Bohr. Hemoglobin's oxygen
binding affinity is inversely related both to acidity and to the
concentration of carbon dioxide. it also means the reciprocal
coupling of proton and oxygen binding termed as Bohr effect.
Q.2 Binding of oxygen with hemoglobin at sea level as well as
at high altitude?
The amount of hemoglobin in blood increases at high altitude.
This is one of the best-known features for hemoglobin.
Increasing the amount of hemoglobin in the blood increases
the amount of oxygen that can be carried. so, at high altitudes
the atmospheric pressure as well as the partial pressure of
oxygen is lower than that at the ground level. This leads to low
concentration of the oxygen in the blood. For this reason,
binding affinity decreases.

Q.3 What information is observed from titration curve of
glycine?
The titration curve of Glycine has two regions of buffering
power. At pKa 2.34, glycine is a good buffer near this Ph. The
other buffering zone is centered on a pH of 9.60. Glycine is not
a good buffer at the pH of intracellular fluid (or) blood, about
7.4.

Q.4 Differentiate between enantiomer and diastereomer?
 Enantiomers are the chiral molecules that are mirror images of
one another and are not superimposable. Diastereomers are
the stereomer compounds with molecules that are not
mirrored images of one another and that are not
superimposable

Q.5What are alpha beta and gamma carbon?
 The alpha carbon in organic molecules refers to the first carbon
atom that attaches to a functional group, such as a carbonyl.
The second carbon atom is called the beta carbon and the
carbon present next to the beta carbon is the gamma carbon.

Q.6 How A and B antigen differ from O antigen?

Blood group A – has A antigens on the red blood cells with anti-
B antibodies in the plasma. blood group B – has B antigens with
anti-A antibodies in the plasma. ... blood group AB – has both A
and B antigen and have no antibodies A and B. Blood group O
has no antigens, but they contain both anti-A and anti-B
antibodies in the plasma.
Q.7 What is the structure of amino acid?
Each amino acid has the same fundamental structure, which
consists of a central carbon atom, also known as the alpha (α)
carbon, bonded to an amino group (NH2), a carboxyl group
(COOH), and to a hydrogen atom. ... Every amino acid also has
another atom or group of atoms bonded to the central atom
known as the R group.
Q.8 heme interaction.
Heme synthesis starts in mitochondria with the condensation of
succinyl-CoA with the amino acid glycine, activated by pyridoxal
phosphate. ALA synthase is the rate-limiting enzyme of heme
synthesis.                                           Haem-haem
interaction consists of a change of tension at the haems, caused
by a transition between two alternative quaternary structures
of the protein. Dr Perutz describes how spin changes that
accompany reaction with ligands alter the oxygen affinity of the
haems.the cooperative interaction between dioxygen binding
sites that occurs when dioxygen binds to hemoglobin; as
oxygenation proceeds, the combination of additional dioxygen

Q.9 Selenocysteine:
 the 21st amino acid, exists naturally in all kingdoms of life as
the defining entity of seleno proteins.
 Functions: Selenocysteine is an analogue of cysteine (Figure
3.5). In place of a sulfur atom in the cysteine, selenocysteine
contains selenium (Se). Several human proteins and enzymes
are selenoproteins. Selenocysteine is located in the active sites
of enzymes that participate in oxidation–reduction reactions.
Q.10 What is fibrinogen?
 Fibrinogen is a glycoprotein complex, made in the liver, that
circulates in the blood of all vertebrates. During tissue and
vascular injury, it is converted enzymatically by thrombin to
fibrin and then to a fibrin-based blood clot. Fibrin clots function
primarily to occlude blood vessels to stop bleeding.
Q.11 What is the function of sorbitol?
 Sorbitol is a sugar alcohol found in fruits and plants with
diuretic,      laxative        and         cathartic      property.
Unabsorbed sorbitol retains water in the large intestine
through osmotic pressure thereby stimulating peristalsis of the
intestine and exerting its diuretic, laxative and cathartic effect.
Sorbitol is also used as sweetener.
Q.12 What is the sucrase location and function?

 The sucrase-isomaltose enzyme is found on the surface of the
intestinal epithelial cells, which are cells that line the walls of
the intestine. These cells have fingerlike projections called
microvilli that absorb nutrients from food as it passes through
the intestine

Q.14 Domain in tertiary structure.
The tertiary structure of large complex protein is often
described in term of physically independent region called
structural domain. A domain is a section of protein structure
sufficient to perform a particular chemical or physical task such
as binding of substrate or another ligand.
