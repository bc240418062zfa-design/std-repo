Lesson 01

                           What is Morphology?
Topic 01: Introduction

What does morphology study?

Morphology studies form or forms, specifically in linguistics, it refers to the

internal structure of words.

What are the goals of morphological analysis?

The goals of morphological analysis include understanding how words are formed

and their grammatical properties.

What is the place of morphology in the general theory of grammar?

Morphology is a distinct component of languages or grammars within the broader

field of linguistics.

What is the origin of the term "morphology"?

The term "morphology" has its roots in the Greek word "morph," meaning 'shape'

or 'form.'

Topic 02: Morpheme

What is a morpheme?

A morpheme is the smallest linguistic unit with a grammatical function, and it can

be a whole word or a meaningful part of a word.

How can morphemes be defined in terms of sound and meaning?

Morphemes can be defined as pairings between sound and meaning, which may

not always have a concrete or continuous form.

What is the difference between a stem and a root in morphology?

A stem is a base unit to which other morphological pieces are attached, while a

root is a morphologically simple unit that constitutes the core of a word.

Topic 03: Morphology in Action

Give examples of novel words and word play.

Novel words and word play involve creating new words or variations using

existing word pieces, such as "unbreak" and "uncry."

Topic 04: Abstract Morphological Facts

What is an example of a language with no morphological plural?

Indonesian and Japanese are examples of languages with no morphological plural

forms.

Topic 05: Background & Beliefs

How do linguists differentiate between languages and Language?

Linguists differentiate between individual languages (small-l language) and

Language in general (capital-L Language).

What foundational belief do linguists hold about morphology?

Linguists believe that morphology is a distinct component of languages or

grammars.

What methodological attitude do linguists adopt, according to the lesson?

Linguists adopt an attitude of skeptical realism and a "anything goes" approach to

linguistics, not being tied to specific theories.

Topic: 06: Intro to Morphological Analysis

What are the two basic approaches in morphological analysis?

The two basic approaches in morphological analysis are analysis and synthesis.

What is Principle 1 in morphological analysis?

Principle 1 states that forms with the same meaning and the same sound shape in

all their occurrences are instances of the same morpheme.

Explain Principle 3 in morphological analysis.

Principle 3 states that not all morphemes are segmental, meaning some morphemes

may not be composed of individual segments or sounds.

What is the significance of Principle 4 in morphological analysis?

Principle 4 states that a morpheme may have zero as one of its allomorphs

provided it has a non-zero allomorph, emphasizing the variation in how

morphemes can be realized.

Can you explain Principle 1 in morphological analysis?

Principle 1 in morphological analysis states that forms with the same meaning and

the same sound shape in all their occurrences are instances of the same morpheme.

In other words, if two forms have identical meanings and sound the same

whenever they appear, they can be considered as manifestations of a single

morpheme.

What is Principle 4 in morphological analysis, and why is it significant?

Answer 3: Principle 4 in morphological analysis states that a morpheme may have

zero as one of its allomorphs provided it has a non-zero allomorph. This principle

is significant because it acknowledges that morphemes can have variations,

including a zero realization, depending on the context. It helps us understand the

flexibility and complexity of morphological analysis.

Topic: 07: What is a Word

How do linguists define a word?

Various ways to define a word exist, but there is no entirely satisfactory definition.

What is the significance of lexical categories like noun, verb, adjective, and

adverb?

Lexical categories tell us how a word is generally used in a sentence. For example,

a noun can be the subject of a sentence, while a verb cannot.

Give an example of a sentence where identical-sounding words belong to

multiple categories.

"MINERS REFUSE TO WORK AFTER DEATH" is an example where identical-

sounding words belong to multiple categories. "REFUSE" can be both a noun and

a verb.

Explain the significance of lexical categories like noun, verb, adjective, and

adverb.

Lexical categories, such as noun, verb, adjective, and adverb, are labels used by

linguists to describe how a word is generally used in a sentence. For example, a

noun typically functions as the subject of a sentence, while a verb indicates an

action. These categories help linguists analyze sentence structure and understand

the roles different words play in communication.

Topic: 08: Types of Words

What is the difference between content words and function words?

Content words (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs) carry meaning, while function

words (pronouns, prepositions, etc.) serve grammatical purposes.
