Question 1: Explain with an example that as the size decreases, its surface area to volume
(S/V) ratio increases. (5 Marks)

Answer:

   •   For a cube of side length L:
       • Surface area (SA) = 6 × L × L
       • Volume (V) = L × L × L
       • Therefore S/V = (6 × L^2) / (L^3) = 6 / L
   •   As L gets smaller, 6/L gets larger.
   •   Example:
       • Cube with side = 2 mm
       – SA = 6 × 2 × 2 = 24 mm²
       – V = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 mm³
       – S/V = 24 / 8 = 3 per mm
       • Cube with side = 1 mm
       – SA = 6 × 1 × 1 = 6 mm²
       – V = 1 × 1 × 1 = 1 mm³
       – S/V = 6 / 1 = 6 per mm
   •   Halving the side length doubles the S/V ratio (from 3 to 6).
   •   In biology, smaller cells can exchange nutrients and wastes more quickly because they
       have a higher surface area relative to their volume.

Question 2: What is quorum sensing? (5 Marks)

Answer:

   •   Quorum sensing is a way bacteria “talk” to each other by releasing and sensing small
       molecules called autoinducers.
   •   Steps:
          1. Each cell makes and secretes autoinducers at a low rate.
          2. As the number of cells grows, autoinducer concentration outside the cells rises.
          3. When the concentration reaches a threshold, autoinducers bind to receptor
              proteins in or on bacterial cells.
          4. The receptor-autoinducer complex turns on or off specific genes across the
              population.
