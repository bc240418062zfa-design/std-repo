Key Concepts / Terms
1. Lenneberg

Lenneberg (1967) argued that language is a biologically triggered behavior and proposed the
Critical Period Hypothesis, stating that language must be acquired before puberty.

2. Competence vs Acquisition – What is difficult to measure?

Competence (knowledge of language) is difficult to measure directly because it exists in the
mind, while acquisition is observed through language performance.

3. Structuralism

A linguistic approach that views language as a system of patterns and structures learned through
practice and habit formation.

4. UG is concerned with Competence

Universal Grammar focuses on knowledge of language (competence) rather than language use
(performance).

5. Textual Orientation

Studies how vocabulary and grammar organize discourse and create cohesion across sentences
and texts.

6. Salience / Perceptual Saliency

The idea that learners notice and learn linguistic forms that are more noticeable or prominent in
the input.

7. Teachability Hypothesis

Pienemann's theory stating that instruction is effective only when learners are developmentally
ready for the next stage. Stages cannot be skipped.

8. Acculturation / Acculturation Model

Schumann's model proposes that SLA success depends on how closely learners adapt to the
target language community socially and psychologically.

9. CDS

CDS = Child-Directed Speech, the special speech style adults use when talking to children. It
may facilitate language acquisition.

10. Psycholinguistics Perspective on Interaction

Interaction helps learners process input, negotiate meaning, notice gaps in knowledge, and
facilitate language development.

11. Input Hypothesis Critique

      Vague and imprecise.
      Difficult to test empirically.
      Does not explain internal acquisition processes clearly.

12. Negative Evidence

Information showing learners what is incorrect in a language, often through feedback, correction,
or recasts.

13. Fossilization

The persistence of non-native language forms despite extensive exposure and learning
opportunities.

14. Affective Filter Hypothesis (Krashen)

Learners with low anxiety, high motivation, and positive attitudes acquire language more
effectively because the affective filter is low.

15. Long's Interaction Hypothesis

Language learning is facilitated through interaction and negotiation of meaning during
communication.

16. Anderson ACT Model

Learning moves through three stages:

   1. Cognitive Stage
   2. Associative Stage
   3. Autonomous Stage

17. Specific Language Impairment (SLI)

A language disorder where children experience language difficulties despite normal intelligence.

1. Short Note on Nature vs Nurture Debate
The Nature vs Nurture debate concerns whether language acquisition results primarily from
innate biological abilities (nature) or environmental experience and learning (nurture). Nativists
such as Chomsky argue that humans possess an inborn language faculty, whereas behaviorists
emphasize learning through environmental input and reinforcement. Modern views generally
recognize contributions from both biological and environmental factors.
