Note on barking deer:/ description of barking deer.
It is among the smallest deer species. It is an omnivore and eats grass, fruit, shoots, seeds, bird
eggs, and small animals, and occasionally scavenges on carrion. Its calls sound like barking,
often when frightened by a predator, hence the common name "barking deer".
Feeding habit:Diet includes grasses, low growing leaves, tender shoots and fruits.
Native: Brunei Darussalam; Indonesia; Malaysia; Thailand
Threats: Illegal hunting and poaching Hunting and poaching was assessed as a serious threat to
Barking deer in PLNP and surrounding areas (Choch).

What is the bobcat and what are the threats to the bobcats: /Note on Lynx
Rufu/ characteristics of Lynx Rufu.
IUCN Red list Threat Status: Least concern
Bobcats (Lynx rufus) may look cute and fluffy, but don't be fooled into thinking they're anything
like a house cat. One of the four species of lynx, these wildcats are around twice the size of the
average domestic cat and are fierce predators. Found throughout much of North America, they
live in a diverse range of habitats. Bobcats got their names because of their tails. Though many
felines have long, sinuous tails, an adult bobcat's averages just 6 to 7 inches in length; the word
bobcat is a reference to this stubby appendage.
Threats: As solitary and far-ranging mammals, interactions between humans and bobcats are
rare, but humans are the bobcat's largest threat. Bobcats need large areas of interconnected wild
lands to thrive. Land development, over-hunting and trapping bobcats for their fur are just a few
reasons bobcat populations can decline.

Native habitat of indian pangolin:
Indian Pangolin is widely distributed in India, except the arid region, high Himalayas and the

Pakistan, Nepal and Sri Lanka.
