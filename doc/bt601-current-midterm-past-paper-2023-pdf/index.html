            2. Protein and glycoprotein encoded by orthomyxovirus
Orthomyxoviridae proteins?

   •    Hemagglutinin (HA): A surface glycoprotein responsible for virus attachment and entry into host
        cells.
   •     Neuraminidase (NA): A surface glycoprotein that cuts sialic acid from host-cell membranes, so
        helping the release of new virions from infected cells and the spread of the virus throughout an
        infected host.
   •    Matrix protein (M1): A protein that links the viral envelope (lipid bilayer) to the viral
        ribonucleoproteins (RNPs) and mediates viral gathering and budding.
   •     Nucleoprotein (NP): A protein that complexes with viral RNA to form the viral RNPs
        responsible for viral genome replication and transcription.
   •    RNA polymerase: A trimeric complex composed of three viral proteins (PB1, PB2, and PA) that
        catalyze viral RNA synthesis (replication and transcription) in the host cell nucleus.

             4. Scanning electron microscope and transmission electron microscope
                 Microscopy? 5 Marks
    •    SEM An electron gun produces a finely focused beam of electrons called the primary electron
         beam. These electrons pass through electromagnetic lenses and are directed over the surface
         of the specimen. The primary electron beam hits electrons out of the surface of the specimen,
         and the secondary electrons thus produced are transmitted to an electron collector, amplified,
         and used to produce an image on a viewing screen or photographic plate. The image is called a
         scanning electron micrograph.
    •    TEM Focused beam of electrons from an electron gun passes through the ultrathin section of
         the specimen. Beam is focused on a small area of the specimen by an electromagnetic
         condenser lens. Instead of being placed on a glass slide, the specimen is usually placed on a
         copper mesh grid

            7. Different Segments of the Bunya virus genome in terms of proteins they encode?(3)

Bunyavirus genomes consist of three RNA segments called large, medium, and small (L, M, and S,
respectively) that encode four structural proteins (RNA polymerase or L protein; two glycoproteins, Gn
and Gc; and nucleocapsid protein, N), as well as one or two nonstructural proteins (NSm and NSs).

            8. What are effects of attack of virus on host cell? (3)
When viruses attack host cells, they replicate inside the host to produce new viral particles and use the
host’s metabolism, which can damage or destroy the host cell. The process can start host defense
mechanisms that may cause tissue damage, causal to the symptoms of the disease. Consequently, the
damage can cause pathological effects, which could result in inflammation, tissue damage, cell death,
and organ dysfunction, having grave implications for the host’s health.
