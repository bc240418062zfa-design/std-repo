Mycoremediation
Process
Mycoremediation utilizes fungi for bioremediation, specifically their ability to
break down pollutants and contaminants. This process involves introducing
fungi to contaminated environments, where they can degrade harmful
substances.

Fungi secrete enzymes and other compounds that can effectively remove
toxins, heavy metals, and other pollutants from soil, water, and air. The
breakdown of these pollutants can lead to cleaner and safer environments.

Oil Spill Cleanup
Example
Bioremediation plays a crucial role in cleaning up oil spills. Microorganisms,
like bacteria and fungi, break down hydrocarbons in crude oil, converting
them into less harmful substances.

The use of bioremediation techniques has been effective in restoring
ecosystems damaged by oil spills.

Plastic Degradation
                      Bioremediation also holds potential for degrading plastic waste,
                      a major environmental concern. Specific microorganisms have
                      been identified that can break down plastic polymers into
                      smaller, less harmful compounds.

                      This biodegradation process involves enzymatic activity, where
                      microorganisms utilize plastic as a carbon source for growth
                      and energy. While research is ongoing, bioremediation has the
                      potential to provide sustainable solutions for plastic pollution.
