Explain and Name the types of four stages insect larvae.
About 75% of all insect species go through the four stages of complete metamorphosis - egg,
larva, pupa, and adult. The larva is a specialized feeding stage that looks very different from the
adult.
1) The Egg:Majority of insects begin their life in fertilized eggs. Egg laying amongst insects
depends on the species it belongs to, for example, cockroaches or mantids lay their eggs in a
bunch attached together that forms the ootheca or egg packet.
2) The Larvae:Insect larvae have several distinct ways of hatching. While caterpillar larvae bite
their way out of the eggs during the hatching process. The insect larva undergoes several stages
of molting where it sheds old cuticles multiple times to get new cuticles. Insect larvae vary in
shape depending on the species, therefore they are divided into five kinds. The caterpillar-like
larvae are eruciform, the maggot-like larvae are vermiform, the wire worm-like larvae are
elateriform, the grub-like larvae are scarabaeiform and the long, elongated larvae are
campodeiform. These larvae then transform into pupa and enter the next stage.
3) The Pupa:During this stage, the pupa is mostly resting and inactive. The larval tissues
collapse and reorganize the adult form of the pupa. After this process is over, the pupa molts and
emerges out as an adult with a pair of wings. In the pupa stage, the wings develop into its
complete form and the adult insect enters the world.
4) The Adult:Adult insects have tactile hair, which is found on the antennae, legs, torsal regions
of the body, and helps in signaling the insect to its external environment. Tactile hair covers the
entire surface of an insect. In this stage, the adult insect is sexually mature and spends its time
mostly reproducing. The mating in adult insects vary depending upon the species.

Name the parts of thorax and abdomen of insect.
Thorax:

The thorax, comprises three large segments:
    Prothorax (anterior most segment),
    Mesothorax (middle), and
    Metathorax (posterior).
Abdomen:
The basic number of segments in the abdomen is eleven plus the telson which bears the anus.
Each segment of the abdomen consists of a
    Dorsal sclerite, the tergum, and a
    Ventral sclerite, the sternum,
These joined to one another laterally by a pleural membrane. On the abdomen are various organs
such as the ovipositor, sting, cerci and styli.

How allergy is caused?
Allergy (hay fever, asthma, etc.) are frequently caused by exposure of susceptible persons to
dried insect scales and hairs.

Define disease with an example:
Any harmful deviation from the normal structural or functional state of an organism is called
Disease.
Example: the Yellow Fever and the Malaria is the best examples of the disease.

What are tracheal gills?
Insects which in their early stage of live in water cannot, of course, breathe air into their bodies
through spiracles during that period of their live. There are enclosed in such cases and the animal
obtains and usually through special structures called tracheal gills.

What are the Different characteristics to identify the insect./ characteristics of
insects.
1= Bilaterally symmetrical. 2= Consisting of a series of segments. (a) Head (b) thorax (c)
abdomen. 3= The animal is covered by a Shell like skeleton present outside the body, horny in
nature. 4= Three pairs of jointed legs attached to the thorax. 5= Pair of antenna. 6= Mouth parts.
7= Two pair of wings. 8= Breathing through Air tubes. 9= Reproductive organs present near the
hinder end of the body.

What are the Insect-borne diseases:
Insect-borne diseases are viral and bacterial illnesses from insect (bug) bites.
    mosquitoes are known for spreading the Zika virus, Yellow Fever, and Malaria.
    Malaria (protozoan): Anopheles species of mosquito.
    Lymphatic filariasis (nematode worm): Culex, Anopheles, Aedes species of mosquito.
    Dengue (virus): Aedes species of mosquito.
    Leishmaniasis (protozoan): mainly Phlebotomus species of sandfly.

Types of insect head:
Based on the inclination of long axis of the head and orientation of mouth parts there are three
types of insect heads.
    Hypognathous (Hypo – Below: Gnathous – Jaw )
    Prognathous : (Pro – infront: Gnathous – Jaw )
    Opisthognathous : (Opistho – behind: Gnathous – Jaw )

What are the Four types of larvae:

1= Larvae of butterflies and moths are commonly called caterpillars, 2= Those of beetles are
usually called grubs, 3= Those of flies are called maggots, 4= Larvae found boring in wood,
however, whether they will become moths, beetles or other insects, are uniformly called borers.

Describe nematode disease and mention its vector name and its pathogen?
A number of genera and species of nematodes are highly damaging to a great range of hosts,
including foliage plants, agronomic and vegetable crops, fruit and nut trees, turfgrass, and forest
trees. Some of the most damaging nematodes are: Root knot (Meloidogyne spp.); Cyst
(Heterodera and Globodera spp.); Root lesion (Pratylenchus spp.); Spiral (Helicotylenchus spp.);
Burrowing (Radopholus similis); Bulb and stem (Ditylenchus dipsaci); Reniform (Rotylenchulus
reniformis); Dagger (Xiphinema spp.); Bud and leaf (Aphelenchoides spp.); and Pine Wilt
Disease (Bursaphelenchus xylophilus). The only known nematode vectors are in the genera
Xiphinema and Longidorus.

Differentiate between pulivillus, pulvilli, and epodium ?
Pulivillus: a soft, padlike structure located at the base of each claw on the feet of certain insects.
Pulvilli: Pulvilli are lobes or padds that are located between the tarsal claws of many insects.
Empodium: Between the pulvilli there is often a spine that is called the empodium.
