Question:

Biselining a requirement affects its tractability,do you agree or not.in very short word

Yes, I agree.

Little bit explain

Certainly. "Biselining" refers to making small, undocumented changes to a requirement without
updating its formal documentation. This can affect the requirement's traceability, which is the ability
to track the development and testing of the requirement through various stages of a project.

When requirements are changed without proper documentation, it becomes difficult to trace back
how and why the requirement was altered. This can lead to confusion, misinterpretation, and
challenges in ensuring that the final product aligns with the original intention. Properly maintaining
traceability is important for understanding the rationale behind requirements and ensuring that the
final product meets the intended goals.

Answer:

Failure cost has two components: internal failure cost and external failure cost. Internal failure cost
requires rework, repair, and failure mode analysis. On the other hand, external failure cost involves
cost for complaint resolution, product return and replacement, help-line support, warranty work,
and law suits.
