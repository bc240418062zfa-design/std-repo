                                 Phylum Protozoa
INTRODUCTION
The protozoans are single-celled eukaryotic organisms which exhibit incredible diversity
in terms of form, habitat, and behavior.

GENERAL CHARACTERISTICS
1.   They are unicellular which exhibit in solitary or colonial form.
2.   They are round, oval or elongated in shape and body surface is usually flattened.
3.   They lack a cell wall, giving them flexibility in shape and movement
4.   Protoplasm is divided into ectoplasm and endoplasm.
5.   Nucleus is visible surrounded by nuclear membrane.
6.   Respiration occurs through body surface by diffusion.
7.   They can reproduce both by asexually and sexually.
8.   Locomotion occurs through pseudopodia, cilia, or flagella.
9.   They are classified into four classes on the basis of their locomotion included;
        a. Class Sarcodina or Rhizopoda
        b. Class Ciliata or Ciliophora
        c. Class Mastigophora or Flagellata
        d. Class Sporozoa

Examples: Amoeba proteus, Entamooeba histolytica, Paramecium, Euglena,
Plasmodium vivax, Volvox, Chlamydomonas and Leishmania.

                                  Amoeba proteus
CLASSIFICATION
Phylum: Protozoa
 Sub-phylum: Plasmodroma
  Class: Rhizopoda
    Order: Lobosa

GENERAL CHARACTERISTICS
1.   Amoeba is a microscopic about 0.25mm in size and irregular in shape.
2.   It has a large contractile vacuole and various food vacuoles in the endoplasm.
3.   Locomotion occurs through pseudopodia.
4.   Reproduction occurs through binary fission or multiple fission.

HABIT AND HABITAT
Amoeba commonly found in freshwater ponds.

                            Paramecium caudatum
CLASSIFICATION
Phylum: Protozoa
 Sub-phylum: Ciliophora
  Class: Ciliata
    Order: Holotricha

GENERAL CHARACTERISTICS
1. Paramecium is slipper-like in shape about 0.3mm in size.
2. Body is surrounded by a thin layer of pellicle which covered by various cilia helps in
   locomotion.
3. They have a specialized feeding groove called the oral groove, which leads to a
   mouth-like structure called the cytostome.
4. Nutrition is holozoic.
5. Reproduction occurs by both asexually and sexually.

HABIT AND HABITAT
Paramecium commonly found in freshwater ponds.

                                        Euglena
CLASSIFICATION
Phylum: Protozoa
 Sub-phylum: Plasmodroma
  Class: Mastigophora
    Order: Euglenoidina

GENERAL CHARACTERISTICS
1.   Euglena is oval and spindle in shape about 50-100µm in size.

Euglena commonly found in freshwater ponds and moist organic matter.

                                    Plasmodium
CLASSIFICATION
Phylum: Protozoa
 Sub-phylum: Plasmodroma
  Class: Sporozoa
    Order: Haemosporida

GENERAL CHARACTERISTICS
Plasmodium has two hosts so it is called as digenetic. In mosquito, the fertilized eggs
called ookinetes forms oocysts which grows to form sporozoite stage. These sporozoites
transfer into human blood by mosquito biting, where they damages the red blood cells
and forms merozoites. These merozoites grows to form male and female gametes. Here
we can say that Plasmodium has two types of life stages; gametogenesis in human while
sporogenesis in mosquito.

HABIT AND HABITAT
Plasmodium is a parasitic protozoan, it is responsible for causing malaria in humans.

                                  Phylum Porifera
INTRODUCTION
The poriferans are commonly known as sponges. They play a crucial role in aquatic
ecosystem.

DISTINGUISHING CHARACTERS
      Simplest and primitive organisms
      First multicellular animals
      Porous body structure
      Cellular level of body organization
      Sessile and benthic organisms

GENERAL CHARACTERISTICS
1. Body Structure:

Porifera are the simplest type of multicellular animals. They are sessile and lack true
tissues and organs.

2. Porous structure:

They consist of a porous body covered in tiny openings called ostia, through which water
is drawn.

3. Water Canal System:

Water flows through the sponge's body, facilitated by specialized cells called
choanocytes which flow out through large opening called osculum.

4. Skeleton:

Skeleton consists of spongin fibres and siliceous or calcareous spicules.

5. Nutrition:

They show holozoic nutrition which involves absorbing or ingesting solid organic food
particles from water.

6. Reproduction:

They reproduce either sexually by releasing male and female gametes into the water and
asexually by budding. They have also capability of regeneration
