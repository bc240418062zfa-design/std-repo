   Introduction to Discourse Analysis:
Discourse analysis is the study of language in use, focusing on how people
communicate and construct meaning through spoken or written texts. It examines
not only individual sentences but also the broader context, social interactions, and
cultural norms that shape communication. Discourse analysts explore various
aspects, including language patterns, power dynamics, and the impact of context
on meaning.
   Two Strands of Discourse Analysis:
There are two main strands within discourse analysis:
      o Micro-level analysis: This examines the structure of individual
          utterances, such as sentences or phrases. Researchers analyze linguistic
          features, syntax, and semantics to understand how meaning is
          conveyed.
      o Macro-level analysis: This zooms out to consider larger patterns, such
          as entire conversations, narratives, or discourses. It looks at social
          contexts, power relations, and cultural norms that influence
          communication.
   Discourse Markers and Conversation:
   Discourse markers are words or phrases that help organize and manage
   conversations. They signal transitions, emphasize points, or express attitudes.
   Examples include "so," "right," "okay," and "anyway." These markers play a
   crucial role in maintaining smooth communication.
   Discourse and Sociolinguistics:
   Sociolinguistics investigates how language varies across different social
   groups, regions, and contexts. It explores questions like why people speak

  differently based on their background, identity, and social status.
  Sociolinguists analyze language use in real-world situations, considering both
  individual and societal factors.
  Discourse and Speech Acts:
  Speech acts refer to utterances that not only convey information but also
  perform actions. When someone says, "I would like the kimchi; could you
  please pass it to me?" they express a desire (locutionary act) and make a
  request (illocutionary act). Speech acts highlight the pragmatic functions of
  language beyond literal meaning.
  Pragmatics and the Acquisition of Discourse:
  Pragmatics explores how context influences language use. It includes
  understanding implicatures, indirect speech, and politeness. When children
  acquire language, they learn not only grammar but also pragmatic rules for
  effective communication.
  Discourse: Gender, Racism, and Religion:
  Discourse analysis sheds light on how language reflects and perpetuates social
  issues. Researchers examine gendered language, racist discourse, and
  religious narratives. By analyzing texts, they uncover hidden biases and power
  dynamics..
