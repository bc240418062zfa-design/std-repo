Parental care:
Parental care can be defined as any non-genetic contribution by a parent that increases the fitness
of offspring, and can occur before or after laying or birth.
Parental care in mammals:
In mammals, lactating female nourishes the young and is involved in most of the caring
behaviors. Males usually play no role in raising the young. In some mammalian species, such as
lions, males protect the females and young. In such species a male or small group of males takes
care of many females at once in a harem.
How polygynous occur if there is no parental care is required:
Males in mammals and birds do not stay with their partner if young ones can feed and care for
themselves after hatching. Males of these species maximize their reproductive success by
seeking other mates. Polygyny is more common in such animals.
How verve monkey show social behavior: long
Vervet monkeys produce a complex set of alarm calls. They give distinct alarm calls for
predators like leopards, eagles or snakes. Alarm call for leopard is a loud barking sound. For an
eagle a short double-syllabled cough. For a snake, a “chutter”. Upon hearing an alarm call, other
vervets behave in an appropriate way: Run up a tree on hearing alarm call for leopard, Look up
on hearing alarm for an eagle, Look down on hearing alarm for a snake. Infant vervets give and
understand alarm calls, but in a relatively undiscriminating way. They give “eagle alarm” on
seeing any bird. With age, they fine-tune their calls by learning from other members of the
group.
Diffirent Types of territory:
Mating Territory: places of mating for the partner.
Breeding Territory: The breeding territory is relatively small. It usually contains only a nesting
or mating site.
Feeding Territory: It tends to be larger than the breeding territory because it must contain
sufficient food to support the owner of the territory and any mate or offspring that may also be
residing there.
Multi- purpose Territory: This type of territory is generally the largest as it includes aspects of
both breeding and feeding territories.

Parental care in birds:
Birds show maximum parental care. Most newly hatched birds cannot care for themselves. They
require a large, continuous food supply. Catering the needs of young is difficult for a single
parent. To enhance chances of viability of offspring, most birds form monogamous pairs. Male
stays with its mate and helps her till offspring are able to fulfill their own needs. Lactating
female nourishes the young. She provides most of the required care.
What are the Cognitive maps, how the birds show cognitive maps behavior:
Cognitive Maps: “A representation in the nervous system of the spatial relationships between
objects in an animal's surroundings”. It involves relating landmark positions to one another.
Cognitive maps follow abstract geometric rule—identifying halfway point between landmarks.
Reduce the amount of detail required to remember an object's location.
Cognitive Map—Example: Clark's nutcracker birds. Store thousands of pine seeds in fall at
thousands of hiding places. Caches—distributed over a large area (35 km2). During winter, they
relocate their caches.
Types of cues used in migration and navigation:
Navigational abilities rely on several sensory cues (signals). Guide in orientation and path
finding. Some types of cues are not perceivable by human beings.
Migratory and Navigational Cues: 1= Sun compass 2= Celestial cues 3= Earth's magnetic field
4= Visual cues 5= Auditory cues 6= Olfactory cues.
Advantages of territorial behavior:
1= Territorial behavior forms an important part of social behavior. 2= Territory provides a base
which the male can advertise himself to female, and attracted the female. 3= Provides a protected
place for courtship, mating and parental care to young. 4= Provided safety where young can live
freely and without any interference.
Define cognition:
“The process of intellect, represented by awareness, understanding, reasoning, recalling
memories and judgments”. It is the Most complex form of learning, also Characteristically
exhibited by primates.
Define Foraging behavior:
The behavior that includes all activities of an animal related to search, recognize and capture
food items is called foraging behavior.
Define Altruism:
Altruism is a selfless behavior in which an individual endangers his own survival or sacrifices
some of its own reproductive potential to benefit another individual.
Social organization:
A social organization is a large group of members who intercommunicate extensively, exhibit
division of labour, bear some relatively permanent social relationship to one another, Such
organization exists in both invertebrates and vertebrates. arthropods such as ants, bees and
termites are the best examples of the social organization.
In list the animals that show altruistic behavior:
Social insects such as ants, wasps, bees and termites and squirrel, stinging bees, Vampire bats,
Emperor penguins, Vervet monkeys, Cleaner fish, Primates, Wolves, dolphins.

It Involves mate choice. Members of one sex choose mates on the basis of particular
