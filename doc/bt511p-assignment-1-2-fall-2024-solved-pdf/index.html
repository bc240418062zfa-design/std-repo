Question# 2:
While preparing a solution you have mistakenly raised the volume of solution than the
intended volume. Can you check the new molarity of solution? If YES, explain how you
will determine it with an example and if NO, explain the reason? (5 marks)
Yes, you can determine the new molarity when volume is increased. Here's how:
Formula: M₁V₁ = M₂V₂
(Where M₁ = initial molarity, V₁ = initial volume, M₂ = new molarity, V₂ = new volume)
Example:
Initial solution: 2M NaCl, 100mL
Accidentally increased to: 150mL
Calculation:
1. Rearrange formula to solve for M₂:
 M₂ = (M₁V₁)/V₂
2. Plug in values:
 M₂ = (2M × 100mL)/150mL
3. Solve:
 M₂ = 1.33M
This works because:
- Total moles of solute remain constant
- Only volume changes
- Concentration decreases proportionally as volume increases
The solution is now more dilute (1.33M) than the intended concentration (2M).

b) Name (1 mark):
Membrane/Syringe filter
Functions (2 marks):
1. Sterilization function:

- Used for sterilizing heat-sensitive solutions that cannot be autoclaved

- Physically removes microorganisms by passing solution through tiny pores (0.22 μm)

- Ideal for sterilizing cell culture media, buffers, and biological solutions
2. Purification/filtration function:
- Removes particles, precipitates, and debris from solutions

- Different pore sizes available (0.22 μm to 0.45 μm) for various applications

- Enables preparation of sterile, particle-free solutions for cell culture and molecular biology
- Can be used to clarify solutions before analytical procedures
The filter consists of a membrane housed in a plastic holder with inlet and outlet ports, designed to attach to
syringes for easy filtration under pressure.
