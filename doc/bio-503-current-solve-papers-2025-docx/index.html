Question 1
    1. Define free energy. Difference between exergonic and endergonic reactions
Answer
Free energy is the energy available to do work in a system.
Exergonic reactions release energy and happen naturally (spontaneously). Example: breaking down food to get energy.
Endergonic reactions need energy to happen and don’t occur on their own. Example: making food in plants using sunlight.
Question 2
How enzyme lowers the Ea barrier?
Answer
Enzymes lower the activation energy by helping reactants come together in the right way, making it easier for the reaction to happen. They speed up reactions by reducing the energy needed to start them.
Question 3
Components of x ray instrument
 Answer
An X-ray instrument has these main parts:
    1. X-ray Tube: Makes X-rays.
    2. Control Panel: Lets you adjust settings.
    3. Power Supply: Provides power to the X-ray tube.
    4. Collimator: Shapes the X-ray beam.
    5. Detector or Film: Captures the X-rays.
    6. Shielding: Protects from extra radiation.
    7. Imaging System: Displays the X-ray images.
Question 4
Types of centrifugation
Answer
The main types of centrifugation are:

    1. Differential Centrifugation: Separates particles by size and weight.
    2. Density Gradient Centrifugation: Sorts particles by their density using a layered solution.
    3. Isoelectric Focusing: Separates proteins by their charge at different pH levels.
    4. Rate-Zonal Centrifugation: Separates particles by size and shape using a gradient.
Question 5
Dipole dipole forces
Answer
Dipole-dipole forces are the attractions between the positive and negative ends of two polar molecules. These forces occur because the molecules have opposite charges that attract each other.
Question 6
Difference between heat and temperature
Answer
Heat is the energy that moves between things because of temperature differences.
Temperature tells us how hot or cold something is.
Question 7
X_Ray Crystallography
 Answer
X-ray crystallography is a method used to find out the structure of molecules. A crystal of the substance is hit with X-rays, and the way the X-rays bounce off helps scientists see how the atoms are arranged. It’s often used to study things like proteins.
Question 8
Translatory motion and its types
Answer
*Translatory Motion:*
- An object moves from one place to another without rotating.
- Its position changes, but it doesn’t spin around.
*Types of Translatory Motion:*
1. *Straight Line Motion*: Moving in a straight line (e.g., car on road).
2. *Curved Line Motion*: Moving in a curved path (e.g., ball rolling on ground).
3. *Oscillating Motion*: Moving back and forth repeatedly (e.g., pendulum
Question 9
What is Fluorsescence
 Answer
Fluorescence is when a substance absorbs light and then releases it as a glow. This glow is usually a different color (longer wavelength) than the light it absorbed. It’s commonly seen in things like glow-in-the-dark materials and is used in science for things like medical tests and imaging.
Question 10
Choromosomes
Answer
Chromosomes are tiny structures inside cells that carry our genetic information. They help decide things like how we look and how our bodies work. Humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes, one set from each parent.
Question 11
Difference between isobaric and ischoeric
Answer
Isobaric: A process that happens at constant pressure.
Isochoric: A process that happens at constant volume.
Question 12
What is HPLC
Answer
HPLC (High-Performance Liquid Chromatography) is a method used to separate and analyze different parts of a liquid by passing it through a special column. It’s commonly used in labs for testing and quality control.
Question 13
What is denaturation and protein folding
Answer
Denaturation: When a protein loses its shape and stops working, usually due to heat or chemicals.
Protein Folding: When a protein takes its correct shape to do its job properly.
Long question 1
Spin spin coupling
Answer
Spin-spin coupling is when the magnetic spins of nearby atoms in a molecule affect each other. This creates extra peaks in a special type of test called NMR, which helps scientists figure out how atoms are connected in a molecule.
Question 2
Quasi equilibrium process
Answer
 A quasi-equilibrium process is a process that happens very slowly, so the system stays close to equilibrium at all times. It’s almost like the process is in balance throughout, but not exactly. This allows the system to adjust gradually without big changes, making it easy to analyze.
Question 3
Chemical structure of DNA
Answer
The chemical structure of DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid) is made up of two long chains of nucleotides twisted into a double helix. Each nucleotide consists of:
    1. A sugar molecule (deoxyribose)
    2. A phosphate group
    3. A nitrogenous base (Adenine, Thymine, Cytosine, or Guanine)
The two strands are connected by pairs of nitrogenous bases: Adenine (A) pairs with Thymine (T), and Cytosine (C) pairs with Guanine (G). These base pairs are held together by hydrogen bonds, forming the “steps” of the double helix.
Question 4
Define cofactor, coenzyme, prosthetic group, active site
Answer
    1. Cofactor: A helper molecule that helps enzymes do their job.
    2. Coenzyme: A special type of cofactor made of vitamins that helps enzymes.
    3. Prosthetic Group: A helper molecule that is strongly attached to an enzyme and is essential for its function.
    4. Active Site: The part of the enzyme where the molecule it works on (substrate) fits and reacts.
Question 5
Define thermodynamics and expain its four laws
Answer
Thermodynamics is the study of how energy moves and changes, like how heat turns into work or how energy flows in systems.
Here are the four laws in simple terms:
    1. Zeroth Law: If two things are the same temperature as a third thing, they are also the same temperature as each other.
    2. First Law: Energy can’t be created or destroyed, only changed from one form to another (like heat turning into work).
    3. Second Law: Things naturally move toward more disorder (more chaos or randomness).
