Reproductive Patterns in Animals:
All invertebrates and vertebrates have one of the three reproductive patterns.
1= Oviparous: These are the animals which lay eggs and development takes place outside the
body of animals. Fertilization may be internal or external. Majority of invertebrates and many
vertebrates are oviparous.
2= Ovoviparous: These are the animals which retain eggs into the body but the young one gets
all its food from yolk of egg. Both fertilization and development is internal. Many members of
annelids, brachiopods, gastropods, fishes and reptiles.
3= Viviparous: These are the animals in which eggs develop into the body and the young one
gets its nourishment from mother. Both fertilization and development is internal. Scorpions, most
mammals, elasmobranch fishes, few amphibians and reptiles are viviparous.

Types of variables:
Parameters in experiment that may change is called Variables.
   1. Independent Variable: This is the parameter manipulated or tested in the experiment. In
      the example, the independent variable is the daylight length, as it is being tested to
      observe its effect on the reproductive readiness of mice.
   2. Dependent Variable: This is the parameter measured to determine the outcome of the
      experiment. In the example, the frequency of mating in mice is the dependent variable,
      and its variation is observed based on changes in the independent variable (daylight
      length).
   3. Control Variable: This is a parameter that is kept constant to prevent its influence on the
      results. In the example, temperature is a control variable, as it is maintained constant to
      ensure that any observed changes in mating frequency are attributed to variations in the
      independent variable (daylight length).

How regulation occur in aquatic animals

Aquatic animals regulate water differently based on their environment. Freshwater animals, in
hypotonic environments, conserve ions to prevent loss. Marine animals, in hypertonic
environments, manage water to avoid salt buildup and dehydration. Each adapts to maintain
internal balance amid varying external conditions.

What is the Biological magnification?
Biological magnification is the process where the concentration of certain substances, like toxins
or pollutants, increases as they move up the food chain. Organisms at higher trophic levels
accumulate more of these substances, posing risks to top predators and humans consuming
contaminated species.

How decomposer help in nutrient recycling process.
Decomposers, mainly bacteria and fungi, play a crucial role in nutrient recycling. They break
down dead organic matter into simpler compounds using extracellular enzymes. By doing so,
decomposers release locked minerals, prevent environmental clutter, and recycle essential
nutrients, ensuring the sustainability and balance of the ecosystem.

Applications of zoology in medical research:
Zoology plays a crucial role in medical research by contributing to the understanding of diseases
caused by animals. Through techniques and protocols, scientists diagnose diseases and elucidate
the underlying mechanisms. For instance, in the case of dengue, understanding how Aedes
mosquitoes transmit the disease is vital. Zoological insights aid in designing preventive measures
and medications. Moreover, zoology facilitates medical advancements by studying the effects
and mode of action of new drugs on animal models before their application in human medicine,
ensuring safety and efficacy.

Difference between ectosymbiosis and Endosymbiosis:
Ectosymbiosis:
It is a symbiosis in which symbionts live on the body surface of the host or in the digestive tract.
E.g., Lice, barnacles and cleaner fish
Endosymbiosis:
It is the relationship in which one symbiont live within the tissue of other. E.g., nitrogen fixing
bacteria in root nodules, algae inside the corals.

Describe the threatens to biodiversity:
Biodiversity of all natural areas is threatened mainly due to habitat loss, acid rain, pollution,
urbanization and agriculture. Human is exploiting 40% of earth net primary production.

Function of placenta:
Placenta is s temporary organ which connects developing fetus to mother via umbilical cord.
Human have placenta formed form chorion and allantois. The tiny chorionic villi sent out from
blastocyst form placenta. Fetus is fed through placenta. And primarily functioning as provide
Nutrient and oxygen exchange, waste elimination, and hormone production during pregnancy.

Hormones during birth:
During birth, hormones play vital roles. Relaxin, produced by the ovaries and placenta, helps
separate the mother's pelvic bones for easier passage of the baby. Adrenocorticotropic hormone
(ACTH) from the baby's pituitary gland stimulates adrenal glands to release prostaglandins,
working with oxytocin. Oxytocin, triggered by uterine wall stretch, causes powerful contractions.
These hormones collectively facilitate the birthing process, ensuring the expulsion of the baby
and later the placenta in a sequence called afterbirth.

What are the steps of fertilization:

Fertilization involves the fusion of egg and sperm, forming a diploid zygote. There are two
types: external (in water) and internal (within an animal's body). It can occur through copulation,
cloacal connection, or via spermatophore. The vitelline membrane surrounds the egg, with
receptors for sperm binding. Sperm carry lysins in their acrosomal head, dissolving the egg
membrane. Only one sperm successfully fertilizes the egg, and egg activation follows, involving
biochemical changes to complete fertilization and initiate embryonic development.
