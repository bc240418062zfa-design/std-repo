FROM MIDTERM SYLLABUS
Objectives of Ethology:
Involves understanding of:
• Stimuli that elicit behavior
• Physiological mechanisms mediating response
• Animal's experiences that influence behavior
• Role of behavior in survival & reproduction
• Evolutionary history of behavior
Definition of Habituation and example:
  “A loss of responsiveness to stimuli that convey little or no new information”.
 Examples:
 Many mammals and birds recognize alarm calls of members of their species. If these
   calls prove fake repetitively, they stop responding (“cry-wolf” effect)
 • A threatened squirrel gives signals. Others recognize signal and go to nearest refuge.
    An individual who gives many false alarms is also recognized. Its signals are
    ignored
Imprinting with example:
 “Formation of a long lasting behavioral attachment to a particular individual or object
    at a specific stage in life”.
 Examples:
 Seagulls, sensitive period for a parent to bond with its young lasts one to two days. If
   bonding does not occur, parent will not care for the infant, leading to death of the
   offspring.
 Classic experiment of Konrad Lorenz with geese. Konrad Lorenz made goslings to
    imprint on him. Goslings followed him as though he was their mother.
Reflex action:
Reflex action is a sudden and involuntary response to stimuli. A reflex action requires a
reflex arc which consists of:
      Reflex arc components:
      ▪ A sensory organ (receptor),
      ▪ A sensory nerve (afferent nerve),
       ▪ The spinal cord or brain,
       ▪ An intermediate motor or efferent nerve
      .▪ A motor organ or effector.
Web making process:

 • When a spider begins a web, it releases a silk thread. It anchors the thread to some
    object such as a branch.
 • As the spider moves back and forth, it adds more threads, strengthening the web
    and creating a pattern.
  • Lines that go from the center of the web outward are called “radial lines“. They
     support the web.
 • Threads that go around and around the web are called “orb lines.”
 • The main reason spiders spin webs is to catch the prey. When an insect, such as a
    fly, flies into a spider’s web, it gets stuck on the sticky threads.
 • When a spider catches prey in the sticky strands of its web, it approaches the
    trapped insect and uses its fangs to inject venom. The venom either kills or
    paralyzes the prey.
Fixed Action Patterns (Instincts):
 • It is a sequence of unlearned, innate behaviour that is unchangeable.
 • Once initiated; it is usually carried to completion.
 • It is hardwired, however, not purely genetic, may improve with experience.
 • FAP is triggered by an external sensory stimulus known as “sign stimulus”.
Innate behavior:
 “ is inborn or innate sequence of activities which are predictable, species specific,
 genetically controlled and independent of past experience.”
Behavior that is developmentally fixed is called innate behavior. Innate behavior is
controlled by genetics. These behaviors produce responses that are automatic without
any prior experience. Since this type of behavior follows a fixed pattern (FAP) which is
predictable and found in all members of a species it is also called stereotyped
behavior.
 Examples: Early survival, reproduction, kinesis, taxis. Sneezing, shivering, yawning,
   quickly pulling your hand away from a hot surface, blinking your eyes.
Types of innate behavior:
 There are different types of innate or stereotyped behavior.
    1) Taxis
    2) Irritability
    3) Kinesis
    4) Instincts
    5) Reflexes
    6) Motivation
Learned behavior:
Learned behavior is one that an organism develops as a result of experience.
Thorpe defined learning as a long lasting adaptive change in behavior resulting from
past experience; hence it is acquired during the life time of an individual.
 Examples:
 1. By watching their mother, baby ducks learn how to avoid danger and to know what
    is good to eat. This is an example of learned behavior.
 2. Prairie dogs learns by experience to only make alarm calls when they hear
    predators.

Types of learning:
     1) Habituation
     2) Imprinting
     3) Classical conditioning
     4) Trial and error learning
     5) Latent learning
     6) Insight
     7) Reasoning
     8) Cognition
