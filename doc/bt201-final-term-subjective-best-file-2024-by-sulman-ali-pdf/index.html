Importance of Bacteria? OR Role of Bacteria in Environment?
The ecosystem, both on land and in the water, depends heavily upon the activity of bacteria. The
cycling of nutrients such as carbon, nitrogen, and sulfur is completed by their ceaseless labor.
Decomposition is the breakdown of these organisms, and the release of nutrients back into the
environment, and is one of the most important roles of the bacteria.

Food Web? / Food Web and Structure of Communities
Definition of Food Web: A food web summarizes the feeding relations in a community. It
illustrates the complex interactions between different species, where each species is connected to
others through predator-prey relationships.
Structure of Community: The structure of a community can be influenced by the feeding
activities of keystone species, which control community dynamics. For instance, in a simple food
web, plants are eaten by grasshoppers and squirrels. Predators like foxes and eagles consume
these herbivores, and decomposers like earthworms and bacteria break down dead organisms.
Keystone species, such as certain predators, can enhance species diversity by preventing
competitive exclusion.

Correct sequencing of Organism DNA protein RNA function’s arrange them
in order:
In organisms, DNA, RNA, and proteins play crucial roles in various functions. Here is the order
in which they function:
1. DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid): DNA is the genetic material that carries the instructions for
the development, functioning, and reproduction of all living organisms. It serves as a blueprint
for the synthesis of RNA and proteins.
2. RNA (Ribonucleic Acid): RNA is a molecule that is transcribed from DNA and plays a vital
role in protein synthesis. There are different types of RNA, including messenger RNA (mRNA),
transfer RNA (tRNA), and ribosomal RNA (rRNA).
3. Protein: Proteins are large, complex molecules made up of amino acids. They perform a wide
range of functions in cells, including catalyzing chemical reactions, providing structural support,
transporting molecules, and serving as signaling molecules
In summary, DNA provides the instructions, RNA helps in the synthesis of proteins based on
those instructions, and proteins carry out various functions essential for the organism's survival
and functioning.

Less correlation between mRNA and protein abundance:
The lack of correlation between mRNA and protein abundances is a phenomenon observed in
cells. Control mechanisms at the ribosomes, where mRNA is translated to peptides, contribute to
this disparity. Translational control mechanisms enable cells to selectively translate certain

mRNAs while blocking others, influencing protein production. This lack of direct correlation
suggests that post-transcriptional processes, such as translational control, play a crucial role in
regulating protein abundance independently of mRNA levels.

What is industrial ecology:
The design of the industrial infrastructure such that it consists of a series of interlocking
"technological ecosystems" interfacing with global natural ecosystems. Industrial ecology takes
the pattern and processes of natural ecosystems as a design for sustainability. It represents a shift
in paradigm from conquering nature to becoming nature.

What happen when organisms have limited access to resources ?/ Optimal
foraging theory
Optimal foraging theory attempts to model how organisms feed as an optimizing process.
Evolutionary ecologists predict that if organisms have limited access to energy, natural selection
is likely to favor individuals that are more effective at acquiring energy and nutrients. Many
animals select food in a way that appears to maximize the rate at which they capture energy.
Plants appear to allocate energy to roots versus shoots in a way that increases their rate of intake
of the resources that limit their growth. Plants in environments with abundant nutrients but little
light tend to invest more energy in the growth of stems and leaves and less in roots. In
environments rich in light but poor in nutrients, plants tend to invest more energy in the growth
of roots.

Endosymbiotic theory and origin of eukaryotic evolution?
Endosymbiotic Theory: The endosymbiotic theory suggests that eukaryotic cells originated
from the symbiotic relationship between several prokaryotic cells. This prolonged association led
to the integration of these cells, forming organelles like mitochondria and plastids, which possess
their own genetic material and protein-synthesizing capabilities.
Origin of Eukaryotic Evolution: Eukaryotic evolution involved the incorporation of free-living
bacteria into a host cell, establishing a symbiotic relationship. Over time, these bacteria became
integral parts of the eukaryotic cell, contributing to its complexity and ability to synthesize
proteins through distinct systems.

Which is earliest organisms which introduce oxygen in environment?
The earliest organisms to introduce oxygen into the environment were cyanobacteria. These
photosynthetic bacteria, also known as blue-green algae, began producing oxygen through
photosynthesis around 2.4 to 2.5 billion years ago during the Great Oxygenation Event (GOE).
This event significantly increased the levels of oxygen in Earth's atmosphere, paving the way for
the evolution of more complex aerobic life forms.

Keystone specie in detail;
A keystone species is a species that has a disproportionately large effect on community structure
relative to its biomass or abundance. Keystone species differ from foundation species in two
main ways: they are more likely to belong to higher trophic levels (to be top predators), and they
act in more diverse ways than foundation species, which tend to modify their environment.
