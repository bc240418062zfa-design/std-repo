1. Rf Value of Chromatography:
The Rf (Retention factor) value in chromatography is the ratio
of the distance travelled by the solute to the distance
travelled by the solvent front.
2.Function of Pipette:
A pipette is a laboratory tool used to transport a measured
volume of liquid. It is commonly used in biology, chemistry,
and medical labs to ensure precision and accuracy in liquid
handling.
3. Ethics of the Killing Jar Method for Insects:
The ethicality of using a killing jar for insects can be
contentious. Some argue it is a necessary method for
scientific collection and study, provided it is done humanely

and ethically. Others argue it is inhumane and alternatives
should be sought.
4. Steps of Pipette Calculation: Pipette calculation steps
involve:
 - Setting the desired volume.
 - Attaching a pipette tip.
  - Aspirating the liquid by immersing the tip in the liquid and
releasing the plunger.
  - Dispensing the liquid by pressing the plunger to the first
stop, then the second stop to expel any remaining liquid.
5. Chemicals Used in Preservation of Dead Specimen:
Common chemicals include formalin, ethanol, methanol, and
acetone. Formalin (a solution of formaldehyde in water) is
widely used for preserving biological specimens.
6. Butterfly Preservation Mount Method:
The method involves:
  - Killing the butterfly humanely (often using a killing jar with
ethyl acetate).
  - Spreading the wings and pinning them in place on a
spreading board.
 - Allow the butterfly to dry and then mount it on a display
board using entomological pins.
7. Calibration of Micropipette: Calibration involves:

  - Weighing a known volume of water dispensed by the
pipette.
 - Comparing the weight to the expected value.
 - Adjusting the pipette as needed to ensure accuracy.
8.Retardation Factor (Rf):
 Same as the Rf value in chromatography, it's the ratio of the
distance travelled by the solute to the distance travelled by
the solvent front.
9. Calculation of Cell Size:
Cell size can be calculated using a microscope by:
 - Measuring the field of view diameter at a given
magnification.
 - Counting the number of cells that fit across the diameter.
  - Dividing the field of view diameter by the number of cells
to get the size of one cell.
10. Preservation of Vertebrae: Vertebrae can be preserved
using:
  - Cleaning and removing any flesh or tissue.
 - Soaking in a solution of hydrogen peroxide or bleach to
whiten.
  - Drying completely before storage or display.
11. Relation Between Field of View and Magnification:

 The field of view decreases as magnification increases. This is
an inverse relationship where higher magnification provides a
smaller view of the sample.
12.Procedure and Observation of Gram Staining:
  - Procedure:
   - Prepare a bacterial smear on a slide and heat-fix it.
   - Stain with crystal violet for 1 minute.
   - Rinse with water and apply iodine solution for 1 minute.
   - Decolorize with alcohol or acetone for 20 seconds.
   - Counterstain with safranin for 1 minute.
   - Rinse and blot dry.
  - Observation:
    - Gram-positive bacteria appear purple (retain crystal
violet).
    - Gram-negative bacteria appear pink/red (retain
safranin).

13. Insect Preservation Methods: Methods include:
  - Drying and mounting.
  - Preservation in alcohol.
  - Freezing.

  - Using chemicals like formalin or ethyl acetate.
 I prefer drying and mounting for display purposes as it
maintains the physical characteristics of the insects well.
14. Function of Glycerin in Preservation:
Glycerin is used to prevent desiccation and shrinkage of
biological specimens by retaining moisture.
15.Contrast Adjustment in Compound Microscope:
The condenser and diaphragm are parts of the microscope
used to adjust the contrast of the image.
16.Preserving Dry Dead Animals:
This involves:
  - Cleaning and removing any internal organs.
  - Drying the animal thoroughly.
  - Treating with preservatives like borax or formalin.
17.Difference Between RCF and RPM:
  - RCF (Relative Centrifugal Force) measures the force
exerted by a centrifuge.
  - RPM (Revolutions Per Minute) measures the speed of
rotation.
  RCF is dependent on RPM and the radius of the rotor.
18.Steps of Hematoxylin Stain:

  - Fix the tissue sample.
  - Stain with hematoxylin to highlight cell nuclei.
  - Counterstain with eosin to highlight other cell
components.
19. Use of Centrifugation: Centrifugation is used to separate
components of a mixture based on density by spinning at
high speed.
20. Effect of Decreased Field of View on Magnification:
As the field of view decreases, magnification increases,
providing a more detailed but narrower view of the sample.
21. Use of Methylene Blue in Wet Mount:
Methylene blue is used to stain cells, making their structures,
such as the nucleus, more visible under a microscope.
22.Difference Between Fixative and Preservatives:
 - Fixatives stabilise tissues and cells to prevent decay and
maintain structure for examination.
 - Preservatives prevent decay by inhibiting the growth of
microorganisms.
23. Formulating a Preservation Solution with Picric Acid,
Acetic Acid, and Formalin:
  - Mix picric acid, acetic acid, and formalin in specific
proportions.

  - The resulting solution is called Bouin's solution,
commonly used for preserving invertebrates.
24. Applications of Paper Chromatography:
  - Separating and identifying amino acids, proteins, and
other small molecules.
  - Testing the purity of compounds.
  - Analysing mixtures of compounds.
25.Using Paper, Formalin, and Alcohol for Preservation:
These materials preserve animals by:
  - Preventing microbial growth.
  - Dehydrating tissues.
  - Fixing tissues to maintain structural integrity.
26.Preservation of Invertebrates:
Invertebrates can be preserved by:
  - Storing in alcohol or formalin.
  - Drying and pinning.
  - Using a killing jar followed by mounting.
