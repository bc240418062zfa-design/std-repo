  1. Alpha Helix and Beta Sheets:
  ·    Alpha helix: A secondary structure in proteins where the polypeptide chain forms a helical shape stabilized
  by hydrogen bonds.
  ·    Beta sheets: Another secondary structure where the polypeptide chain folds into a sheet-like structure held
  together by hydrogen bonds.
  2. Sequence Tagged Sites (STS):
  ·    Short DNA sequences with unique locations in a genome, used as landmarks for genome mapping.
  3. Chromosome Jumping:
  ·    A technique used in genome mapping where large DNA fragments are cloned, and then the sequence is
  determined. This helps to "jump" over large regions of the genome.
  4. Way of Increasing the Expression of Cloned Genes:
  ·    Methods include optimizing codon usage, using strong promoters, adding enhancer sequences, and choosing
  appropriate vectors.
  5. Reverse Transcriptase:
  ·    Enzyme that synthesizes DNA from an RNA template. It's essential in reverse transcription, a process used
  to convert RNA into complementary DNA (cDNA).
  6. Bio Panning:
  ·    A technique in phage display where a library of phages displaying different peptides or proteins is exposed
  to a target, and those that bind are selectively enriched.
  7. Genome Mapping:
  ·    Determining the physical locations of genes or other features on a genome.
  8. Type III Restriction Modification System:
  ·    A bacterial defense system where the enzyme recognizes specific DNA sequences, cleaves foreign DNA,
  and modifies the host DNA to protect it from cleavage.
  9. Cosmid:
  ·    A type of hybrid cloning vector that contains both plasmid and lambda phage elements, allowing the cloning
  of larger DNA fragments.
  10. RAPD (Random Amplification of Polymorphic DNA):
  ·    A PCR-based technique using short, arbitrary primers to amplify random segments of genomic DNA, used
  for fingerprinting.
  11. Multiplex PCR and Real-Time PCR:
  ·    Multiplex PCR: Simultaneous amplification of multiple DNA targets in a single reaction.
  ·    Real-time PCR: Monitoring the amplification of DNA in real-time using fluorescent dyes or probes.
  12. Interrupted Mating:
  ·    A technique used in bacterial genetics to map the relative positions of genes on a bacterial chromosome by
  stopping conjugation at different time points.

   18. Significance of Inverse PCR:
   ·   Inverse PCR is used to amplify DNA flanking a known sequence. It is valuable for studying regions
