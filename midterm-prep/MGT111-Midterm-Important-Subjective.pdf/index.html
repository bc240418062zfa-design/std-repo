  1. What is the difference between a Ministry and a Department?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A Ministry is a division or a group of divisions entrusted with one major area of government work; for example, the Ministry of Finance has several divisions (Finance Division, Revenue Division, Economic Affairs Division). The Minister is the political head of the ministry and provides policy guidelines, while the Secretary is its administrative head. A Department, on the other hand, is a distinct area, division or branch of an organization over which a manager has authority for the performance of specified activities. Departments are the working units through which the policies of a ministry are actually executed.

  2. List the advantages and disadvantages of 'Departmentalization by Time'.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Departmentalization by time means organizing activities according to the time when they are performed, e.g. work in shifts (morning, evening, night). Advantages: (1) Services can be provided round the clock, e.g. hospitals, police and PTV; (2) costly equipment and machinery is used to its full capacity; (3) processes which cannot be stopped can continue without break; (4) it suits the convenience of different types of customers at different times. Disadvantages: (1) Supervision of night shifts is difficult; (2) coordination between shifts may be weak and work may suffer at the change of shift; (3) workers in odd shifts face health and social problems; (4) additional allowances increase the cost.

  3. Give your point of view about the Rational Decision Making Model.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The Rational Decision Making Model assumes that the decision maker is fully rational and chooses the best possible alternative. Its steps are: identifying the problem, collecting relevant information, developing alternatives, evaluating each alternative against set criteria, selecting the best alternative, implementing it and finally evaluating the results. In my view, it is a useful and logical model because it reduces guesswork and bias. However, in real life complete rationality is rarely possible because of limited information, time and resources, so managers often have to settle for a satisfactory rather than the perfect decision.

  4. What are the constraints in making decisions through the Rational Decision Making Model?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The main constraints are: (1) Limited information — the decision maker can never collect complete information about all alternatives; (2) Time and cost constraints — collecting and analysing information takes time and money; (3) Limited mental capacity — a human mind cannot process unlimited data, a condition called bounded rationality; (4) Organizational constraints — policies, rules and the authority structure restrict free choice; (5) Environmental uncertainty — future conditions cannot be predicted accurately; (6) Personal values and emotions of the decision maker also influence the choice. Due to these constraints, decision makers usually 'satisfice' instead of optimize.

  5. What are the advantages of the Planning Commission of Pakistan?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The Planning Commission is the highest body for national planning. Its advantages/roles are: (1) It prepares a comprehensive national plan for the socio-economic development of the country, so development is systematic rather than haphazard; (2) It formulates annual plans and the Annual Development Programme within the framework of the national plan; (3) It monitors the implementation of all major development projects and programmes; (4) It continuously evaluates the economic situation and coordinates economic policies; (5) It technically appraises development projects of federal ministries and provincial governments for CDWP/ECNEC, which avoids wastage of resources. Thus it brings coordination, expertise and continuity to the development process.

  6. Give your views about the advantages of the Planning Commission's function of continuous evaluation of the economic system.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Continuous evaluation of the economic situation is one of the most valuable functions of the Planning Commission. Its advantages are: (1) The government comes to know, on an ongoing basis, whether the economy is moving in the planned direction; (2) Weak areas and bottlenecks are identified in time, so corrective measures can be taken before the situation worsens; (3) Economic policies of different ministries can be coordinated in the light of fresh evaluation; (4) It provides reliable data for preparing the next annual and five-year plans; (5) It helps in the efficient allocation of scarce resources to the sectors that need them most. Without continuous evaluation, planning would become a one-time, blind exercise.

  7. What do you know about the Regulating Act 1773 passed by the British Parliament?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The Regulating Act 1773 was passed by the British Parliament to regulate the affairs of the East India Company in India. Before this Act, the Company ruled the Indian territories almost independently, and its servants were involved in corruption and misuse of power. Through this Act, the British Government, for the first time, asserted its control over the Company: it granted the British government powers to regulate the Company's affairs in India, made the Governor of Bengal the Governor-General, and set up a Supreme Court at Calcutta. However, the British government was still not ruling India directly; all powers of ruling were exercised through the Company.

  8. What would you suggest to bring reforms in the civil services of Pakistan?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The following reforms are suggested: (1) Recruitment should be strictly on merit through a transparent competitive examination, free from political interference; (2) Training should be modernized and made continuous so that officers keep pace with new knowledge; (3) Specialization should be encouraged instead of excessive reliance on generalists; (4) Pay and service conditions should be improved so that talented people are attracted and corruption is reduced; (5) Promotion should be based on performance and merit, not merely on seniority; (6) Tenure of postings should be protected so officers can work without fear of arbitrary transfers; (7) Accountability and performance evaluation systems should be made effective.

  9. In your view, which factors can contribute to the success of reforms in the civil service of Pakistan?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The success of civil service reforms depends on: (1) Strong political will — reforms fail when governments announce them but do not implement them; (2) Merit-based recruitment and promotion, which create a professional service; (3) Continuity of policies — reforms must not be reversed with every change of government; (4) Adequate financial resources for better pay, training and equipment; (5) Support of the civil servants themselves, who should be taken into confidence instead of being treated as opponents of reform; (6) Effective monitoring and evaluation of the reform programme; (7) A corruption-free environment and fair accountability. Without these factors, reform reports remain only on paper.

  10. What are the effects of specialization on government officials?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Specialization means an official acquires deep knowledge and skill in one particular field. Its effects on government officials are: (1) The official becomes an expert and performs his work more efficiently and quickly; (2) Quality of decisions in technical matters improves; (3) The official's work becomes narrow — he knows more and more about less and less, and may lose sight of the overall picture; (4) Too much specialization can make the official rigid and less adaptable to other assignments; (5) It improves the official's career prospects in his own field but may limit his mobility to other departments. On balance, specialization is beneficial if balanced with general administrative ability.

  11. Why is public administration less efficient than private administration?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Public administration is often less efficient because: (1) It works under rigid rules, regulations and procedures (red tape), which delay decisions; (2) There is no profit motive and no competition, so there is little pressure to reduce cost; (3) Political interference in postings, transfers and decisions disturbs merit and continuity; (4) Goals of public organizations are multiple, vague and sometimes conflicting, so performance cannot be measured easily; (5) Public servants have security of service, which may reduce the fear of poor performance; (6) Every action is subject to public, media and legislative scrutiny, which makes officials over-cautious; (7) Resources and budgets are limited while public demands are unlimited.

  12. What would you suggest to the in-charge of a matrix organization to improve coordination?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    In a matrix organization an employee reports to two bosses — the functional manager and the project manager — so coordination is the biggest problem. The in-charge should: (1) Clearly define the authority and responsibility of both managers so that overlapping orders are avoided; (2) Hold regular joint meetings of functional and project teams to share information and resolve conflicts; (3) Develop a common plan and schedule agreed by both sides; (4) Encourage open communication and teamwork instead of departmental thinking; (5) Settle disputes between the two bosses quickly at a higher level; (6) Train employees to work under dual command and keep both managers informed. These steps reduce confusion and improve coordination.

  13. How do missions, goals and objectives empower employees?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A mission tells employees the overall purpose of the organization; goals state what the organization wants to achieve, and objectives convert goals into specific, measurable targets. When these are clearly communicated, employees are empowered because: (1) They know exactly what is expected of them and can plan their own work; (2) They can take day-to-day decisions themselves, without waiting for orders, as long as they serve the objective; (3) Clear objectives give them a sense of direction and ownership; (4) Their performance can be judged against known standards, which is fair and motivating; (5) They feel part of a larger purpose, which increases commitment. Thus clarity of mission, goals and objectives turns subordinates from mere order-takers into responsible decision makers.

  14. Describe the concept of a formal organization and give three examples from real life.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A formal organization is a deliberately planned structure of roles and relationships, created by management to achieve specific goals. Its relations are official, based on authority, rules and procedures, and are usually shown on an organization chart. Work, authority and responsibility of every position are clearly defined. Examples from real life: (1) A government ministry, e.g. the Ministry of Education, with its divisions and officers working under defined rules; (2) A university, with a Vice-Chancellor, deans, chairmen and teachers in a fixed hierarchy; (3) A bank or a company like PTCL, where employees work in defined positions under written rules and a chain of command.

  15. What are the benefits of an open system?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    An organization which interacts with its environment is called an open system. Its benefits are: (1) It receives inputs (resources, information, public demands) from the environment and adjusts its outputs accordingly, so it remains relevant; (2) It gets feedback from the environment, which helps it correct mistakes and improve performance; (3) It can adapt to changes — political, social, technological — and therefore survives longer; (4) Interaction with other organizations brings new ideas, technology and resources; (5) It understands the needs of the people it serves, so public satisfaction increases. A closed system, by ignoring its environment, becomes outdated and inefficient.

  16. What are the components of a national plan?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A national plan is a comprehensive document for the socio-economic development of a country. It comprises a number of small projects — projects are to the plan what bricks are to a building. Its main components are: (1) Objectives and targets of development for the plan period; (2) Assessment of human and material resources available; (3) Sector-wise programmes and projects (agriculture, industry, education, health, energy, transport, etc.); (4) Financial outlay — how much money will be spent on each sector and how it will be raised; (5) Annual plans and the Annual Development Programme made within its framework; (6) Machinery for implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the projects included in it.

  17. Describe at least one advantage and one disadvantage of 'Expert Power'.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Expert power is the power a person has because of his special knowledge, skill or expertise, which others need. Advantage: The expert's advice is usually accepted willingly because people trust his knowledge; he can influence decisions and get work done efficiently even without a high formal position — e.g. a technical specialist guiding a whole project. Disadvantage: Expert power is limited to the area of expertise only; outside his field the expert has no influence. Also, if the expert leaves the organization, his power goes with him, and sometimes experts misuse their knowledge or resist sharing it, creating dependence and problems for the organization.

  18. What is the difference between programmed and non-programmed decisions? Explain with examples.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Programmed decisions are taken for routine, repetitive problems for which policies, rules and procedures already exist. They are usually taken at lower levels and involve little risk. Example: sanctioning casual leave of an employee, or re-ordering office stationery when stock falls to a fixed level — the course of action is already decided. Non-programmed decisions are taken for new, unique and unstructured problems for which no ready-made procedure exists. They are taken at higher levels and involve greater risk and judgment. Example: deciding whether the organization should launch an entirely new service, enter a new area, or how to deal with a sudden financial crisis.

  19. In your point of view, why is human resource planning necessary?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Human resource planning means forecasting the future manpower needs of an organization and arranging the right number of people, with the right skills, at the right time. It is necessary because: (1) It ensures that the organization does not face shortage or surplus of staff; (2) It helps in planning recruitment, selection and training in advance; (3) It enables the organization to meet its future goals and expansion programmes; (4) It controls labour cost by avoiding unnecessary hiring; (5) It identifies surplus staff and arranges their retraining or redeployment; (6) It helps in succession planning for key positions. Without HR planning, an organization faces staff crises whenever work expands or employees leave.

  20. When was the Economic Pool established? Give a few main features of the Economic Pool.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The Economic Pool was constituted in 1950. Its main features were: (1) Its officers were drawn from the CSP (Civil Service of Pakistan) and from the accounts and finance services, i.e. it was a pool of officers meant for economic and financial assignments; (2) It was created to provide officers with economic and financial expertise for posts dealing with finance, planning and development; (3) It was, however, not made a regularly constituted service — it remained a pool rather than a formal service with its own cadre rules; (4) Its officers could be posted to economic ministries and organizations as per requirement.

  21. Explain decentralization and discuss the disadvantages of decentralization.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Decentralization means the systematic dispersal of authority to lower levels of the organization, so that decisions are taken close to the place of action instead of everything being decided at the top. Its disadvantages are: (1) Lack of uniformity — different units may adopt different policies and standards; (2) Coordination becomes difficult because many centres of decision making work independently; (3) It requires a large number of trained and competent managers at lower levels, who may not be available; (4) It is costly because services and staff are duplicated in every unit; (5) Control by top management becomes weak, and local units may misuse authority or ignore the organization's overall interest; (6) In emergencies, quick and unified action becomes difficult.

  22. How does 'Pluralistic Decision Making' make public and business management different?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Pluralistic decision making means that decisions are not taken by one authority alone; many groups — political parties, pressure groups, media, public representatives and affected interests — participate in and influence the decision. In public management, decisions are pluralistic: a government manager has to consult and satisfy many groups, seek consensus, and work in the open under public scrutiny. In business (private) management, decision making is comparatively unitary: the owners/managers decide quickly, mainly keeping profit and the market in view, without needing public consensus. Thus pluralism makes public decisions slower and more consultative, but also more democratic, whereas business decisions are faster and profit-oriented.

  23. When a project has been completed, which things should be considered and ensured before closing the project?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Before closing a project, the following should be considered and ensured: (1) All the planned work and outputs of the project have actually been completed according to specifications; (2) Final evaluation of the project is done — whether it achieved its objectives within the approved cost and time; (3) Accounts are finalized — all payments made, advances adjusted and audit completed; (4) Assets, equipment and records of the project are properly handed over to the operating department; (5) Project staff are released or reassigned in an orderly way; (6) A completion report (PC-IV) is prepared and approved by the competent authority; (7) Arrangements exist for the operation and maintenance of the facility created by the project. Only then should the project be formally closed.

  24. Elaborate the difference between 'Public' and 'Private' conflict.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A public conflict is a disagreement or clash of interests that takes place in the public sphere and involves the government, public organizations or the community at large — e.g. a dispute between citizens and a government department, or between two public agencies. It is visible, reported by the media, and is settled through law, courts, administration or political processes. A private conflict, on the other hand, is a disagreement between individuals or private parties in their personal or business capacity — e.g. between an employer and an employee of a private firm, or between two business partners. It is usually settled privately, through negotiation or private legal action, and does not directly involve public authority or public interest.

  25. Give the ways through which the management of an organization can control weak delegation.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Weak delegation means authority is given in name only, or is misused, so work suffers. Management can control it by: (1) Defining clearly, in writing, what authority is delegated, for what purpose and with what limits; (2) Selecting the right person — delegating only to subordinates who are competent and trained for the task; (3) Fixing responsibility along with authority, so the subordinate remains answerable for results; (4) Establishing a reporting and feedback system through which the manager regularly reviews the progress of delegated work; (5) Setting standards and deadlines against which performance can be checked; (6) Keeping the right to intervene, guide and, if necessary, withdraw the authority; (7) Avoiding both over-centralization and total abdication — delegation with control, not delegation with abandonment.

  26. In your opinion, why is a constitution an obligatory document for a state?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A constitution is obligatory for a state because: (1) It establishes the basic structure of the state — legislature, executive and judiciary — and defines their powers, so government works under law and not by the will of individuals; (2) It distributes powers between the federation and the provinces and between different organs, preventing conflict and misuse of power; (3) It guarantees the fundamental rights of citizens and protects them against the state itself; (4) It provides the procedure for making laws, holding elections and changing governments peacefully; (5) All other laws derive their validity from it — any law against the constitution is void. Without a constitution there would be no legal basis of government and no protection of rights, so it is an obligatory document for every modern state.

  27. What are the risks in delegation?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The main risks in delegation are: (1) The subordinate may misuse the delegated authority for personal benefit; (2) An incompetent or inexperienced subordinate may take wrong decisions and damage the organization's work; (3) The subordinate may exceed his authority or act beyond the prescribed limits; (4) Responsibility may become confused — the manager may blame the subordinate although, legally, the manager himself remains responsible to his superiors; (5) Too much delegation may weaken the manager's own control and knowledge of the work; (6) Different subordinates may interpret delegated powers differently, creating inconsistency. These risks are controlled by careful selection, clear limits and regular reporting.

  28. What are the benefits of democracy?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The main benefits of democracy are: (1) Government is of the people — rulers are elected by the citizens and remain accountable to them, and can be removed through elections; (2) Fundamental rights and freedoms of citizens are protected; (3) It promotes equality — every citizen has an equal vote and equal status before law; (4) Decisions are taken after open discussion and debate, which improves their quality and acceptance; (5) It provides a peaceful method of changing governments, avoiding revolutions and violence; (6) It develops a sense of participation and responsibility among citizens; (7) Minorities get protection and a voice through representation and law.

  29. What steps should a manager take for an appraisal decision?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    For a fair appraisal (performance evaluation) decision, a manager should take these steps: (1) First, know the job and the standards of performance expected from the employee; (2) Observe and collect facts about the employee's actual performance during the whole period — not only recent events; (3) Compare actual performance with the set standards and identify strengths and weaknesses; (4) Discuss the appraisal with the employee, listen to his point of view and explain the assessment; (5) Decide the result — promotion, reward, training or corrective action — strictly on merit and recorded evidence; (6) Record the decision on the prescribed performance evaluation report honestly and without bias; (7) Communicate the decision and guide the employee for future improvement.

  30. What is the difference between the Human Relations School and the Behavioral School of thought?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The Human Relations School, founded by Elton Mayo through the Hawthorne Studies, emphasized that social factors, informal groups, workers' feelings and attitudes affect productivity more than physical conditions or money. It treated the organization as a social system and stressed communication and participation. The Behavioral School went further and applied the methods of psychology and social science to the study of individual and group behaviour in organizations. Thinkers like Maslow, McGregor and Herzberg studied motivation, needs, leadership and decision making scientifically. In short, Human Relations focused on group relations and workers' satisfaction, while the Behavioral School studied individual behaviour, motivation and organizational behaviour in a more scientific and broader way.

  31. Briefly explain the main features of the Classical School of Thought.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The Classical School of Thought (associated with F.W. Taylor, Henri Fayol and Max Weber) has the following main features: (1) Emphasis on formal structure — the organization is viewed as a formally designed structure of positions; (2) Division of work and specialization — work is divided so that each person becomes expert in his part; (3) Hierarchy and unity of command — a clear chain of command in which each subordinate receives orders from one boss; (4) Emphasis on efficiency, economy and rationality — there is 'one best way' of doing each job (Taylor's scientific management); (5) Impersonality — work is done according to rules, without personal favour or prejudice (Weber's bureaucracy); (6) It ignores the human and social element — man is treated as an economic being motivated mainly by money. Fayol's 14 principles and POSDCORB-type functions also belong to this tradition.

  32. What is the Hawthorne Experiment? Describe its main points, findings and effects on employee performance.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The Hawthorne Studies were conducted by Elton Mayo and his associates at the Western Electric Company's Hawthorne plant (1924–1932). Main points: the studies began with illumination experiments to see the effect of light on productivity, and continued with relay-assembly tests, interviewing programmes and observation of work groups. Findings: (1) Productivity increased not because of physical conditions but because workers received attention — the famous 'Hawthorne effect'; (2) Social and psychological factors matter more than physical conditions; (3) Informal groups develop their own norms and strongly influence the behaviour and output of members; (4) Workers are not merely 'economic men' — recognition, belonging and communication motivate them; (5) A worker's attitude depends on his social situation inside and outside the factory. Effects: the studies founded the Human Relations School, shifted management's focus from machines to men, and led to better communication, participation and welfare practices, which improved employee performance and satisfaction.

  33. Give examples of departmentalization and the factors on which departmentalization depends.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Departmentalization means grouping activities and people into departments. Its common bases (with examples) are: (1) By function — Finance, Production, Marketing departments; (2) By product — a company having separate departments for cars, trucks and tractors; (3) By territory/region — Northern and Southern regional offices of a bank; (4) By customer — a bank's separate counters/departments for individual and corporate customers; (5) By time — morning, evening and night shifts in a hospital; (6) By process or equipment — spinning, weaving and dyeing sections in a textile mill; (7) By simple number — grouping a fixed number of persons under one supervisor. The choice depends on factors like the nature of work, size of the organization, area of operations, types of customers and the need for specialization and control.

  34. Define 'organization' and explain the steps of organizing.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    An organization is a group of two or more people working together in a structured way to achieve common goals; organizing is the management activity of arranging people and resources to implement plans. The steps of organizing are: (1) Determination of objectives — knowing what is to be achieved; (2) Division of work — breaking the total work into activities and jobs; (3) Departmentalization — grouping similar activities into departments; (4) Assignment of duties — allotting jobs to suitable persons; (5) Delegation of authority — giving each person the authority needed for his job; (6) Establishing relationships — defining who reports to whom (hierarchy) so that coordination becomes possible. Through these steps a formal structure is created in which every person knows his work, authority and superior.

  35. Why is planning important?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Planning is the first and most important function of management. It is important because: (1) It gives direction — everyone knows the goals and the way to achieve them; (2) It reduces uncertainty by anticipating future changes and preparing for them; (3) It ensures economical use of resources, as work is done according to a thought-out scheme instead of guesswork; (4) It provides the basis for coordination between departments; (5) It facilitates control, because performance can be compared with the planned targets; (6) It reduces overlapping and wasteful activities; (7) It helps in decision making by evaluating alternatives in advance. Without planning, an organization drifts and reacts to events instead of shaping them.

  36. What is departmentalization by enterprise (function)? Explain its six functions.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Departmentalization by enterprise means grouping the activities of an undertaking according to the basic functions it performs, as identified by Henri Fayol. Every industrial undertaking performs six groups of activities: (1) Technical activities — production, manufacturing and adaptation of products; (2) Commercial activities — buying, selling and exchange; (3) Financial activities — search for and optimum use of capital; (4) Security activities — protection of property and persons; (5) Accounting activities — stock-taking, balance sheets, costs and statistics; (6) Managerial activities — planning, organizing, commanding, coordinating and controlling. Departments are created on the basis of these functions, e.g. a production department, a sales department and a finance department.

  37. What is 'departmental centralization'? Explain.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Departmental centralization means that within a department (or organization), authority and decision making are concentrated at the top — with the head of the department — instead of being dispersed among subordinate officers. Under it, important and even minor decisions are referred upward, files move to the head for orders, and lower officers mainly implement instructions. It provides uniformity of policy, close control and consistency of decisions, and is useful when subordinates are inexperienced. However, it causes delays, overloads the head, and discourages initiative at lower levels. The opposite arrangement, dispersing authority downward, is called decentralization.

  38. What are the effects of delegation in an organization?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Delegation means assigning work and authority to subordinates. Its effects on the organization are: (1) It reduces the burden of top managers, who can concentrate on policy and important matters; (2) Work is done faster because decisions are taken at the level where the work is actually performed; (3) It trains and develops subordinates, who learn by exercising authority — it prepares future managers; (4) It motivates employees, who feel trusted and show initiative; (5) It improves coordination when authority is matched with responsibility; (6) On the negative side, poor delegation can cause misuse of authority, inconsistent decisions and loss of control. Overall, proper delegation makes the organization more efficient and flexible.

  39. What do you know about the Police Act of the British period?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The Police Act was passed in 1861 by the British government in India after the War of Independence of 1857. Its main features were: (1) It created a uniform, organized police force in the provinces of British India; (2) The police was placed under the control of the provincial government, with an Inspector General at the top in each province; (3) In the districts, the Superintendent of Police worked under the general control of the District Magistrate — a dual control system; (4) The main duties of the police were maintenance of law and order, prevention and detection of crime. The structure and functioning of the modern police in Pakistan is still largely based on the Police Act 1861.

  40. Explain public goods.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Public goods are goods and services which are provided for all members of society, usually by the government, and which have two special characteristics: (1) Non-excludability — once the good is provided, no one can be excluded from its use, even if he does not pay for it; e.g. street lights, national defence, roads and clean air; (2) Non-divisibility/non-rivalry — the use of the good by one person does not reduce its availability for others. Because private firms cannot charge each user, public goods are financed through taxes and supplied by the state. Examples: national defence, public parks, television broadcasts, roads and flood control.

  41. Differentiate between a loan and a grant.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A loan is money given by one party (e.g. the federal government, a bank or a foreign agency) to another with the condition that it will be repaid, usually with interest, within a fixed period. It creates a liability for the borrower. A grant, on the other hand, is a gift of money given for a specific purpose which does not have to be repaid and carries no interest. Grants are usually given to support development projects, weaker units or specific programmes — e.g. federal grants to provinces. In short: a loan must be returned with interest; a grant is non-returnable financial assistance.

  42. Name three factors that affect motivation.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Three important factors affecting motivation are: (1) Needs and desires of the individual — unsatisfied needs (food, security, recognition, achievement, as in Maslow's hierarchy) drive a person to work; the stronger the need, the stronger the motivation; (2) Rewards and incentives — pay, promotion, appreciation and other benefits encourage better performance, while their absence demotivates; (3) Work environment and leadership — fair treatment, good relations with the supervisor, participation in decisions and a healthy atmosphere keep employees motivated, whereas favouritism, fear and poor conditions destroy motivation. Other factors include the nature of the job itself, opportunities for growth and the behaviour of the work group.

  43. How did the Local Government System 2000 ensure the participation of citizens in the system?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The Local Government System introduced in 2000 (Devolution Plan) ensured citizens' participation in the following ways: (1) Elected councils (Union, Tehsil and District Councils) were created at the doorstep of the people, so citizens directly elected their local representatives; (2) Reserved seats were provided for women, peasants/workers and minorities, bringing all sections of society into the councils; (3) Citizen Community Boards (CCBs) were formed through which citizens could identify, execute and monitor local development projects with government matching funds; (4) Monitoring committees of citizens were set up to oversee the working of local offices like police, health and education; (5) Musalihat (reconciliation) committees involved citizens in resolving local disputes. Thus the system shifted power from the bureaucracy to the elected representatives and the community.

  44. What are the benefits of socialization?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Socialization is the process through which an individual learns the values, norms, culture and expected behaviour of a group or organization and becomes its functioning member. Its benefits are: (1) The new member learns his role and what is expected of him, so he performs confidently; (2) It creates a sense of belonging and loyalty towards the organization; (3) It transmits the culture and traditions of the organization to new members, ensuring continuity; (4) It reduces anxiety and mistakes of newcomers and improves cooperation with colleagues; (5) It develops shared values, which makes coordination and teamwork easier; (6) In society, it turns individuals into responsible citizens who respect laws and social norms.

  45. Is public administration a science or an art?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Public administration is a combination of both science and art. It is a science because it has a systematized body of knowledge, definite principles and techniques (like POSDCORB activities — planning, organizing, staffing, directing, coordinating, reporting and budgeting) which can be studied and applied anywhere. It is an art because the actual implementation of rules, policies and principles requires skill, judgment and experience; two administrators with the same rules produce different results according to their personal ability. So, public administration is a science in its theory and an art in its practice.

  46. How can PTV use time departmentalization in its functions? Give advantages.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Pakistan Television can departmentalize by time by organizing its transmissions and staff in time periods/shifts — morning transmission, afternoon programmes, prime-time and late-night transmission — each with its own production and duty staff. For example, agricultural and religious programmes in the morning for farmers and home viewers, children's programmes in the afternoon, and news, dramas and family programmes in the prime time in the evening. Advantages: (1) Programmes reach the right audience at the time suitable for them; (2) Studios, equipment and staff are used throughout the day instead of remaining idle; (3) Viewers get service for more hours; (4) Different categories of staff (news, entertainment, education) can work in their specialized time slots, improving quality.

  47. When you made the decision about which college or university to attend, did you go through each step of rational decision making? If not, explain why?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Normally a student does not follow every step of the rational model in choosing a college or university. The rational steps would require identifying all available institutions, collecting complete information about fees, faculty, ranking and career prospects, evaluating every alternative on fixed criteria and then selecting the best one. In practice, the student usually considers only a few known institutions, relies on incomplete information, advice of family and friends, distance from home and affordability, and selects the first option that appears 'good enough'. This happens because of bounded rationality — limited time, limited information and limited processing capacity — so the decision is a satisficing decision rather than a fully rational, optimizing one.
