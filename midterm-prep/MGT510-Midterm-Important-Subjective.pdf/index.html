  18. Explain the concepts of "appreciation of a system" and "variation" in an organization.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    • Appreciation for a System: A system is a set of functions that work together for an aim. Management must understand the interrelationships between subsystems rather than managing individual parts, avoiding "sub-optimization" caused by internal competition. • Theory of Variation: Industrial processes contain variation. Management must distinguish between Common Causes (80-90% inherent to the system) and Special Causes (10-20% from external disruptions). Tampering with a stable system by treating a common cause as a special cause will only increase variation.

  19. Explain the Customer-Craft or Inspection era.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Until the 19th century, unique goods were handcrafted in small volumes by skilled craftsmen with informal inspections. Industrialization and the factory system divided trades into specialized tasks, decreasing worker autonomy. Mass production led to formal inspection functions using gauges to detect flaws, separate bad parts, and ensure parts were interchangeable.

  25. How do you see the future of Quality Management in the increasing global market place?

    The future will be shaped by demanding global customers who expect high quality, added value, and an excellent organization behind the product (accurate billing, reliable delivery). Opposing economic pressures will demand higher quality at lower prices.   26. What are the different costs included in Total Quality Cost? What is the benefit of keeping it at a minimum?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Total quality cost includes Prevention Costs, Appraisal Costs, and Failure Costs (subdivided into Internal Failure and External Failure Costs). • Benefit: Minimizing total quality costs means producing the right product the first time and every time, which drastically reduces failure expenses and acts as an investment that saves massive revenue (the "return on quality").

  27. How does Crosby comment on the Zero Defect (ZD) Concept? Is it rationally possible?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Crosby states that Zero Defects is not a motivational program but a performance standard aimed at doing the job right the first time by preventing defects. He argues that human error is caused by a lack of attention rather than a lack of knowledge. Because people operate on dual standards (accepting mistakes at work but expecting perfection in personal lives), pledging a conscious effort toward ZD eliminates the massive waste of rework and repair.

  28. What is PDCA? Explain why it is called a cycle.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    PDCA stands for Plan, Do, Check, Act. It is a four-step model used for continuous improvement. It is called a cycle because it is a continuous loop; if a planned change is successfully checked, it is standardized on a wider scale, and if it fails, the loop repeats to identify new opportunities.

  29. Discuss the self-oriented meeting behaviors of group members.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Self-oriented members focus on fulfilling personal needs rather than attending to the needs of the group. Their specific behaviors include: Controlling (dominating), Withdrawing (remaining silent), Seeking attention, Diverting (changing topics to personal interests), Excluding others, Belittling contributions, and Blocking progress by repeatedly raising rejected issues.

  30. What benefits can an organization achieve after getting itself registered with ISO 9001 (2000) for its quality management systems (QMS)?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    After ISO 9001 registration an organization can achieve the following benefits: • A documented, well-defined quality management system that brings consistency to its processes. • Improved process control, efficiency and productivity, with reduced waste and rework. • Greater customer confidence and satisfaction, because registration is independent proof that the organization can consistently meet customer and regulatory requirements. • Better market access and competitiveness, as many customers and tenders require ISO 9001 certification. • Reduction in on-site customer (second-party) audits, since a third-party certificate is accepted instead. • A culture of continual improvement through corrective action, internal audits and management review.

  31. What relationship exists between quality and profits? How can increased quality of design assure increased profits for an organization?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Quality and profits are directly linked. Higher quality reduces the costs of rework, scrap, warranty claims and lost customers, while at the same time it increases customer satisfaction, loyalty and market share. The result is higher revenue at lower cost, hence higher profits. Increased quality of design means designing products with features and performance that customers value more. Such products command better prices, win repeat business and positive word of mouth, and face fewer returns and complaints. Because the design meets customer needs more exactly, the organization sells more at better margins, so increased quality of design assures increased profits.

  32. Briefly describe the important characteristics of quality for a service organization.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Because services are intangible and produced and consumed simultaneously, quality for a service organization is judged through characteristics such as: • Timeliness: the service is provided within an acceptable time frame. • Completeness: the customer receives everything that was promised or needed. • Courtesy: employees are polite, friendly and respectful. • Consistency: the same level of service is delivered every time and by every employee. • Accessibility and convenience: the service is easy to obtain. • Accuracy: the service is performed correctly the first time. • Responsiveness: personnel react quickly and resolve problems promptly.

  33. Briefly describe the important characteristics of quality for a goods-producing (manufacturing) organization.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    For manufactured goods, quality is commonly described through Garvin's eight dimensions: • Performance: the primary operating characteristics of the product. • Features: the secondary characteristics that supplement the basic functioning. • Reliability: the probability of failure-free operation over a specified time. • Conformance: the degree to which the product meets established specifications and standards. • Durability: the amount of use obtained before the product deteriorates or needs replacement. • Serviceability: the speed, courtesy and ease of repair. • Aesthetics: how the product looks, feels, sounds, tastes or smells. • Perceived quality: the customer's overall impression based on brand image and reputation.

  34. How will you critically analyze the work of Juran? Do you think all of his proposed ten steps were originally his own work?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Juran's great contribution was to present quality as a management discipline rather than a purely statistical one. His Trilogy (quality planning, quality control, quality improvement), the concept of fitness for use, the application of the Pareto principle to quality problems, and his Quality Control Handbook gave managers a complete, practical system for managing quality, and his teaching in Japan in 1954 helped transform Japanese industry. Not all of his ten steps were entirely original. Juran openly built on the work of others: statistical methods came from Shewhart, the Pareto principle from Vilfredo Pareto, and project-by-project improvement drew on contemporary management practice. His originality lay in selecting, integrating and translating these ideas into a form that top management could understand and apply throughout the organization.

  35. How does fact-based decision making help an organization in bringing continuous improvement in its processes and practices?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Fact-based decision making means that decisions are taken on the basis of measured data and information rather than opinion or guesswork. It helps continuous improvement in the following ways: • Data reveals where the problems actually are, so improvement effort is directed at the real causes (for example through Pareto analysis). • Measurement establishes a baseline, against which the effect of every improvement can be verified. • Analysis of data distinguishes common causes from special causes of variation, preventing wrong actions and tampering. • Facts create agreement and commitment among employees, because the need for change is visible to everyone. In this way the organization repeatedly identifies weaknesses, improves them and holds the gains, which is the essence of continual improvement.

  36. Explain the SIPOC diagram and its basic elements.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    SIPOC is a high-level process map that summarizes a process on a single page before detailed mapping begins. Its name comes from its five elements: • Suppliers: those who provide the inputs to the process (internal or external). • Inputs: the materials, information, resources or requirements entering the process. • Process: the sequence of steps that transforms the inputs into outputs, shown at a high level (usually 4-6 steps). • Outputs: the products, services or information produced by the process. • Customers: those who receive the outputs (internal or external). SIPOC helps a team agree on the boundaries of a process, identify its suppliers and customers, and see the process as a whole before improving it.

  37. Explain return on quality (ROQ) and its principle.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Return on quality (ROQ) is a financial approach to quality improvement which holds that quality improvement efforts should be directed at those areas that give the greatest financial return. Its principle is that a firm should invest in improving quality only up to the point where the additional revenue and savings generated by the improvement exceed the cost of making it. ROQ therefore links quality initiatives to profitability: it identifies which quality improvements customers value enough to pay for, and avoids spending on quality features that add cost without adding customer value.

  38. Why do personal values influence quality?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Personal values are the deeply held beliefs that guide an individual's behaviour at work. They influence quality because quality is ultimately produced by people: an employee who personally values honesty, responsibility and pride in work will follow standards, report defects and care about the customer even when nobody is watching, while an employee whose values tolerate shortcuts will produce defects. Since an organization's quality culture is the sum of the values of its members, personal values directly shape attitudes toward workmanship, customers and improvement, and therefore the quality of output.

  39. What is meant by the involvement of people in an organization? Explain its benefits.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Involvement of people means that employees at all levels are fully engaged in the organization's work and their abilities are used for the organization's benefit; it is one of the principles of quality management. People are the essence of an organization, and quality cannot be achieved by management instruction alone. Its benefits include: • Motivated, committed employees who take ownership of quality in their own work. • Problems are identified and solved where they occur, by the people who know the processes best. • Better ideas for improvement through suggestions, teams and participation in decision making. • Higher morale, lower absenteeism and turnover, and a stronger quality culture. • Improved communication and trust between management and employees.

  40. Write a note on the Australian Business Excellence Award and the Canadian Award for Business Excellence.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    The Australian Business Excellence Award is Australia's national quality award, administered under the Australian Business Excellence Framework. It assesses organizations against criteria such as leadership, strategy and planning, information and knowledge, people, customer and market focus, innovation and quality, and business results, and is used both for recognition and for self-assessment and improvement. The Canadian Award for Business Excellence (now the Canada Awards for Excellence) is Canada's national award, administered by the National Quality Institute. It recognizes organizations that demonstrate excellence in quality, customer service, leadership, planning, people focus and process management, and it similarly encourages organizations to assess themselves against a national excellence framework.

  41. How does Deming measure quality?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Deming held that quality can only be measured in terms of the customer's needs and satisfaction: 'Quality can be defined only in terms of the agent' — the one who judges it, ultimately the customer. Operationally, he measured quality through the statistical behaviour of processes: a process that is in statistical control, with continually reduced variation around the target, produces predictable uniformity and dependability at low cost, which is Deming's measure of quality. Quality is therefore measured not by inspection results alone but by process capability, reduced variation and the degree to which output meets present and future customer needs.

  42. Which documents are required for registration under ISO 9001?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    For ISO 9001 registration an organization must prepare and maintain documented information including: • A quality policy and measurable quality objectives. • A quality manual describing the QMS, its scope and the interaction of its processes. • Documented procedures required by the standard (control of documents, control of records, internal audit, control of nonconforming product, corrective action and preventive action). • Work instructions and process documents needed for effective planning and control of processes. • Quality records that provide evidence of conformity and of the effective operation of the QMS (audit results, management reviews, training records, inspection and test records, corrective actions, etc.).

  43. What are the factors that affect the performance of an employee within a system?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    According to Deming, most of the variation in an employee's performance is caused by the system in which he works, not by the employee himself. The main factors are: • The design of the job and the process the employee must follow. • The quality of incoming materials, tools, equipment and information provided. • Training and the employee's understanding of what is expected. • Supervision and leadership style, including whether fear is present. • Working conditions and environment. • Feedback: whether the employee receives timely information about his performance. • Motivation, recognition and barriers that rob pride of workmanship, such as numerical quotas and defective inputs. Management, which owns the system, is therefore responsible for most performance problems.

  44. Differentiate between the mechanistic model and the total quality (cultural) view of organizations.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    In the mechanistic model the organization is viewed as a machine: work is divided into specialized tasks, authority flows through a rigid hierarchy, rules and procedures govern behaviour, rationality is structured and formal, and stability is valued while change is resisted and imposed from above. The role of management is to command and control. In the total quality view (closely related to the cultural model) the organization is viewed as a living, learning system bound together by shared values and culture. Structures are flatter and team-based, employees participate in decisions, change and learning are valued in themselves, and management leads, coaches and removes obstacles so that people can satisfy customers and improve processes continuously.

  45. Why do organizations adopt the MBNQA (Baldrige) framework?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Organizations adopt the Malcolm Baldrige National Quality Award (MBNQA) framework because: • It provides a complete set of criteria (leadership, strategic planning, customer focus, measurement and analysis, workforce focus, operations focus and results) against which the whole organization can be assessed. • It enables self-assessment: an organization can identify its strengths and opportunities for improvement even without applying for the award. • It promotes sharing of best practices and gives national recognition and prestige to winners. • It links all improvement activities to business results and customer satisfaction.

  46. List the four parameters (categories) of Juran's 'fitness for use'.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Juran defined quality as fitness for use, which has four parameters: • Quality of design: the features and performance designed into the product to meet customer needs. • Quality of conformance: the degree to which the produced product conforms to the design. • Availability: the readiness of the product for use when needed, covering reliability and maintainability. • Field service: the support provided to the customer after delivery, such as installation, spares and complaint handling.

  47. What is Juran's contribution to total quality management?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Juran's main contributions are: • The definition of quality as 'fitness for use'. • The Juran Trilogy: quality planning, quality control and quality improvement. • Application of the Pareto principle ('the vital few and the trivial many') to quality problems. • The project-by-project, breakthrough approach to quality improvement and his ten steps to quality improvement. • His Quality Control Handbook (1951) and his teaching of quality management to Japanese industry from 1954, which greatly influenced Japan's quality revolution.

  48. What are the purposes of the Deming Prize and the European Quality Award?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Both awards exist to recognize excellence and to promote quality improvement. The Deming Prize, instituted in Japan in 1951 by the Union of Japanese Scientists and Engineers (JUSE), is awarded to companies and individuals that have achieved outstanding results through company-wide quality control based on statistical methods. The European Quality Award, launched by the European Foundation for Quality Management (EFQM) in 1992, recognizes European organizations that demonstrate excellence in their management and results. Beyond recognition, both awards provide assessment models that any organization can use for self-evaluation and improvement.

  49. Explain the implications of 'psychology' as a component of Deming's Profound Knowledge.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Psychology helps us understand people, their interactions and why they behave as they do. People differ from one another, learn in different ways and at different speeds, and have an innate need for relationships, self-esteem, dignity and joy in work. A leader must recognize these differences and use them to optimize everyone's abilities. When managers do not understand psychology, they do not understand motivation, they resort to carrots and sticks and other forms of induced motivation that have no positive long-term effect, they do not understand the process of change and the resistance to it, they revert to coercive and paternalistic approaches, and they create cynicism, demoralization, guilt, resentment and turnover. The implication is that management must rely on intrinsic motivation, remove fear, and preserve people's positive innate attributes instead of destroying them with ranking, quotas and extrinsic controls.

  50. What were Taylor's goals to increase productivity, and what were the drawbacks of his approach?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Frederick W. Taylor, in his system of scientific management, aimed to increase productivity by scientifically studying each job, breaking it into simple tasks, setting time standards, selecting and training workers for specific tasks, separating planning from execution, and paying piece-rate incentives. The drawbacks were that work became repetitive and dehumanizing, workers were treated as machine parts with no say in decisions, quality suffered because speed was rewarded over workmanship, and the approach ignored the social and psychological needs of people, creating conflict between management and labour.

  51. Briefly explain how management can be improved.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Management can be improved by adopting the philosophy of total quality: • Top management must accept long-term responsibility for quality and show constancy of purpose instead of chasing short-term results. • Managers should acquire profound knowledge — appreciation of the system, understanding of variation, theory of knowledge and psychology. • Decisions should be based on data and facts rather than opinion and blame. • Fear should be driven out, barriers between departments broken down, and employees trained, empowered and involved in improvement. • Processes should be improved continuously (for example through PDCA) rather than relying on inspection and exhortation. • Suppliers should be treated as long-term partners, and success measured by customer satisfaction.

  52. Explain Shewhart's concept of quality.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Walter A. Shewhart, the father of statistical quality control, viewed quality as having two sides: a subjective side — what the customer thinks, feels and wants (the goodness of a product in use) — and an objective side — the measurable physical characteristics of the product, independent of human opinion. He introduced the control chart to distinguish common-cause from special-cause variation, and his cycle (specification, production, inspection) became the origin of the PDCA cycle. For Shewhart, quality is achieved by understanding and reducing variation in processes.

  53. What processes are involved in quality control?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Following Juran, quality control is a regulatory process consisting of three steps: • Evaluate actual operating performance (measure the quality of the process or product). • Compare actual performance with quality goals and standards. • Act on the difference (take corrective action to restore the status quo and bring performance back to the goal). Quality control thus detects and corrects sporadic departures from standards, holding the gains achieved by quality improvement.

  54. What benefits are derived from the use of mutually beneficial supplier relationships and the process approach by an organization?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Mutually beneficial supplier relationships enhance the ability of both parties to create value: the organization gets reliable, consistent inputs, joint problem solving and lower total cost, while the supplier gets stable demand and clear requirements; both share information and improvement plans. The process approach — managing activities as interrelated processes — gives the organization control over the linkages between processes, more predictable and consistent results, efficient use of resources, and the ability to focus improvement on the processes that matter most to customers.

  55. On what basis is the Deming Prize awarded to companies? Elaborate your answer with the help of an example.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    The Deming Prize (Deming Application Prize) is awarded by the Union of Japanese Scientists and Engineers (JUSE) to companies that have achieved distinctive performance improvement through the application of company-wide quality control (CWQC) based on statistical thinking. A committee examines the company against criteria such as: management policies and their deployment, organization and operations, education and dissemination, collection and use of information, analysis, standardization, control, quality assurance, results achieved, and future plans. The examination includes document review and on-site visits, and the company must show sustained results over several years. For example, a manufacturing company that deployed quality policies to every department, trained all employees in statistical methods, standardized its processes and could demonstrate several years of improving defect rates and customer satisfaction would qualify for the prize.

  56. Why did Deming believe that managers should stop making decisions purely based on cost?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Deming's fourth point is to end the practice of awarding business on the basis of price tag alone. A decision based purely on initial cost ignores the cost of poor quality that follows: cheap purchased items that vary, fail or need inspection and rework raise the total cost of the finished product. Managers should instead minimize total cost and build long-term relationships of loyalty and trust with single suppliers, because lowest initial price often means highest overall cost and loss of quality.

  57. The European Quality Award is divided into two parts. To whom are these awarded?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    The European Quality Award was divided into two categories so that organizations of different sizes could compete fairly: one award for large companies and their business units, and a separate award for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs). (A category for public-sector organizations was added later.)

  58. What were the 'structured rationality' and the philosophy towards change in the mechanistic model?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Structured rationality means that the organization is run like a machine through formal, logical arrangements: goals are broken down into specialized tasks, each job is precisely defined, authority follows a clear hierarchy, and behaviour is governed by written rules, procedures and standards. Decisions are expected to be rational and impersonal, based on position rather than on the person. The philosophy towards change in this model is conservative: the system is designed for stability and predictability, so change is viewed with suspicion, is resisted by the structure, and when it occurs it is planned and imposed from the top in a controlled manner. Learning and adaptation from below are not expected.

  59. How does Juran's philosophy fit well into the existing management structure of an organization?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Juran argued that managing for quality uses the same universal processes as managing any other function, so it fits the existing management structure without revolutionary change. Just as finance is managed through financial planning, financial control and financial improvement, quality is managed through quality planning, quality control and quality improvement (the Juran Trilogy). Managers can therefore apply familiar concepts — goals, budgets, measurement, projects and accountability — to quality, assigning responsibility within the normal hierarchy and project organization they already have.

  60. How is quality defined by the five leaders of the quality revolution?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    • Deming: quality is a predictable degree of uniformity and dependability, at low cost, suited to the market — defined by the customer. • Juran: quality is fitness for use. • Crosby: quality is conformance to requirements. • Feigenbaum: quality is the total composite of product and service characteristics through which the product in use will meet the expectations of the customer (total quality control). • Ishikawa: quality means quality of work, quality of service, quality of information, quality of process, quality of people and quality of objectives — in short, consumer satisfaction through company-wide quality control.

  61. Why did Deming believe that a business should not exist simply for profit?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Deming held that the aim of a business is to stay in business and provide jobs through innovation, research, constant improvement and maintenance — not merely to make money. Profit is a result and a means of survival, not the purpose. A business that exists simply for profit focuses on short-term figures: it cuts training, maintenance and quality, squeezes suppliers and employees, and destroys the long-term capability on which future profits depend. Constancy of purpose — serving customers and society better over the long run — is what keeps a company alive; profit then follows as a by-product of quality and customer loyalty.

  62. What are appraisal costs? Explain with examples.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Appraisal costs are the costs incurred to determine the degree of conformance of products and services to quality requirements — the costs of inspection, testing and measurement. They are part of the (unavoidable) cost of conformance. Examples include: • Incoming inspection and testing of purchased materials. • In-process inspection and laboratory testing. • Final inspection and functional testing of finished products. • Calibration and maintenance of measuring and test equipment. • Quality audits and field performance testing. • Salaries of inspection and test personnel.

  63. Discuss performance appraisal as a barrier to pride of workmanship.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Deming's twelfth point requires removing barriers that rob people of pride of workmanship, and he identified the annual performance appraisal (merit rating) as one of the biggest such barriers. Appraisal judges the person although most variation in performance comes from the system, which the individual does not control. It therefore: • Destroys teamwork, because individuals compete for ratings instead of cooperating. • Encourages short-term performance and gaming of figures at the expense of long-term improvement. • Creates fear, resentment and demoralization among those rated low. • Leaves people bitter and unable to take pride in work for which the system, not they, is responsible. Other barriers to pride of workmanship include numerical quotas, defective materials and tools, poor training and supervisors who do not listen.

  64. What are the characteristics of a quality management system (QMS)?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    A quality management system is the set of interrelated processes an organization uses to direct and control quality. Its characteristics include: • It is documented (policy, objectives, manual, procedures and records) and formally approved by top management. • It is based on the process approach and the PDCA cycle. • It is customer-focused and aims at continual improvement. • Responsibilities and authorities are clearly defined. • Its performance is monitored, measured, audited and reviewed by management, with decisions based on facts.

  65. Differentiate between the Statistical Quality Control era and the Strategic Quality Management era.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Statistical Quality Control (SQC) era: • Primary concern: control of processes through statistical techniques. • Quality viewed as a problem to be solved, mainly within manufacturing. • Emphasis: product uniformity with reduced inspection, using tools such as control charts. • Role of quality professionals: troubleshooting and the application of statistical methods. Strategic Quality Management era: • Primary concern: the strategic impact of quality on competitiveness. • Quality viewed as a competitive opportunity defined by the customer. • Emphasis: the market and customer needs, with quality integrated into strategic planning. • Role of quality professionals: strategic goal setting, planning and management of quality at the top level.

  66. Enlist the three eras in the evolution of quality management and state the primary concern of each.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    • Inspection era: quality was achieved by detecting and sorting out defective products after production; primary concern — detection. • Statistical Quality Control era: statistical techniques (pioneered by Shewhart) were used to control processes and reduce variation; primary concern — control. • Quality Assurance / Strategic era: quality was built into the whole production chain and later managed strategically for competitive advantage; primary concern — coordination of the entire chain, and finally strategic impact.

  67. What is the difference between quality planning and quality control?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Quality planning is the process of identifying the customers and their needs, developing products and processes that can meet those needs, and deploying the plans to the operating levels; it is done before operations begin and sets the quality goals. Quality control is the operational process of meeting the goals during operations: evaluating actual performance, comparing it with the goals, and acting on the difference. Planning therefore creates the plan and the standards, while control detects and corrects deviations from them in day-to-day work.

  68. Enlist the functions performed by a quality department. Also enlist the stages of the product life cycle.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Functions of a quality department typically include: • Quality planning and setting quality standards and policies. • Quality assurance: designing and auditing the quality system. • Quality control: inspection, testing and measurement of materials, processes and products. • Analysis of quality costs and quality data, and reporting to management. • Corrective action follow-up and customer complaint handling. • Training and promotion of quality awareness, and coordination of improvement projects. Stages of the product life cycle: introduction, growth, maturity and decline. Quality activities apply at every stage, from design and development through production, delivery and after-sales service.

  69. Compare the views of Crosby and Deming on quality.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Crosby defines quality as 'conformance to requirements' and manages it through his four absolutes: quality is conformance to requirements; the system of quality is prevention; the performance standard is zero defects; and the measurement of quality is the price of nonconformance. His 14 steps are a prescriptive, company-wide programme heavily based on management commitment and employee motivation toward zero defects. Deming defines quality in terms of the customer's present and future needs and a predictable degree of uniformity and dependability. His approach rests on profound knowledge, statistical control of processes, continual improvement through PDCA and his 14 points; he opposed slogans, targets and exhortation, holding management (the system) responsible for most quality problems. In short, Crosby gives a managerial programme centred on requirements and zero defects, while Deming gives a philosophy centred on the customer, variation and the system.

  70. What are the four parts of Deming's System of Profound Knowledge?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    • Appreciation for a system: understanding the organization as a network of interdependent components working together toward a common aim. • Knowledge of variation: understanding common and special causes of variation and the difference between them. • Theory of knowledge: understanding that knowledge comes from theory, which can be tested and used for prediction; experience alone teaches nothing. • Psychology: understanding people, their differences, intrinsic motivation and need for dignity and joy in work.

  71. How was quality assured during the Industrial Revolution?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Before industrialization, skilled craftsmen controlled the quality of their own work through the craft/guild system. With the Industrial Revolution and the factory system, work was divided among many unskilled workers, so quality could no longer rest with the individual craftsman. Quality was assured by inspection: foremen and later full-time inspectors checked output and sorted out defective items at the end of production. This inspection-based approach — detecting defects rather than preventing them — dominated until statistical methods were introduced in the twentieth century.

  72. Differentiate between a document and a record.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    A document is information that states what is to be done — such as a quality manual, procedure, work instruction or specification. Documents are living: they are approved, issued, reviewed and revised, and users must work to the current version. A record is evidence of what has been done — such as inspection results, audit reports and training records. Records are not revised; they are completed at the time of the activity and retained as proof that requirements were met.
