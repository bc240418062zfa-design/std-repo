  1. What is an idea? Discuss the prime jobs of a television producer.
    Topic: Creativity and Idea Generation

    An idea is any conception existing in the mind as a result of mental understanding, awareness or activity. It is a thought, a notion, an impression, an opinion, a point of view, a belief, a plan of action, a concept developed by the mind and a theme. The prime job of a producer is to generate an idea of a programme and then carry it through every stage: presentation and refinement of the idea, developing a concept, deciding the contents, engaging a researcher, getting the script written, hunting the talent (actors, anchors, newscasters), preparing the Programme Budget Estimate, making the arrangements, coordinating with different sections, discussing the set with the set designer for indoor programmes, carrying out reconnaissance of locations for outdoor programmes, discussing shots with the cameraman, planning, casting, scheduling and supervising rehearsals and recording, and finally editing, audio mixing, graphics, titles and promos until the programme is ready for telecast.

  2. Describe the three phases of preparing a television programme.
    Topic: Creativity and Idea Generation

    The preparation of a TV programme passes through three phases. (1) Pre-production phase: this is the first stage and involves the "library work", i.e. research and script writing; the basic ideas and approaches of the production are developed and set in motion in this phase. (2) Production phase: this is the second stage and involves the "leg work", i.e. field and studio work, in which everything comes together in a final performance, live or recorded. (3) Post-production phase: this is the third stage and involves the "laboratory work", i.e. editing and mixing; it also includes striking the sets, handling final financial obligations and evaluating the effect of the programme. It is rightly said that the most important phase of production is pre-production, because a production misdirected at that stage cannot be saved by any amount of time, talent or editing expertise.

  3. What are the purposes of television?
    Topic: Creativity and Idea Generation

    Television serves many purposes for its viewers. The purposes of television listed in the handouts are: information, education, edification, acculturation, persuasion, propaganda, entertainment, amusement and info-tainment. Through these purposes television informs the public about events, educates and improves its audience, shapes culture and opinion, persuades and propagates ideas, and at the same time entertains and amuses the viewers.

  4. Discuss the pre-requisites (qualities) of a creative producer/director.
    Topic: The Creative Producer/Director

    A creative producer has to present the idea and work till its execution to make the programme ready for telecast, so he/she must possess certain characteristics and qualities of mind and soul. These include being creative, innovative, analytical, logical and critical, with deep observation, vast study, visualization power, decision power, sense of humour, sense of proportion, common sense, aesthetic sense, great exposure, editorial judgment, initiative and self-starting ability. A producer must also be a team leader with forbearance, tolerance, practical experience, general knowledge, courage, confidence, time management, accuracy, precision, conviction, consistency, ethics, communication skills, motivation, determination, patience, fairness in controversy and objectivity, besides being unbiased, unprejudiced, investigative, inquisitive, informative, technically sound, of pleasant personality, and capable of command and control.

  5. Explain selective exposure, selective perception and selective retention.
    Topic: The Creative Producer/Director

    These three terms describe how audiences select and process media content. Selective exposure means we use the medium we want — out of the many channels and programmes available, viewers choose the one they prefer at a particular time. Selective perception means we understand the things we like — viewers interpret media content according to their own liking and point of view. Selective retention means we remember the content we need — out of everything watched, the audience retains only the material that is useful or important to them. A producer must keep these tendencies in mind and prepare the programme to the utmost of his/her abilities.

  6. What ingredients are included in the presentation of an idea for a TV programme? Explain any five.
    Topic: Refining an Idea for Production

    The presentation of an idea is an art; whatever a producer visualizes about a future programme should be presented in black and white for the consideration of the authorities. The ingredients of the presentation are: Title — the name of the programme, which is the face of the programme and should be representative, comprehensive, self-explanatory and meaningful. Type — the form of programme, e.g. drama, magazine show, documentary or specific-audience programming. Language — the mode of communication (international, national, provincial or regional). Genre — the kind of contents, e.g. comedy, tragedy, thriller, romance, fiction. Frequency — the rate of telecast (daily, weekly, fortnightly, monthly). Duration — the length of time of the programme. Target audience — the potential viewers. Format — the arrangement of sequences and the order of appearance of performers. Theme — the central idea of the programme, normally called the one-liner in the telecast industry.

  7. Define concept, construct and variable. Differentiate between independent, dependent, intervening and extraneous variables with examples.
    Topic: Concept Development and Research

    A concept is a general notion, a conception, a comprehension or a thing formed in the mind by mentally combining all the characteristics of something; for example, "mass" is a concept. A construct is the combination of two concepts — "mass" and "media" are two concepts, while "mass media" is a construct that gives a third meaning as a whole phrase. A variable is the changing value of the quantity or quality of a thing or concept. An independent variable is the variable whose value is independent to change, denoted by "x" (e.g. speed, or media exposure). A dependent variable is the variable whose value depends on the independent variable, denoted by "y" (e.g. distance covered, or socialization from media). An intervening variable affects the relation between the independent and dependent variables (e.g. quality of the road; tune-in time). An extraneous variable does not directly influence that relation and is not as pertinent (e.g. traffic conditions; attention of the viewer).

  8. What is research? Write down the steps of the research procedure.
    Topic: Concept Development and Research

    Research is constant navigation to know the truth; it is a continuous effort to dig out facts, an endeavour to find something new, a new interpretation of existing knowledge and an addition to already present knowledge. According to Kerlinger, it is the systematic, controlled, critical and empirical investigation of hypothetical propositions about presumed relations among natural phenomena. The research procedure consists of the following steps: (1) selection of a problem/topic, (2) framing the research question, (3) making a hypothesis, (4) review of available literature, (5) experimental design, (6) data collection, (7) analysis and interpretation of data, (8) presentation of research, and (9) replication. Research is the base of any programme, especially talk shows and documentaries, and it makes a production factually and technically sound.

  9. What are the basic ingredients of a plot? Discuss the components of a good script.
    Topic: Script Writing

    The plot of any story, drama, novel or play has five basic ingredients: Introduction (the beginning), Complication (development of complications), Culmination (the climax of conflict), Solution (resolution of issues) and Conclusion (the ending). A good script has three components: Purpose, which justifies its viewership; Simplicity, which makes it look true; and Familiarity, which makes it directly communicable. The situations in the play should be easily assimilated, there must be no loophole in the story, and the conflict and its resolution, the setting of the problem and the explanation of factors involved should all be woven properly. The characters must be intensely human and close to real life, because the more universal the theme, the greater the audience.

  10. Write down the Do's and Don'ts of script writing.
    Topic: Script Writing

    Do's of script writing: the opening should be catchy to hold the audience; the style, diction and contents should introduce the play; logical development of the story is necessary; the tempo should be fast with planned forward action; the element of suspense is vital and surprise grabs attention; language should be used according to the situation and story; a definite ending is a must; all problems should be solved in the end and all characters accounted for; a tragic ending may be satisfactory if a moral can be deduced; and policy matters should be dealt with carefully. Don'ts of script writing: profanity and blasphemy; vulgarity and obscenity; belittling of any race or caste; criticism of any religion or sect; making physical deformities humorous; crime, murder and suicide should be discouraged; degrading any colour or creed; use of poor grammar; offending humour and offensive statements; a too talky script; unintentional interruptions; and words with multiple meanings should be shunned.

  11. What is an actor-proof script? Also explain storyboard, camera shot sheet and editing log.
    Topic: Script Writing

    A television script should be an "actor-proof" script, written in such a way that it cannot be misinterpreted by the performers; therefore its sentences should be short, crisp, clear and simple. A storyboard is the outline of the production: a visual, shot-by-shot depiction of the project in which critical frames are sketched much like a comic strip, so that clients, crew and financiers can share and approve the director's vision; the director decides in it the plan of action and how the shooting is to be done. A camera shot sheet is prepared from the finished script and is simply a breakdown for each camera position, listing each camera's shots in the order they appear in the script — for a four-camera studio shoot, four shot sheets are generated, one for each camera. An editing log is an essential preparation for post-production: it allows the editor to note down the in and out points of all visual and auditory material on the source tapes, with notes on the usefulness of individual shots or sound bites; the use of SMPTE time code, an absolute frame reference, makes editing logs all the more effective.

  12. Discuss the pre-production phase with reference to indoor and outdoor productions.
    Topic: Pre-Production Phase

    The pre-production phase is the foremost stage and the foundation of any production. Technically there are two kinds of productions, indoor and outdoor, and both require different prerequisites. For an indoor production the requirements include set designing (the director sits with the designer to discuss the set to be erected in the studio), properties or "props" (furniture, decoration pieces, room accessories and other items to be used on the set), wardrobe, makeup and get-up of the artists. For an outdoor production, reconnaissance is necessary — the survey of the location, in which the director along with the cameraman inspects the spot and plans camera placement, shooting angles and other recording matters; travelling expenses for transportation and accommodation of staff and artists, and a surplus budget for unforeseen expenditures must also be catered for. In both cases the producer first prepares the budget, plans the talent, the ENG facilities (cameraman, audio engineer and light man) and the performers, and studies and digests the script through three readings before casting begins.

  13. What is the Programme Budget Estimate (PBE)? Write its essentials and differentiate it from the Programme Budget Proposal (PBP).
    Topic: Pre-Production Phase

    The Programme Budget Estimate (PBE) is prepared by the producer, who manages the finances of the production on behalf of the organization or channel. In it, the whole cost of direct and indirect expenditures is mentioned for approval from the finance department. The PBE is made for a complete quarter, that is, thirteen weeks, and all expected expenses are catered in it. Its essentials are: talent fee, script fee, research fee, transportation, logistics, accommodation and unforeseen expenses. The Programme Budget Proposal (PBP), on the other hand, is prepared for each episode and is always sent before any recording; it includes the payments of the different talents in the programme so that the accounts department can prepare the cheques well in time. Thus the PBE is a quarterly estimate of all production costs, while the PBP is the per-episode proposal sent before recording.

  14. Discuss casting in a TV programme. What does a director watch for in an artist, and what types of rehearsals are held?
    Topic: Selection of Content and Talent

    Casting is the selection of the required talent for a programme. The producer must be very keen at this stage, because a poor cast can ruin a very good script, while a carefully chosen cast can make even a poor script into a fairly decent show. It is best to cast an artist by watching him/her on screen with proper makeup and getup, delivering the original lines and dialogues. During auditions the director watches for: displaying an understanding of lines, varying speed according to the material, expressing emotions without shouting, giving expressions, stresses and pauses, throwing cue lines to co-artists, and delivering the dialogue in true spirit. After casting, rehearsals are fixed, which include: simple rehearsal; dry rehearsal (in the studio or on location with talk-back microphones); camera rehearsal (for the placement and movement of cameras and all instruments); full rehearsal; and final rehearsal, held just before recording, in which the director obtains the accurate timing of the programme. The first rehearsal is generally informal — merely a reading of the script without microphone or camera, in which the director explains his idea of the script and the effect he wishes to create.

  15. Write the checklists for outdoor and indoor production planning.
    Topic: Programme Planning

    For an outdoor production (mostly single camera, as in news coverage, media briefings, dramas and documentaries), the checklist includes: camera, neck microphone, hand microphone, cable microphone, cordless microphone, baby light, kit light, sun gun, monitor, reflector, tapes, battery charger, transport, makeup and costumes, confirmation of participants and location management; for outstation recordings, tour authorization, accommodation and boarding and lodging are added, and the technical staff required is the director, cameraman, light man, boom operator and driver. For an indoor production (mostly three cameras, as in talk shows, current affairs discussions and dramas), the checklist includes: studio requisition, three cameras, microphones (neck, hand, cable and cordless), set installed, lighting, panel/switcher, camera control unit, audio console, cassette recorders and players, functional monitors, tapes, makeup, properties, wardrobe, costumes, confirmation of participants and gate passes; the technical staff required includes the director, cameramen, lighting director, light men, boom operator, CCU engineer, audio engineer, recording engineer, set designer, studio hands and floor managers.

  16. What is an OB van? Explain EFP (Electronic Field Production).
    Topic: Programme Planning

    An OB (Outside Broadcast) van is a vehicle that has the provision of panel, switcher, camera control unit, audio console, cassette recorders and cassette players; it provides the director with all the technical facilities of a studio outside the studio, and is used for outdoor broadcasts such as live coverage of national events and sports. EFP (Electronic Field Production) is the makeshift studio environment created outdoors when the OB van is used and the cameras are patched, i.e. they give their output to the panel directly. In other words, EFP is television production that takes place outside a formal studio in a practical location, with the emphasis on high-quality multi-camera photography, advanced graphics and sound, as in awards shows, concerts and sporting events.

  17. Discuss the types of sets used in television production.
    Topic: Production Phase: Sets and Design

    TV sets are classified in several ways. By construction: (1) Traditional sets, with arches and building designs, mostly used in historical plays; these are also called hard or real sets because they are made of hard material like wood and metal. (2) Contemporary sets, mostly graphical with computer-generated effects; these are called soft or virtual sets because they are made by computer graphics using chroma key and animations. (3) Lighting sets, in which different lighting patterns and colours give a symbolic representation of the mood of the programme. (4) Black screen sets, in which the studio wall horizon is used without light, giving an impact of darkness with a spot light on the characters. (5) Single screen, two-screen and three-screen sets, used according to the number of persons appearing, e.g. two-screen sets for two persons talking in a talk show and three-screen sets for panelists on both sides of an anchor. By design category: non-representational sets provide general background scenery (news, interviews, talk shows); representational sets serve fictional story content (drama, documentary, magazine shows); and abstract sets are stylized sets used for musical concerts, mega shows and variety shows.

  18. Explain the different camera movements used in television production.
    Topic: Camera Work

    Camera functions are grouped into four kinds of movement. (1) Movement of the lens of the camera only: zoom in (image coming close to the viewer), zoom out (image going away from the viewer), focus (image becoming sharp) and defocus (image getting blur). (2) Movement of the camera itself: pan right, pan left (horizontal movement of the camera head), tilt up and tilt down (vertical movement of the camera head). (3) Movement of the camera with tripod or pedestal: dolly in (camera moved close to the object), dolly out (camera moved away from the object), track right and track left (camera moved to the right or left side of the object); pedestal movement also includes truck (left or right) and arc (around the subject at a fixed distance). (4) Use of crane and jib: in a crane arrangement the cameraman sits on equipment like a crane for free movement over a crowd, as in music shows, concerts and award ceremonies, while in a jib arrangement the camera is placed on a triangular stay-sail-like equipment and the operator controls it from the ground. Movements are also classified as primary (movement of the subject), secondary (movement of the camera) and tertiary (movement caused by a sequence of shots or transitions such as cuts, dissolves, fades and wipes).

  19. What is a shot? Write down the types of shots used in television.
    Topic: Camera Work

    A shot is the frame being captured through the camera lens — the picture being photographed or the scene being recorded. The types of shots used in television production are: Extreme Close Up (ECU), Big Close Up (BCU), Close Up (CU), Medium Close Up or bust shot (MCU), Medium Shot (MS), Medium Long Shot (MLS), Long Shot (LS) and Very Long Shot (VLS). In addition, there are the Top/High angle shot (looking downwards), the Low angle shot (looking upwards), the Two shot (which can be CU, MCU or MS), the Over The Shoulder shot (OTS), the moving-subject-walking-into-space shot and the differential shot. Special lenses support these shots: the wide-angle lens is used when there is small space available for camera movement and gives a broader perspective, while the fish-eye lens creates a dramatic effect and shows a panoramic view of the scene.

  20. Define a microphone. Discuss the types of microphones according to technique and direction.
    Topic: Light and Audio

    A microphone is a transducer that converts acoustic or sound energy into electrical energy; without it, audio in media would not exist. According to technique, microphones are of three types: moving coil/dynamic, ribbon, and capacitor/condenser. According to direction (directional characteristics), they are: omni-directional or multi-directional, which pick up sound all around; bi-directional, which pick up sound from the front and rear; and uni-directional, which pick up sound from the front only. The unidirectional family is further divided into cardioid (about 180 degrees of acceptance, with a heart-shaped pattern), super-cardioid (about 160 degrees), hyper-cardioid and lobar (about 140 degrees, including shotgun microphones), and parabolic, the most highly directional. According to usage, microphones include neck microphones, hand microphones, cordless and cable microphones, and boom microphones (boom handy for outdoor and boom on tripod for indoor use).

  21. Differentiate between dynamic and condenser microphones. What are phantom power and dual redundancy?
    Topic: Light and Audio

    The dynamic (moving-coil) microphone is considered the most rugged professional microphone. Its advantages are that it is rugged, lower in cost and requires no power; its disadvantages are lower sensitivity and power output, larger size and weight, slower response time, and it is not the best choice for maximum audio quality. It works when sound waves move a coil of fine wire, attached to a diaphragm, within the magnetic field of a permanent magnet, generating a small electrical current. The condenser (capacitor) microphone is capable of top-notch audio quality: its advantages are greater sensitivity, better audio quality and extremely small size; its disadvantages are higher self-noise, fragility, higher cost, and proneness to weather problems and RF interference. It works on the capacitor principle, in which sound waves cause fluctuations in an electrical charge that must be amplified by a pre-amplifier. Phantom power is the arrangement in which the power supply for a condenser microphone is built into the audio mixer or board, so the mic cord both delivers the signal to the mixer and carries power to the pre-amp. Dual redundancy is the technique of using two miniature condenser microphones together on important productions, so that if one mic's battery fails, the other can immediately be switched on.

  22. What is phase cancellation? How can it be reduced or eliminated?
    Topic: Light and Audio

    Phase cancellation is the phenomenon that results in low-level and hollow-sounding audio when two or more microphones pick up sound from the same audio source. Because the sounds arrive at the microphones at slightly different times, they end up out of phase and, to various degrees, cancel each other out. There are four ways to reduce or eliminate phase cancellation when multiple microphones are used on a set: (1) place the microphones as close as possible to the sound sources; (2) use directional microphones; (3) turn down microphones any time they are not needed; and (4) carefully check and vary the distances between the sound sources and the multiple microphones — a speaker's microphone should be placed at one-third or less the distance of the next nearest microphone.

  23. Describe the television studio complex (control room and studio floor) and differentiate between production staff and production crew.
    Topic: Day of Recording and Production

    The television studio complex consists of two main areas. The control room is the operational command centre where programme elements are directed and coordinated; it has a panel for recording, a switcher for inter-cutting and switching between the outputs of different cameras, a camera control unit, an audio console, a recorder and a communication system for the cameramen, artists and audience. The studio floor is the area where the actual activity of production takes place — the set of a talk show, drama, news bulletin or play with its performers. The production staff are concerned primarily with the programme's content and development and include the producer, director, assistant director, lighting director, designer and production assistant. The production crew are concerned with equipment operation and include the cameraman, audio engineer, audio technicians, video technicians, floor manager, studio hands, studio supervisor, switcher and recording engineer. Television production is a team operation, and technical as well as human communication and harmony are vital for its success.

  24. What is linear editing? Differentiate between linear editing and non-linear editing (NLE).
    Topic: Editing, Mixing and Effects

    There are two types of editing techniques. Linear editing is time-bound, because a particular time code is followed to access different data; it is done on and by videocassettes and tapes, using videocassette players and recorders. In the editing suite, Player 1 supplies the basic (main) video, Player 2 supplies the secondary data such as background voice, graphics and names to be superimposed or inserted on the primary video, and the recorder takes the final output given by both players. Non-linear editing (NLE) is not time-bound, because with a single keystroke or mouse click different data can be accessed; it is done on computers, as the data is transferred from tapes to computers, and various software programmes are used in it. NLE is a modern, non-destructive method that involves being able to access any frame in a video clip with the same ease as any other. The choice between linear and non-linear editing depends on the producer, according to the needs of the production and the available technical facilities.

  25. Define news. Discuss the qualities, elements and factors of newsworthiness.
    Topic: Selection of the News

    News is something new, fresh, unusual, unique, strange and exclusive. It may be defined as an accurate fact or idea that will interest a large number of viewers; strangeness, abnormality, unexpectedness and nearness of an event all add to interest in a news story. As Lord Northcliffe said, "if a dog bites a man it is no news, but if a man bites a dog it is news." The qualities of news are that it should be accurate, balanced, truthful, recent, exact, objective, impartial, unbiased, concise, brief and clear. The elements of news are: timeliness/immediacy, proximity, consequence, prominence, suspense, mystery, oddity, conflict, progress, action, interest and human emotions. Broadcast news follows twelve factors of newsworthiness: timeliness, proximity, exceptional quality, possible future impact, prominence, conflict, the number of people involved or affected, consequence, human interest, pathos, shock value and the titillation component.

  26. Explain the inverted pyramid style of news writing and the contents of a news story.
    Topic: Writing the News

    The news story is radically different from all other kinds of composition. A story, novel or play opens gradually, gives the major information in the middle and ends soon after the climax, following the upright pyramid style of fiction writing. A news story, on the contrary, starts with the climax and leaves the details for the closing sentences; it opens with a bang, mentions all concrete things at the start and then describes the relevant detail. This is the inverted pyramid (inverse triangle) style. The contents of news are the five Ws and one H: What (what happened), When (when it occurred), Where (the place of the incident), Who (the persons involved), Why (the reason of the issue) and How (the sequence of the event). The lead is the heading of the news that contains these five Ws and one H. The intro is the first and foremost part of the news, containing the gist of the whole news. The credit line, mentioned at the start, gives the place of the news with its source. The intro is followed by the body text, which contains the details of the story. News by content is of three types: news based on facts, news based on statements and news based on action.

  27. What is the role of a sub-editor in news editing? What is "subbing"?
    Topic: Editing the News

    The sub-editor plays an important role in shaping news into an attractive and pleasant form. The editing desk of a news channel is the hub around which all activities of news production revolve. Although reporters exercise an initial discrimination in selecting news, it is in the newsroom that the news is finally dressed for the bulletin: it is polished and improved, and the errors that creep into a reporter's copy are corrected. Since the sub-editor has the last word in the production of the news bulletin, he/she has to be careful, intelligent, knowledgeable, alert and skilled. The first essential of news editing is accuracy, because the channel's reputation for fairness and reliability rests upon an accurate presentation of facts. Editing the news copy is called "subbing" in journalistic terms: the sub-editor reads the story to get its general sense, reads it again marking mistakes and corrections, gives it a suitable headline, cuts it according to the time available, and keeps in mind the channel's policy, the class of viewers interested in the news and its news value. A good sub-editor can make a first-rate bulletin out of third-rate copy, while a poor sub-editor can spoil the work of the best reporter.

  28. Differentiate between hard news, soft news and investigative reports.
    Topic: Compilation of News Bulletin

    While compiling a news bulletin, news is broadly broken into three categories. Hard news stories make up the bulk of news reporting and typically embody the traditional news values; hard news consists of basic facts — news of important public events such as government actions, international happenings, social conditions, the economy, crime, environment and science — and has significance for a large number of people; the lead stories of a television newscast are usually hard news, with either a hard lead (the most important information first) or a soft lead (used to attract the viewer's attention). Soft news, or features, covers a wide territory and relies on human interest for its news value; features appeal to people's curiosity, sympathy, skepticism, disbelief or amazement and can be about places, people, animals, topics, events or products — for example, the birth of a kangaroo at a local zoo. Investigative reports unearth significant matters of public importance through non-routine information-gathering methods and are primarily concerned with exposing corruption in high places; because they require a good deal of time and money, they are generally longer than typical news items and are packaged in documentaries or in a 10–15 minute segment of a news magazine programme.

  29. What points should be kept in mind while presenting a news bulletin?
    Topic: Presentation of News Bulletin

    The most important prerequisite for the presentation of any item is that it be news — either significant or having human appeal — and of mass appeal for the greatest number of viewers. Anchors usually save their most important item for second place, and an item of national or international interest is considered most important. The news anchor attempts to tie up today's story with the news of yesterday, forming a "continued story" effect, and includes some human-interest stories, inserting short, bright and fast-moving items between long news features. Weather is a subject of universal interest, while a financial statement will interest only a limited class, so items must be selected accordingly. The producer must avoid being alarmist, because television has a whole-family audience, and must avoid shocking, frightening and distasteful items as far as possible. He/she must also take care to avoid charges of defamation, libel or slander, blasphemy, immoral telecast, contempt of court or sedition. The time of day influences selection — for example, topics like cancer or reptiles are not suitable for mealtime bulletins. Above all, accuracy is essential, because an inaccurate item telecast in a bulletin cannot be killed as it can be in a newspaper.

  30. What are special bulletins? Give examples of specialized bulletins and channels.
    Topic: Special Bulletins

    Besides general news bulletins, which include all kinds of news items for a wide-ranging public, special bulletins are prepared for specified audiences on specified channels. Examples include: trade/industry bulletins, covering business, buying and selling and stock exchange matters, with information presented by scrolling on the screen all the time; commerce/business bulletins on import and export issues; agriculture bulletins for farmers and peasants about seeds, fertilizers, insecticides, pesticides and livestock; sports bulletins and live transmissions of tournaments; show biz bulletins about film, radio, theatre and television artists; fashion bulletins; music channels with charts, albums and singer gossip; drama channels; weather reports, which appear in all bulletins though there is no specified weather channel in our country; educational channels for distance learning; religious channels; health programmes about hygiene, diseases and treatments; scientific bulletins about discoveries and inventions; food channels; documentary/investigation programmes; and programmes for youth and women. To make news more comprehensive, correspondents are instructed to prefer simple words — for example, write "buy" instead of "purchase", "call" instead of "summon", "show" instead of "display" and "help" instead of "aid" or "assist".

  31. Define the following terms: casting, frame, shot, teleprompter, vision mixer, EFP and ENG.
    Topic: Technical Codes and Terminology

    Casting is the process of talent hunt for any programme; the casting director finds actors for the parts in the script, normally through auditions. A frame is one of the many single photographic images in a motion picture. A shot is a part of a film between two cuts. A teleprompter, also known as an autocue, is a display device that prompts the person speaking with an electronic visual text of a speech or script; the screen is in front of the lens of the camera and the words are reflected to the eyes of the speaker using a one-way mirror, so the newsreader can read while keeping direct eye contact with the lens. A vision mixer, also called a video switcher, video mixer or production switcher, is a device used to select between several different video sources and, in some cases, composite (mix) video sources together and add special effects. EFP (Electronic Field Production) is a television industry term for production that takes place outside a formal studio, in a practical location or special venue, with emphasis on high-quality multi-camera photography, advanced graphics and sound. ENG (Electronic News Gathering) is the broadcasting term for collecting TV news with electronic (video) equipment; it can mean anything from a lone reporter taking a single camcorder out to get a story to an entire television crew taking a satellite truck on location to do a live report for a newscast.
