  13. What is a landrace?

Landrace populations are often variable in appearance, but they can be identified by their appearance and have a certain genetic identity.

  24. Write a note on plant quarantine regulations.
    Topic: Plant Genetic Resources, GSPC and Policy

    Plant quarantine regulations are promulgated by the national and the state governments to prevent the introduction and spread of harmful pests and pathogens. Plant quarantine will be justified only when the pest has no natural means of spread and when they are based on biological considerations only, i.e. pest/pathogen introduction risks and the available safeguards. In general, risks are more with the introduction of vegetative propagules than with true seed; in case of true seed, risks are more with deep-seated infections than with surface-borne contamination. Pathogens like viruses, downy mildews, smuts and many bacteria carried inside the seed without any external symptoms are especially risky, and bulk introductions are always risky. Quarantine practices in most countries have at least three common functions: (1) exclusion or regulatory actions to prevent or reduce the risk of entry of exotic pathogens, pests or parasites along artificial pathways; (2) containment, suppression or eradication of pests or pathogens that have been recently introduced; (3) assisting exporters to meet the quarantine requirements of importing countries.

  46. Describe breeding and its types in detail.
    Topic: Breeding Systems — Inbreeding and Outbreeding

Its types include: • Pure-breeding: the mating of males and females of the same breed or type; a purebred flock can be managed as a single flock. The goal of purebred sheep production is to provide superior genetics (seed stock) to the commercial sheep industry. • Inbreeding: a system of breeding in which closely related animals are mated; technically, the mating of animals more closely related than the average relationship within the breed or population concerned. Its primary genetic consequence is to increase the frequency of pairing of similar genes. • Out-breeding: the mating of animals of the same breed but which have no closer relationship than at least 4 to 6 generations; the recommended breeding practice for most purebred sheep breeders. • Line breeding: mating of more distantly related animals that can be traced back to one common ancestor. • Crossbreeding: the mating of animals of different breed compositions or types — not indiscriminate mixing, but a systematic utilization of different breed resources to produce crossbred progeny of a specific type. It is used extensively in the commercial sheep industry, and the majority of slaughter lambs are crossbred.

  62. Differentiate between gene flow and genetic drift.
    Topic: Population Genetics — Gene Flow, Genetic Drift, Founder and Bottleneck Effects • 5 marks marks

    • Gene flow occurs via mixing of genes with other populations, while genetic drift takes place when the allele frequency is changed between two generations of a population. • Genetic drift takes place between two generations, whereas gene flow takes place between two populations. • Genetic drift occurs in only one species, while gene flow could take place between either two populations or two species. • Physical barriers matter for gene flow but not for genetic drift.

  63. Differentiate between genetic drift and natural selection.
    Topic: Population Genetics — Gene Flow, Genetic Drift, Founder and Bottleneck Effects • 3 marks marks

    Natural selection is a natural process that results in the survival and reproductive success of individuals or groups; it shows the positive changes in the genome that may give its possessor an adaptive advantage, and it will always result in the selection of the allele that gives an advantage to its possessor. Genetic drift shows changes in the genome that may be advantageous, deleterious or may have no effect — it is solely based on luck. Genetic drift can cause advantageous genes to be removed in the following generations. Genetic drift is largely influenced by population size, whereas natural selection is not.

  64. When does the founder effect occur? Define the founder effect.
    Topic: Population Genetics — Gene Flow, Genetic Drift, Founder and Bottleneck Effects • 2 marks marks

    A founder effect occurs when a new colony is started by a few members of the original population. In population genetics, the founder effect is the loss of genetic variation that occurs when a new population is established by a very small number of individuals from a larger population. It is the effect on the resulting gene pool that occurs when a new isolated population is founded by a small number of individuals possessing limited genetic variation relative to the larger population from which they have migrated.

    Topic: Cryopreservation and Storage • 3 marks marks

    Cryo is a Greek word (krayos — frost); it literally means preservation in a "frozen state." Cryo-preservation or cryo-conservation is a process where organelles, cells, tissues, extracellular matrix, organs or any other biological constructs susceptible to damage caused by unregulated chemical kinetics are preserved by cooling to very low temperatures (typically -80 °C using solid carbon dioxide or -196 °C using liquid nitrogen). Cryopreservation can be done: over solid carbon dioxide (at -79 °C); in a low-temperature deep freezer (at -80 °C); in vapor phase nitrogen (at -150 °C); and in liquid nitrogen (at -196 °C). Examples include semen, blood, special cells for transfusion, stem cells, umbilical cord blood, tissue samples, eggs (oocytes), embryos, and plant seeds or shoots preserved for conservation purposes.

  131. What is the major objective of cryopreservation?
    Topic: Cryopreservation and Storage • 2 marks marks

    The objective of cryopreservation is to minimize damage to biological materials, including tissues, mammalian cells, bacteria, fungi, plant cells, and viruses, during low-temperature freezing and storage.

  132. Enlist the various methods of storage.
    Topic: Cryopreservation and Storage • 3 marks marks

    There are various methods of storage: 1. Cryopreservation — generally involves storage in liquid nitrogen. 2. Cold storage — involves storage in low and non-freezing temperature. 3. Low pressure storage — involves partially reducing the atmospheric pressure of the surroundings. 4. Low oxygen storage — involves reducing the oxygen level but maintaining the pressure. The maintenance of the frozen cells or material at a specific temperature is very important; in general the temperature is kept at -70 to -196 °C, and prolonged storage is done at -196 °C in liquid nitrogen, with a continuous supply of nitrogen to prevent damage.

  133. Describe the different freezing methods in cryopreservation.
    Topic: Cryopreservation and Storage • 5 marks marks

    Cryopreservation is based on the ability of certain small molecules to enter cells and prevent dehydration and formation of intracellular ice crystals, which can cause cell death and destruction of cell organelles during the freezing process. The sensitivity of cells to low temperature depends on the plant species. The main freezing methods are: 1. Slow freezing method — the tissue or plant material is slowly frozen at a slow cooling rate; the advantage is that plant cells are partially dehydrated and survive better. Organs are equilibrated with a cryoprotectant solution and cooled at 0.5–2 °C per minute; seeding is induced, and after equilibration the embryos are cooled to -60 °C or lower before transfer to liquid nitrogen. Frozen embryos must be slowly thawed (less than 25 °C per minute) to prevent osmotic shock. 2. Rapid freezing method — cooling is terminated at -30 to -40 °C and embryos are then plunged into liquid nitrogen for rapid cooling to -196 °C. Thawing is performed rapidly (200 to 500 °C per minute) to prevent recrystallization. 3. Combined freezing method — a combination of both slow and rapid freezing methods, carried out in a stepwise manner. 4. Dry freezing method — dehydrated cells and seeds are stored. 5. Vitrification — the process of cooling where the water in the tissue becomes glass rather than crystals; glass is a liquid that is too cold (too viscous) to flow — solidification due to increased viscosity rather than crystallization.

  134. What is ultra-rapid freezing?
    Topic: Cryopreservation and Storage • 2 marks marks

    In this technique, serial equilibration of embryos is done in high concentration of DMSO (3–5 M) supplemented with sucrose (0.3 to 0.5 M). The embryos are then plunged into liquid nitrogen. Thawing is then done with a warm water bath (approximately 500 °C per minute).

  135. Write the types of cryoprotectants.
    Topic: Cryopreservation and Storage • 3 marks marks

    There are two types of cryoprotectants: 1. Membrane permeating — which can freely diffuse through the membrane, such as glycerol (G), ethylene glycol (EG) and dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO). 2. Non-membrane permeating — which cannot permeate the cell membrane, such as sugars.

  136. What are the obstacles of cryopreservation?
    Topic: Cryopreservation and Storage • 5 marks marks

    • The temperature required can be as low as -90 °C or below, and the technique is very expensive. • Ice formation can result in needle-shaped crystals, resulting in damage to the cell membrane. • Unequal distribution or over-distribution of cryoprotectants. • Thermal gradients can induce mechanical stress due to uneven expansion or contraction in the biomaterial. • The cooling rate required for optimal survival varies by several orders of magnitude between different cell types. • Mass transfer limitations.

  137. What is the future of cryopreservation?
    Topic: Cryopreservation and Storage • 5 marks marks

    The vitrification method of cryopreservation may bring new opportunities to research protocols. It is still an experimental procedure. There are two major concerns about vitrification — toxicity of high-concentration vitrification solutions being one. Vitrification has been used as the sole procedure for day-3 human embryos and for human blastocysts, with excellent survival and pregnancy rates. The challenge now is to find a protocol to successfully vitrify human oocytes, for which the slow-freezing method has yet to produce acceptable results.

  138. Write the application of cryopreservation in medical science.
    Topic: Cryopreservation and Storage • 2 marks marks

    In medical science, cryopreservation is used for the long-term storage and transport of biological specimens for future use or as a record of biodiversity. It is routinely applied to store semen for artificial insemination and fertility treatment, blood and stem cells for transfusion and therapy, umbilical cord blood, eggs (oocytes) and embryos in assisted reproduction, and tissue samples such as tumors and histological cross-sections for diagnosis and research.

  139. What is horizontal gene transfer?
    Topic: Gene Transfer • 3 marks marks

    Horizontal gene transfer is known to occur between different species, such as between prokaryotes and eukaryotes, and between the three DNA-containing organelles of eukaryotes — the nucleus, the mitochondrion and the chloroplast. It is basically the transfer of genes between organisms via methods other than asexual or sexual reproduction.

  140. Differentiate between vertical and horizontal gene transfer.
    Topic: Gene Transfer • 3 marks marks

    Vertical gene transfer: the transfer of genes from parents to offspring; transfer of genetic material occurs through sexual or asexual reproduction — genes are passed down from parent to progeny. Horizontal gene transfer: transfer of genes occurs between different species; transfer of genetic material occurs through methods other than asexual or sexual reproduction.

  141. In how many ways do bacteria transfer their DNA horizontally? Explain.
    Topic: Gene Transfer • 3 marks marks

    There are three ways for bacteria to transfer their DNA horizontally: 1. Conjugation — the transfer of DNA directly from one cell to another through cell-cell contact, often involving plasmids. 2. Transformation — bacteria are capable of taking up DNA directly from their environment and incorporating it into their genomes, known as natural transformation. 3. Transduction — the transfer of DNA from one cell to another by a virus.
