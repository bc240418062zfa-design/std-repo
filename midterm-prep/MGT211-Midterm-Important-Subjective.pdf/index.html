  1. Discuss the legal position of a sole proprietor.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    A sole proprietor is the sole owner of the business and has no separate legal identity from it: the business and the owner are treated as one and the same person in the eyes of law. He owns all the assets, is entitled to all the profits, and bears unlimited liability, i.e. he is personally responsible for all the debts and obligations of the business, even beyond the amount he has invested. He can sue and be sued in his own name, and the life of the business ends with his death, insanity or insolvency.

  2. Write the advantages and disadvantages of sole proprietorship.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Advantages: (1) Easy and inexpensive to form, with no legal formalities like registration. (2) The owner receives the entire profit. (3) Complete control and quick decision making. (4) Direct contact with customers and workers. (5) Secrecy of business matters can be maintained. (6) It is an independent form of business and easy to dissolve. Disadvantages: (1) Unlimited liability of the owner. (2) Limited capital, as resources depend on one person. (3) Limited managerial ability and specialization. (4) Uncertain and short life; the business may end with the owner's death or illness. (5) A single person bears all the losses.

  3. What are the characteristics of a partnership?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    The main characteristics of a partnership are: (1) It is formed by two or more persons (minimum 2, maximum 20; 10 in case of banking business). (2) There must be a lawful business. (3) There is an agreement (partnership deed) between the partners. (4) Profits and losses are shared in the agreed ratio. (5) Every partner is an agent as well as a principal of the firm. (6) Liability of partners is unlimited. (7) It has no separate legal entity apart from the partners.

  4. Differentiate between a limited partner and an unlimited (general) partner.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    A limited partner contributes capital to the firm and his liability is limited to the amount of capital he has contributed; he does not take part in the management of the business. An unlimited (general) partner, on the other hand, takes active part in the management of the firm and his liability is unlimited, i.e. he is personally responsible for all the debts of the firm to an unlimited extent, and his personal property can be used to pay the firm's debts.

  5. Differentiate between dissolution of partnership and dissolution of partnership firm.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Dissolution of partnership means a change in the relation of partners, e.g. when a partner retires, dies or becomes insolvent, or a new partner is admitted; the firm continues its business under a new agreement. Dissolution of the firm means the complete end of the business: all partners cease to be partners, the assets are sold, liabilities are paid off and the business is closed down entirely.

  6. What is a private limited company and a public limited company? Explain with reference to name/title and number of members.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    A private limited company is a company which restricts the right to transfer its shares, limits its members to a maximum of 50 (minimum 2), and prohibits any invitation to the public to subscribe for its shares; the word 'Private Limited' is used with its name. A public limited company is a company which does not have these restrictions: its minimum members are 7 and there is no maximum limit; it can invite the public to subscribe for its shares and its shares are freely transferable; the word 'Limited' is used with its name.

  7. Why do people like to invest in a Joint Stock Company?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    People prefer to invest in a joint stock company because: (1) liability of shareholders is limited to the value of their shares; (2) shares are easily transferable, so investment can be converted into cash at any time; (3) the company has a separate legal entity and perpetual life, so the investment is safe and long-lasting; (4) even a small investor can participate and earn dividends; and (5) the company is managed by professional and experienced directors.

  8. What is meant by double taxation in a Joint Stock Company?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Double taxation means that the same income is taxed twice. A joint stock company first pays tax on its profits as a separate legal entity. Then, when the remaining profit is distributed to the shareholders as dividend, the shareholders again pay tax on that dividend income. In this way the company's profit is taxed at two stages, which is a major disadvantage of the company form of organization.

  9. Discuss the effects of a company being a separate legal entity.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Because a company is a separate legal entity distinct from its members: (1) it can own property, enter into contracts, and sue or be sued in its own name; (2) the liability of its members is limited to their shareholding; (3) it has perpetual succession, i.e. its life is not affected by the death or insolvency of members; (4) shares can be transferred without affecting the existence of the company; and (5) the personal assets of members cannot be used to pay the company's debts.

  10. Define Articles of Association and state what they contain.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    The Articles of Association are the rules and regulations of a company which govern its internal management and define the rights, duties and powers of its members and officers. They contain matters such as: issue and transfer of shares, calls on shares, appointment, election, powers and remuneration of directors, voting rights of members, conduct of meetings, payment of dividends, and the borrowing powers of the Board of Directors.

  11. Write any five key points of the statutory report.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    The statutory report, sent to every member before the statutory meeting, contains: (1) the total number of shares allotted and the amount received on them; (2) an abstract of the receipts and payments of the company since its incorporation; (3) the names, addresses and occupations of the directors, auditors, managers and secretary; (4) particulars of any contract which is to be submitted to the meeting for approval; and (5) the extent to which underwriting contracts have been carried out and any commission or brokerage paid on the issue of shares.

  12. Write down the objectives of the Annual General Meeting (AGM).
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    The objectives of the Annual General Meeting are: (1) to present and adopt the annual accounts, balance sheet and the directors' and auditors' reports; (2) to declare dividends; (3) to elect or re-elect directors in place of those retiring; (4) to appoint auditors and fix their remuneration; and (5) to discuss and decide any other matters concerning the working and progress of the company, so that shareholders can review the performance of the management.

  13. If you were a member of a company, how could the company be wound up in critical conditions?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Winding up means bringing the life of a company to an end and distributing its assets among creditors and members. A company may be wound up in three ways: (1) Compulsory winding up by the court, e.g. when the company fails to submit the statutory report, does not commence business within one year of incorporation, or is unable to pay its debts. (2) Voluntary winding up, when the period fixed for the company expires or the members pass a special resolution to wind it up. (3) Winding up subject to the supervision of the court. A liquidator is appointed who realizes the assets, pays the liabilities and distributes the surplus among the members.

  14. What do you mean by 'aids to trade'? Discuss any three aids to trade.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Aids to trade are the institutions and services which assist and support the process of trade by removing the difficulties of time, place, finance, risk and information. Three important aids to trade are: (1) Warehousing, which provides storage facilities and makes goods available when they are demanded, removing the hindrance of time. (2) Transportation, which carries goods from the place of production to the place of consumption, removing the hindrance of place. (3) Insurance, which protects traders against risks of fire, theft and accidents. Other aids include banking (finance) and advertising (information).

  15. Discuss the benefits of a strategic alliance.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    A strategic alliance is an arrangement in which two or more organizations collaborate on a project for mutual gain while remaining independent. Its benefits are: (1) organizations share risks, costs and resources; (2) each partner can benefit from the other's expertise, technology and experience; (3) it helps a business enter new and foreign markets easily; (4) it increases competitiveness against bigger rivals; (5) partners can achieve economies of scale; and (6) it is more flexible than a merger because the organizations do not lose their separate identity.

  16. Briefly describe the parties involved in a franchise agreement.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    There are two main parties in a franchise agreement: (1) the franchisor, the owner of the business who possesses the rights to a trademark, trade name, product or business system and grants these rights to another party; and (2) the franchisee, the person or business who purchases the right to use the franchisor's trademark, products and business methods in a specified area, in return for an initial fee and regular royalty payments, and who agrees to follow the franchisor's conditions.

  17. Discuss the various disadvantages of franchising.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    The disadvantages of franchising are: (1) the franchisee has to pay an initial franchise fee and continuous royalty, which reduces profit; (2) the franchisee lacks independence because he must follow the rules and standards of the franchisor; (3) the failure or bad reputation of the franchisor or of other franchisees affects his business; (4) there are restrictions on selling or transferring the franchise; and (5) the franchisee cannot introduce his own ideas or local variations easily.

  18. Differentiate between merger and acquisition.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    A merger is the combination of two or more organizations into a single new organization; the merging organizations lose their separate identities and a new entity is formed, usually by mutual agreement. An acquisition takes place when one firm purchases another firm (its shares or assets) and the acquired firm becomes part of the acquiring firm; the acquiring firm keeps its identity while the acquired firm may lose its separate existence. A merger is generally a friendly combination of equals, whereas an acquisition may be friendly or hostile.

  19. Write the definition of tariff. Why does the government impose tariffs?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    A tariff is a tax imposed by a government on goods imported into a country. The government imposes tariffs in order to: (1) protect domestic industries from foreign competition by making imported goods more expensive; (2) earn revenue for the government; (3) reduce the import of luxury or harmful goods; (4) improve the balance of payments by discouraging imports; and (5) encourage the use of locally produced goods.

  20. Define balance of payments. When is the balance of payments favorable and unfavorable?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    The balance of payments is the systematic record of all economic transactions (exports and imports of goods and services, and capital movements) between the residents of a country and the rest of the world during a given period. The balance of payments is favorable when the total receipts (payments coming into the country) are greater than the total payments made to other countries, i.e. the country receives more than it pays. It is unfavorable when the total payments exceed the total receipts, i.e. the country pays more to other countries than it receives from them.

  21. Define balance of trade. What are trade surplus and trade deficit?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    The balance of trade is the difference between the value of a country's merchandise (visible) exports and its merchandise imports during a given period. If the value of exports is greater than the value of imports, the balance of trade is favorable and this situation is called trade surplus. If the value of imports is greater than the value of exports, the balance of trade is unfavorable and this situation is called trade deficit. If exports and imports are equal, the balance of trade is in equilibrium.

  22. What is globalization? What is the role of GATT and WTO in globalization?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Globalization is the process of increasing integration and interdependence of the economies of different countries, through the free flow of goods, services, capital, technology and information across borders. GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade) was the treaty designed to remove barriers to the free flow of goods, services and capital between nations by reducing tariffs and quotas through negotiations. The WTO (World Trade Organization), established on 1 January 1995 as the successor of GATT, supervises international trade agreements, provides a forum for trade negotiations and rules on trade disputes between nations, thereby promoting free and fair global trade.

  23. Discuss the legal and political barriers to international trade.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Legal and political barriers are the restrictions imposed by governments on foreign trade. They include: (1) Tariffs, taxes on imported goods which make them expensive. (2) Quotas, limits on the quantity of specific products that may be imported. (3) Embargo, a complete ban on trade with a particular country, usually for political reasons. (4) Subsidies and dumping regulations used to protect local industry. (5) Licensing requirements and unreasonable quality and safety standards designed to reduce imports. (6) Political instability, changes in government policies, and laws relating to foreign investment and repatriation of profit, which create risk and uncertainty for international traders.

  24. What is a business plan? Discuss the purpose and significance of a business plan.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    A business plan is a written document that describes the business, its objectives and the strategies for achieving them; it contains financial information, production plans, personnel policies and market analysis. Its purpose and significance are: (1) it provides a roadmap and direction to the entrepreneur; (2) it helps in estimating the required capital and in convincing banks and investors to provide finance; (3) it identifies potential problems and risks in advance; (4) it sets goals against which actual performance can be measured and controlled; and (5) it guides day-to-day decisions and the efficient use of resources.

  25. Discuss some of the concerns an entrepreneur may have when starting a new business.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    An entrepreneur starting a new business is concerned about: (1) selecting a suitable business idea and product; (2) deciding the form of ownership (sole proprietorship, partnership or company); (3) arranging sufficient capital and finance; (4) choosing a suitable location; (5) legal formalities, registration and government regulations; (6) recruiting suitable employees; (7) competition in the market and attracting customers; and (8) the risk of failure, because a large number of new businesses fail due to lack of managerial experience, insufficient capital and weak control systems.

  26. Briefly discuss the common motivators for an entrepreneur to start a new venture.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    The common motivators for an entrepreneur to start a new venture are: (1) the desire to be his own boss and work independently; (2) the desire to earn more money and improve his financial position; (3) the desire for recognition, status and prestige in society; (4) the urge to use his skills, experience and creative ideas; (5) dissatisfaction with the existing job; (6) the desire to provide employment to family members or to continue a family business; and (7) the wish to avail a market opportunity or government incentives.

  27. Write any five characteristics of entrepreneurs.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Five characteristics of entrepreneurs are: (1) Risk taking: they are willing to take calculated risks. (2) Self-confidence: they believe in their own abilities. (3) Independent mind: they like to work independently and be their own boss. (4) Perceptive and opportunity-oriented: they can identify and avail business opportunities. (5) Innovative and hardworking: they introduce new ideas and work with dedication and persistence to make the venture successful.

  28. What are the causes of failure of a business?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    The main causes of business failure are: (1) lack of managerial experience or inexperience of the owner; (2) insufficient capital and poor financial management; (3) weak or insufficient control systems; (4) neglect of business and lack of proper planning; (5) poor location and lack of market research; and (6) external factors such as economic recession, competition and natural disasters.

  29. Discuss and give examples of what you believe is the most common form of business ownership in Pakistan.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    The most common form of business ownership in Pakistan is sole proprietorship. Most small shops, traders, retailers, workshops and service providers in every city and town are owned and managed by a single person. For example, a grocery store owner, a tailor, a barber or a small restaurant owner invests his own capital, takes all decisions himself, receives the entire profit and bears all the risk. This form is most common because it is easy to start without legal formalities, requires small capital, gives complete control to the owner and is suitable for small-scale businesses providing personalized services.

  30. Define the four core functions of management.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    The four core functions of management are: (1) Planning: deciding in advance what is to be done, setting objectives and selecting the courses of action to achieve them. (2) Organizing: arranging and structuring work, resources and authority relationships to accomplish the goals. (3) Leading: motivating, directing and influencing employees so that they work willingly towards organizational objectives; it includes communication and leadership. (4) Controlling: monitoring performance, comparing it with the set standards and taking corrective action where needed.

  31. Distinguish between management and leadership. Can a person be an effective manager without being a leader?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Management is the formal process of planning, organizing, leading and controlling the work of organization members and using organizational resources to achieve goals; a manager derives authority from his formal position. Leadership is the ability to influence and motivate people to work willingly towards objectives; a leader derives power from his personal qualities and the acceptance of followers. Every leader is not a manager, but an effective manager should also be a leader: a manager who cannot lead can only get work done through authority and rules, whereas a manager with leadership qualities inspires employees and achieves better results. So a person can hold a managerial position without being a leader, but he will be less effective.

  32. What is Douglas McGregor's Theory X and Theory Y? Which one is more preferred in today's organizations?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Theory X assumes that people are naturally irresponsible and uncooperative: they dislike work, avoid responsibility, lack ambition and must be controlled, directed and threatened with punishment to make them work. Theory Y assumes that people are not lazy by nature: work is as natural as play, and under proper conditions people seek responsibility, exercise self-direction and use their creativity to achieve objectives to which they are committed. Theory Y is more preferred in today's organizations because modern management emphasizes participation, empowerment, teamwork and self-control, which produce higher motivation and performance than the authoritarian approach of Theory X.

  33. What is Maslow's hierarchy of needs model? What are the management implications of Maslow's hierarchy of needs theory?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 10 marks marks

    Maslow's hierarchy of needs model states that human needs are arranged in a hierarchy of five levels, and a person is motivated to satisfy the lower-level needs before moving to the higher ones: (1) Physiological needs: basic needs for food, water, shelter and clothing. (2) Safety/security needs: protection from danger, job security and a safe working environment; e.g. a person who buys a burglar alarm is satisfying this need. (3) Social needs: needs for love, affection, belonging and acceptance by others. (4) Esteem needs: needs for self-respect, status, recognition and appreciation. (5) Self-actualization needs: the need to achieve one's full potential and personal growth. Management implications: (1) Managers should identify the level at which each employee's needs are currently unsatisfied, because a satisfied need no longer motivates. (2) Pay and working conditions should be adequate to satisfy physiological and safety needs before higher incentives can work. (3) Social needs can be satisfied through teamwork and a friendly environment. (4) Esteem needs can be met through recognition, titles, promotion and appreciation. (5) Self-actualization can be supported through challenging assignments, training and opportunities for growth. (6) As employees progress, managers must change the incentives they offer accordingly.

  34. Write a criticism of Maslow's theory of needs.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Maslow's theory is criticized on the following grounds: (1) The strict hierarchy is not always followed; a person may pursue higher-level needs even when lower needs are not fully satisfied. (2) The order and importance of needs differ from person to person and from culture to culture. (3) The theory is difficult to test and measure in practice. (4) Satisfaction of one level does not always lead to the next level in every individual.

  35. How are Herzberg's hygiene factors related to Maslow's hierarchy of needs theory?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Herzberg's hygiene factors (company policy, supervision, salary, working conditions and job security) correspond to the lower-order needs in Maslow's hierarchy, i.e. physiological and safety needs; their absence causes dissatisfaction, but their presence does not motivate. Herzberg's motivators (achievement, recognition, responsibility, advancement and growth) correspond to Maslow's higher-order needs, i.e. esteem and self-actualization, which actually motivate employees to perform better. Thus the two theories are complementary: hygiene factors relate to lower-level needs and motivators to higher-level needs.

  36. Describe how equity theory explains the motivation of employees.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Equity theory states that employees compare the ratio of their inputs (effort, education, experience, time) to their outcomes (pay, promotion, recognition) with the input-outcome ratio of other employees. If an employee perceives that his ratio is equal to that of others, he feels equity and remains motivated. If he perceives inequity, e.g. he is under-rewarded compared to others, he becomes dissatisfied and tries to restore equity by reducing his effort, demanding more pay, changing the comparison person, or leaving the organization. Therefore managers must ensure fair and transparent reward systems to keep employees motivated.

  37. Describe the reinforcement theory of motivation.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Reinforcement theory states that behavior can be encouraged or discouraged by means of its consequences, i.e. rewards or punishments. Behavior which is rewarded (positive reinforcement, e.g. praise, bonus, promotion) is likely to be repeated, while behavior followed by punishment or by no reward tends to disappear. Managers can therefore shape employees' behavior by immediately rewarding desired performance and discouraging undesirable behavior.

  38. Performance appraisal is an effective technique of motivation. Discuss.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Performance appraisal is the systematic evaluation of an employee's performance against set standards. It motivates employees because: (1) it recognizes and appreciates good performance, satisfying esteem needs; (2) it links rewards, increments and promotions with performance, so employees work harder; (3) it provides feedback, helping employees know their strengths and weaknesses and improve; and (4) it identifies training needs and supports career development. A fair and transparent appraisal system therefore increases employees' commitment and performance.

  39. What is job analysis? Why is job analysis important for an organization?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Job analysis is the process of determining the tasks, duties and responsibilities of a particular job and the necessary credentials (knowledge, skills and abilities) required to perform it. It results in two documents: job description (what the jobholder does, how and why) and job specification (the qualifications required). Job analysis is important because it is the basis of all human resource activities: it helps in recruitment and selection, in designing training programs, in performance appraisal, in fixing fair compensation, in job evaluation and in ensuring the right person is placed on the right job.

  40. Differentiate between job description and job specification.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    A job description is a written statement of what a jobholder does, how the job is done and why it is done; it describes the job itself, i.e. its title, duties, responsibilities, working conditions and relationship with other jobs. A job specification is a statement of the human qualifications needed to perform the job, i.e. the education, experience, skills, knowledge and personal characteristics required of the person who will hold the job. In short, job description is about the job, while job specification is about the person required for the job.

  41. Job enlargement and job enrichment are used interchangeably. How will you comment on this statement?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    The statement is not correct; the two are different techniques of job design. Job enlargement means assigning additional activities of the same level to an employee, i.e. increasing the number and variety of tasks (horizontal expansion) to reduce monotony. Job enrichment means redesigning the job to give the employee more responsibility, autonomy and control over his work (vertical expansion), making the job more meaningful and motivating. Thus enlargement increases the quantity of tasks, whereas enrichment increases the depth and responsibility of the job.

  42. What are the various off-the-job training techniques?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Off-the-job training is given away from the actual workplace. Its techniques include: (1) lectures and classroom instruction; (2) conferences, seminars and workshops; (3) case studies; (4) role playing; (5) vestibule training, in which employees learn on the actual equipment but in a separate training area; (6) films and video presentations; and (7) programmed instruction and computer-based training.

  43. Why is employee turnover considered bad for the organization?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Employee turnover is considered bad because: (1) the organization loses trained and experienced employees in whom it has invested; (2) it increases the cost of recruiting, selecting and training new employees; (3) the productivity and quality of work suffer during the vacancy and learning period; (4) it disturbs teamwork and lowers the morale of remaining employees; and (5) high turnover creates a bad image of the organization in the labour market.

  44. Describe the tasks involved in developing a human resource plan.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 10 marks marks

    Human resource planning is the process of deciding the right quantity and quality of employees needed, when and where they will be needed. The tasks involved are: (1) Environmental scanning: analyzing external factors (economy, labour market, laws, technology) and internal factors (objectives, strategies, policies) that affect human resource needs. (2) Forecasting demand: estimating the number and type of employees the organization will require in the future. (3) Forecasting supply: estimating the employees available from internal sources (promotions, transfers) and external sources (labour market). (4) Comparing demand and supply: identifying shortages or surpluses of employees. (5) Action planning: preparing plans for recruitment, selection, training, promotion, or, in case of surplus, for layoffs and retrenchment. (6) Implementation and evaluation: putting the plan into action and monitoring it, with feedback for future planning.

  45. What is recruitment? What are the different ways of recruitment?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Recruitment is the process of attracting and discovering potential candidates for actual or anticipated job vacancies, so that suitable people apply for the jobs. The different ways (sources) of recruitment are: Internal sources: promotion, transfers, and recommendations of existing employees. External sources: (1) advertisements in newspapers and online job portals; (2) employment agencies and executive search firms; (3) educational institutions and campus recruitment; (4) walk-in applicants and unsolicited applications; (5) labour unions; and (6) the company's own website and social media.

  46. Differentiate between patterned (structured) and unpatterned (unstructured) interviews.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    In a patterned/structured interview, the interviewer asks the same predetermined series of questions, in the same order, to every candidate, and the answers are evaluated against a standard pattern; it is more reliable and comparable. In an unpatterned/unstructured interview, there is no fixed set of questions; the questions can be changed or adapted to meet the respondent's intelligence and understanding, and the conversation develops freely; it gives deeper information but is less standardized and more open to bias.

  47. Define marketing and briefly discuss the stages (evolution) of marketing.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Marketing is the process of planning and executing the conception, pricing, promotion and distribution of ideas, goods and services to create exchanges that satisfy individual and organizational objectives; in simple words, it is identifying and satisfying the needs and wants of customers profitably. The stages of the evolution of marketing are: (1) Production-oriented stage: focus on producing goods efficiently and in quantity, assuming customers buy what is available and affordable. (2) Product-oriented stage: focus on product quality and features. (3) Sales-oriented stage: focus on aggressive selling and promotion to sell what is produced. (4) Marketing-oriented stage: focus on identifying and satisfying the unsatisfied needs of customers better than competitors. (5) Social/societal-oriented stage: satisfying customer needs while also protecting the welfare of society.

  48. Differentiate between transactional marketing and relationship marketing.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Transactional marketing focuses on single, short-term sales transactions; the aim is to make a sale, with little emphasis on the customer after the sale. Relationship marketing focuses on building long-term, value-added relationships with customers and suppliers, developed over time; the aim is customer satisfaction, loyalty and repeat business rather than a one-time sale. Modern organizations increasingly prefer relationship marketing because retaining a customer is cheaper than attracting a new one.

  49. Define utility and its forms.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Utility is the want-satisfying power of a good or service, i.e. its ability to satisfy a human need. Its forms are: (1) Form utility: created by changing the form of a product, e.g. converting wood into furniture. (2) Place utility: created by making a product available where it is wanted. (3) Time utility: created by making a product available when it is wanted, e.g. through warehousing. (4) Possession (ownership) utility: created when a good or service is obtained and the buyer gets the right to use or consume it.

  50. Who are stakeholders? Name the different types of stakeholders of a business.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Stakeholders are all the individuals and groups who have an interest in, and are affected by, the activities and performance of a business. The different types of stakeholders include: owners/shareholders, creditors, employees, customers, suppliers, government, competitors and the general public/society. A business must consider the interests of all these groups in its decisions.

  51. What is corporate social responsibility? Explain briefly.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Corporate social responsibility (CSR) means that a business should think about benefiting society and avoid activities harmful to society, in addition to earning profits. It includes dimensions such as hiring minority workers, making safe products, minimizing pollution, using energy wisely and providing a safe work environment. A socially responsible business fulfills its obligations towards employees, customers, the community and the environment.

  52. Define business ethics. What is the importance of business ethics?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Business ethics refers to the standards of moral behavior and the principles of right and wrong that guide the conduct of a business and its members. Its importance is: (1) it builds the goodwill, trust and long-term image of the business among customers and society; (2) it improves relations with employees and increases their commitment; (3) it helps the business avoid legal penalties and conflicts with government and society; (4) unethical practices can negatively affect the firm's value (stock price); and (5) it promotes fair competition and protects the interests of all stakeholders.

  53. What are the primary functions of the CEO of an organization?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    The chief executive officer (CEO) is the top executive of the organization. His primary functions are: (1) to formulate and communicate the mission, objectives and major policies of the organization; (2) to make major decisions regarding strategy, resources and investments; (3) to appoint and supervise senior managers; (4) to represent the organization before the board of directors, government and the public; and (5) to monitor overall performance and ensure that organizational goals are achieved.

  54. Differentiate between salary and wages.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Wages are the payment made to workers for short-term periods, usually calculated on an hourly, daily or weekly basis or per unit of work, and are generally paid to manual/production workers. Salary is a fixed payment for a longer period, usually monthly, paid to office staff, managers and professionals regardless of the exact number of hours worked. In short, wages are time- or piece-based short-term payments, while salary is a fixed periodic payment.

  55. Define mission statement.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    A mission statement is a written statement that defines the basic purpose of an organization: what business it is in, whom it serves, and what it wants to achieve. It guides the formulation of objectives and strategies and communicates the organization's direction to employees and other stakeholders.

  56. What is a psychological contract?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    A psychological contract is the set of unwritten expectations and mutual beliefs between an employee and the organization about what each will give and receive in the employment relationship, e.g. the employee expects fair treatment, job security and growth, while the organization expects loyalty, honesty and good performance. If either side feels the contract is violated, motivation and trust decline.

  57. What is the role of a member in a cooperative society regarding his liability?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    In a cooperative society, the liability of each member is limited to the amount of share capital he has contributed or agreed to contribute. A member is not personally liable for the debts of the society beyond his share capital, so his personal property cannot be claimed for the society's obligations.
