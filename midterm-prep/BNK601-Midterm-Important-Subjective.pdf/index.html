  1. What is the objective behind establishing the office of Banking Mohtasib?
    Topic: Banking Mohtasib & Customer Rights • 3 marks marks

    The main objective of the Banking Mohtasib is to understand and find out injustice done by anyone to the customer. The examples are: wrong charges levied on the customers, extra commissions charged, incorrect interest rates given on deposits or incorrect interest rate charged on loans and advances, complaints regarding cheque collections, inward and outward transfer of domestic and foreign currency, etc. Thus the Banking Mohtasib is supposed to address all the grievances faced by the customers of the bank.

  2. In 2006, a commercial bank aggressively marketed for Term Deposits and told its customers that no penalties would be charged if premature encashment were desired. As a result of the campaign, many customers placed sizeable funds on 5-year Term Deposits with the bank. However, during 2007, when some of the depositors wanted to prematurely encash their deposits, they were threatened with substantial penalties by the bank. The bank defended the penalty by saying that it was empowered through its "Account Terms and Conditions" to alter any condition unilaterally at any time it chose; the new penalty was included in its Schedule of Charges dated January 1, 2007, though penalties were not invoked until July 1, 2007. In your opinion, whose claim was right — customer or bank? Justify your answer with reasons.
    Topic: Banking Mohtasib & Customer Rights • 5 marks marks

    The bank's stance is incorrect, as it had promised the customers to return the money to customers if they desired to withdraw the same without any penalty. The deposits were placed by customers based on the above-said promise by the bank, and now the bank cannot go back on its promise. This is a classic example to be referred to the Banking Mohtasib.

  3. What are the rights of a customer in a bank?
    Topic: Banking Mohtasib & Customer Rights

    A bank customer generally has the following rights: 1. Right to open and operate an account and to receive the banking services agreed with the bank (deposits, withdrawals, cheque book, transfers, etc.). 2. Right to secrecy — the bank must keep the customer's account and transactions confidential and must not disclose them except where the law or banking practice permits. 3. Right to be informed — the customer must be told the terms and conditions of the account, the applicable rates of profit/interest, and the schedule of charges; charges cannot be levied arbitrarily or without notice. 4. Right to receive an accurate statement of account and to have errors and wrong/unauthorized entries corrected. 5. Right to withdraw his deposits in accordance with the terms agreed, and to close the account after settling dues. 6. Right to fair and honest treatment — no misrepresentation or unfair practice by the bank (e.g., a bank cannot go back on a promise on the basis of which a deposit was placed). 7. Right of redressal — the customer may complain to the bank and, if not satisfied, to the Banking Mohtasib (and the State Bank of Pakistan) for investigation and redress of grievances such as wrong charges, extra commissions or incorrect interest rates.

  4. An agent sells goods on credit to a person on behalf of his principal without making proper enquiry about the solvency of that person. Afterwards, it reveals that the person was insolvent at the time of sale. Principal sues his agent for loss. Is principal's claim right?
    Topic: Agency Law • 3 marks marks

    It seems from the above that selling on credit is the normal way of doing business. So as far as selling on credit is concerned, the agent is entitled to do so. However, he sold goods to a person who was insolvent at the time of sale. The principal is justified and must sue his agent for loss, and the principal's claim is right. The agent must have used reasonable care and diligence while selling the goods on credit. He should by no means have sold on credit to an insolvent person.

  5. Write down any five topics covered in Part IIA (Transaction of Banking Business Illegally By Companies, etc.) of Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962.
    Topic: Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962 • 5 marks marks

    Following are the topics covered in Part IIA — Transaction of Banking Business Illegally By Companies: 1. Call for information as required by central bank. 2. Power to make orders and decrees. 3. Conservation of assets. 4. Submission of financial statements.

  6. What are the minimum paid-up capital and reserves requirements for banking companies under the Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962?
    Topic: Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962

    These requirements are contained in Section 13 of the Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962. Under this section: 1. The subscribed capital of a banking company shall not be less than one-half of its authorized capital. 2. The paid-up capital of a banking company shall not be less than one-half of its subscribed capital. 3. A banking company incorporated in Pakistan, and a banking company incorporated outside Pakistan carrying on banking business in Pakistan, must maintain the prescribed minimum aggregate of paid-up capital and reserves; the minimum amount is prescribed and enhanced from time to time (through the State Bank of Pakistan), and a banking company that falls short of the requirement cannot lawfully continue its banking business.

  7. Write down any five provisions of Section 10 (Disqualifications of the Managing Director) of SBP Banking Services Corporation Ordinance, 2001.
    Topic: SBP Banking Services Corporation Ordinance, 2001 • 5 marks marks

    A person shall be disqualified from being the Managing Director if: 1. He is a member of Senate or National Assembly. 2. He is an employee of the Federal Government or of a Provincial Government. 3. He has not settled any dues of any bank. 4. He is holding a position of office like a secretary or treasurer in a political party. 5. He is a major shareholder in another bank.

  8. What are the types of Mortgage?
    Topic: Mortgages & Shares

    In practice there are two types of mortgages, which are given below: 1. Registered or Legal Mortgage: This is created through a formal document called a mortgage deed. The mortgage deed is registered with the Registrar of Titles. It is comparatively expensive as it involves stamp duty and registration fee. 2. Equitable Mortgage: This is created by deposit of title deeds by the mortgagor. A memorandum regarding deposit of title deeds is also signed by the respective parties. Clear title of the mortgagor must be ascertained by the mortgagee.

  9. What are preference shares? State their main features and types.
    Topic: Mortgages & Shares

    Preference shares are shares which carry a preferential right (i) to receive dividend at a fixed rate before any dividend is paid on the ordinary (equity) shares, and (ii) to repayment of capital before the equity shareholders in case the company is wound up. Main features: 1. Dividend is paid at a fixed rate and takes priority over equity dividend. 2. Capital is repaid before equity capital on winding up. 3. Normally preference shareholders do not have voting rights, except on matters affecting their rights. Types of preference shares: • Cumulative and non-cumulative (whether unpaid dividend accumulates or not). • Redeemable and irredeemable (whether the company can buy them back or not). • Participating and non-participating (whether they also share in surplus profits after equity dividend). • Convertible and non-convertible (whether they can be converted into equity shares).

  10. Differentiate between the money market and the capital market, and name the main instruments of each.
    Topic: Financial Markets & Instruments

    Money market is the market for short-term funds (normally up to one year), while the capital market is the market for long-term funds. Instruments of the money market include: bills of exchange, repurchase agreements (repos) and federal funds. (Stocks are NOT a money market instrument.) Instruments of the capital market include: stocks, debentures and WAPDA bonds. (Federal funds are NOT a capital market instrument.)

  11. Because of their diversified portfolio, what services can banks provide to their customers? Explain briefly.
    Topic: Financial Markets & Instruments

    Because of diversification of their portfolio, banks can provide the following services to their customers: 1. Risk sharing — by holding a diversified portfolio of assets, a bank spreads risk and offers depositors a safer return than they could get by lending directly. 2. Liquidity — banks convert illiquid assets (long-term loans) into liquid liabilities (deposits withdrawable on demand or at short notice). 3. Information — banks collect and process information about borrowers and projects, and provide information services to customers, reducing the cost and risk of lending for the ordinary saver. So the correct set of services is: risk sharing, liquidity and information — all of these.

  12. What are the contents of the Memorandum of Association of a company under the Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962?
    Topic: Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962

    The Memorandum of Association of a company contains clauses relating to: 1. The name of the company. 2. The liability of the members. 3. The authorized capital of the company. (The number of shareholders is not a content of the Memorandum of Association.)

  13. State the prohibitions regarding employment in a banking company under Section 11 of the Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962.
    Topic: Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962

    Under Section 11 of the Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962, no banking company shall employ, or be managed by: 1. A managing agent — no banking company shall employ or be managed by a managing agent. 2. A person who is, or at any time has been, adjudicated insolvent. 3. A person who is convicted by a criminal court of an offence involving moral turpitude. All of the above statements are true; none of them is false.

  14. What restrictions does Section 19 of the Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962 place on the payment of dividend by a banking company?
    Topic: Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962

    Under Section 19 of the Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962, no banking company shall pay any dividend on its shares until all its capitalized expenses have been completely written off. Dividend can be paid only after adequate provision has been made, to the satisfaction of the auditors, on account of: 1. Depreciation in the value of its investments in shares, debentures or bonds (other than approved securities), where such depreciation has occurred; and 2. Bad debts, where such debts exist. (Note: merely earning a profit is not, by itself, the condition stated in Section 19.)

  15. What is a Certificate of Moratorium under Section 36 of the Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962? Who issues it and for what maximum period?
    Topic: Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962

    A moratorium is a temporary suspension/postponement of the banking company's obligations (e.g., repayment of deposits) granted to give a bank in difficulty time to reorganize. Under Section 36 of the Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962, the Federal Government can issue a Certificate of Moratorium upon an application of the State Bank of Pakistan, for a maximum period of six months.

  16. What penalty is provided if a banking company defaults in complying with the policy or directions of the State Bank of Pakistan?
    Topic: Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962

    If any default is made by a banking company in complying with the policy determined, or a direction given, by the State Bank, every director and other officer of the banking company and every other person who is knowingly a party to such default shall, by order of the State Bank, be liable to a penalty which may extend to twenty thousand rupees; and where the default is a continuing one, to a further amount which may extend to one thousand rupees for every day after the first during which the default continues.

  17. Which parties are engaged in a letter of credit? Describe the role of each.
    Topic: Letter of Credit, Islamic Banking & Nationalization

    The following parties are engaged in a letter of credit: 1. Advising bank — the bank in the exporter's country which advises (authenticates and forwards) the credit to the beneficiary. 2. Confirming bank — a bank which adds its own confirmation/undertaking to pay, in addition to that of the issuing bank. 3. Negotiating bank — the bank which negotiates the documents/drafts presented by the beneficiary under the credit. (In addition, the issuing/opening bank issues the credit on behalf of the importer/applicant, and the beneficiary is the exporter.) All of the given banks — advising, confirming and negotiating — are parties engaged in a letter of credit.

  18. What is Modaraba?
    Topic: Letter of Credit, Islamic Banking & Nationalization

    Modaraba is a business in which a person participates with his money and another with his efforts or skill, or both his efforts and skill; it shall include a unit, trust and mutual fund by whatever name called. In other words, it is a form of partnership/Islamic financing in which one party (the investor, rabb-ul-mal) provides the capital and the other party (the modarib) provides the management and effort, and the profit is shared in an agreed proportion.

  19. What are the demerits of nationalization of banks?
    Topic: Letter of Credit, Islamic Banking & Nationalization

    The demerits of nationalization of banks include: 1. Excessive government control, leading to decisions on non-professional considerations. 2. Neglect of personalized services to the customers. 3. Mismanagement, leading to an alarming size of non-performing loans portfolio. (Equitable distribution of credit to the different sectors, industries and regions is a merit — not a demerit — of nationalization.)

  20. When did the State Bank of Pakistan start its operations? What was its original capital under Section 4 of the State Bank of Pakistan Act, 1956?
    Topic: State Bank of Pakistan & Microfinance

    The State Bank of Pakistan started its operations on 1st July, 1948. According to Section 4 of the State Bank of Pakistan Act, 1956, the original capital of the Bank was Rs. 30 million.

  21. State the minimum paid-up capital requirement under Section 10, and the punishment for mismanagement under Section 23, of the Microfinance Institutions Ordinance, 2001.
    Topic: State Bank of Pakistan & Microfinance

    Section 10: No microfinance institution shall operate in a specified province unless it has a paid-up capital of not less than two hundred and fifty million rupees (Rs. 250 million), or such higher amount as may be prescribed from time to time, for microfinance institutions to whom a licence to operate in a specified province is issued. Section 23: If any officer of a microfinance institution mismanages the affairs of the microfinance institution, or misuses his position for gaining direct or indirect benefit for himself or any of his family members, he shall be punishable with imprisonment for a term which may extend to three years.

  22. Define a "Trust".
    Topic: Contract Act & Agency Concepts

    A trust is "an obligation annexed to the ownership of property, and arising out of a confidence reposed in and accepted by the owner, or declared and accepted by him, for the benefit of another, or of another and the owner." The person who reposes or declares the confidence is the one who creates the trust (the author/settlor), and the owner who accepts the obligation is the trustee.

  23. What is misrepresentation? Explain with an example.
    Topic: Contract Act & Agency Concepts

    Misrepresentation is a false statement of fact, made innocently (without intention to deceive), which induces another person to enter into a contract. Example: Miss Naila says to Miss Amna that her land produces 40 mounds of wheat per acre. Miss Amna believes it to be true and buys that land. Later it appears that the land produces only 10 mounds of wheat per acre. Miss Naila has committed misrepresentation (not coercion, undue influence or mistake).

  24. What are the different kinds of agents?
    Topic: Contract Act & Agency Concepts

    Agents may be classified as follows: 1. General agent — an agent authorized to do all acts connected with a particular business or employment of his principal, in the ordinary course of that business. 2. Special agent — an agent appointed for a specific, single act or transaction only. 3. Private agents — agents who are individuals or companies (as opposed to public agents, who act for the government/public bodies). Mercantile agents (such as factors, brokers and auctioneers) are agents who, in the customary course of their business, have authority to sell goods, consign goods for sale, buy goods, or raise money on the security of goods on behalf of their principals.

  25. Write down five provisions of unclaimed deposits and articles of value under the Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962.
    Topic: Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962 • 10 marks marks

    The Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962 deals with unclaimed deposits and articles of value (handouts lecture 13) broadly as follows: 1. Return to the State Bank: Every banking company shall, within the prescribed time after the close of each calendar year, submit to the State Bank of Pakistan a return of all deposit accounts which have not been operated upon for ten years, and of articles of value placed in its safe custody and remaining unclaimed for the prescribed period. 2. Particulars of the owners: The return contains the names and addresses of the depositors/owners and the amount or description of each unclaimed deposit or article of value, so far as these are known to the banking company. 3. Transfer of unclaimed deposits: Deposits which remain unclaimed for the prescribed period of ten years are transferred/paid over to the Federal Government through the State Bank, and the banking company is discharged from its liability to that extent. 4. Articles of value: Articles of value remaining unclaimed may, after due notice to the owner at his last known address, be opened and disposed of by the banking company in the prescribed manner, and the proceeds dealt with like unclaimed deposits. 5. Protection of the owner's claim: The transfer does not extinguish the right of the true owner; the particulars of unclaimed deposits are published by the State Bank so that the owner or his legal heirs may claim the amount in the prescribed manner.

  26. What are the obligations of an agent to the principal?
    Topic: Agency Law

    An agent has the following obligations towards his principal: 1. An agent must obey the reasonable instructions given by the principal. 2. The agent must not do acts that have not been expressly or impliedly authorized by the principal. 3. The agent must use reasonable care and skill in performing his duties. 4. The agent must be loyal to the principal; he must refrain from putting himself in a position that would ordinarily encourage a conflict between his own interests and those of the principal. 5. The agent must keep the principal informed as to all facts that materially affect the agency relationship.

  27. What are the duties of a customer towards his banker?
    Topic: Banker-Customer Relationship

    The customer has the following duties towards his banker: 1. To present cheques for payment and collection within the business hours of his banker (Section 72 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881). 2. To see that the cheques and other instruments are presented for payment within a reasonable time from the date of their issue (Section 84 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881). 3. To take reasonable care of the safe custody of his cheque book; if a customer fails in this duty, he is held responsible for his negligence in leaving his cheques unprotected. 4. To draw the cheques very carefully and in such a way that there is no room left for any fraudulent alteration or addition.

  28. Define consortium finance.
    Topic: Banking Finance Concepts • 3 marks marks

    Consortium finance is a method of financing in which two or more banks or financial institutions jointly provide a loan or credit facility to a single borrower, usually for a large project whose financing requirement is beyond the exposure limit or capacity of one bank. The participating institutions share the amount of finance, the risk and the security in agreed proportions, common documentation is executed, and one institution (the lead bank) normally arranges and administers the facility on behalf of the consortium.

  29. Define hypothecation.
    Topic: Banking Finance Concepts • 3 marks marks

    Hypothecation is a charge created on movable property (such as goods, stocks or vehicles) in favour of the lender to secure a loan, without transferring either the ownership or the possession of the property to the lender. The borrower retains possession and use of the goods, while the bank holds a charge over them; if the borrower defaults, the bank can take possession of the hypothecated property and sell it to recover its dues.

  30. Define money market.
    Topic: Financial Markets & Instruments • 3 marks marks

    The money market is the market for short-term funds, i.e. for funds lent and borrowed for a short period, normally up to one year. It deals in highly liquid, low-risk instruments such as bills of exchange, repurchase agreements (repos), federal funds, treasury bills and bankers' acceptances. It provides short-term liquidity to banks, businesses and the government, and it is the market through which the central bank operates its monetary policy.

  31. Differentiate between blank endorsement and full endorsement.
    Topic: Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 • 5 marks marks

    Blank endorsement: An endorsement is blank when the endorser merely signs his name on the back of the instrument and does not name any endorsee. Its effects are: (i) the instrument becomes payable to bearer; (ii) it can be transferred by mere delivery, without any further endorsement; and (iii) any holder may convert it into a full endorsement by writing the name of an endorsee above the endorser's signature. Full endorsement: An endorsement is full (special) when the endorser, besides signing his name, directs the amount of the instrument to be paid to a specified person, or to his order. Its effects are: (i) the instrument remains an order instrument; (ii) only the named endorsee can further transfer it, and that too by his own endorsement followed by delivery; and (iii) it cannot be negotiated by mere delivery.

  32. Briefly explain R-14 auto loans.
    Topic: SBP Prudential Regulations • 5 marks marks

    Regulation R-14 of the State Bank of Pakistan's Prudential Regulations for Consumer Financing deals with auto loans, i.e. loans extended by banks/DFIs to individuals for the purchase of vehicles. Its main features are: 1. The loan is given for the purchase of a new or used vehicle for personal use, and the vehicle is hypothecated/registered in favour of the bank until the loan is repaid. 2. The borrower must contribute a minimum equity (down payment) from his own sources, so that the bank finances only a prescribed portion of the vehicle's value. 3. The total financing to a borrower, and the maximum tenure of the auto loan, are subject to the limits prescribed by the State Bank, and the borrower's overall debt burden must remain within the prescribed debt-burden ratio. 4. The vehicle must remain comprehensively insured throughout the loan period, with the bank's interest noted in the insurance policy. 5. Banks must assess the repayment capacity of the borrower and observe all other applicable prudential requirements before approving the loan.

  33. Write a note on Regulation R-8 (Classification and Provisioning) and Regulation R-6 (Margin Requirements).
    Topic: SBP Prudential Regulations • 5 marks marks

    Regulation R-8 — Classification and Provisioning: Under the State Bank's Prudential Regulations, every bank/DFI must regularly classify its loans and advances on the basis of the period for which they have remained overdue, into categories such as Other Assets Especially Mentioned (OAEM), Substandard, Doubtful and Loss. Against the classified loans the bank must make provision at the rates prescribed by the State Bank (after deducting the value of eligible security), so that expected losses are recognized in time and the bank's accounts show a true picture. Regulation R-6 — Margin Requirements: This regulation requires banks/DFIs to obtain and maintain the prescribed margin while lending against securities, such as shares, commodities and other marketable assets. The margin is the difference between the market value of the security and the amount of the loan; it protects the bank against a fall in the value of the security. Banks must value the securities prudently, monitor the margin regularly and call for additional security or reduce the loan if the margin falls below the prescribed level.
