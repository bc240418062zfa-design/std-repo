  1. Define teacher professionalism. What are its main features?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Teacher professionalism means meeting certain standards in education and is related to proficiency. It is the quality of one's practice and can be interpreted as a multi-dimensional structure including one's work behaviors and attitudes. It is commonly discussed on sociological, educational and ideological bases in the literature. Its main features are: (1) it is related to the improvement in the quality of service rather than the enhancement of status; (2) it is measured by the best and the highest standards; (3) it demands professional autonomy, a defined body of knowledge and a code of ethics; (4) its meaning is dynamic, changing in response to external pressures, public discourses and scientific developments; and (5) it is a process more than an outcome, in which standards create a professional environment of 'best practice' procedures.

  2. Differentiate between a profession and an occupation.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A profession is a distinct category of occupational work which performs a crucial social function. It requires a significant period of hands-on practical experience (institutional preparation), a generalized and systematic body of knowledge, prolonged specialized training, autonomy in decision making, licensing, certification and entry requirements, and self-control of behavior through codes of ethics. An occupation, on the other hand, is any work or job a person does for livelihood; it does not necessarily require a defined body of knowledge, professional autonomy or a code of conduct. Occupation and profession also differ with regard to moral values: a profession carries a distinct ethical dimension and provides an important public service, while an occupation may not. Teaching, nursing and similar services are therefore accepted as 'semi' or 'quasi' professions because they possess most, but not all, characteristics of full professions like medicine and law.

  3. What are the characteristics of a profession? Explain any five.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The characteristics of a profession identified in the literature are: (1) A defined body of knowledge — professionals have generalized and systematic knowledge and theoretically and practically grounded expertise. (2) Autonomy — professionals have autonomous decision-making powers, free from external pressures, and require a high degree of individual autonomy or independence of judgment for effective practice. (3) Code of ethics — professions locate their activities outside the market by using ideal-regarding criteria and control behavior through a code of conduct and a public ethical code of practice. (4) Licensing, certification and entry requirements — a professional must pass prescribed examinations and meet entry standards to attain a certain level of knowledge. (5) Important public service — professions provide an important public service and perform a crucial social function. (6) Institutional preparation — a long period of education, training and hands-on practical experience. Publications, however, are not a characteristic of a profession.

  4. Explain Hargreaves' four historical ages of teacher professionalism.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Hargreaves (2000) analyzes the development of teacher professionalism as passing through four historical phases in many countries. (1) The pre-professional age: teaching was managerially demanding but technically simple; the teacher had to obey organizational orders at any cost and teach according to set standards. (2) The age of the autonomous professional: autonomy was considered an important component of the teaching profession and teachers gained considerable pedagogical freedom. (3) The age of the collegial profession: this age draws attention with increasing efforts to create strong professional cultures of collaboration, common purpose and shared practices to cope with uncertainty and rapid change. (4) The post-professional age: it is marked by a struggle between forces and groups intent on deprofessionalizing the work of teaching. Today's era of polarized directions is defined by Hargreaves as postmodern professionalism.

  5. Differentiate between old professionalism and new (transformative) professionalism.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Old professionalism is attributed to have: exclusive membership, conservative practices, self-interest, external regulation, being slow to change and reactive. It emphasized the exclusionary nature of the profession — what to include and what to exclude — and located professional activities outside the market through ideal-regarding criteria. New (transformative) professionalism, in contrast, is attributed to have: inclusive membership, a public ethical code of practice, a collaborative and collegial orientation, an activist orientation, being flexible and progressive, responsive to change, self-regulating, policy-active, enquiry-oriented and knowledge building. The new understanding of teacher professionalism provides professional space and conditions for teachers to take responsibility in their practices, and reflects a rapid shift from occupational to organizational values.

  6. What is professionalization? How is it different from professionalism?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Professionalization is a process in which a professional group pursues, develops, acquires and maintains more characteristics of a profession. It is related to 'promoting the material and ideal interests of an occupational group' and is a measure of the social strength and authority of that group. Its outcome is a higher degree of autonomy for the occupation. Professionalism, on the other hand, is the quality of practice itself — the conduct, demeanour and standards which guide the work of professionals, and the extent to which members demonstrate the attributes of a profession in their behavior and attitudes. In short, professionalization is the collective process of becoming a profession, while professionalism is the standard of behavior and practice expected from its members. 'Professionality' is known as the instantiation (embodiment) of professionalism in an individual practitioner.

  7. Define ethics. Differentiate between ethics and morals.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Ethics are the fundamental ways of human behavior or basic principles of social and personal life. Ethics is a set of moral principles concerned with the moral philosophy or codes practiced by a person or group of people; it is the discipline dealing with what is good and bad, and can be viewed as a 'philosophy of morality' as it deals with ought and ought not (Mohony, 2009). Ethics are considered to be the rules or code of conduct provided by external sources in the workplace or governing bodies, and are dependent on others for definition. Morals, in contrast, are the internal principles of an individual's life — principles of habits with respect to right or wrong conduct. Morals prescribe dos and don'ts and are ultimately a personal compass of right and wrong. A person may strictly follow ethical principles yet not have any morals at all.

  8. What is a code of ethics? Discuss the purpose of a code of professional conduct.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A code of professional ethics is a set of acceptable standards of personal and business behavior, values and guiding principles, often established by a professional organization to aid and direct its members in performing their work. Its importance for a profession cannot be overstated. The purpose of a code of ethics is that the practitioner behaves responsibly; it wins the trust of the public and public bodies (government), expresses the ethical dimension of the profession in a code of practice, and provides self-regulation to the profession. The Alberta Teachers' Association, for example, presented a Code of Professional Conduct in relation to the teacher's conduct toward pupils, colleagues and school authorities. Drafting the codes of ethics and representing the profession before the public is known as self-regulation.

  9. What are the 3 Rs of ethics? Explain briefly.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The 3 Rs of ethics are Respect, Responsibility and Rules. (1) Respect: a professional must show respect for students, colleagues, parents and all stakeholders; teachers are representative of moral values and virtues, and their behavior reflects ethical and moral integrity. (2) Responsibility: teaching is deemed a profession with responsibility, trust and truthfulness; the teacher performs moral duties formally and informally and is considered to be in the same role inside and outside the classroom. (3) Rules: ethics provide the rules or code of conduct given by external sources, professional organizations and governing bodies, which every professional is restricted to follow for the benefit of stakeholders. 'Review' is not included in the 3 Rs of ethics.

  10. Define accountability and explain its types.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Accountability means holding actors to account for their actions and performance; teachers work under a social contract in which the responsibility of teaching within certain parameters is set by society, and teaching is a notion of regulated autonomy because it acts on behalf of the state in the best interest of its citizens. The course discusses five types of accountability. (1) Political accountability: democratic institutions and processes hold actors to account. (2) Organizational accountability: answerability within the organization's hierarchy and rules. (3) Legal accountability: answerability under laws and regulations. (4) Professional accountability: answerability to the standards, code of ethics and peer judgment of the profession itself. Accountability connects with public trust, public accountability and membership of professional societies, which are among the characteristics of teacher professionalism derived from the analysis of literature.

  11. Explain the qualities of an effective teacher as a person.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The teacher's personality is one of the first sets of characteristics to look for in an effective teacher, because how a teacher presents himself makes an impression on administrators, colleagues, parents and students, and the way a teacher relates to pupils has an impact on the students' experience in the class. Positive qualities of an effective teacher as a person include: assumes ownership for the classroom and the students' success; uses personal experiences as examples in teaching; dresses appropriately for the position; maintains confidential trust and respect; enjoys teaching and expects students to enjoy learning; looks for the win-win solution in conflict situations; listens attentively to student questions and comments; responds to students with respect, even in difficult situations; communicates high expectations consistently; conducts one-on-one conversations with students; has positive dialogue and interactions with students outside the classroom; invests time with single students or small groups; and speaks in an appropriate tone and volume.

  12. What are the red flags of ineffective teaching? Discuss with examples.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Red flags are the warning signs of ineffective teaching and unprofessional behavior. Red flags for the teacher's personality include: being confrontational with students, lacking conflict resolution skills, and having classroom discipline problems; working actively with students is not a red flag. Red flags of ineffective teaching include: the teacher keeps an unclean or disorderly classroom; believes that teaching is just a job; arrives late to school and class on a regular basis; is not sensitive to a student's culture or heritage; expresses bias (positive or negative) with regard to students; exhibits defensive behavior for no apparent reason; and does not accept responsibility for what occurs in the classroom. Such behavior damages the interpersonal climate, whereas teachers' smiling and joking has a significant positive influence on the interpersonal climate of the classroom.

  13. What are instructional organizers? Explain their role in classroom management and organization.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Instructional organizers are the arrangements and displays through which a teacher organizes the classroom environment and communicates the plan of work — for example, rules posted on the wall, the daily lesson objectives and activities posted in the classroom, seating arrangements and classroom displays. A well-organized classroom has various instructional organizers in it, and classroom displays often reveal how a teacher uses the space. Their role in classroom management and organization is that: an observer can quickly understand the work by viewing the daily lesson objectives and activities posted; students know the daily plan because an agenda of objectives and activities is given; positions of chairs in groups or around tables promote interaction; clear expectations and firm, consistent responses manage student behavior; and established routines develop student responsibilities and leadership. A good plan does not guarantee high-quality instruction, but a poor plan most certainly contributes to ineffective instruction.

  14. Explain the teamwork model of Katzenbach and Smith. Differentiate between pseudo, real and high-performance teams.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The Teamwork Model was developed by Katzenbach and Smith in 1993. It classifies teams by their performance and working approach. (1) Working group/team: members interact mainly to share information and best practices; there is no common purpose requiring joint work. (2) Pseudo team: a group that calls itself a team but does not focus on collective performance; it performs worse than a working group. (3) Real team: members share common goals and share some responsibility; a small number of people with complementary skills who are equally committed to a common purpose and hold themselves mutually accountable. (4) High-performance team: it meets all the conditions of a real team and additionally has members who are deeply committed to one another's personal growth and success. Sharing common goals and responsibilities is therefore the characteristic of a real team.

  15. What is effective professional development? Explain its elements and features.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Professional development (PD) is important for teachers to learn and apply new knowledge; in the long run, training is imperative for teachers. Effective professional development is the structured professional learning that improves teachers' practice and students' results. According to the course, its elements include: content focused learning, active learning and a collaborative approach; reflective practice is not counted among its elements. Feedback and reflection are the two powerful tools in effective professional development. Its main feature is structured professional learning with a clear purpose, adequate duration and follow-up support such as coaching and expert guidance. Barriers to the implementation of PD at school level include lack of time, lack of resources and poor working conditions, which affect teachers' motivation, morale and performance. Peer gathering, however, is not a standard mode of professional development; standard modes include workshops, seminars, conferences and in-service training programs.

  16. Discuss the perspectives on professionalism found in the literature.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Different perspectives on professionalism are found in the literature. (1) The professionalization project: it focuses on the development of the knowledge profession and the improvement of standards for professional performance; two perspectives are used to discuss it. (2) The logic of professionalism: it examines professionalism as one of the logics (with the free market and bureaucracy) by which work is organized and controlled. (3) High expectations in modern society: professionalism is seen as a response to the rising expectations society places on professionals. Professional autonomy, however, is not a perspective but a characteristic of professionalism. Other perspectives discussed in the course include the idealistic perspective (emphasizing service and altruism), the critical/critique perspective (emphasizing decision makers' recognizing inequities in both schools and society, and the exclusionary nature of professions) and the plural perspective. The ethical and altruistic character of professionalism is connected to the power imbalance between the professional and the client.

  17. Explain Freidson's three logics of professionalism.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Freidson (2001) identified the three different logics by which occupational work can be organized and controlled. (1) Logic of the free market: everyone is free to buy or sell goods and services; work is controlled by consumer choice, competition and the profit motive. (2) Logic of the bureaucracy: the production and distribution of goods and services is planned, controlled and regulated by the administration of a large organization through rules, hierarchy and managerial control. (3) Logic of professionalism: work is controlled by the professionals themselves, who possess specialized knowledge, enjoy freedom of establishment and occupational autonomy, and regulate their work through collegial authority and a code of ethics. Teachers, however, do not have freedom of establishment; they are directly employed by schools. The characteristics related to the logic of market do not include boundary work, which belongs to the logic of professionalism.

  18. What are professional standards for teachers? Discuss the national professional standards for initial teacher preparation in Pakistan.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Professional standards are the statements of what teachers should know, value and be able to do; standards create a professional environment of 'best practice' procedures enabling organizations to confidently create systems, policies and procedures. A professional standard has three components: knowledge and understandings (what a teacher knows), dispositions (behaviors, attitudes and values) and performance and skills (what a teacher can do). There are ten professional standards for initial teacher preparation in Pakistan. They include: Subject Matter Knowledge (demands teachers to have understanding of the national curriculum framework), Human Growth and Development (identifying developing abilities of students and guiding students with diverse emotional and psychical challenges), Knowledge of Islamic Ethical Values, Learning Environment (how learning takes place in the classroom) and Assessment (fair, objective assessment and reporting to students and families).

  19. Define phronesis. Who proposed the idea of phronesis and why is it important for teachers?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Phronesis is usually translated as practical wisdom. The idea of phronesis was proposed by Aristotle. It is the intellectual virtue of deliberating well about what is good and advantageous in particular situations — knowing what to do, in the right way, at the right time, for the right reasons. Phronesis is important for teachers because teaching cannot be reduced to technical rules (techne) or theory (episteme) alone; every classroom situation is unique, and the teacher must exercise situated professional judgment. The individual has the capacity to develop morally over their lifetime (Kohlberg), and practical wisdom grows through experience, reflection and practice — the performance dimension of teaching improves, like any other performance, with appropriate practice. A professional who acts according to his/her own vision, knowledge, attitudes, skills and responsibility exercises this practical wisdom as autonomy in the classroom.

  20. What is cooperative learning? Explain its main features.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Cooperative learning is an instructional strategy in which students work in a small group to achieve the common goals. Its main features, as summarized by Mill (1990), are: groups are formed on the basis of heterogeneity, with mixed-ability students and differences in learning abilities, cultural diversity and gender; students work interdependently and share responsibility for the group's outcome; leadership skills in group work are designed to build team skills and social skills, helping students engage in cooperative interaction and show mutual respect; and the teacher facilitates rather than directs, visiting each group and asking questions. Social interactions between students and teachers contribute to creating a healthy learning process, and the social context provides opportunities for meaningful advisement, development of friendships and testing of ideas and talents. Students engaging with each other during learning is known as collaborative learning.

  21. Discuss the role of a teacher in education and in a student's life.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Teachers play a key role in education and in the student's life; in many cases of a successful student there seems to be a good teacher, and the relationship between them is harmonious, with complete dedication and affection from the teacher towards the student. A teacher transforms society through teaching and sets examples for the coming generations — some of the greatest teachers were the cause of political and industrial revolutions around the world, and the vision of great teachers helped various societies to gain self-sufficiency and financial freedom. The teacher has to imbibe the education of his subject completely to deliver it when needed, impart knowledge within a short time in the most understandable way, avoid creating confusion or misrepresenting education, and set himself as a role model, for at school a teacher is like a parent to the student. The teacher also helps students overcome problems of behavior and thought with special affection and attention, guides them about future studies and careers, and maintains a good set of manners, because students generally keep a watch on the teacher's life.

  22. Explain the trait approach and the cognitive approach to values education.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The trait approach is based on the view that values education should comprise predetermined traits or qualities that can be taught; it assumes a fixed list of virtues that teachers transmit to students. The cognitive approach, in contrast, is called 'cognitive' because it bases values education, like intellectual education, on the active thinking of students about values. The focus of the cognitive theorists is therefore to improve reasoning and facilitate movement through the six stages of moral development identified by Kohlberg (1975) towards moral autonomy, rather than to differentiate between right and wrong decisions. Values clarification is based on the notion of values-relativity, in contrast to the trait approach. The challenge of teaching values education is to focus on the need for teachers to create warm and supportive classroom environments in which students feel free to express their thoughts and feelings and to be tolerant of different student opinions.

  23. Discuss the Islamic perspective of ethics in education.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Islam places knowledge at the center of ethics in education. The Holy Prophet (PBUH) said: 'Getting knowledge is obligatory to every Muslim man and woman.' Providing education is a moral duty, and teaching is a moral and ethical activity in which teachers are representative of moral values and virtues; society expects that their behavior reflects ethical and moral integrity. According to the knowledge of Islamic ethical values, the meaning of 'Maroof' is good (that which is known and recognized as right), as opposed to evil. The positive and negative aspects of a teacher's personality become part of the student's personality, because students imitate their teachers; therefore the teacher performs his moral duty formally and informally and is considered to be in the same role inside and outside the classroom. Due to a lack of ethical considerations in educational setups, discipline problems are increasing day by day.

  24. What is teacher autonomy? Discuss its importance for the teaching profession.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Teacher autonomy refers to the professional independence of teachers in schools — the concept of teachers practicing according to their own vision, knowledge, attitudes, skills and responsibility. Autonomy is one of the main focuses featured in professionalism characteristics. Its importance is that: professional practitioners require a high degree of individual autonomy or independence of judgment for effective practice; autonomy not only functions as a buffer against the pressures on teachers but is also a means of strengthening them in a personal and professional sense; it has an opposite function to organizational control; and professionals should have autonomous decision-making powers free from external pressures. Autonomy was considered an important component of the teaching profession in the age of the autonomous professional. However, autonomy is regulated: teaching is a notion of regulated autonomy because teachers act on behalf of the state in the best interest of its citizens, and due to market influence in education, teachers' autonomy decreases.

  25. Discuss the barriers to teacher professionalism.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Several barriers hinder teacher professionalism. Poor working conditions affect teachers' motivation, morale and performance. Public school teachers do not have a high level of authority, and teachers are monitored by administrators for the consistency between their performance and set standards, which limits professional judgment. Market influence and managerial controls reduce teachers' autonomy, while a lack of time, resources and support obstructs the implementation of professional development at school level. Being elitist may create mistrust among colleagues in an organization, and faculty political interaction affects school-based leadership adversely. Autonomy, however, is not a barrier to teacher professionalism; it is one of its essential characteristics. Overcoming these barriers requires professional development, collaborative cultures and recognition of teaching as a dynamic and innovative profession.

  26. What is activist teacher professionalism? Discuss its key concepts and ethical practices.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Activist teacher professionalism is a form of new professionalism in which teachers act collectively and democratically to improve education and society, rather than working as isolated individuals. Collaboration with the stakeholders outside the school is called democratic professionalism, and it is a key expression of the activist stance. The development of activist teacher professionalism is based on concepts of identity, community and collective action within a democratic framework. Its ethical practices involve how people deliberate together, recognize inequities and work for social justice; the critique perspective, which emphasizes decision makers' recognizing inequities in both schools and society, supports this orientation. Activist professionalism is inclusive, collaborative and collegial, responsive to change, policy-active and enquiry-oriented, matching the features of new (transformative) professionalism.

  27. Explain the concept of instructional awareness and how it can be developed.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Instructional awareness (or intellectual awareness) is the teacher's conscious understanding of what happens in his/her teaching and how it affects students' learning; the first step in improving teaching is called instructional awareness. Faculty members must develop instructional awareness to improve their teaching. It can be developed by: reviewing videotaped samples of teaching, which is one of the best ways to develop it; observing others teach, preferably the same subject; reading about teaching performance; and obtaining feedback about one's teaching from students and peers — feedback is the central theme of communication theory and a dominant mechanism of experiential learning. Appropriate practice therefore consists of observation of other teachers, reading about teaching performance and obtaining feedback about one's teaching. Discussion with peers alone, without these strategies, does not develop instructional awareness.

  28. Discuss the challenges faced by the education system in developing countries with reference to teacher professionalism.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The major challenge faced by the education system in developing countries is quality. With the advent of technology and the impact of social media, ethical standards are being questioned and multiple challenges are coming up. Changes in the school system have led to new challenges for schools: teachers face low status and pay, poor working conditions, weak institutional preparation and limited opportunities for professional development, while being monitored against standards they have little authority to shape. Lack of confidence among teachers can lead to unprofessional behavior and social mistakes, and a lack of ethical considerations increases discipline problems. Improving quality requires focus on capacity building, personalized teaching and learning activities, strong professional cultures of collaboration, and treating teaching as a dynamic and innovative profession in which teachers — the key agents to bring change in the school — are trusted as professionals.
