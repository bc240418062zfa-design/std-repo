EDU401 – Contemporary Issues and Trends in Education: Midterm Important Subjective (43 Solved Q&As)

  1. Define contemporary, trends and issues.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Contemporary means related to the present time — what we experience in education today; a scenario of the present time is called contemporary. Trends are the positive things that are happening in education — the patterns that we like to see and continue. Issues are the negative side: the not-so-good things that happen in education.

  2. What does Article 37-B of the Constitution of Pakistan say about education? Also mention Article 25-A.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    According to the 1973 Constitution of Pakistan, Article 37-B states that the state shall remove illiteracy and provide free and compulsory secondary education. There are two points in this article: first, the removal of illiteracy; second, free and compulsory secondary education — which is even better than the conventions studied, because secondary education means education up to level 10 (matric). Article 25-A further makes the state responsible for providing free and compulsory education to all children of the age of five to sixteen years.

  3. Write a short note on the Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989).
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    The Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989) explicitly recognized education as a fundamental right. Under it, states make primary education compulsory and available free to all children, encourage the development of different forms of secondary education including general and vocational education, and ensure equal opportunity for all students. Education of a child does not stop at the elementary level; vocational education helps a student to earn a living.

  4. Describe the goals of Education for All (EFA).
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    The Dakar Framework for Action set the following goals: (1) expanding and improving comprehensive early childhood care and education; (2) ensuring that by 2015 all children, particularly girls and children of ethnic minorities, have access to free and compulsory primary education; (3) ensuring that the learning needs of all young people and adults are met through equitable access to appropriate learning and life-skills programmes; (4) achieving a 50 percent improvement in levels of adult literacy by 2015; (5) eliminating gender disparities in primary and secondary education (disparity to be removed in the first five years, equality by 2015); and (6) improving all aspects of the quality of education so that recognized and measurable learning outcomes are achieved by all.

  5. What are the obstacles in the way of free and accessible education?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Incorporating free and accessible education as a guiding principle requires change in education systems, and this change process is faced with several obstacles: existing attitudes and values; lack of understanding; lack of necessary skills; limited resources; and inappropriate organization. These change processes often begin at a small scale and involve important shifts at the system level as well as the societal level.

  6. Write the strategies for developing accessible curricula.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    1. Providing a flexible timeframe for students studying particular subjects. 2. Giving greater freedom to teachers in choosing their working methods. 3. Allowing teachers the opportunity of giving special support in practical subjects (e.g. orientation, mobility) over and above the periods allotted for more traditional subjects. 4. Allotting time for additional assistance for classroom-based work. 5. Emphasizing aspects of pre-vocational training.

  7. Discuss health as an equity issue in education. / What health issues do we face in education?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Health is an important equity issue in education: without proper health care a child cannot make it through even the primary years of education, because a healthy body has a healthy mind. The main health issues are: poor nutrition and low protein intake, which lower achievement; iron deficiency, which reduces energy and learning; malnutrition on one side and obesity on the other — obese children are teased and bullied at school, which lowers their self-esteem and attendance; poor physical environment, lack of exercise, and poor ventilation and air quality, which compromise concentration and memory and reduce the performance of teachers and students. Unequal social conditions produce unequal health outcomes: government facilities do not guarantee proper treatment and private health care is frightfully expensive.

  8. What is silent exclusion?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Silent exclusion is the second equity issue in education. If a child never comes to school he is excluded; if he comes and drops out, he is excluded. Silent exclusion refers to the children who come to school yet gain nothing. These students are low attenders, repeaters and low achievers; they are present in the classroom but teachers do not give them attention. Along with the dropouts, these students are also affected by poverty and are vulnerable to dropping out.

  9. Define racism, sexism and classism.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Racism is the belief that one's own culture or racial heritage is innately superior to that of others, hence the lack of respect or appreciation for those who belong to a different race. Sexism is a system of attitudes, actions and structures that subordinates others on the basis of their sex, where the usual victims are women. Classism is distancing from, and perceiving, the poor as 'the other'.

  10. What reforms can address the socio-cultural issues in education?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    In teacher training we focus a lot on content and professional characteristics, but we do not focus on how to deal with minorities. The needed reforms are: include content on dealing with minorities in teacher training so that a more tolerant society can emerge; create an interface with minorities by bringing them into classrooms and grounds for constructive, healthy activities; make Ethics a compulsory subject for all students, as all religions teach the same values; develop public-private partnerships to build resources, bringing minorities into play through private members of society; and encourage public curricula reforms — textbooks should include the contributions of minorities to the society of Pakistan, including disadvantaged groups and females.

  11. What is peace? What are the other names/words for peace?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Peace is a concept of societal friendship and harmony in the absence of hostility and violence; in a social sense it means a lack of conflict and freedom from fear of violence between individuals or groups. Another word for peace is equality, and other words for equality are equity, symmetry, reciprocity, equal rights and equal dignity. Peace has levels such as personal peace, intergroup/social peace — respect for other groups within a nation, justice, tolerance and cooperation.

  12. What is peace education?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Peace education is the process of acquiring the values, knowledge, attitudes, skills and behaviors to live in harmony with oneself, with others and with the natural environment. It is the pedagogical efforts to create a world at peace. Peace education activities promote the knowledge, skills and attitudes that help people prevent the occurrence of conflict, resolve conflicts peacefully, and create social conditions conducive to peace; core values of nonviolence and social justice are central to it. Its purposes include the elimination of social injustice, the rejection of violence and the abolition of war.

  13. Write the components of a peace education curriculum.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Some components of a peace education curriculum are: emotions and feelings; anger and anger management; violence in media; communication; empathy; cooperation; self-esteem; tolerance; conflict resolution; and keeping safe. It also covers how emotions affect behavior and the effect of violence on the emotions and feelings of children.

  14. What do peace studies do? Why is peace education needed?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Peace studies is a process, and peace education seeks to build peace through pedagogy. Peace studies explore the handling of conflict by peaceful means: conflict can be resolved through argumentation, but how we resolve a conflict peacefully is what peace studies study — only then can peace prevail. Peace studies focus less on actors and focus more on cultures, and the study of peace is intimately related to the study of conflict.

  15. 'Peace education appreciates the richness of the concept of peace.' Explain.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Peace education appreciates the richness of the concept of peace because peace is not merely the absence of war or violence (negative peace); it is also the presence of conditions of well-being and just relationships (positive peace). The concept covers harmony with oneself, with others and with the natural environment, and includes values such as justice, tolerance, cooperation and equality. Because the concept is so rich and multi-dimensional, peace education draws on knowledge, attitudes and skills from many fields to build a complete culture of peace.

  16. Describe the four stages in the development of peace studies.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Peace studies developed through four stages. In the multi-disciplinary stage, a university or conference invites specialists from several disciplines to contribute to peace studies from their own angle. In the inter-disciplinary stage, dialogue among approaches is encouraged, for example by addressing the same event, phase in history or problem. In the cross-disciplinary stage, this dialogue goes further into mutual learning — a psychologist may pick up a sociological hypothesis and explore its psychology, and vice versa. In the trans-disciplinary stage, the problem itself determines the choice of intellectual tools, which usually come from the tool chests of several disciplines. Peace studies are therefore trans- rather than inter-disciplinary: in a good peace researcher, the PhD field is no longer visible.

  17. Give the attitudes and values of peace education.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Core values of nonviolence and social justice are central to peace education. The attitudes and values it promotes include tolerance, empathy, cooperation, respect for other groups within the nation, justice and a concern for others' feelings. It develops self-awareness and awareness of one's own needs, understanding of the nature of conflict and peace, and skills of cooperation, conflict avoidance and conflict resolution, so that learners have the capacity and motivation, individually and collectively, to live in peace with others.

  18. What feelings are associated with conflicts?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 2 marks marks

    The feelings commonly associated with conflict are: anger, frustration, fear and excitement. Each actor in a conflict has its own angle, and parties experience these feelings in different intensities depending on how they perceive the situation.

  19. Describe the components of a conflict situation.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    The issues in a conflict situation consist of: the facts — the present situation and problems; the goals — how things ought to be, the future conditions sought; the methods — the best, easiest, quickest and most ethical ways; the values — beliefs about priorities that should be observed in choosing goals and methods; and the history — what has gone on before. The components of a conflict situation are: frustration, when you feel blocked; conceptualization of the problem — what is going on; conceptualization of behaviors and intentions — what does that mean; and the outcome — emotional, cognitive and behavioral.

  20. Briefly describe the kinds of conflict styles. When is each style effective?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    There are five conflict styles. Competing ('Might makes right') is high on assertiveness and low on cooperativeness — used when quick decisive action is needed, when the issue is vital and the right course is clear, or on important issues where unpopular action must be taken. Collaborating ('Two heads are better than one') is high on both assertiveness and cooperativeness — used when it is important that both sides be integrated. Compromising is moderate on both — each side sacrifices part of its goals for a common agreement. Avoiding is low on both — its goal is to delay; used when the issue is trivial, when the costs of conflict outweigh the benefits of resolution, or when you know you cannot be satisfied. Accommodating ('Kill your enemies with kindness') is low on assertiveness and high on cooperativeness — its goal is to yield; used when harmony is important, when you are wrong, when creating goodwill is paramount, or when it is important for others to learn from experience. In animal terms these styles are shown as the Shark (forcing/competing), the Owl (confronting/collaborating), the Fox (compromising), the Turtle (withdrawing/avoiding) and the Teddy Bear (smoothing/accommodating).

  21. What is the Thomas-Kilmann Inventory (TKI)? Write its properties/benefits.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    The Thomas-Kilmann Inventory (TKI) is an instrument that assesses an individual's preferences or inclinations in handling conflict. It helps a person identify his or her own conflict style, which is useful when one gets into a conflict. It compares individual styles with those of other managers who have taken the assessment, and suggests ways to most effectively use and expand one's repertoire of conflict management skills. The five styles it measures — competing, collaborating, compromising, avoiding and accommodating — are understood in relation to two dimensions: assertiveness and cooperativeness.

  22. What is the SAGE conflict response style?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    The Sage is a conflict response style with a win/win orientation. The Sage is a problem solver who uses cooperative problem solving, with emphasis on preserving the relationship and meeting one's own goals as well as those of others.

  23. Write the rules for handling conflict on the road (road rules of conflict handling).
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    1. Go slow: remember, it takes time to settle a conflict; go slow at first because conflict resolvers usually encounter some rough roads in the beginning, and keep using your I-way map (I-messages) to reach safer roads. 2. Detour ahead: sometimes tempers are flaring so much that it may be necessary to take a temporary detour from the communication I-way; once tempers have calmed down, you may begin using I-statements to solve the problem. 3. Exit ahead: most conflicts can be resolved through effective communication; however, if you begin to feel threatened or unsafe, you should exit the communication I-way and seek safety.

  24. What is a madrassa? From which word does 'madrassa' originate?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    'Madrassa' originates from the Arabic word 'Darasa', meaning to learn/study. A madrassa is a religious educational institution where Islamic education is imparted. In Pakistan, madaaris are considered non-governmental organizations (NGOs) that provide free education, boarding and lodging to their students and help to educate the poverty-stricken children of society. Each madrassa is controlled by a board or Wafaq, which runs its own system of education, holds annual exams and distributes certificates.

  25. Name the five wafaqs (madrassa boards) of Pakistan.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    There are five madrassa boards or wafaqs in Pakistan, all members of Ittehad Tanzeemat Madaris Deenia (ITMD), established in 2003: 1. Tanzeem-ul-Madaris (Barelvi) — about 8,000 registered madaaris. 2. Wafaq-ul-Madaris Al-Arabia (Deobandi) — about 16,800 registered madaaris. 3. Wafaq-ul-Madaris Al-Salafia (Ahl-e-Hadith), established 1955, headquarters Faisalabad — about 1,400 registered madaaris. 4. Wafaq-ul-Madaris Al-Shia (Jaffari Shia), established 1960, headquarters Lahore — about 413 registered madaaris. 5. Rabita-ul-Madaris (Jamaat-e-Islami) — about 1,000 registered madaaris.

  26. Give the growth of madaaris in Pakistan (number of madaaris in different years).
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    The number of madaaris in Pakistan has grown steadily: 137 before 1947; 245 in 1947; 908 in 1971; 2,861 in 1988; 6,761 in 2000; and 19,104 in 2010. This growth shows the continuous expansion of madrassa education in the country since independence.

  27. What are the madrassa debate challenges?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    1. Registration: registration of madaaris is a major problem debated across the board; the exact figure of madaaris is not clearly identified, as only those registered with one of the five wafaqs can be numbered, while a large number operate without any formal affiliation and registration. 2. Curriculum: the madrassa curriculum is under heated debate inside the madaaris and beyond; according to one school of thought it does not address the modern requirements of the present era, and many observers believe some subjects taught foster provocation leading students to extremism and militancy. 3. Reforms: since 1999 the issue of madaaris reforms has been negotiated between the government and the administrations of wafaqs, but the demands of the madaaris, which of them have been accepted, and the stance of the government are not clear. 4. Fund raising: some analysts believe madaaris take funds from foreign countries to promote their agendas and are used as platforms for exerting the influence of favored sects. 5. Foreign students: funding the education of foreign students is an important topic of debate; the modalities of education of foreign students in Pakistani madaaris are yet to be resolved.

  28. Write a note on foreign students and foreign funding in madaaris.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Foreign students have been studying religious education subjects in Pakistan for a long time; students from more than 64 countries, including the US, UK, Africa, India, China and Russia, are getting education from Pakistani seminaries. On funding, the madaaris want to remain independent in their financial matters and do not allow state intervention regarding their sources of funding; some madaaris maintain their budgets through audits and present them before students and administration. It is argued that if foreign NGOs and institutions can donate towards education and health sectors, they should be allowed to support madaaris on the ground of Islamic brotherhood, provided funds are sent through the respective embassies to ensure transparency.

  29. Explain 'Madrassa and Politics'.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    The political role of the madrassa has been the main focus and concern of policymakers, analysts and the media. Madaaris resist government control because they believe that in religious schools the curriculum ought to be decided by religious experts. Some boards fully support their political parties — for example, Rabita-ul-Madaris fully supports Jamaat-e-Islami — so madaaris are linked with religio-political movements and influence national politics, while policymakers remain concerned about their role in radicalization and reforms.

  30. Give your suggestions about the successful stories of Indonesia and Cuba for madrassa education.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Indonesia has granted the responsibility of the madrassa education system to the Ministry of Religious Affairs, integrating religious education into the national system. The Cuban model of education is a comprehensive, state-supported system with high literacy; the handouts note that the Cuban model for the education system can also be comprehensively applied to our madrassa education system. The suggestion for Pakistan is to learn from these successful stories: bring madaaris into a regulated national framework (as in Indonesia) and apply a comprehensive, organized model (as in Cuba) to reform madrassa education.

  31. What are the recommendations for madrassa reforms for the government?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    For the government: (1) madrassa boards must be taken into confidence before any step of reform; the reform committee should include senior ulema rather than general bureaucrats, and bureaucratic obstacles in the registration of madaaris should be removed; (2) the government should accept the lower-level degrees (Sanvia Aama and Sanvia Khasa) as equal to the formal educational system; (3) measures should be taken to enhance and rationalize the economic benefits and avenues available to madrassa graduates and religious scholars, providing them comparable benefits and job opportunities in all departments; (4) wafaqs should be affiliated with the Federal Ministry of Education or the Federal Ministry of Religious Affairs, madaaris should be linked with the HEC, and education grants should be distributed through it; (5) madrassa teachers and staff should be provided advanced training to enhance their capacity and keep scholars updated on current affairs; (6) madrassa boards should be given the status of an independent examination board, and by endorsing madrassa certificates as equal to other educational certificates, discrimination should be eliminated so madrassa graduates can serve in government and private organizations on merit.

  32. Write the policy recommendations for civil society for madrassa reforms.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    (1) Every segment of society should express its disassociation with mischievous elements among them who try to create differences; all pillars of civil society, including madaaris, should establish relations with each other and work together to promote peace and harmony in society. (2) Improved monitoring mechanisms for madaaris through effective public-private partnerships should be evolved to ensure the credibility of madaaris. (3) Dialogue between diverse segments of society (Muslims, non-Muslims, liberal and conservative, non-religious, etc.) should be a constant practice, and elements displaying extremist tendencies should be invited and engaged rather than ignored. (4) Civil society and academic institutions should act as a bridge between various segments by promoting dialogue, and should provide analytical and informative material regarding social issues, making it accessible to the trendsetters among all segments of society.

  33. Give the world literacy rates and the literacy rates of males and females in Pakistan.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    In the world, the literacy rate is 88.6% for males and 79.7% for females. Almost three-quarters (75%) of the world's 775 million illiterate adults are found in only ten countries — India, China, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nigeria, Ethiopia, Egypt, Brazil, Indonesia and the Democratic Republic of Congo — and two-thirds of all illiterate adults are women; extremely low literacy rates are concentrated in South and West Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa. In Pakistan, the literacy rate is 69% for males and 45% for females.

  34. Describe the gender difference in literacy / problems of literacy in Pakistan.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    There is a great difference in the enrollment of boys as compared to girls in Pakistan. According to UNESCO figures, primary school enrollment for girls stands at 60% as compared to 84% for boys, while the secondary school enrollment rate stands at a lower rate of 32% for females and 46% for males. Literacy rates are much lesser for females than for males: in rural areas 52% of girls are not enrolled in schools, and 67% of women are illiterate. Poverty is the main cause of the low literacy rate all over the world; in Pakistan less than 2% is invested in the education sector, and the number of schools is more for boys than for girls.

  35. Write the three levels of literacy.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    There are three levels of literacy: (1) Baseline literacy — the basic ability to read and write, e.g. the ability to read a newspaper and write a letter in Urdu; (2) Functional literacy — the ability to use reading, writing and calculation skills effectively in one's own activities and community development; and (3) Multiple literacy — the advanced ability to use literacy in multiple forms and contexts, including information, media and digital literacies, for lifelong learning.

  36. What is the net enrollment rate in the primary schools of Pakistan?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    The net enrollment rate (NER) is an indicator of the level of accessibility of education for school-age children; its status in Pakistan is more male and less female. Pakistan's net enrollment rate in primary education was at the level of 73.8% in 2015, up from 73% in the previous year.

  37. What are the challenges to implement mother tongue as a medium of instruction?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    It is practical only in homogeneous communities. No teacher recruitment and training modalities have been developed yet. As a result of external production, the Textbook Boards have not been able to prepare even basic elementary readers in any of the mother tongues. The current political unrest makes it difficult to implement changes in local contexts.

  38. Give recommendations about the medium of instruction.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Children's right to basic education through mother tongue should be guaranteed in at least the first three grades. The choice of medium of instruction in a school should be determined by the school heads in consultation with the local government. English should be taught as a subject from grade one onwards. The medium of instruction in madaaris should be determined by their respective management in consultation with the Department of Education.

  39. What are the factors which influence the teacher's attitudes?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Teachers' positive attitudes towards free and accessible education depend strongly on their experience with learners who are perceived as challenging. The factors which influence teachers' attitudes are: teacher education, the availability of support within the classroom, class size and overall workload. Negative attitudes of teachers and adults (parents and other family members) are the major barrier to the inclusion of marginalized students, because children do not have prejudices unless adults show them. Changes in attitude involve significant changes in conceptions and role behavior.

  40. Write the three basic fundamental principles of a rights-based education approach.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    A rights-based education approach has three basic fundamental principles that need to be looked at: (1) access to free and accessible education, and equality; (2) inclusion and non-discrimination; and (3) the right to education — its content and processes.

  41. Write the aim of knowledge in the elements of curricula.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    The elements of curricula include knowledge, attitudes/values and skills. The aim of knowledge in the elements of curricula (of peace education) is to develop: knowledge of NGOs and their impact on social change; knowledge of worldwide and local grassroots initiatives; principles of restorative justice; listening and dialoguing; and the importance of nonviolence. In terms of the knowledge of peace, it includes awareness of one's own needs and self-awareness, understanding the nature of conflict and peace, conflict analysis, enhancing knowledge of the community, and the mediation process.

  42. Why is there a need for an international language?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 2 marks marks

    An international language is needed because foreign languages provide a competitive edge in career choices, as one is able to communicate in a second language. Foreign language study enhances listening skills and memory. One participates more effectively and responsibly in a multicultural world if one knows another language; it also helps in global communication, trade, education and understanding of other cultures.

  43. How is Islamic education valued in Pakistan? / How can religion help in equity issues in education in Pakistan?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Islam is the religion of the majority in Pakistan and Islamic education shapes the values, character and daily life of the people. Religious education, along with the madaaris, has played a vital role in the history of Islam and in the education of the poverty-stricken children of society, providing free education, boarding and lodging. Islamic education is valued for promoting moral values, tolerance and harmony; therefore religion can help in equity issues in education in Pakistan by teaching equality, discouraging discrimination, and motivating communities to send all children — including girls and minorities — to school.
