  1. Describe the characteristics of a small organization.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The characteristics of a small organization are: 1. Non-bureaucratic: Personal, informality is the rule in small organizations. 2. Organic: Small organizations are free-flowing and adaptive. 3. Entrepreneurial: Small organizations have the ability to keep the employees on their toes. 4. Normative reward systems: Small organizations use salary plus bonuses. 5. Fast feedback: In small organizations, feedback is fast. 6. Face-to-face communication: Small organizations mostly use face-to-face communication.

  2. Discuss the concepts of task variety and analyzability in departmental technology.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Task variety and analyzability are the two most important characteristics of departmental technology for understanding organizational design and technology. Task Variety: Task variety is the number of exceptions in the work, i.e., the frequency of unexpected or novel events that occur in the work process. A routine technology is characterized by few exceptions (low variety), while a non-routine technology is characterized by many exceptions (high variety). Task Analyzability: Analyzability is the extent to which work can be reduced to mechanical steps and participants can follow an objective, computational procedure. When tasks are analyzable, employees rely on well-defined steps, procedures and rules; when tasks are not analyzable, employees rely on judgment, intuition and experience. The combination of variety and analyzability produces four categories of departmental technology: routine (low variety, high analyzability), craft (low variety, low analyzability), engineering (high variety, high analyzability) and non-routine (high variety, low analyzability).

  3. What is bureaucracy? Describe the features of bureaucracy given by Max Weber.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Bureaucracy is the exercise of control on the basis of knowledge, expertise or experience. Weber identified bureaucracy as the most efficient form of organization. Its features are: 1. Rules and procedures: Bureaucracy formalizes behaviour through rules and standard operating procedures. 2. Specialization and division of labour: Tasks are subdivided into separate jobs in which an individual specializes. 3. Hierarchy of authority: A chain of command through which officials are accountable to higher officials. 4. Technically qualified personnel: Officials are selected on the basis of technical competence, trained and given careers in the organization. 5. Written communication and records: Written rules, decisions and records are maintained in files. 6. Centralization: Authority and control remain concentrated at the top of the hierarchy.

  4. How does the environment influence organic and mechanistic structures?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Burns and Stalker studied the relationship between the external environment and the internal organization. Their finding was: "When the external environment was stable, the internal organization was characterized by rules, procedures, and a clear hierarchy of authority; in a rapidly changing environment the internal organization was much looser, free-flowing, and adaptive." Thus, a stable environment leads to a mechanistic structure (rigid, bureaucratic, with clear rules and hierarchy), while a rapidly changing environment leads to an organic structure (flexible, adaptive, with loose rules and decentralized decisions).

  5. What is an organic organization?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    The flexibility of an organic organization is attributed to people's freedom to create and introduce new ideas. An organic organization encourages a bottom-up innovation process: ideas bubble up from middle- and lower-level employees because they have the freedom to propose ideas and to experiment. Organic form: - Employees contribute to the common tasks of the department. - Tasks are adjusted and redefined through teamwork. - There is less hierarchy of authority and control, and only a few rules. - Knowledge and control of tasks are located anywhere in the organization. - Communication is horizontal.

  6. Define implicit (tacit) knowledge and explicit knowledge.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Explicit knowledge is formal, systematic knowledge that can be codified, written down, and passed on to others in documents or general instructions. Tacit (implicit) knowledge is based on personal experience, rules of thumb, intuition, and judgment. It includes professional know-how and expertise, individual insight and experience, and creative solutions that are often difficult to communicate and pass on to others.

  7. There are three types of control systems: bureaucratic control, market control and clan control. When should bureaucratic control be used in an organization?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    Bureaucratic control uses rules, standards, hierarchies and legitimate authority to influence and shape the behaviour of employees. It is the approach used in most medium-sized and large organizations. Market control uses the external market mechanism, such as price competition, to establish the standards used to control the units within the corporation; it is most useful in organizations where outputs are clearly identified and a great deal of competition exists. Clan control uses cultural values, traditions, shared beliefs and trust to encourage employees to conform to organizational goals; it is often used in small, informal organizations and in organizations with a strong culture and little emphasis on formal controls. Bureaucratic control should be used where work is routine and standardized, where tasks can be clearly specified in rules and procedures, and where managers need formal monitoring of compliance — as in medium-sized and large organizations with a formal hierarchy.

  8. What kind of knowledge is better for research: explicit or tacit knowledge? Explain.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    Explicit knowledge (codified knowledge) is formal, systematic knowledge that can be codified, written down, and passed on to others in documents or general instructions. Tacit knowledge (implicit knowledge) is based on personal experience, rules of thumb, intuition, and judgment; it includes professional know-how and expertise, individual insight and experience, and creative solutions that are difficult to communicate. For research, explicit knowledge is easier to collect, verify and build upon because it is documented in books, journals and databases. However, tacit knowledge is equally valuable in research because the researcher's experience, insight and judgment guide the interpretation of data and the generation of new ideas. In practice, good research combines both: explicit knowledge provides the foundation, while tacit knowledge provides the insight needed to create new knowledge.

  9. Differentiate between a goal and a strategy.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A goal is a desired future state that the organization attempts to realize; goals are important because they legitimize the organization and justify its existence. A strategy is the plan for interacting with the competitive environment to achieve organizational goals. In other words, goals define where the organization wants to go, and strategies define how it will get there.

  10. Define task variety and task analyzability. When is a Management Information System (MIS) necessary for an organization?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Task variety is the frequency of unexpected and novel events that occur in the work process (exceptions in the work). Task analyzability is the extent to which the work can be reduced to mechanical steps and participants can follow an objective, computational procedure. A Management Information System (MIS) is necessary when the organization needs decision support and reporting: computer-based information systems that provide information and support for effective managerial decision making and day-to-day operations. An MIS is a system that gathers comprehensive data, organizes it and summarizes it in a form valuable to managers; it typically consists of data and data-processing hardware and software, and its typical application areas are manufacturing, marketing, production, finance and human resources.

  11. Differentiate between small and large organizations.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Small and large organizations differ in their structure and functioning. A small organization is personal and informal, organic and free-flowing, with face-to-face communication, fast feedback, normative rewards (salary plus bonuses) and an entrepreneurial orientation. A large organization is bureaucratic and formal, with written rules and procedures, a tall hierarchy of authority, specialized departments, formal written communication and control through standards and reports. Large organizations also enjoy economies of scale and greater resources, while small organizations are more responsive and flexible in adapting to environmental change.

  12. How can the environment weaken the structure design of an organization?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    Uncertainty in the environment weakens the structure design of an organization: as uncertainty increases, the speed of change becomes so fast that the old design is unable to cope with the environment, so the structure must be redesigned frequently. To cope, organizations increase the use of teams, project managers, task forces and integrators for coordination, and management's attention shifts from internal activities to monitoring the external environment.

  13. Discuss the collectivity stage of the organizational life cycle.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Collectivity is the second stage of the organizational life cycle. If the leadership crisis is resolved, strong leadership is obtained and the organization begins to develop clear goals and direction. Departments are established along with a hierarchy of authority, job assignments, and a beginning division of labour. Employees identify with the mission of the organization and spend long hours helping the organization succeed. Members feel part of a collective, and communication and control are mostly informal, although a few formal systems begin to appear.

  14. What are the features of a learning organization?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    A learning organization effectively acquires, creates and transfers knowledge across the company and modifies its activities to reflect new knowledge and insight. In a learning organization, everyone throughout the company is connected to the information network and has complete information about all aspects of the business, enabling them to fully participate in solving problems, making decisions and moving the organization forward. Its key features are: a structure based on horizontal teams and empowered employees; a strong culture of openness, equality and continuous improvement; open information sharing; decentralized decision making; and empowered employees with knowledge-based authority.

  15. Why is knowledge management particularly important to a company that wants to learn and change continuously rather than operate in a stable state?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    Knowledge management is important to the organization in the following ways: 1. It helps drive innovation. 2. It better leverages the expertise of people across the organization. 3. It makes it easier to find and reuse business best practices. 4. It accelerates the production and delivery of innovative products and services to customers. 5. It increases the potential for replication and scale. 6. It helps protect against knowledge loss as employees leave the organization. 7. It protects against competitive erosion. For a company that wants to learn and change continuously, these benefits are essential: continuous learning depends on capturing, sharing and applying knowledge, whereas a company operating in a stable state can rely on fixed routines and procedures.

  16. What are the impacts of information technology (IT) on organization structure?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    Information technology has changed the traditional approach to organizational design in the following ways: 1. Small headquarters: IT decreases the requirement of physical presence, so headquarters can be kept small. 2. Flatter organizations: IT enables flatter structures with fewer levels of management. 3. Decentralized decision making: Information is available at lower levels, so decisions can be decentralized. 4. Increased coordination: IT improves coordination across departments and locations. 5. More formal management systems: IT supports formal systems for control and reporting. 6. More professional staff: IT increases the proportion of professional and technical staff in the organization.

  17. Why do organizations intend to become global?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Global companies operate in many countries. As multinational enterprises, they establish production and distribution facilities worldwide. When an organization intends to become global, it can take advantage of the following factors: 1. Economies of scale. 2. Economies of scope. 3. Global e-commerce. 4. Implementation of global learning.

  18. In which situation must an organization NOT adopt a horizontal structure?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A horizontal structure should not be adopted when the organization is working in a stable environment, because it does not need fast and quick responses to environmental changes. Similarly, when the environment is stable, decision making at the lower level is not necessary and there is no need to use cross-functional teams; a vertical (functional) structure is more appropriate in such conditions.

  19. Differentiate between contextual dimensions and structural dimensions of an organization.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Contextual dimensions describe the organizational setting that influences and shapes the structural dimensions; they characterize the whole organization as well as its environment, and include size, organizational technology, the environment, goals and strategy, and culture. Structural dimensions provide labels for describing the internal characteristics of an organization and create a basis for measuring and comparing organizations. They include formalization, specialization, hierarchy of authority, centralization, professionalism and personnel ratios.

  20. What is ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning)?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) refers to a networked information system that integrates many functional areas of the organization, such as accounting, human resources, finance and marketing. ERP uses huge databases that combine all of a company's data into a single system, enabling information to be shared across departments so the whole organization works from the same data.

  21. How can information technology be used as a strategic weapon?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Using information technology as a strategic weapon is the highest level of application of information technology. IT can help build and enhance strategy by providing better data and information within the organization (internal application) as well as helping the organization redefine and support its relationships with customers, suppliers and other organizations (external application). Internal applications include networking, intranets and enterprise resource planning (ERP) systems. Extranets, e-commerce and network structures are external applications.

  22. Information technology: how important is it for you, being a student, in your routine life? What will be the effect if it is not available?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    Information technology is very important in a student's routine life. Through the internet and the university's Learning Management System (LMS), a student attends online lectures, downloads handouts and lecture slides, submits assignments and quizzes, checks results and announcements, and communicates with instructors and class fellows through e-mail and discussion forums. Library resources, research articles and e-books are also accessed online. If IT were not available, learning would become slow and costly: students would have to depend on physical libraries and printed material, communication with teachers would be delayed, assignments would be prepared and submitted manually, and access to current knowledge and research would be severely limited.

  23. How can you identify if a company has a mechanistic structure?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    A mechanistic structure can be identified by the following characteristics: - Decisions are made by top management (centralization). - Communication is vertical. - Tasks are rigidly defined. - There is a clear hierarchy of authority. - Knowledge and control of tasks are centralized. - Employees are expected to obey and follow the instructions of the boss. A company showing these features — rigid rules, centralized decisions and vertical communication — has a mechanistic structure.

  24. From structure, how can we define whether an organization is service-related or production-related?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    The structure of an organization reflects whether it uses service or production (manufacturing) technology. A service-related organization typically has: fewer boundary spanners dealing with customers in a structured way but high direct customer contact, decentralized decision making (decisions are made close to the customer), lower formalization (few rigid rules, because services are intangible and customized), highly skilled employees, and an emphasis on interpersonal skills. A production-related organization, in contrast, has more boundary spanners, centralized decision making, higher formalization, employees with relatively lower technical skills at the operating level, and an emphasis on technical skills, because its output is a tangible, standardized product.

  25. Are service technologies different from manufacturing technologies? Explain.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    Yes, service technologies differ from manufacturing technologies in several ways: - Output: Service output is intangible (e.g., advice, entertainment), while manufacturing output is tangible (physical products). - Production and consumption: In services, production and consumption are simultaneous; in manufacturing they are separate. - Customer contact: Services require direct interaction with customers; manufacturing has little direct contact. - Quality measurement: Service quality is perceptual and difficult to measure; manufacturing quality is measured by direct counts and physical inspection. - Site selection: Services must be located near customers; manufacturing location depends on raw materials, labour and transport. - Inventory: Services cannot be stored; manufactured products can be inventoried. Because of these differences, service organizations need structures with decentralized decisions, lower formalization and employees with strong interpersonal skills.

  26. Why is decentralized decision making necessary for a large organization?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    In a large organization, operations are spread over many departments, products and locations, and the volume of decisions is far too great for top management alone. Decentralized decision making is necessary because: - It allows faster responses to local problems and customer needs. - Lower-level managers have more direct knowledge of their operations, so decisions are better informed. - It reduces the information overload on top management, freeing it for strategic decisions. - It motivates and develops lower-level managers by giving them authority and responsibility. Therefore, as an organization grows in size, decision-making authority must be pushed down to lower levels.

  27. Why is goal setting important for an organization?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Goal setting is important for an organization because: - Goals give the organization direction and a sense of purpose; they state where the organization wants to go. - Goals legitimize the organization and justify its existence to stakeholders. - Goals serve as a standard against which performance can be measured and controlled. - Goals guide and motivate employees by focusing their efforts on common targets. - Goals help in decision making by providing criteria for choosing among alternatives. - Goals reduce uncertainty and conflict by clarifying priorities for all members.

  28. Name some factors causing environmental complexity for an organization. How might this environmental complexity lead to organizational complexity? Explain.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    Environmental complexity is caused by the number and dissimilarity of external elements that affect the organization, such as numerous competitors, diverse customers and markets, many government regulations, varied suppliers, rapid technological change and different social and economic conditions across countries. Environmental complexity leads to organizational complexity because the organization must create matching internal units to deal with each important external element: it establishes more departments and boundary-spanning roles, adds managers and specialists, and increases differentiation among departments. In this way, a complex environment produces a complex internal structure, because each part of the organization deals with a different part of the environment.

  29. Is it feasible for an organization to change its domain? Can you think of any organization that changed its environmental domain recently?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    Yes, it is feasible for an organization to change its domain. The domain is the chosen environmental field of action — the products, services and markets the organization serves. When the environment changes or new opportunities appear, organizations redefine their domain by entering new industries, offering new products or serving new customers, and by withdrawing from old ones. For example, many mobile phone companies changed their domain from simple voice services to mobile internet, mobile banking and digital entertainment services. Similarly, traditional bookstores and retailers have shifted their domain towards e-commerce. Such domain changes are a normal strategic response to environmental change, although they require new technology, skills and structures.

  30. Can we use SWOT analysis for a school or university? Explain.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Yes, SWOT analysis can be used for a school or university, because it is a general tool for analysing any organization's internal and external situation. The internal factors are its strengths (e.g., qualified faculty, good reputation, modern laboratories, strong results) and weaknesses (e.g., limited budget, shortage of teachers, outdated infrastructure). The external factors are opportunities (e.g., new programmes in demand, online education, industry partnerships, government funding) and threats (e.g., competing institutions, changing government policies, declining enrolment). By identifying these four factors, the school or university can formulate strategies that use its strengths to capture opportunities, overcome weaknesses and defend against threats.

  31. Discuss the impact of information technology on the structure of smaller organizations.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Information technology has a strong impact on smaller organizations. IT allows a small organization to perform like a large one: with computers, the internet and e-commerce, it can reach customers worldwide, coordinate with suppliers and partners, and manage information efficiently with very few employees. IT enables smaller organizations to keep flatter structures, make faster decisions, outsource non-core activities and operate as network structures linked electronically with other organizations. At the same time, IT reduces the need for middle managers and clerical staff in small organizations, because information is available directly to decision makers. Overall, IT makes smaller organizations more flexible, responsive and competitive.

  32. Differentiate between adversarial and partnership-oriented interorganizational relationships.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    In an adversarial relationship, organizations view each other with suspicion and distrust; they compete aggressively, keep information secret, monitor each other closely and try to gain advantage at the other's expense. Relationships are short-term and based on strict contracts and arm's-length bargaining. In a partnership-oriented relationship, organizations develop trust and share information, employees and resources; they invest in each other's success, solve problems jointly and accept mutual dependence. Relationships are long-term and cooperative, with frequent communication and a willingness to adjust for each other's benefit. Modern organizations increasingly prefer partnership orientation because it reduces uncertainty and improves performance.

  33. "Different departments should have different technology." Explain this statement while accepting or rejecting it.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    This statement should be accepted. According to Perrow's framework, departments differ in task variety and task analyzability: a research department faces high variety and low analyzability (non-routine technology), a production department faces low variety and high analyzability (routine technology), and an engineering department faces high variety with high analyzability (engineering technology). Because each department performs a different function and deals with different kinds of tasks, a single uniform technology cannot fit all departments. Moreover, the appropriate structure also differs with technology: routine departments work best with formal, centralized structures, while non-routine departments need flexible, decentralized structures. Hence different departments should indeed have different technologies suited to their tasks.

  34. What is clan control? In which organizations is it used?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    Clan control is the use of cultural values, traditions, shared beliefs and trust to encourage employees to conform to organizational goals. Instead of formal rules (bureaucratic control) or market prices (market control), clan control relies on socialization: employees internalize the organization's values and control their own behaviour. Clan control is used in small, informal organizations and in organizations where tasks are non-routine and difficult to measure, where teamwork is essential and where a strong organizational culture exists. It is also common in organizations that emphasize employee commitment and shared values rather than formal monitoring.

  35. Differentiate between the internal and external environment of an organization.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    The internal environment consists of elements inside the organization that affect its operations, such as its culture, structure, employees, management, technology and resources. Managers can influence and change these elements directly. The external environment consists of all elements outside the boundary of the organization that may affect it, such as customers, competitors, suppliers, government, the economy, technology and social trends. These elements are largely beyond management's control, so the organization must monitor and adapt to them.

  36. What is horizontal communication? How does it differ from vertical communication?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Horizontal communication is the exchange of information across departments or among people at the same organizational level. It is used for coordination, problem solving and sharing information between units, for example through cross-functional teams, task forces and integrators. Vertical communication, in contrast, flows up and down the chain of command: downward communication carries instructions, goals and policies from top management to subordinates, while upward communication carries reports, feedback and problems from subordinates to top management. Horizontal communication supports coordination across the organization, whereas vertical communication supports control and reporting within the hierarchy.
