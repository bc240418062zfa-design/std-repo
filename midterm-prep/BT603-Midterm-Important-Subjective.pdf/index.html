  1. What is the definition of fermentation?
    Topic: Fermentation Basics & Definitions

    Fermentation is a metabolic process that releases energy from sugar or other organic substrates through various enzyme actions. It is a type of anaerobic biochemical process.

  2. Describe the procedure of fermentation.
    Topic: Fermentation Basics & Definitions • 5 Marks marks

    Fermentation is an anaerobic biochemical process. In fermentation, the first step is the same as cellular respiration, which is the formation of pyruvic acid by glycolysis, where a net 2 ATP molecules are synthesized. In the next step, pyruvate is reduced to lactic acid, ethanol or other products. Here NAD+ is formed, which is re-utilized back in the glycolysis process.

  3. Define medium.
    Topic: Fermentation Basics & Definitions

    The medium is the intervening substance through which metabolic requirements are conveyed or physical forces are transmitted.

  4. Define biotransformation. Give examples.
    Topic: Fermentation Basics & Definitions

    Biotransformation is the conversion of molecules from one form to another within an organism. Examples: Starch → Maltose; Starch → Glucose; Benzaldehyde → L-PAC.

  5. Define rheology.
    Topic: Fermentation Basics & Definitions • 2 Marks marks

    Rheology relates to the viscosity of the medium: the individual components of the medium can influence the viscosity of the final medium and its subsequent behaviour with respect to aeration and agitation. A medium with a high viscosity needs a higher power input for effective stirring.

  6. Define metabolites and write the types of metabolites.
    Topic: Fermentation Basics & Definitions

    Metabolites are the intermediates and products of metabolism; all the compounds and substances that are steps of a metabolic reaction are called metabolites. Types: (1) Primary metabolites — those directly involved in the (growth) process. (2) Secondary metabolites — those not directly involved in those metabolic processes, but usually having an important ecological function. (3) Tertiary metabolites — these may involve acids, e.g. citric acid, oxalic acid, glutamic acid and amino acids.

  7. Write down the two sub-processes of fermentation.
    Topic: Fermentation Basics & Definitions

    The fermentation process has two sub-processes: (1) Up-stream process (steps 1–5) — an industrial term describing the steps followed for the production of a specific product by industrial means. (2) Down-stream process (steps 6–7) — downstream processing includes the recovery of the products in a pure state and the effluent treatment. Product recovery is carried out through a series of operations including cell separation by settling, centrifugation or filtration; disruption of cells (if the product is intracellular); extraction and purification of the product. Finally, the effluents are treated by chemical, physical or biological methods.

  8. What are the basic components of fermentation?
    Topic: Fermentation Basics & Definitions

    1. Organism (the hero of the fermentation process). 2. Fermenter (the stage on which the microorganism acts/grows). 3. Media (formulation). 4. Sterilization. 5. Monitoring/control (environmental conditions). 6. Recovery of the product (isolation, purification, etc.). 7. Effluent treatment (processing of the materials that remain after the fermentation process).

  9. Write down the basic fermentation steps in large-scale fermentation.
    Topic: Fermentation Basics & Definitions

    1. Inoculum preservation. 2. Inoculum build-up. 3. Pre-fermenter culture. 4. Production fermentation. (These are followed by product recovery and effluent treatment.)

  10. Which ingredients are used in fermentation?
    Topic: Fermentation Basics & Definitions

    Sugar; bacterial culture; ethanol; lactic acid; hydrogen gas (H₂); water; carbon sources and energy sources; nitrogen sources; vitamins and growth factors; minerals and trace elements; buffers; inducers; precursors; chelators; inhibitors (e.g. KMS in beer medium); and antifoams.

  11. Write the types of fermentation on the basis of oxygen.
    Topic: Classification of Fermentation • 5 Marks marks

    The O₂ supply must be according to the need of the fermentation process: (1) Aerobic fermentation — carried out in the presence of oxygen; organisms use oxygen for the conversion of complex organic compounds. Oxygen is actually required for the reproduction and growth of microorganisms. (2) Semi-anaerobic fermentation — the organism makes ATP by aerobic respiration, but is capable of switching to anaerobic respiration if oxygen is absent; oxygen is needed, but not much more. (3) Anaerobic fermentation — carried out in the strict absence of oxygen (the presence of oxygen may be toxic); it involves the conversion of complex organic compounds into simpler ones in the absence of oxygen.

  12. What are the types of fermentation on the basis of the products?
    Topic: Classification of Fermentation

    On the basis of the products formed: 1. Beer fermentation. 2. Yogurt fermentation. 3. Citric acid fermentation. 4. Pickle fermentation, and many more.

  13. Name the major groups of important fermentations (on the basis of product).
    Topic: Classification of Fermentation

    1. Lactic acid fermentation. 2. Alcohol fermentation. 3. Acetic acid fermentation. 4. Butyric acid fermentation.

  14. Write the types of fermentation on the basis of scale.
    Topic: Classification of Fermentation • 3 Marks marks

    1. Laboratory scale fermentation — carried out in flasks; cultures may be mixed by means of shaking in a controlled environment chamber. 2. Pilot scale fermentation. 3. Industrial scale fermentation. Pilot and industrial scale fermentations are normally carried out in stirred, aerated vessels, termed as fermenters.

  15. Write the types of fermentation on the basis of water/moisture.
    Topic: Classification of Fermentation • 5 Marks marks

    (1) Solid-State Fermentation (SSF) — the microorganisms grow on a moist solid with little or no 'free' water, although capillary water may be present. Examples: mushroom cultivation, bread-making, processing of cocoa, and traditional foods such as miso (soy paste), soy sauce, tempeh (soybean cake) and gari (cassava). SSF is also called Koji fermentation. (2) Submerged/Liquid Fermentation (SmF) — the microorganisms grow in a submerged state within the fermentation media; it may use a dissolved substrate (e.g. sugar solution) or a solid substrate suspended in a large amount of water to form a slurry. The organism is grown in a liquid medium which is vigorously aerated and agitated. Used for pickling vegetables, brewing beer and producing wine and soy sauce. (3) Surface culture / jelly fermentation — the microorganisms grow on the surface of the fermentation media, on a liquid medium without agitation; after incubation the culture filtrate is separated from the cell mass to recover the product. Citric acid is commonly produced this way, with Aspergillus niger growing as a thick floating mycelial mat.

  16. Write the types of fermentation on the basis of mixing.
    Topic: Classification of Fermentation

    (1) Static fermentation — no mixing during the process; a surface culture, un-aerated and unmixed bioreactor can be used, in which the bed is static or mixed only very occasionally (once or twice per day) and air is circulated around the bed but not blown forcefully. (2) Shake-flask fermentation — on a small scale, a simple shaking procedure is applied during fermentation; shake culture flasks are usually applied to aerobic processes and are a small-scale equivalent of the stirred tank bioreactor. (3) Stirred fermentation — proper mixing during the process, done by an agitator/impeller; the medium must be stirred to keep the cells in suspension. Stirrers range from simple magnetic stirrers and flat blade turbine impellers to marine impellers.

  17. On what bases can fermentation be classified?
    Topic: Classification of Fermentation

    Fermentation can be classified into different types on the basis of: 1. Oxygen (O₂) need. 2. Moisture/water (H₂O) content in the medium. 3. Production scale. 4. Process. 5. State/mixing. 6. Product. 7. Organism.

  18. What is fed-batch fermentation?
    Topic: Classification of Fermentation • 2 Marks marks

    An enhancement of the closed batch process is the fed-batch fermentation. In fed-batch fermentations, sterile culture medium is added either continuously or periodically to the inoculated fermentation batch.

  19. Differentiate between batch culture and continuous culture.
    Topic: Classification of Fermentation

    Batch culture: it is a closed batch; all the nutrients are added at once in the beginning; the product is extracted at the end of the batch; productivity level is low; it is used to extract secondary metabolites such as antibiotics; growth conditions can be changed by altering pH and dissolved oxygen; large fermenters are used; it has less chance of contamination; the microbes go through three phases – lag, exponential and stationary; nutrients are consumed at a slower rate. Continuous culture: it is an open batch; nutrients are added throughout the batch; the product is continuously removed while the batch is operating; productivity level is high; it is used to extract primary metabolites such as amino acids and proteins; growth conditions have to be maintained throughout the operation; small-size fermenters are used; it has a high chance of contamination; the microbes go through a very short lag phase and then maintain themselves at the exponential phase; nutrients are consumed rapidly.

  20. What does the medium need to do?
    Topic: Fermentation Media & Nutrients

    The medium must grow the microorganism so that it produces biomass and product, and it should not interfere with downstream processing.

  21. Write the requirements of a good fermentation medium.
    Topic: Fermentation Media & Nutrients • 5 Marks marks

    All micro-organisms require water, sources of energy, carbon, nitrogen, mineral elements, and possibly vitamins, plus oxygen (if aerobic), CO₂, etc. A suitable medium: will produce the maximum yield of product or biomass per gram of substrate used; will produce the maximum concentration of product or biomass; will permit the maximum rate of product formation; will give the minimum yield of undesired products; and will be of consistent quality and readily available throughout the year. Detailed investigation is needed to establish the most suitable medium for an individual fermentation process.

  22. Differentiate between crude media and defined media.
    Topic: Fermentation Media & Nutrients • 3 Marks marks

    Crude media: made up of unrefined agricultural products, e.g. containing barley; cheap, but composition is variable; used for large-volume inexpensive products, e.g. biofuel from whey. Defined media: like those used in the laboratory, e.g. minimal salts medium; expensive, but composition is known and should not vary; used for expensive low-volume products, e.g. anticancer drugs.

  23. What is selective media? Give examples.
    Topic: Fermentation Media & Nutrients

    Selective media are designed to suppress the growth of unwanted bacteria and encourage the growth of the desired microbes. Examples: Bismuth sulfite, used to isolate the typhoid bacterium Salmonella typhi (it inhibits gram-positive bacteria and most gram-negative intestinal bacteria other than S. typhi); Sabouraud's dextrose agar (pH 5.6), used to isolate fungi that outgrow most bacteria at this pH; Eosin Methylene Blue (EMB) agar; Hektoen Enteric (HE) agar; MacConkey agar.

  24. Differentiate between differential media and selective media.
    Topic: Fermentation Media & Nutrients • 3 Marks marks

    Differential media make it easier to distinguish colonies of the desired organism from other colonies growing on the same plate; pure cultures of microorganisms have identifiable reactions with differential media in tubes or plates. Selective media are designed to suppress the growth of unwanted bacteria and encourage the growth of the desired microbes.

  25. Differentiate between differential media and reducing media.
    Topic: Fermentation Media & Nutrients • 3 Marks marks

    Differential media make it easier to distinguish colonies of the desired organism from other colonies growing on the same plate, through identifiable reactions of pure cultures in tubes or plates. Reducing media: the cultivation of anaerobic bacteria poses a special problem because anaerobes might be killed by exposure to oxygen, so special media called reducing media must be used. Reducing media contain ingredients such as sodium thioglycolate, which chemically combine with dissolved oxygen and deplete the oxygen in the culture medium.

  26. What is the importance of enrichment culture in fermentation?
    Topic: Fermentation Media & Nutrients • 3 Marks marks

    Enrichment culture is used when bacteria present in small numbers can be missed, especially if other bacteria are present in much larger numbers; it is then necessary to use an enrichment culture. This is often the case for soil samples. The medium (enrichment medium) for an enrichment culture is usually liquid and provides nutrients and environmental conditions that favour the growth of a particular microbe but not others. In this sense it is also a selective medium, but it is designed to increase very small numbers of the desired type of organism to detectable levels.

  27. What are the micro and macro minerals used in the fermentation process?
    Topic: Fermentation Media & Nutrients

    In many media, magnesium, phosphorus, potassium, sulphur, calcium and chlorine are (macro) components, and because of the concentrations required they must be added as components. Others such as cobalt, copper, iron, manganese, molybdenum and zinc are also essential but are usually present as impurities in other major ingredients.

  28. Name the trace elements used in fermentation media.
    Topic: Fermentation Media & Nutrients

    The role of trace elements in medium formulation can be significant. Cultured cells normally require Fe, Zn, Cu, Se, Mn, Mo and B. These are often present as impurities in other media components.

  29. Write the sources of inorganic nitrogen compounds.
    Topic: Fermentation Media & Nutrients • 5 Marks marks

    Inorganic nitrogen may be supplied as ammonia gas and ammonium salts or nitrates. Ammonia has been used for pH control and as the major nitrogen source in a defined medium for the commercial production of human serum albumin by Saccharomyces cerevisiae. Ammonium salts such as ammonium sulphate will usually produce acid conditions, as the ammonium ion is utilized and the free acid is liberated. On the other hand, nitrates will normally cause an alkaline drift as they are metabolized. Ammonium nitrate will first cause an acid drift as the ammonium ion is utilized (nitrate assimilation is repressed); when the ammonium ion has been exhausted, there is an alkaline drift as the nitrate is used as an alternative nitrogen source.

  30. What are the factors influencing the choice of carbon source?
    Topic: Fermentation Media & Nutrients

    The rate at which the carbon source is metabolized influences the formation of biomass or the production of primary or secondary metabolites; fast growth due to high concentrations of rapidly metabolized sugars is often associated with low productivity of secondary metabolites. The main product of the fermentation will often determine the choice of carbon source, particularly if the product results from its direct dissimilation. In fermentations such as ethanol or single-cell protein production, where raw materials are 60–77% of the production cost, the selling price of the product is largely determined by the cost of the carbon source. The method of media preparation, particularly sterilization, may affect the suitability of carbohydrates: it is often best to sterilize sugars separately because they may react with ammonium ions and amino acids to form black nitrogen-containing compounds which partially inhibit growth. Starch gelatinizes when heated in sterilization, giving very viscous liquids, so only concentrations up to 2% can be used without modification. The choice of substrate may also be influenced by government legislation and by the purity of the carbon source (e.g. metallic ions must be removed from carbohydrate sources used in some citric acid processes).

  31. Write a brief note on molasses as a fermentation substrate.
    Topic: Fermentation Media & Nutrients • 5 Marks marks

    Molasses is used in the production of high-volume/low-value products such as ethanol, SCP, organic and amino acids and some microbial gums. In 1980, 300,000 tons of cane molasses were used for amino acid production in Japan (Minoda, 1986). Molasses or sucrose may also be used for the production of higher-value/low-bulk products such as antibiotics, specialty enzymes, vaccines and fine chemicals. The cost of molasses is very competitive when compared with pure carbohydrates. However, molasses contains many impurities, and molasses-based fermentations will often need a more expensive and complicated extraction/purification stage to remove the impurities, and effluent treatment will be more expensive because of the unutilized waste materials still present.

  32. What are the limitations of starch as a fermentation substrate?
    Topic: Fermentation Media & Nutrients

    Starch has to be broken down into sugar, and the sugar then has to be broken down into simple sugars to allow the yeast to react with these sugars during fermentation (rising). We cannot ferment starch naturally because starches are too complex for the yeast to digest; they are made up of multiple simple sugars combined.

  33. Write the carbohydrate composition of barley malt.
    Topic: Fermentation Media & Nutrients • 5 Marks marks

    Barley grains may be partially germinated and heat-treated to give the material known as malt, which contains a variety of sugars besides starch. Malt is the main substrate for brewing beer. Malt extracts may also be prepared from malted grain. (In the solved paper, the reducing sugars in barley malt are given as 3–4% of dry weight.)

  34. What are the advantages/importance of oils and fats in fermentation?
    Topic: Fermentation Media & Nutrients

    Oils were first used as carriers for antifoams in antibiotic processes. Vegetable oils (olive, maize, cotton seed, linseed, soya bean, etc.) may also be used as carbon substrates, particularly for their content of the fatty acids oleic, linoleic and linolenic acid, because costs are competitive with those of carbohydrates. A typical oil contains approximately 2.4 times the energy of glucose on a per-weight basis. Oils also have a volume advantage: it would take 1.24 dm³ of soya bean oil to add 10 kcal of energy to a fermenter, whereas it would take 5 dm³ of glucose or sucrose added as 50% w/w solutions. Oil-based fed-batch fermentations permit the maximum working capacity of a vessel to be used more successfully than carbohydrate feeds, where a larger spare capacity must be catered for. Oils also have antifoam properties, which may make downstream processing simpler, but normally they are not used solely for this purpose.

  35. What is the significance of hydrocarbons and their derivatives in industrial fermentation?
    Topic: Fermentation Media & Nutrients • 2 Marks marks

    It was considered that hydrocarbons and their derivatives might have a potential role as feedstocks in the microbial production of higher-value products such as intermediates, pharmaceuticals, fine chemicals and agricultural chemicals.

  36. Write the uses of water in industrial fermentation.
    Topic: Fermentation Media & Nutrients • 5 Marks marks

    Water is the major component of all fermentation media, and is needed in many of the ancillary services such as heating, cooling, cleaning and rinsing. When assessing the suitability of a water supply, it is important to consider pH, dissolved salts and effluent contamination; the mineral content of the water is very important.

  37. Name two important precursors in fermentation.
    Topic: Fermentation Media & Nutrients • 2 Marks marks

    1. Phenoxyacetic acid. 2. Cyanides. (Phenylacetic acid is the most widely used precursor in penicillin production.)

  38. Write the advantages of undefined (complex) media.
    Topic: Fermentation Media & Nutrients • 5 Marks marks

    Undefined (complex) media use natural materials which are much cheaper than pure substrates, so they are economical for large-scale processes. (As noted in the source, their drawbacks are that variations in composition make small yield improvements difficult to detect, and undefined media often make product recovery and effluent treatment more problematical, because not all components of a complex nutrient source will be consumed by the organism; the residual components may interfere with recovery and contribute to the BOD — biological oxygen demand — of the effluent.)

  39. Write the two modes of sterilization in fermentation.
    Topic: Scale-up, Sterilization & Process Control

    In-situ sterilization — sterilization is done on the spot/inside the fermenter, maintaining high temperature by steam. Ex-situ sterilization — sterilization is done outside the fermenter, e.g. in an autoclave.

  40. Differentiate between lab scale and pilot scale fermentations.
    Topic: Scale-up, Sterilization & Process Control

    Lab scale fermentation: small-scale fermentation mostly run at lab scale, either in flasks of different quantities or in a fermenter; the sterilization mode at this scale is always ex-situ; the inoculum that runs the fermentation process is prepared in slants/small flasks. Pilot scale fermentation: a pilot scale plant offers flexibility, versatility and a broad range of equipment to rapidly convert laboratory results into industrial processes; pilot plant fermentation 'mimics' the industrial scale fermentation process by carrying out the industrial process on a smaller scale.

  41. What is the meaning of scale-up?
    Topic: Scale-up, Sterilization & Process Control

    Scale-up means increasing the production scale — for example, from the laboratory scale to the pilot plant scale, or from the pilot plant scale to the industrial scale.

  42. What is the role of pH in fermentation?
    Topic: Scale-up, Sterilization & Process Control

    pH is an important factor in fermentation because it can affect the rate and efficiency of the fermentation process as well as the quality of the final product. The optimal pH for a particular fermentation process depends on the specific microorganisms being used and the desired end product: some microorganisms may require a higher pH in order to grow and produce the desired product, while others may prefer a lower pH.

  43. What are the methods of controlling pH in fermentation?
    Topic: Scale-up, Sterilization & Process Control

    1. Keeping the partial pressure of CO₂ constant. 2. Pumping base into the culture. 3. Combining pumping base and sparging gases. 4. Using only sparging gases.

  44. Why is mixing done in fermentation?
    Topic: Scale-up, Sterilization & Process Control

    Mixing in fermentation is done: to disperse the air bubbles; to suspend the cells; and to enhance heat and mass transfer in the medium.

  45. Write the estimation of the carbon (oxygen balance) formula used in fermentation.
    Topic: Scale-up, Sterilization & Process Control • 2 Marks marks

    C_L = C* − (1/k_La) { (dC_L/dt) + X·Q_O₂ }, where C_L is the dissolved oxygen concentration, C* the saturation concentration, k_La the volumetric oxygen transfer coefficient, and X·Q_O₂ the oxygen uptake of the culture.

  46. What are the microorganisms used in fermentation?
    Topic: Microorganisms, Enzymes & Products

    Bacillus, Lactobacillus, Micrococcus, Streptococcus, Pediococcus and Leuconostoc are the common fermenting bacteria. Moreover, Cladosporium, Aspergillus, Trichothecium, Paecilomyces, Fusarium and Penicillium are the common fungal genera found in fermented products.

  47. What are the requirements of microorganism growth?
    Topic: Microorganisms, Enzymes & Products

    All micro-organisms require water, sources of energy, carbon, nitrogen, mineral elements, and possibly vitamins, plus oxygen (if aerobic), CO₂, etc.

  48. How can enzymes be classified (constitutive and inducible)?
    Topic: Microorganisms, Enzymes & Products

    Enzymes can be classified as constitutive enzymes or inducible enzymes. Constitutive enzyme: an enzyme that is synthesized continually, regardless of growth conditions. Inducible enzyme: an enzyme that is synthesized only in the presence of the substrate that acts as an inducer.

  49. Differentiate between intracellular and extracellular enzymes.
    Topic: Microorganisms, Enzymes & Products

    Intracellular enzymes are those which remain within the cell and are responsible for catalyzing all those processes needed in cells. Extracellular enzymes are those which are released by the cells to perform a job external to the cell, e.g. digestive enzymes.

  50. What are the common fermentation products?
    Topic: Microorganisms, Enzymes & Products • 2 Marks marks

    Acids, enzymes, gases, alcohols, hormones, single cell protein, yogurt, bread and pickles, etc.

  51. Enlist the five commercially important groups of fermentations.
    Topic: Microorganisms, Enzymes & Products • 5 Marks marks

    There are five major groups of commercially important fermentations: 1. Those that produce microbial cells (or biomass) as the product. 2. Those that produce microbial metabolites. 3. Those that produce microbial enzymes. 4. Those that produce recombinant products. 5. Those that modify a compound which is added to the fermentation — the transformation process.

  52. Write the names of five recombinant products.
    Topic: Microorganisms, Enzymes & Products • 5 Marks marks

    Interferons; insulin; human serum albumin; clotting factors VIII and IX; epidermal growth factor; calf chymosin; bovine somatostatin.

  53. What are biofertilizers?
    Topic: Microorganisms, Enzymes & Products • 3 Marks marks

    Biofertilizers are low-cost, renewable sources of plant nutrients which supplement chemical fertilizers. They are nothing but selected strains of beneficial soil microorganisms cultured in the laboratory and packed in a suitable carrier. They can be used either for seed treatment or for soil application.

  54. What are bioweapons? Also write three examples.
    Topic: Microorganisms, Enzymes & Products • 5 Marks marks

    Biological weapons (often termed 'bio-weapons', 'biological threat agents' or 'bio-agents') are living organisms or replicating entities (viruses, which are not universally considered 'alive') that reproduce or replicate within their host victims. NATO defines a biological weapon as 'the provision of any infectious agent or toxin by any means of delivery in order to cause harm to humans, animals, or plants.' Examples: smallpox, anthrax, botulinum toxin, aflatoxin, Clostridium perfringens.

  55. What is site-directed mutagenesis?
    Topic: Strain Improvement & Microbial Techniques (from Solved Paper) • 2 Marks marks

    Site-directed (site-specific) mutagenesis commonly involves using mutagenic oligonucleotides in a primer extension reaction with DNA polymerase. This method allows a point mutation, or deletion or insertion of small stretches of DNA, to be introduced at specific sites.

  56. Write two examples of methylotrophic yeasts.
    Topic: Strain Improvement & Microbial Techniques (from Solved Paper)

    Methylotrophic yeasts such as Hansenula polymorpha and Pichia pastoris.

  57. Define Henry's Law.
    Topic: Strain Improvement & Microbial Techniques (from Solved Paper)

    Henry's Law describes the solubility of O₂ in nutrient solution in relation to the O₂ partial pressure in the gas phase: C* = P_O / H, where C* is the oxygen saturation concentration of the nutrient solution, P_O is the partial pressure of the gas in the gas phase, and H is Henry's constant, which is specific for the gas and the liquid phase.

  58. What is the replica plating method?
    Topic: Strain Improvement & Microbial Techniques (from Solved Paper) • 5 Marks marks

    Mutants can be detected by selecting or testing for an altered phenotype. Positive selection involves the selection of mutant cells and rejection of non-mutated cells. To identify mutants that have gained some physical trait, replica plating is used for negative selection. Negative selection identifies mutants that have lost some physical trait — for example, auxotrophs that have nutritional requirements not possessed by the parent (non-mutated) cell.

  59. Write the phenotypic characteristics used to characterize and identify microorganisms.
    Topic: Strain Improvement & Microbial Techniques (from Solved Paper) • 3 Marks marks

    Microscopic morphology; metabolic differences (biochemical testing); serology; fatty acid analysis.

  60. Draw/describe a graph showing the effect of dissolved oxygen concentration on the Q_O₂ of a microorganism.
    Topic: Strain Improvement & Microbial Techniques (from Solved Paper)

    As the dissolved oxygen concentration increases from zero, the specific oxygen uptake rate (Q_O₂) of the microorganism rises steeply (almost linearly), because oxygen is the limiting substrate. Above a certain level — the critical dissolved oxygen concentration — the curve levels off into a plateau: Q_O₂ becomes constant and independent of the dissolved oxygen concentration, because some other factor (the organism's maximum respiratory capacity) becomes limiting. Therefore, maximum biomass production is achieved by maintaining the dissolved oxygen concentration above the critical level.

  61. Bull et al. (1979) cited criteria in the choice of a process organism. Write these criteria.
    Topic: Strain Improvement & Microbial Techniques (from Solved Paper) • 10 Marks marks

    The criteria for the choice of a process organism include: (1) Nutritional characteristics — the organism should grow well on cheap, readily available substrates and should not have complex/exacting nutritional requirements. (2) Optimum environmental conditions — its optimum temperature, pH and oxygen requirements should be convenient (e.g. an optimum temperature above 40°C reduces cooling costs). (3) Stability — the organism should be genetically and physiologically stable, and amenable to genetic manipulation/strain improvement. (4) Productivity — it should give a high yield of product in a short fermentation time. (5) Ease of product recovery — the product should be easily separated from the culture (e.g. extracellular product, easy cell harvest). (6) Safety — the organism should be non-pathogenic and generally regarded as safe, and should not produce toxins or undesirable by-products.

  62. Draw/describe a flow chart of the industrial strategy for the usage of microorganisms.
    Topic: Strain Improvement & Microbial Techniques (from Solved Paper) • 5 Marks marks

    Industrial strategy for the usage of microorganisms: (1) Isolation of microorganisms from natural sources (soil, water, etc.) → (2) Screening for the desired product/activity → (3) Selection of the best producer strain → (4) Strain improvement (mutation, recombination, protoplast fusion) → (5) Preservation/maintenance of the improved culture (e.g. lyophilization, culture collections such as NCTC) → (6) Inoculum development (build-up, normally 3–10% of medium volume) → (7) Medium formulation and optimization → (8) Fermentation (lab scale → pilot scale → industrial scale, i.e. scale-up) → (9) Downstream processing (product recovery and purification) → (10) Effluent treatment and product marketing.

  63. How is the oxygen demand of an industrial fermentation process satisfied? What factors decide the dissolved oxygen level?
    Topic: Additional Important Subjective Questions

    The oxygen demand of an industrial fermentation process is normally satisfied by aerating and agitating the fermentation broth. The solubility of oxygen is low (in pure water it is about 8 mg/L at 4°C) and is governed by Henry's law, so maximum biomass production is achieved by maintaining the dissolved oxygen concentration greater than the critical level, i.e. by satisfying the organism's maximum specific oxygen demand. Production of 100 g of biomass from a hydrocarbon requires approximately three times the amount of oxygen needed to produce the same amount of biomass from a carbohydrate.

  64. Write a note on the methods used to determine/control the dissolved oxygen concentration in a fermenter.
    Topic: Additional Important Subjective Questions

    Two methods have been employed to achieve the lowering/measurement of the dissolved oxygen concentration — the static method and the dynamic method. The sulphite oxidation technique is also used to measure the oxygen transfer capability of a fermenter and does not require the measurement of dissolved oxygen concentrations. In practice, the dissolved oxygen is controlled through aeration rate, agitation speed and the design of the sparger/impeller system.

  65. What is an inoculum? What quantity of inoculum is normally used, and why is inoculum development important?
    Topic: Additional Important Subjective Questions

    The inoculum is the actively growing culture of the production microorganism that is transferred into the fermenter to start the production fermentation. The quantity of inoculum normally used is between 3 and 10% of the medium volume. Inoculum development (preservation → build-up in slants/small flasks → pre-fermenter culture) is important because a healthy, active and sufficiently large inoculum shortens the lag phase, reduces the risk of contamination and ensures a reproducible fermentation.

  66. What is strain improvement? Name the techniques used for it.
    Topic: Additional Important Subjective Questions

    Strain improvement involves the continual genetic modification of the culture to increase product yield and process performance. Techniques include induced mutation followed by screening/selection (including replica plating for auxotrophs), site-directed mutagenesis, recombination, and protoplast fusion — protoplast fusion has been demonstrated as an efficient way to induce heterokaryon formation and recombination with high frequency. Auxotrophy (the inability of an organism to synthesize a particular organic compound required for its growth) is widely used as a selectable marker in these programmes.

  67. How are industrial cultures preserved? Write a note on lyophilization.
    Topic: Additional Important Subjective Questions

    Improved production strains are preserved so that their properties do not change; master cultures are deposited in culture collections such as the NCTC (National Collection of Type Cultures). Lyophilization (freeze-drying) is a standard long-term preservation method: the culture is frozen and the water is removed by sublimation under vacuum, after which the dried culture can be stored for years and revived by rehydration when needed.

  68. What is the Plackett–Burman design and when is it used in fermentation?
    Topic: Additional Important Subjective Questions

    The Plackett–Burman design is a statistical (fractional factorial) experimental design used to screen a large number of independent variables — such as nutrients, antifoam, pH and temperature — for their effect on a fermentation, using relatively few experiments. When more than five independent variables are to be investigated, the Plackett–Burman design may be used to identify the variables that significantly affect medium performance before detailed optimization.

  69. Why does foaming occur in fermentations, and how is it controlled?
    Topic: Additional Important Subjective Questions

    Foaming occurs because of proteins and other surface-active components of the medium combined with vigorous aeration and agitation. Foam is unavoidable in many processes and can cause removal of cells from the medium, which will lead to autolysis, loss of product and contamination risk; therefore an antifoam agent should be used. Many antifoams are of low solubility and must be added carefully, because excess antifoam can reduce oxygen transfer. Oils can also serve as antifoam carriers/agents.

  70. How are pure cultures isolated? Write a note on the streak plate method.
    Topic: Additional Important Subjective Questions

    The isolation method most commonly used to get pure cultures is the streak plate method: a mixed culture is streaked over the surface of an agar plate in a pattern that progressively dilutes the cells, so that individual cells grow into separate, well-isolated colonies; a single colony is then picked as the pure culture. Pure cultures are characterized and identified by phenotypic characteristics such as microscopic morphology, metabolic (biochemical) differences, serology and fatty acid analysis.

  71. What are precursors in fermentation? Explain with the example of penicillin production.
    Topic: Additional Important Subjective Questions

    Precursors are compounds added to the fermentation medium that are directly incorporated into the product molecule, increasing its yield and directing its formation. In penicillin production, phenylacetic acid is still the most widely used precursor: it is incorporated into the side chain of penicillin G. Phenoxyacetic acid similarly directs production towards penicillin V. Precursors must be added at controlled rates because some (e.g. phenylacetic acid and cyanides) can be toxic to the culture at high concentrations.

  72. Why must the fermentation medium and fermenter design be considered together? Also state why medium development must consider scale-up.
    Topic: Additional Important Subjective Questions

    The medium will affect the design of the fermenter to be used — for example, a medium with a high viscosity needs a higher power input for effective stirring, and the medium's foaming and oxygen-transfer behaviour influences aeration/agitation design. Fermentation medium can be a significant proportion of the total product cost, and the problem of developing a process from the laboratory to the pilot scale, and subsequently to the industrial scale, must also be considered when selecting a suitable medium, because a medium that works in shake flasks must also perform in stirred, aerated production vessels.
