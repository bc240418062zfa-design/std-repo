  1. Define ovoviviparous.
    Topic: Short Questions (2 Marks)

    Those insects having eggs that remain in the body of the parent for a time after hatching are termed ovoviviparous.

  2. What is meant by contact insecticides?
    Topic: Short Questions (2 Marks)

    It was formerly supposed that certain materials, called contact insecticides, kill insects by contact with their bodies. They cause death by entering the spiracles and closing them up, thus producing suffocation.

  3. What is seminal receptacle?
    Topic: Short Questions (2 Marks)

    A side pouch called seminal receptacle is connected with the vagina. It is for the storage of the sperms which fertilize the eggs.

  4. Write down the scientific name of early shoot borer.
    Topic: Short Questions (2 Marks)

    The scientific name of sugarcane early shoot borer is Chilo infuscatellus. Its egg parasitoid Trichogramma chilonis is released in the field for its biological control.

  5. Define Arthropods.
    Topic: Short Questions (2 Marks)

    The largest group of all animals is the Arthropoda. Its members are found in the seas, in fresh water, on land or even flying freely. It is a group with remarkable differences of structure and it is so abundant that all the other animals taken together are less than one-sixth as many as the arthropods.

  6. What is Biological Control?
    Topic: Short Questions (2 Marks)

    Biological control is the manipulation of biological organisms to control pests by: 1. Release of predators/parasites/diseases of an insect or weed. 2. It can be time consuming, expensive and difficult.

  7. Write down the Order of termites.
    Topic: Short Questions (2 Marks)

    Blattodea is the order of termites (formerly placed in the order Isoptera).

  8. What is the appearance of adult rice-ear bug?
    Topic: Short Questions (2 Marks)

    The adult of Leptocorisa oratorius (rice ear-head bug) is green, light brown or mixed yellow in color. The adults are slender and about 20mm long.

  9. Why Fuzz is used?
    Topic: Short Questions (2 Marks)

    Fuzz is used in production of mattresses, surgical cotton, photographic film and paper.

  10. What is the function of maxillae and mandibles?
    Topic: Short Questions (2 Marks)

    The mandibles are a pair of jaws present behind the labrum which cut and chew the food and move sideways. The maxillae, present behind each mandible at the side of the mouth, help in holding and manipulating the food, and the maxillary palpus is concerned with the sense of smell.

  11. Define transmission.
    Topic: Short Questions (2 Marks)

    Transmission is the passing of a pathogen causing communicable disease from an infected host individual or group to a particular individual or group, regardless of whether the other individual was previously infected.

  12. Give the name of the vector in which malaria is transmitted.
    Topic: Short Questions (2 Marks)

    In the case of malaria, the vector is the anopheline (female Anopheles) mosquito and the disease-causing organism is the malaria parasite (Plasmodium).

  13. What is coxa?
    Topic: Short Questions (2 Marks)

    The coxa is the segment of the leg that articulates with the body, frequently partly lying in a more or less cup-shaped hollow of the latter. It may be short or long and is generally freely movable on the body and powerful.

  14. Name the mouth parts of insects.
    Topic: Short Questions (2 Marks)

    The mouth parts of insects consist of: labrum (front lip), a pair of mandibles (jaws), a pair of maxillae with maxillary palpi, labium (hinder lip), hypopharynx (tongue-like structure) and epipharynx (inner surface of the labrum forming the roof of the mouth).

  15. What is ETL (Economic Threshold Level)?
    Topic: Short Questions (2 Marks)

    The Economic Threshold Level (ETL) is the population density at which control measures should be determined (applied) to prevent an increasing pest population from reaching the Economic Injury Level.

  16. Define economic threshold.
    Topic: Short Questions (2 Marks)

    Economic threshold is the pest population at which a grower must take action to prevent a pest population from reaching the economic injury level.

  17. What are the characteristics of insects?
    Topic: Short Questions (2 Marks)

    Insects (Hexapoda) have the body grouped in three distinct sections: head, thorax and abdomen. The head bears one pair of antennae and the mouth parts; the thorax bears three pairs of jointed legs and usually two pairs of wings; the body is covered by a chitinous exoskeleton and breathing is by air tubes (tracheae).

  18. Write the names of types of head of insects.
    Topic: Short Questions (3 Marks)

    There are three types of heads in insects: 1. Hypognathous 2. Prognathous 3. Opisthognathous

  19. Describe the breathing of insects.
    Topic: Short Questions (3 Marks)

    1. Respiration in insects is accomplished by a method which is nearly unique. 2. The oxygen needed, instead of being drawn into lungs and there being taken up by the blood and carried to the parts of the body where it is needed, as in man, is carried directly to those parts by a system of air tubes (tracheae) which open along the sides of the body through openings called spiracles.

  20. What is sanitary control?
    Topic: Short Questions (3 Marks)

    Sanitary control consists of methods to avoid introducing a pest into a field by: 1. Cleaning field equipment 2. Planting certified seed 3. Quarantines

  21. What are attractants?
    Topic: Short Questions (3 Marks)

    Sometimes called attrahents, attractants are materials used to lure insects to traps and poisoned baits. The use of geraniol in Japanese beetle traps, sugar sirups for ant baits, and fermenting sirup solutions for trapping various moths are good examples.

  22. Define ovipositor.
    Topic: Short Questions (3 Marks)

    The ovipositor is a tube-like organ at the end of the abdomen which is used for the laying of eggs. It usually consists of about three pairs of parts, long or short, slender or stout, used for making a hole or sawing a slit in the object in which the eggs are placed and for guiding the eggs into the hole thus made.

  23. How the brain form in insects?
    Topic: Short Questions (3 Marks)

    The brain is the largest ganglion of the body, situated above the esophagus. It is produced by the fusion of several ganglia (the ganglia of the front segments of the head). Below the esophagus is a second ganglion, called the sub-esophageal ganglion.

  24. Write down the symptoms of damage of red cotton bug.
    Topic: Short Questions (3 Marks)

    Red cotton bug: Dysdercus cingulatus; Family: Pyrrhocoridae; Order: Hemiptera. Symptoms of damage: red stained lint and rotting bolls; inner boll wall with warty growth or water soaked spots; young bolls abort and turn dark brown. The bacterium Nematospora gossypii enters the site of injury and stains the fiber.

  25. Write down the symptoms of damage of sugarcane mealy bug.
    Topic: Short Questions (3 Marks)

    Symptoms of damage: 1. Nymph and adult suck juice from cane in groups and reduce the vigour of the plant. 2. It also attacks roots. 3. Honey dew secretion leads to development of sooty mould which gives blackish appearance to canes. Mealybug feeding in general includes stunting, chlorosis, defoliation and wilting.

  26. Mode of damage of fruit borer (spotted bollworm).
    Topic: Short Questions (3 Marks)

    Bolls showing regular, circular bore holes. Larvae seen feeding on the boll by thrusting their heads alone inside and leaving the rest of the body outside. Presence of granular faecal pellets outside the bore hole. A single larva can damage 30-40 bolls.

  27. Damage caused by termites.
    Topic: Short Questions (3 Marks)

    They enter through cut ends of setts and feed on the soft tissue. The tunnel is filled with the soil. The termites attack setts, shoots, canes and also stubbles. Entire shoot dries up and can be pulled out easily.

  28. What are repellents? Explain some repellents.
    Topic: Short Questions (3 Marks)

    1. Repellents are those materials used to keep insects away from crops, animals and man. 2. Various coal-tar and pine products are rather general insect repellents. 3. Naphthalene is a clothes moth repellent and oil of citronella is a common ingredient of preparations used to keep mosquitoes away from people.

  29. Write commercial products produced by insects.
    Topic: Short Questions (3 Marks)

    A group of insects is responsible for a number of commercial products: 1. Silk 2. Honey 3. Beeswax 4. Shellac

  30. What are physical control methods?
    Topic: Short Questions (3 Marks)

    Physical factors controlling insects are the following: 1. Heat 2. Cold 3. Light 4. Moisture 5. Electricity Devices are available for electrocuting insects, some in the form of electrical window screens for flies and others as electric light traps for moths and beetles.

  31. List the insect borne diseases.
    Topic: Short Questions (3 Marks)

    Insect borne diseases include: Malaria, Bubonic plague, Typhoid, Typhus and Yellow fever. (Also encephalitis through Culex mosquito and amoebic dysentery through house fly.)

  32. What is clean culture?
    Topic: Short Questions (3 Marks)

    The destruction of vegetable trash and weeds is known as clean culture (cultural clean). It is an important factor especially in the control of hibernating insects.

  33. Define lac culture.
    Topic: Short Questions (3 Marks)

    Lac culture is the rearing of the lac insect (Kerria lacca) on host trees for the commercial production of lac, which is refined into shellac, a commercial product used in polishes, varnishes and electrical insulation.

  34. Enlist the plants which require pollination by insects.
    Topic: Short Questions (3 Marks)

    Smyrna fig, clovers, melons, tomatoes, beans, peas, and most of common fruits require insects for their pollination.

  35. How new plant species cause insect outbreak?
    Topic: Short Questions (3 Marks)

    In the course of the development of agriculture, man has increased the number and magnitude of his entomological problems. Introduction of new plant species: before the settlement of a country there were, of course, native insects attacking the various plants growing there; when new plant species are introduced, native insects may attack them, or the pests of the new plants may be introduced without their natural enemies, causing outbreaks.

  36. Explain Digestive system of insects.
    Topic: Long Questions (5 Marks)

    The alimentary canal extends from the mouth through about the center of the body to the anus at the hinder end. It is divided into three parts: 1. Fore intestine: the ingrowth from the mouth. It includes the pharynx, esophagus, crop (for storage of food) and proventriculus or gizzard. The salivary glands lie in the front of the thorax and open into the mouth. 2. Mid intestine: it forms the stomach of the adult insect, where digestion and absorption take place. To increase the absorptive surface it is increased in length by loops and coils. The stomach may develop side pouches or gastric caeca. 3. Hind intestine: the ingrowth from the anus. It is divided into ileum, colon and rectum and is concerned with absorption of water and formation of faeces. The Malpighian tubes, which remove nitrogenous waste, open into the hind intestine near its junction with the mid intestine.

  37. Explain Positions of Head of insects and its types.
    Topic: Long Questions (5 Marks)

    The heads of different insects vary much in form in the location of the mouth. In some cases it is on the underside; in others it is practically on the front. According to position there are three types: 1. Hypognathous: head perpendicular to longitudinal body axis, mouth beneath; for example Locust. 2. Prognathous: head extended to (in line with) longitudinal body axis, mouth forward; for example Weevils. In prognathous heads, labrum is present above the mouth opening. 3. Opisthognathous: head bent underneath thorax during rest, mouth directed backward; for example Aphids.

  38. Describe Metamorphosis.
    Topic: Long Questions (5 Marks)

    In some of the simpler insects, a few changes and growth only are needed to make them mature. These are therefore usually grouped together as the Ametabola, or insects having practically no metamorphosis. The remaining insects, from this standpoint, form two groups: Hemimetabola, or Paurometabola: those which on hatching generally show some resemblance to the adults and reach maturity by a series of gradual changes. The young in the Hemimetabola thus limited are called naiads; those in the Paurometabola, nymphs. Holometabola: those which on hatching are totally unlike the adults and become adults by a somewhat different process, passing through larva and pupa stages. Growth, however, is restricted because the insect is enclosed by a cuticula which, while elastic to some extent, at least at its thinner places, has its limitations in this regard. Thus the insect is unable to reach its adult size within its cuticula, and a process called molting takes place. This is begun by a pouring out of fluid by the outside layer of living cells, the hypodermis, between it and the cuticula, separating the two. Next a split appears somewhere in the cuticula usually along the back and the insect crawls out of its skin, i.e., molts. The feeding periods between the molts (or ecdyses) are called instars.

  39. Difference between the exoskeleton of Mollusks and Arthropods.
    Topic: Long Questions (5 Marks)

    In the possession of this external skeleton these animals have a seeming resemblance to the shells (Mollusca), but the materials of which the skeleton is composed are quite different: being largely calcium carbonate in the Mollusca and chitin, which somewhat resembles horn in its nature, sometimes with calcareous salts deposited in it, in the Arthropoda. The simple members of arthropods have bilaterally symmetrical body, though this condition is concealed somewhat by secondary changes in many of the group. The possession of a bilaterally symmetrical body consisting of a series of segments, an exoskeleton of chitin and the presence of jointed legs are, then, distinctive features of the arthropods.

  40. Write down the characteristics of Arthropods.
    Topic: Long Questions (5 Marks)

    No one feature will serve to separate the arthropods from all other animals, but the possession by an animal of several of those here described will enable the observer to determine in each case whether he is examining one of this group: 1. In arthropods the body is composed of a series of more or less similar pieces or segments placed one behind another, the line of attachment of these to each other being usually somewhat evident on parts of the body at least. 2. Another character of the arthropods is the presence of jointed legs (or appendages of some kind), as is indicated by the name of the group. 3. The surface of the body is covered by a secretion which hardens on exposure to the air, forming an outside shell or external skeleton called exoskeleton, there being practically no internal structures supporting the soft organs within this shell except as ingrowths from the outside. 4. The body is bilaterally symmetrical.

  41. Write down the economically importance of insects.
    Topic: Long Questions (5 Marks)

    1. Of the many thousands of insect species known to man some affect him and his property only slightly; some are beneficial; others are injurious in various degrees. 2. Only rough estimates can be made of the monetary losses and gains by insect activities and even these are made with difficulty. 3. It probably would be safer to give them in bushels, tons, etc., because the relative values of different crops and products change from year to year. 4. No crop of either vegetable or animal origin appears to be entirely free from insect injury. Field, truck and fruit crops, both growing and in storage, household goods and food products, forests and wood products, domestic animals and animal products, all are more or less liable to insect attack. 5. On the beneficial side, insects attack injurious species (parasites and predators), pollinate crops and produce commercial products like silk, honey, beeswax and shellac. In an average year loss to all sorts of crops by insect damage is about 10 per cent.

  42. Explain Biological control.
    Topic: Long Questions (5 Marks)

    1. Parasitic and predaceous insects as well as diseases of insects are vital factors in the natural control of injurious insects. 2. It is possible, however, for man to encourage such beneficial forms by introducing them into new areas and by growing them artificially for liberation whenever they become scarce in nature. 3. Until recent years most of this work dealt with the introduction of parasites from abroad to combat those foreign insect pests brought to this country accidentally but without their natural enemies. 4. Certain native parasites and predators are now being reared in large numbers for liberation in areas badly infested with their host species. 5. Probably the first introduction attempted from a foreign country was the now classic case of a ladybeetle brought from Australia to attack the cottony cushion scale, which had already reached California and was endangering the existence of the citrus industry. 6. Following this successful attempt many other parasites and predators have been imported, which in many instances have proved valuable in reducing the numbers of foreign pests. 7. Parasites should not be expected to kill off all their hosts. Examples of biological control agents: Trichogramma chilonis, T. japonicum, Isotima javensis, Bacillus popilliae (bacteria) and Beauveria bassiana (fungus).

  43. How shoot borer, top borer and internode borer can be biologically controlled?
    Topic: Long Questions (5 Marks)

    Biological control of sugarcane borers: 1. For Early shoot borer: Trichogramma chilonis. 2. For Top borer: Trichogramma chilonis or T. japonicum, Isotima javensis. 3. For Internode borer: Trichogramma chilonis. Supporting cultural measures: cultivation of resistant varieties, irrigation at closer intervals for managing early shoot borer, practice of deep harvesting to destroy stubbles, and removal of water shoots to destroy top shoot borer.

  44. Write the Pests of rice.
    Topic: Long Questions (5 Marks)

    Rice (Oryza sativa L.) is the most important staple food crop of the world; more than 60% of world's population depend on it for food. Major insect pests of rice are: 1. Brown Plant Hopper, Nilaparvata lugens (Homoptera: Delphacidae) - transmits virus disease in rice and causes hopper burn. 2. Yellow Rice Stem Borer, Scirpophaga incertulas (Lepidoptera: Pyralidae). 3. Rice Ear-head Bug, Leptocorisa oratorius (Hemiptera: Alydidae) - adult is green, light brown or mixed yellow in color. 4. Rice Hispa, Dicladispa armigera (Coleoptera: Chrysomelidae). 5. Rice Grasshopper, Hieroglyphus banian (Orthoptera: Acrididae).

  45. Write the Order Coleoptera with examples.
    Topic: Long Questions (5 Marks)

    Coleoptera includes beetles and weevils. Examples: Blister beetle, Boll weevil, Collops beetle, Darkling beetle, Dermestid beetle, Dung beetle, Fig beetle, Flea beetle, Lady beetle, Long-horned beetle, Palo Verde root borer, Metallic wood borer. Mouthparts: chewing; weevil - piercing sucking. Metamorphosis: complete metamorphosis. Beetles are a group of insects that form the order Coleoptera. Their front pair of wings are hardened into wing-cases, elytra, distinguishing them from most other insects. The Coleoptera, with about 400,000 species, is the largest of all orders, constituting almost 40% of described insects and 25% of all known animal life-forms.

  46. Write the characteristics of order Orthoptera.
    Topic: Long Questions (5 Marks)

    1. This order includes: Indian House Crickets, Field Cricket, Short-horned Grasshoppers, Katydid (Long-horned Grasshoppers). 2. Mouthparts: chewing. 3. Metamorphosis: incomplete metamorphosis. 4. Damage: chewing leaves, crop grains. 5. Over 20,000 species worldwide.

  47. Write down the advantage of weeds destruction in cultural control.
    Topic: Long Questions (5 Marks)

    1. The destruction of weeds and brush along fences is of advantage in the reduction of insects. The destruction of vegetable trash and weeds, frequently called clean culture, is also an important factor, especially in the control of hibernating insects. 2. But it results in the elimination of nesting and hiding places for insectivorous and game birds. 3. Weeds not only interfere with successful crop growth by crowding, but they may reduce the vigor of the crop by competing for plant food in the soil. 4. In winter they provide hiding places for hibernating pests. 5. A pest appearing in the spring before its crop food plant is available may find certain weeds palatable until the crop has appeared. 6. Other pests move to late summer and fall weeds after their particular crops have been harvested.

  48. Explain four general types of insect larvae.
    Topic: Long Questions (5 Marks)

    The four general types of insect larvae are: 1. Oligopod (campodeiform) larvae: these have a well developed head, thoracic legs but no abdominal prolegs; they are active larvae, e.g., larvae of ladybird beetles and ground beetles. 2. Scarabaeiform larvae: these are stout, C-shaped, sluggish larvae with well developed thoracic legs, e.g., white grubs of cockchafer beetles. 3. Polypod (eruciform) larvae: these have a cylindrical body with thoracic legs and abdominal prolegs; they are caterpillar-like, e.g., larvae of butterflies, moths and sawflies. 4. Apodous larvae: these are legless larvae with a reduced head, living inside food material, e.g., maggots of flies and larvae of weevils.

  49. Describe sericulture.
    Topic: Long Questions (5 Marks)

    Sericulture is the rearing of silkworms (Bombyx mori) for the production of silk. The silkworm is a holometabolous insect: eggs hatch into larvae (caterpillars) which feed on mulberry leaves and pass through four molts. The mature larva spins a cocoon of silk thread around itself and pupates inside it. The cocoons are harvested and the silk is reeled from them; silk is an important commercial product of insects. Thread or silk is spun backward and forward and around the body until it sometimes forms a complete outer covering called cocoon.

  50. Write the names of classes (main groups) of Arthropods.
    Topic: Long Questions (5 Marks)

    There are 5 classes (main groups) of Arthropods: 1. Hexapoda: insects, six jointed appendages (legs). 2. Chilopoda: centipedes, one pair of legs per body segment, multiple body segments. 3. Diplopoda: millipedes, two pairs of legs per body segment, multiple body segments. 4. Crustacea: crayfish etc., five pairs of jointed appendages. 5. Arachnida: spiders etc., four pairs of jointed appendages.
