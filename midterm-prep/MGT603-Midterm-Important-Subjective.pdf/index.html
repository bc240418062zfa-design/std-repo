  12. Give at least two examples of multinational or local firms that are operating in Pakistan and have initiated backward integration. Justify your answer.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Backward integration means gaining ownership or increased control over a firm's suppliers. Two examples from Pakistan are: 1. Nestle Pakistan: Instead of depending on middlemen and independent milk suppliers, Nestle established its own milk collection and chilling centres across Punjab and buys raw milk directly from farmers. By controlling the supply source of its most critical raw material (milk), the firm has moved backward in its supply chain, ensuring quality, quantity and continuity of supply. 2. Nishat Mills: Nishat developed its own spinning and power generation facilities instead of relying entirely on outside yarn suppliers and the national grid. Producing its own yarn (a key input for weaving) and its own energy gives it control over major inputs, which is backward integration. In both cases the firms took ownership/control of activities that were previously performed by their suppliers, which is the essence of backward integration.

  13. Given below is the mission statement of a company. You are required to evaluate the statement based on the following criteria: Self-concept, Products and services, Market, Concern for public image. Justify your answer. (5 marks) Mission Statement: "GEO informs and entertains while celebrating cultures and prompting dialogue in the spirit of 'live and let live'."
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Evaluation of the GEO mission statement: 1. Self-concept: Weak. The statement does not clearly communicate GEO's distinctive competence — what it does especially well compared with competitors (for example, its news coverage, credibility or reach). It describes activities rather than the firm's unique identity. 2. Products and services: Partially present. "Informs and entertains" broadly indicates news and entertainment services, but the actual products (news channels, entertainment channels, publications) are not specified. 3. Market: Missing. The statement does not identify the market served — which viewers, which geographic area (Pakistan or international), or which audience segments the company targets. 4. Concern for public image: Present and strong. "Celebrating cultures and prompting dialogue in the spirit of live and let live" shows the firm is responsive to social and cultural concerns and wants to be seen as promoting harmony and tolerance. Conclusion: The statement is only partially effective — it is strong on public image but weak on self-concept and silent about the market it serves.

  14. Suppose, according to the IFE Matrix, the weighted score of a certain company is 2.20. How will that company interpret this score in terms of its internal factors?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    The total weighted score of an IFE Matrix can range from 1.0 (low) to 4.0 (high), with 2.5 as the average. A score of 2.20 is below the average of 2.5, so the company is internally weak. It means the firm's weaknesses slightly outweigh its strengths, and its current strategies are not effectively capitalizing on its internal strengths nor fully overcoming its internal weaknesses. Management should identify the functional areas (management, marketing, finance, production, R&D, MIS) carrying low-rated factors and take corrective actions to convert weaknesses into strengths so that the score moves above 2.5.

  15. State the names of Michael Porter's generic strategies and define any two of them.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Porter's three generic strategies are: (1) Cost Leadership, (2) Differentiation, and (3) Focus. 1. Cost Leadership: The firm produces standardized products at the lowest per-unit cost and offers them at the lowest prices to a broad, price-sensitive market. It requires efficient, large-scale facilities, tight cost and overhead control, and vigorous pursuit of cost reductions, while maintaining acceptable quality. 2. Differentiation: The firm creates a product or service that is perceived as unique throughout the industry — through design, brand image, technology, features or customer service — and charges a premium price. Its target customers are relatively price-insensitive and value the uniqueness being offered.

  16. Define the following terms: Horizontal integration, Product development, Concentric diversification, Retrenchment, Joint venture.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 10 marks marks

    1. Horizontal integration: A strategy of seeking ownership of, or increased control over, a firm's competitors. 2. Product development: A strategy that seeks increased sales by improving or modifying present products or services. 3. Concentric diversification: Adding new, but related, products or services. 4. Retrenchment: Regrouping through cost and asset reduction to reverse declining sales and profits. 5. Joint venture: Two or more companies form a temporary partnership or consortium for the purpose of capitalizing on some opportunity.

  17. A good mission statement should include "customer orientation". What does it mean? Explain it with the help of simple examples.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Customer orientation means that a good mission statement reflects the anticipation of customer needs: it identifies the utility (benefit) of the firm's products to its customers instead of merely describing the products themselves. The business is defined in terms of the customer need it satisfies, not the physical product it sells. Examples: A cosmetics firm does not just "sell creams and lotions"; it sells beauty and hope to its customers. A railway company is not in the business of "running trains"; it provides safe and comfortable transportation services. Similarly, a university does not merely "deliver lectures"; it provides education and skills that make its students employable. In each case the mission focuses on the benefit the customer receives, which keeps the firm customer-oriented.

  18. What are strengths and weaknesses of an organization? Explain with the help of examples.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Strengths and weaknesses are an organization's internal, controllable factors, identified through the internal audit of its functional areas (management, marketing, finance/accounting, production/operations, R&D and MIS). Strengths are the internal capabilities and resources that a firm can use effectively to achieve its objectives and exploit external opportunities. Examples: a strong brand image, a skilled and loyal workforce, a strong financial position, modern technology, and an effective distribution network. Weaknesses are internal limitations and deficiencies that place the firm at a disadvantage relative to competitors and hinder the achievement of objectives. Examples: outdated machinery, a poor plant location, weak distribution channels, high employee turnover, inadequate R&D facilities, and poor product quality.

  19. What are the two basic types of R&D that can take place in an organization?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    The two basic types of research and development are: 1. Internal R&D: The organization runs its own research and development department and facilities, and its own specialists develop new products, services and processes. 2. Contract R&D: The organization hires external agencies — independent researchers, universities or research firms — to develop specific products or processes on its behalf, instead of maintaining all research facilities in-house.

  20. Vertical integration is the degree to which a firm owns its upstream suppliers and its downstream buyers. What can be the possible benefits if a firm goes for vertical integration?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 marks marks

    Possible benefits of vertical integration include: 1. Assured supply of raw materials and assured outlets for finished products, reducing dependence on suppliers and distributors. 2. Cost savings, because the margins of middlemen are eliminated and prices remain stable. 3. Better coordination and control over quality at every stage of the value chain. 4. Improved capacity utilization and higher profitability. 5. A stronger competitive position, because rivals find it harder to obtain supplies or shelf space.

  21. Mr. Mansoor is a finance manager of a firm. He is asked to prepare a financial ratio analysis. What can be the possible limitations of his analysis?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    The possible limitations of financial ratio analysis are: 1. Ratios are calculated from historical financial statements, so they describe the past and may not predict future performance. 2. Ratios are based on accounting data; different firms use different accounting policies and methods, which makes comparison difficult. 3. Inflation distorts accounting figures over time, so ratios computed for different periods may not be truly comparable. 4. Seasonal factors can distort a firm's ratios at a particular point in time. 5. A single ratio in isolation is not meaningful; ratios must be compared with industry averages and competitors, and they only point out areas needing investigation — they do not by themselves explain the causes of a problem. 6. Firms in different industries face different conditions, so cross-industry comparison of ratios can be misleading.

    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    Firms pursue mergers and acquisitions for the following reasons: 1. To provide improved capacity utilization. 2. To make better use of an existing sales force. 3. To reduce managerial staff and overhead costs. 4. To gain economies of scale. 5. To smooth out seasonal trends in sales. 6. To gain access to new suppliers, distributors, customers, products and creditors. 7. To gain new technology. 8. To reduce tax obligations. 9. To enter new markets quickly and diversify risk.

  23. List down and explain the five steps for conducting an EFE (External Factor Evaluation) Matrix.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The five steps of an EFE Matrix are: 1. List 10 to 20 key external factors identified in the external audit, writing opportunities first and then threats. Be as specific as possible, using percentages, ratios and comparative numbers. 2. Assign each factor a weight from 0.0 (not important) to 1.0 (very important), based on how important the factor is to success in the industry. The sum of all weights must equal 1.0. 3. Assign a rating from 1 to 4 to each factor, indicating how effectively the firm's current strategies respond to that factor (4 = superior response, 3 = above average, 2 = average, 1 = poor). 4. Multiply each factor's weight by its rating to get the weighted score. 5. Sum all weighted scores to obtain the total weighted score, which ranges from 1.0 to 4.0, with 2.5 as average. A high score shows the firm is responding excellently to opportunities and threats.

  24. What is a Competitive Profile Matrix (CPM)? How does it differ from an EFE Matrix?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A Competitive Profile Matrix (CPM) identifies a firm's major competitors and its particular strengths and weaknesses in relation to a sample competitor's strategic position. It includes both internal and external critical success factors, such as market share, product quality, price competitiveness, financial position and customer loyalty. Ratings in a CPM refer to strengths and weaknesses: 4 = major strength, 3 = minor strength, 2 = minor weakness, 1 = major weakness. The firm and its rivals are rated on the same factors, weights are assigned exactly as in the EFE, and the total weighted scores show the relative competitive position of each firm. Difference from EFE: The EFE evaluates only external factors (opportunities and threats) for a single firm and its ratings show how well the firm's strategies respond to those factors, whereas the CPM compares several competing firms on critical success factors that include internal factors as well.

  25. Name and briefly explain Porter's Five Forces that determine the intensity of competition in an industry.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Porter's Five Forces are: 1. Rivalry among competing firms: The intensity of competition among existing firms in the industry; usually the most powerful force, fought through price cuts, advertising, new products and better service. 2. Potential entry of new competitors: New entrants bring additional capacity and a desire for market share; their threat depends on the barriers to entry, such as capital requirements and economies of scale. 3. Potential development of substitute products: Products of firms in other industries that can perform the same function place a ceiling on the prices firms can charge. 4. Bargaining power of suppliers: Suppliers can raise prices or reduce quality, especially when they are few, organized, or supply a critical input. 5. Bargaining power of consumers: Buyers can force prices down or demand higher quality, especially when they purchase in large volumes or products are undifferentiated.

  26. Differentiate between a vision statement and a mission statement.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The main differences are: 1. Question answered: A vision statement answers "What do we want to become?", while a mission statement answers "What is our business?". 2. Time focus: Vision focuses on the future — the desired position of the firm; mission focuses on the present business and purpose of the firm. 3. Order: The vision statement is developed first, and the mission statement is built upon it. 4. Content: Vision is a short, inspirational picture of the preferred future (ideally one sentence); a mission statement is a more detailed, enduring statement of purpose covering customers, products, markets, technology, philosophy and self-concept. 5. Purpose: Vision provides long-term direction; mission provides the basis for priorities, strategies, plans and work assignments.

  27. What are the essential characteristics of long-term objectives?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Long-term objectives should be: 1. Quantitative — expressed in measurable figures. 2. Measurable — progress toward them can be verified. 3. Realistic — achievable with the resources available. 4. Understandable — clear to the managers and employees who must achieve them. 5. Challenging — demanding enough to motivate improved performance. 6. Hierarchical — arranged from corporate level down to divisional and functional levels. 7. Obtainable — within the firm's reach. 8. Congruent — consistent among organizational units, so that one unit's objective does not conflict with another's. Objectives should also be associated with a time period and established for the overall company as well as for each division.

  28. What is a SWOT analysis? Explain its four elements briefly.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    SWOT analysis is a strategy-formulation matching tool that helps managers develop strategies by matching an organization's internal strengths and weaknesses with its external opportunities and threats. 1. Strengths: Internal capabilities and resources that can be used to exploit opportunities (e.g., strong brand, skilled staff). 2. Weaknesses: Internal deficiencies that hinder performance and must be overcome or minimized (e.g., outdated plant, weak finances). 3. Opportunities: External trends and events that the firm can benefit from (e.g., a growing market, new technology). 4. Threats: External trends and events that could harm the firm (e.g., new competitors, rising costs, new regulations). By matching these factors, managers generate four sets of strategies: SO (use strengths to take advantage of opportunities), WO (overcome weaknesses to pursue opportunities), ST (use strengths to avoid threats) and WT (minimize weaknesses and avoid threats).

  29. Discuss the benefits of strategic management for an organization.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Strategic management offers financial and non-financial benefits. Financial benefits: Research shows that organizations using strategic management are more profitable and successful; they show significant improvement in sales, profitability and productivity compared with firms without systematic planning. Non-financial benefits include: 1. Enhanced awareness of external threats. 2. Improved understanding of competitors' strategies. 3. Increased employee productivity. 4. Reduced resistance to change. 5. A clearer understanding of performance-reward relationships. 6. Better problem-avoidance, because managers anticipate and monitor changing trends. Above all, strategic management enables an organization to be proactive — shaping its own future — rather than merely reactive, and it brings order and discipline to the business.

  30. Enlist and briefly explain any five components of a good mission statement.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Five of the nine components of a mission statement are: 1. Customers: Who are the firm's customers? 2. Products or services: What are the firm's major products or services? 3. Markets: Geographically, where does the firm compete? 4. Technology: Is the firm technologically current? 5. Philosophy: What are the basic beliefs, values, aspirations and ethical priorities of the firm? (The remaining components are: concern for survival, growth and profitability; self-concept/distinctive competence; concern for public image; and concern for employees.)

  31. What is competitive advantage? How can a firm achieve and sustain it?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Competitive advantage is anything that a firm does especially well compared with rival firms; it enables the firm to create superior value for its customers and superior profits for itself. A firm achieves competitive advantage when it implements a value-creating strategy that competitors are not simultaneously implementing, for example through lower costs, superior quality, strong brand image, advanced technology, better customer service or an effective distribution network. To sustain it, the firm must continually adapt to changes in external trends and events and effectively formulate, implement and evaluate strategies that capitalize on its internal strengths and external opportunities while correcting weaknesses and avoiding threats. Because rivals keep imitating, a competitive advantage lasts only until competitors copy or surpass it, so it must be renewed continuously.

  32. Describe the major stages of the strategic management process.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The strategic management process consists of three main stages: 1. Strategy formulation: Developing the vision and mission, performing an external audit to identify opportunities and threats, performing an internal audit to identify strengths and weaknesses, establishing long-term objectives, and generating, evaluating and selecting strategies. 2. Strategy implementation: Establishing annual objectives, devising policies, motivating employees and allocating resources so that the formulated strategies can be executed. It is called the action stage of strategic management. 3. Strategy evaluation: Reviewing the external and internal factors that are the bases of current strategies, measuring performance, and taking corrective actions. Feedback is received at every stage, so the process is continuous and dynamic rather than a one-time activity.

  33. What is a joint venture? Under which conditions is it an especially effective strategy?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A joint venture is a cooperative strategy in which two or more companies form a temporary partnership or consortium for the purpose of capitalizing on some opportunity; the participating firms form a separate organization and share equity ownership in it. A joint venture may be an especially effective strategy when: 1. A privately owned organization forms a joint venture with a publicly owned organization. 2. A domestic organization forms a joint venture with a foreign firm, gaining local management, market knowledge and access in the host country. 3. The distinctive competences of two or more firms complement each other especially well. 4. A project is potentially very profitable but requires extensive resources and carries high risk. 5. Two or more smaller firms join together to compete against a large common competitor.

  34. Explain any three categories of key external forces that affect an organization.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Three of the five categories of key external forces are: 1. Economic forces: Factors such as inflation, interest rates, exchange rates, GDP trends and unemployment that directly affect the buying power of consumers and the cost of doing business. For example, rising interest rates reduce the demand for discretionary goods. 2. Social, cultural, demographic and environmental forces: Changes in population, age distribution, lifestyles, buying habits, attitudes toward work and concern for the environment. These shape customers, markets and products; for example, an aging population increases demand for health-care products. 3. Technological forces: Revolutionary changes such as the Internet, automation and new communication technologies that create new products, new markets and new opportunities, and can make existing products obsolete. Firms must monitor them both as opportunities and as threats. (The remaining two categories are political, governmental and legal forces, and competitive forces.)

  35. Why should an organization develop a written mission statement? Give any five reasons.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    According to King and Cleland, an organization should carefully develop a written mission statement for these reasons: 1. To ensure unanimity of purpose within the organization. 2. To provide a basis, or standard, for allocating organizational resources. 3. To establish a general tone or organizational climate. 4. To serve as a focal point for individuals to identify with the organization's purpose and direction. 5. To facilitate the translation of objectives into a work structure involving the assignment of tasks to responsible elements within the organization. 6. To specify organizational purposes and translate them into objectives against which cost, time and performance parameters can be assessed and controlled.

  36. What is retrenchment? When is retrenchment an especially effective strategy to pursue?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Retrenchment occurs when an organization regroups through cost and asset reduction to reverse declining sales and profits. It is a defensive strategy, also called a turnaround or reorganization strategy, and may involve selling land and buildings, pruning product lines, closing marginal businesses or factories, and reducing the number of employees. Retrenchment may be an especially effective strategy when: 1. The firm has a clearly distinctive competence but has failed to meet its objectives and goals consistently over time. 2. The firm is one of the weaker competitors in a given industry. 3. The firm is plagued by inefficiency, low profitability, poor employee morale and pressure from stockholders to improve performance. 4. The firm has failed to capitalize on external opportunities, minimize external threats, take advantage of internal strengths and overcome internal weaknesses over time. 5. The firm has grown so large so quickly that a major internal reorganization is needed.

  37. Differentiate between market penetration and market development strategies.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Market penetration seeks to increase market share for present products or services in present markets through greater marketing efforts — for example, increasing advertising, sales promotion, the number of salespersons, or offering extensive sales promotions. It is used when current markets are not saturated and the usage rate of present customers can be increased significantly. Market development involves introducing present products or services into new geographic areas — for example, a local firm starting to export its existing products to another country or opening branches in new cities. It is used when new, reliable and inexpensive channels of distribution are available and untapped or unsaturated markets exist. In short: penetration = present products, present markets, more marketing effort; development = present products, new markets.

  38. Discuss the role of a Management Information System (MIS) in strategic management.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    An effective Management Information System gathers, codes, stores, synthesizes and presents information from all functional areas of the business, and supports managers in formulating, implementing and evaluating strategies. Its functional support role is recording and storing data such as accounting records, inventory data and human resource records. Its decision support role goes further: it allows users to ask powerful "what-if" questions — for example, what would happen to profit if prices fell by 10% or costs rose by 15% — so that the consequences of alternative strategies can be tested before decisions are made. By improving the quality and timeliness of information, the MIS improves communication among departments, reduces uncertainty, and enables the firm to respond quickly to changes in the environment.

  39. What are policies in strategic management? Why are they important?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Policies are the means by which annual objectives will be achieved. They include guidelines, rules and procedures established to support efforts to achieve stated objectives; in other words, policies are guides to decision making and address repetitive or recurring situations. Policies are important because: 1. They make day-to-day decisions consistent with the chosen strategies and annual objectives. 2. They coordinate activities and decisions across departments and divisions. 3. They reduce the amount of discretion that managers can exercise, ensuring similar situations are handled similarly. 4. They save time, because recurring problems are already answered. Examples include policies on hiring, quality inspection, credit terms, and research spending as a percentage of sales.
