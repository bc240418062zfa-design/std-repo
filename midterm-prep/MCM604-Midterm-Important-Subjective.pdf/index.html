  1. What is a news agency?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    A news agency is an organization which gathers news from a particular country or all over the world and distributes it to different newspapers, magazines, television, radio, government agencies and organizations. It is also called a press agency, wire service or news service. A news agency does not publish news itself; it collects and distributes news to its subscribers, who pay fees for this service. All mass media depend on news agencies for getting news. They prepare hard news which media can use with little or no modification. Nowadays they provide news through the internet and in bulk.

  2. Write a note on the establishment of news agencies.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    In the newspaper industry the establishment of news agencies was very important, because getting the most updated commercial information nationally and internationally was not easy. This gap was filled by news agencies. In 1835 the French agency Havas was established to engage in international activities, followed by Associated Press (USA) in 1846, Wolff (Germany) in 1849, Tuwora (Austria) in 1850 and Reuters (UK) in 1851. Reuters provided commercial and financial information while Havas was disseminating information and advertising. Havas, Wolff and Reuters signed a treaty to divide the world market into three parts, and each of them was responsible for collecting news in its territory. Initially news agencies used to disseminate news through telegraphy, and now it has been replaced by the internet. The four major international news agencies are: Reuters (Reuter Telegram Company of London), Associated Press (AP), United Press International (UPI) and Agence France-Presse (AFP - Agence Havas of Paris).

  3. Explain the main points of the MacBride Commission Report.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    In the 1970s-80s many people raised concerns about the domination of broadcast media by developed countries. Most channels were airing American movies and series. This unbalanced media coverage was debated under the New World Information and Communication Order (NWICO). These concerns were the basis of creating the MacBride Commission. The commission studied four main aspects of global communication: what is the current status of world communication; what are the problems in the way of a free and fair flow of information; how the needs of the developing countries are to be fulfilled with a free flow of information; and how media can educate public opinion on world issues. Its report, 'Many Voices, One World' (1980), made 82 recommendations calling for a freer flow and a wider and better balanced dissemination of information.

  4. What do you know about the telegraph?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    The word telegraphy is derived from Greek: 'tele' means at a distance and 'graphien' means to write. It is a communication system which sends information through electric signals over a wire between stations. The transmission line was connected with the transmitter and receiver. The prototype was demonstrated by Joseph Henry in 1830, which was refined by Samuel Morse in 1837. The first official telegraph message was sent on 1st May 1844 from Washington, D.C., to Baltimore, Maryland. The telegraph made possible the transmission of information quickly over long distances. It was used first to make business and earn profit. A telegraph link was set up in Britain in 1838 and it unified the British Empire. In 1851 a public telegraph service was introduced in Britain, and by the end of the century the Colonial and India offices were linked to the British Empire; they could communicate with each other within minutes, which earlier took months. British merchants used the telegraph to find the current prices of cotton and other commodities in England, exporting them from India to Egypt.

  5. Explain the concept of Free Flow of Information.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    Free flow of information means the free movement of information, news and advertisements across the world without any hindrance. This idea was promoted by the USA after the Second World War during the Cold War with the Soviet Union. The USA and Western countries wanted freedom of expression and open markets to promote democracy. This is called the capitalism approach, where in the economic and political system a country's trade is controlled by private owners for profit rather than by the state. This concept is used to get benefits on both economic and political fronts. Big media houses of the Western countries tried to persuade others not to put trade barriers on their products and services under the cover of democracy, freedom of expression and the media's role as a watchdog to educate people. Their concept promoted capitalism and individualism.

  6. Discuss the key challenges to freedom of media in Pakistan.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Freedom of media means that news and information will be disseminated without any restrictions of government. Article 19 of the Constitution of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan protects the fundamental rights of individuals and media. Every citizen and media has the right of speech and expression, subject to the restrictions imposed by law. Press freedom has never been consistent in Pakistan. Different governments used different tactics to control the media. In Pakistan there are many constitutional provisions and laws which restrict the freedom of media, and Pakistan's ranking in the World Press Freedom Index issued by Reporters Without Borders has remained low. The predators of press freedom are extremist groups, Islamist organizations and intelligence agencies. The key challenges are: censorship of programmes, taking programmes off air and censoring publications; editors having pressure from media owners, fear of losing advertisements and licence renewal issues; physical threats, violence, harassment and killings of journalists; use of the law on blasphemy against journalists; the government's non-serious attitude to provide protection and solve cases of violence against media professionals; and lack of professional training of journalists and their accountability.

  7. According to John Stuart Mill, a nation cannot be democratically free without a free press, as it is also the duty of the press to evaluate the current government and uphold the truth. Do you think Pakistani media is free enough to scrutinize the government of Pakistan? Justify your answer with supporting arguments.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    John Stuart Mill says in his famous book 'On Liberty' that freedom of expression is the most important and untouchable right of an individual. He argues that freedom of expression matters a lot to people because they can express their beliefs and values publicly. The second reason is that freedom of expression promotes the discovery of truth. It also leads to having more options and perspectives required to take better decisions. Article 19 of the Constitution of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan protects the fundamental rights of individuals and media; every citizen and media has the right of speech and expression subject to the restrictions imposed by law. Pakistani media is among the freest media in Asia, but it faces threats from extremist groups, Islamist organizations, government officials, intelligence agencies, political parties and even from its own workers. Under the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act 2016, criticism of the military and judicial system can lead to imprisonment, yet we see people criticising both institutions in the name of freedom. So the media does scrutinize the government, but within limits imposed by law, threats and ownership pressures.

  8. Explain communication used in the Mughal era.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    The era of the Mughal Empire was from 1526 to 1857. The Mughals ruled parts of Afghanistan, Balochistan and the Indian Subcontinent. The Mughal Empire is known as a 'gunpowder empire' also. During the Mughal period, 'waqi'a-nawis' (news writers) were employed by the king, who were responsible for informing him of the progress and development in the empire. They made arrangements to send post from one part of the country to another. It was done through relays of horses and runners. They built towers at a distance of a few kilometres to see if a runner was coming while clanging the bell, so that the next runner would take his burden quickly.

  9. How did the term 'Third World' become popular around the globe?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    In 1952, French economic historian Alfred Sauvy used the word 'Third World' during the Cold War. It was an era when the world was divided into two blocs. The First World was capitalist, led by the USA and its allies; the Second World was used for the communist bloc of the Soviet Union; and the countries which were not part of these two blocs were called 'Third World' countries. Most of Asia, Africa and Latin America are part of the Third World. Nowadays the phrase 'Third World' is used for underdeveloped or developing countries. In these countries the education level is low, there is a lack of proper infrastructure, poor health care facilities, improper sanitation services and inferior living conditions of the people. The process of industrialization is very slow, people have low levels of per capita income, and they depend on agricultural and commodity exports.

  10. Differentiate between the leftist and rightist approaches in Gramsci's theory of hegemony.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    In 1971, the ideas of the Italian Marxist Antonio Gramsci (1891-1937) got major influence in Britain when his notes prepared in jail were translated into English, titled 'Selections from the Prison Notebooks'. His theory of hegemony was based on his political experience. He explained that it is not necessary for the ruling class to use military force to retain power; they have much influence through intellectual means and moral direction to build a system which supports their aims, through schools, religious bodies and effective use of mass media. The leftist approach uses this theory critically: it exposes how the ruling class maintains dominance by consent rather than force, and how media act as ideological agents of the elite - Herman and Chomsky argued that the propaganda model succeeds because there is no clear indication of government using force to persuade people; the French Marxist Louis Althusser also called the media an 'ideological state apparatus'. The rightist (dominant) approach, on the other hand, presents this order as natural and legitimate, using media, schools and courts to win public consent for the existing system. In international communication, hegemony is used in mass media by doing propaganda and maintaining the ruling elite's ideology: news and entertainment production follows their ideology, and although media is free from direct government control, it acts as an agent to persuade people to accept the dominant ideology.

  11. Discuss the factors behind the rise of Reuters.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Reuters is a news agency established in 1851 in the UK. Reuters' focus was to provide commercial and financial information. As the British Empire spread, Reuters grew along with it. There was a huge demand for business news during the nineteenth century due to the expansion of trade and business. To expand its services, Reuters used new communication technologies like telegraphy to provide information to British and European clients. By 1870, Reuters had its offices in major important cities like Calcutta, Bombay and Point de Galle in Sri Lanka, connected to their head office in London through telegraphic wire, monitoring services in Asia, China, Japan and Australia. In 1914, Reuters News Services had three main channels covering news from London to Bombay, London to Hong Kong and another in Cape Town, Durban and Mauritius. The trade and business volume increased 25-fold from 1800 to 1913, and it was possible with the link between capital and communication; it is also called 'The Reuters Factor'. Reuters also built a good relationship with the British foreign and colonial administration. Although it was an independent news agency, it became the voice of the British Empire and promoted British views, and it supported the Empire by making propaganda during the First World War. By the end of the twentieth century, Reuters had become the world's largest financial data provider plus news provider for newspapers, with regional headquarters in London, New York, Geneva and Hong Kong and offices in 217 cities.

  12. Differentiate between verbal and nonverbal communication.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The foundation of any successful relationship is good communication, whether personal or professional. Communication is a combination of verbal and nonverbal communication. Verbal communication: the use of sounds and words, said or written, to express yourself for communication is called verbal communication. It is interpersonal communication through linguistic means. Nonverbal communication: our nonverbal communication plays an important role - our facial expressions, gestures, eye contact, posture and tone of voice speak loudly. If there is a contradiction, the nonverbal message expresses the true feelings of a person more accurately than his verbal message.

  13. Do you think that the flow of international communication is one-sided, biased and a source of planned propaganda against Muslims? Discuss by giving examples and logical arguments.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Yes, it is true that the flow of international communication is one-sided and biased. Propaganda is used as a weapon to influence international opinion against Muslims. Media portray Muslims as terrorists, rigid and having old ideologies not relevant to the current world. It creates this perception by reporting partial news, in a prejudiced manner, or by ignoring an important aspect. Most Muslim countries are underdeveloped and have fewer economic opportunities, so they cannot counter this flow with strong media of their own. As soon as any incident happens, the media sees who committed it: if the culprit is a white man, he will be declared mentally ill or a disturbed loner; if the same thing is committed by a Muslim, he will be declared a terrorist without establishing his motives and solid evidence. For example, in the Las Vegas shooting, which killed at least 59 in the deadliest US gun attack, the Sheriff said it was not an act of terrorism and nothing linked it to terrorism; had a similar act been committed by a Muslim, he would have been declared a terrorist immediately.

  14. Keeping in mind the era of the Cold War, discuss the major differences between the two world divides, i.e. the developed and underdeveloped countries.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The major difference was ideological. The Soviet bloc believed in communism: everything is owned by the government and divided equally among its people, who work for the government in return. They believed in state power and control. The United States and its allies were capitalist. Three main freedoms were given to people: freedom of press, free and fair elections, and freedom of speech or protest. People could own land and run their businesses, which made a difference between rich and poor. The developed capitalist countries controlled the international flow of news and information through their news agencies and media, while the underdeveloped countries depended on them for technology, finance and even for news about themselves, which created an imbalance in international communication.

  15. What is Social Responsibility Theory?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Social Responsibility is a terminology used for looking after the welfare of the people while generating business activity. It is a belief that the press has a responsibility to inform its public on the issues of concern of their society. Media have obligations towards society, culture, economy and environment. The freedom of media must not be on the basis of harming society and its socio-cultural aspects and economy. Social Responsibility Theory is a new concept introduced in the mid-twentieth century by Robert Hutchins, president of the University of Chicago (the Commission on Freedom of the Press). This model was reshaped by Siebert, Peterson and Schramm in 1956. It encourages total media freedom, but regulated by social responsibilities and external control.

  16. Sensationalism and the irresponsible state of media in Pakistan have put doubts over its working for society. In this situation, what steps should be taken by the government to ensure a balanced form of freedom of media in Pakistan? Discuss only those steps that are feasible in our society.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    The Social Responsibility Theory will help in creating professionalism in media and a responsible form of media freedom by setting up a high level of accuracy, truth and information. Steps feasible in Pakistani society are: 1. Formulate a code of conduct for the press. 2. Improve the standards of journalism. 3. Safeguard the interests of journalism and journalists. 4. Criticise and impose some penalty for violating the code of conduct.

  17. Differentiate between network society and information society.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Information society: Information society is a term used for a society where technology is the driving force behind its transformation and development. It is a society whose economic and cultural activity is based on information, communication and computing technologies. It is an era where society has entered into the information society from the post-industrial society. It is also called the third wave of human civilization and the knowledge society. Information society has transformed almost every aspect of social life - offices, homes, household items, communities, education, health and surveillance. The networks which provide infrastructure for the global economy are called information superhighways. Network society: A network society is a conception of society where key social structures and activities are organised around information computing technologies. It is based on advanced networking technologies to support anytime, anywhere access to high quality content. Castells says networks are making a new social face of our societies and have become the basic units of modern society. It is not only technology that makes modern society; cultural, political and economic factors - religion, social status, cultural values and affiliation with political parties - also play an important role in making up the network society.

  18. Define Development Communication.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Development Communication is the art and science of human communication to facilitate speedy social development and the transformation of a country from poverty to a dynamic modern state. 'Art and science' means the creative use of human communication by research and applying scientific methods to solve development problems.

  19. Define Development Support Communication, and differentiate between Development Communication (DC) and Development Support Communication (DSC).
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Development Support Communication is a process to share information about development and the social change agenda and its implementation. It creates links between beneficiaries, planners, donors and stakeholders. It is target specific and limited. Differences between DC and DSC: Development Communication (DC) - it is used in projects and programmes that are at a large scale (macro level); it requires the use of technology and depends upon technical mass media; its target is general people and not a specific community; it is used to make development in any sector; it is time bound, source oriented and has general goals; the message moves from sender to receiver and travels vertically, from top to bottom. Development Support Communication (DSC) - it is applicable at micro-level projects because of its small scale; it is for specific people or a community, therefore based on interpersonal communication; its goals are clearly defined with specific measures; it is receiver oriented and importance is given to the receiver before starting communication; it has limits in each project and finishes after achieving its goals; it is applicable at grass root levels and requires audience participation.

  20. Discuss the globalization of media in terms of the following parameters: (1) Internationalized media ownership (2) Globalized media content.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    Media globalization is a result of new technologies, and it facilitates all forms of globalization. It increases the interconnectivity between the world, which results in increased trade, services and cultural exchange. TV and internet broadcasts are the main players in bringing people of different cultures and faith closer and finding ways of mutual understanding to move forward. (1) Internationalized media ownership: Most media houses are owned by wealthy corporations which dominate the media market. Local media are either taken over, or their freedom of speech is compromised through profit. The biggest beneficiaries of globalization are the media tycoons. They often merge with big organizations of different industries, and their wrongdoing will not be broadcast on their own channels - for example, GE bought NBC, and GE is one of the biggest pollution generators, but NBC will not talk about pollution caused by GE. (2) Globalized media content: Global media contents are produced to get maximum profit at less cost. They show a diverse range of civilizations and promote the culture of rich countries, which affects the culture of other countries. Multinational media companies are not accountable and only governments can regulate them. As the number of media houses has increased, the diversity in content has not increased. It shapes culture and changes people's minds.

  21. Differentiate between the traditional system and the modern system of communication.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    The traditional system of communication was local, slow and interpersonal. It relied on oral tradition, folk media, town criers, inscriptions, handwritten newsletters, religious gatherings, fairs and travelling performers. Its reach was limited to a community or region, messages travelled slowly, and feedback was immediate but the audience was small. Authority and custom controlled the flow of information, and content was rooted in local culture and religion. The modern system is based on mass media and electronic technology - newspapers, radio, television, satellite and the internet. It is instant, global and largely one-way from large media organizations to mass audiences, although new media has made it interactive and many-to-many. Ownership is concentrated in big corporations, content is produced commercially for profit, and the flow is dominated by developed countries. In short, the traditional system was community-based, slow and two-way on a small scale, while the modern system is technology-based, fast, commercial and global in reach.

  22. Write about the Cold War propaganda against the Third World countries.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    During the Cold War, both superpowers - the USA and the USSR - used international communication as a weapon of propaganda against each other and for the allegiance of the Third World. The USA promoted the 'free flow of information' doctrine to open Third World markets and minds to capitalist values, democracy and American products, using agencies like the Voice of America and the USIA. The Soviet Union used Radio Moscow and its news agency Tass to promote communist ideology and anti-colonial solidarity. Western news agencies (AP, UPI, Reuters, AFP) controlled about 80 percent of the world's news flow, so news about the Third World reached even the Third World itself through Western selection and framing, which presented developing countries through wars, coups, famine and corruption. Third World leaders and scholars - such as Mustapha Masmoudi - protested against this quantitative and qualitative imbalance, arguing that news processed by the West distorted their reality and served as planned propaganda to keep them dependent. This protest produced the demand for a New World Information and Communication Order (NWICO) and the MacBride Commission.

  23. Define and explain Critical Theory.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    Critical Theory is the approach associated with the Frankfurt School (scholars such as Adorno, Horkheimer and later Habermas), which examines society and culture critically, especially the role of power, ideology and domination. It argues that mass media and the 'culture industry' are not neutral: they produce standardized entertainment and news which manufacture consent, create false needs and keep the existing capitalist order stable by distracting people from real social problems. Unlike administrative research, which asks how media can be used more effectively, critical theory asks who owns and controls the media, whose interests media content serves, and how audiences can be emancipated from this domination. It therefore studies media together with the political economy of society, and aims not only to understand the world but to change it by exposing hidden structures of power.

  24. Exemplify a situation describing the hyperreality of media.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    Hyperreality, a concept given by Jean Baudrillard, is a situation in which media images and simulations become more real for people than reality itself, and the distinction between the real and its copy disappears. For example, during televised war coverage, viewers around the world watch smart-bomb camera footage and studio graphics like a video game; the simulation of the war becomes their experienced 'reality', while the actual human suffering on the ground remains invisible. Similarly, a celebrity's carefully produced social-media image is treated by fans as the person's real life, and tourists visit places mainly to reproduce the media image they have already consumed. In such cases the copy no longer represents reality - it replaces it.

  25. Keeping in mind the concept of Dependency Theory, discuss the role of the United States in Afghanistan.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    Dependency Theory holds that core (developed) countries keep periphery (developing) countries dependent on them for finance, technology, trade and information, so that development in the periphery remains controlled and limited. The role of the United States in Afghanistan fits this pattern: Afghanistan depended on the US and its allies for military security, aid, reconstruction funds, technology and even for the news and images through which the world saw the conflict. American media framed Afghanistan for global audiences, while Afghan media depended on foreign funding, equipment and content. Aid and contracts were tied to American strategic and commercial interests, local institutions were built in the image of the core country, and decision-making about Afghanistan's future was dominated from outside. When the core's interest changed, support was withdrawn, showing that the periphery's development had never become self-sustaining - the classic outcome predicted by Dependency Theory.

  26. Discuss the similarity basis in Galtung's theory of structural imperialism with Schiller's definition of cultural imperialism.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Both theories explain domination without direct military force and both see communication and culture as central mechanisms of that domination. Galtung argues that the centre nation maintains dominance over the periphery through a 'bridgehead' - the elite in the centre of the periphery nation whose interests harmonize with the elite of the centre - and he lists communication and cultural imperialism among his five types of imperialism. Schiller defines cultural imperialism as the process by which a society is brought into the modern world system and its dominating stratum is attracted, pressured, forced or bribed into shaping social institutions to promote the values and structures of the dominating centre. The similarity is that in both, the local elite willingly adopts and reproduces the values, institutions and media content of the dominant centre, so information and cultural flows from centre to periphery maintain economic and political dependence. Both therefore treat media and culture not as innocent entertainment but as instruments through which structural power is exercised.

  27. How has the arrival of new media platforms induced development in the globalized world? Discuss.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    New media platforms - the internet, mobile phones, social media and satellite television - have induced development by reducing the cost and time of communication and by breaking the one-way flow of information from North to South. Farmers and traders can check prices and weather instantly; mobile banking and e-commerce have brought financial services to remote areas; telemedicine and online education reach people whom traditional institutions could not serve; and governments can deliver information and services directly to citizens. New media also give developing countries and ordinary citizens the ability to produce and distribute their own content globally, promoting local culture, tourism and small businesses, and enabling public debate and accountability through social media. In this way new media support economic growth, education, health and political participation, although the digital divide still limits these benefits.

  28. Discuss how developed countries influence underdeveloped countries.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    Developed countries influence underdeveloped countries in several connected ways. Economically, through trade, aid, loans and multinational corporations, they control markets, set prices of raw materials and finished goods, and shape local economic policies through institutions like the IMF and World Bank. Politically, they support friendly governments and use diplomacy and aid conditions to protect their interests. Culturally and communicatively, their news agencies, films, television programmes and now digital platforms dominate the global flow of information and entertainment, spreading their languages, lifestyles and consumer values - the process Schiller called cultural imperialism and McLuhan's critics linked to media imperialism. Technologically, developing countries depend on the developed world for hardware, software and expertise, which deepens dependence. Together these influences keep the underdeveloped countries in a periphery position in the world system, as explained by Dependency Theory and Galtung's structural imperialism.

  29. Explain Rostow's evolutionary ladder of development regarding the modernization of a society.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Walt W. Rostow presented modernization as an evolutionary ladder through which every society passes in stages, set out in his book 'The Stages of Economic Growth' (1960). The stages are: (1) the traditional society, based on agriculture and limited technology; (2) the preconditions for take-off, in which education, banking, transport and new elites begin to develop; (3) the take-off, in which investment rises, industries grow rapidly and growth becomes self-sustaining; (4) the drive to maturity, in which technology spreads to all sectors and the economy diversifies; and (5) the age of high mass consumption, in which production shifts to consumer goods and services and living standards rise. In this model, mass media help societies climb the ladder by spreading new ideas, skills and aspirations from the modern sectors and from the West to traditional populations.

  30. How did the 'Arab Spring' begin?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The Arab Spring began in Tunisia in December 2010, when a young street vendor, Mohamed Bouazizi, set himself on fire in protest against police corruption, humiliation and unemployment. His act triggered mass protests against the government of President Zine El Abidine Ben Ali, which were filmed and spread rapidly through satellite television, especially Al-Jazeera, and through social media such as Facebook and Twitter. Within weeks Ben Ali was forced to flee (January 2011). The protests then spread across the Arab world - to Egypt, Libya, Yemen, Syria and Bahrain - as people demanded democracy, dignity, jobs and an end to corruption and authoritarian rule. It is considered a landmark case of new media enabling citizens to organize, bypass state-controlled media and attract global attention.

  31. What do you know about the 'bourgeois public sphere'? Discuss.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The bourgeois public sphere is a concept of Jurgen Habermas. It emerged in the expanding capitalist society of 18th-century Britain (and then France and Germany), when private individuals - the educated, property-owning bourgeoisie - came together as a public in coffee houses, salons, clubs and through newspapers and journals to discuss matters of common concern and to criticize state authority rationally. Its main conditions were that all citizens had access, that participants met as equals, and that debate was governed by reason rather than status or force. Out of these debates, public opinion was formed, which began to check the power of monarchs and governments. Habermas argued that this sphere later declined as media became commercialized and dominated by big business and public relations, turning citizens from participants in debate into passive consumers.

  32. Discuss when the popular music industry got started in the world.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The popular music industry began with the invention of sound recording in the late 19th century - Edison's phonograph (1877) and Berliner's gramophone record - which for the first time allowed music to be manufactured, sold and replayed as a commercial product. In the early 20th century, sheet-music publishing (Tin Pan Alley in New York), record companies and then commercial radio in the 1920s turned popular songs into mass-produced commodities distributed internationally. The industry expanded with film songs, vinyl records, cassettes, CDs and music television, and today it operates through global digital platforms. From the beginning it has been one of the clearest examples of the globalization of media content, with Anglo-American popular music dominating markets worldwide.

  33. In what areas do you think laws are required to be promulgated with regard to cyberspace in Pakistan?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Cyberspace in Pakistan requires clear laws in several areas. First, cybercrime: hacking, identity theft, online fraud, financial scams and unauthorized access to data must be defined and punished, which the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act (PECA) 2016 began to address. Second, online defamation and harassment, especially harassment of women and misuse of personal images, need effective and quick remedies. Third, data protection and privacy: Pakistan still lacks a comprehensive law protecting citizens' personal data held by companies and state institutions. Fourth, intellectual property: online piracy of films, music, books and software needs enforcement suited to the digital environment. Fifth, hate speech, extremist content and fake news require regulation that protects national security without destroying the freedom of expression guaranteed by Article 19. Finally, e-commerce, digital payments and consumer protection online need legal recognition so that citizens and businesses can trust digital transactions.
