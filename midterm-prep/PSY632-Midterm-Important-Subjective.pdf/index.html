  1. What is the purpose of summarizing skill in counseling?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Summarizing is a listening skill in which the counselor pulls together, in a few clear statements, the main points a client has expressed over a longer part of the session. Its purpose is to help the client see the overall picture of what he or she has said, to organize scattered thoughts and feelings into a coherent whole, and to check that the counselor has understood the client correctly. It also helps in moving the session forward, closing one theme and opening another, and it gives the client a sense of being heard and understood.

  2. What is meant by synchrony in counseling?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Synchrony refers to the harmonious coordination between the counselor's and the client's verbal and nonverbal behavior during the session. When the counselor's body posture, movements, tone of voice and pace of speech match and move in rhythm with the client's, a comfortable rapport is established. Synchrony shows that the counselor is fully attending to the client; its absence, such as mismatched posture or hurried speech, can disturb the client and weaken the counseling relationship.

  3. Briefly describe informed consent in counseling.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Informed consent means that before counseling begins, the client is given clear information about the counseling process and agrees to participate on the basis of that information. The counselor explains the nature and goals of counseling, the counselor's qualifications, the techniques that may be used, the limits of confidentiality, session arrangements and fees. Only after understanding these points does the client voluntarily consent, which protects the client's rights, autonomy and trust in the professional relationship.

  4. How does a counselor realize that he/she has reached the stage of burnout?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A counselor realizes that he or she has reached burnout through a set of emotional, physical and behavioral signs. Emotionally there is exhaustion, loss of interest and enthusiasm for clients, feelings of frustration, helplessness and being drained after sessions. Physically there may be constant tiredness, sleep problems, headaches and frequent illness. Behaviorally the counselor may start avoiding clients, cancelling sessions, showing cynicism or detachment, and a noticeable drop in the quality of attention and empathy. When work that was once satisfying begins to feel like a burden and these signs persist, the counselor has reached the stage of burnout and needs rest, supervision and self-care.

  5. What is meant by 'challenging to speak for self' in counseling? Explain with an example.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Speaking for self is an influencing skill in which a person expresses his or her own feelings, needs and views directly in the first person ('I feel...', 'I need...') instead of hiding behind general statements or blaming others. Challenging the client to speak for self means the counselor gently confronts the client's indirect way of talking and encourages direct, honest self-expression, which makes communication clear and takes responsibility for one's own feelings. For example, a client says, 'People make me angry all the time.' The counselor challenges: 'Can you say that speaking for yourself?' and the client reformulates it as, 'I feel angry when my colleagues ignore my suggestions,' which is a direct statement spoken for the self.

  6. Define counseling with reference to Krumboltz (1965) and Burks & Stefflre (1979).
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Krumboltz (1965) defined counseling as an effort to help the client engage in those types of behavior which will lead to a resolution of the client's problems. Burks and Stefflre (1979) defined it as a professional relationship between a trained counselor and a client, usually person-to-person, designed to help clients understand and clarify their views of their lifespace and learn to reach self-determined goals through meaningful, well-informed choices and resolution of problems. Both definitions emphasize helping clients make choices and solve problems. Counseling also helps people recognize their potential and use it effectively.

  7. What is a helping relationship according to Rogers (1961), and what are its main characteristics?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Rogers (1961) defined a helping relationship as one in which at least one of the parties has the intent of promoting the growth, development, maturity, improved functioning and improved coping with life of the other. Its characteristics include affectiveness, meaning it is more affective than cognitive, and intensity, where counselor and client share openly their perceptions and reactions. It is also characterized by growth, privacy and confidentiality of disclosures, support for taking risks and changing behavior, and honesty through open and direct communication.

  8. Distinguish between guidance and counseling.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Guidance is an umbrella term that includes a constellation of services aimed at personal and career development and school adjustment, and it also describes the overall school or college program. Guidance is a relationship between unequals and focuses on helping individuals choose what they value most. Counseling, in contrast, is a special type of helping process in which someone who has a problem receives personal assistance, usually through private discussion. Counseling focuses on helping individuals make changes rather than only making choices.

  9. Explain Blocher's (1966) distinctions between counseling and psychotherapy.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Blocher (1966) pointed out that the goals of counseling are ordinarily developmental-educative-preventive, while the goals of psychotherapy are generally remediative-adjustive-therapeutic. Counseling clients are not considered mentally ill but are viewed as capable of choosing goals, making decisions and assuming responsibility for their behavior. Counseling focuses on the present and future, the person is a client not a patient, and the counselor is a teacher and partner rather than an authority figure. The counselor also focuses on changing behavior, not just creating insight, and does not hide his or her own values although they are not imposed on clients.

  10. Describe other major differences between counseling and psychotherapy in goals, process, setting and duration.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    In psychotherapy the goal is to change personality and involve a quite complete change of basic character structure, while counseling helps people utilize existing resources and deal with developmental tasks appropriate to their age. Psychotherapy emphasizes the past, whereas counseling emphasizes the present, and psychotherapy aims at treatment while counseling aims at growth. Psychotherapy usually involves 20 to 40 sessions over six months to two years, while counseling is limited to about 6 to 12 sessions and usually less than six months. Psychotherapists are more apt to work in hospitals or private practice, while counselors are more apt to work in educational settings.

  11. Why are ethical codes needed in counseling, and what unethical behaviors do they guard against?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Ethical codes are suggested standards of conduct based on a consensus value set, formalized to professionalize and protect the association, control internal disagreement, protect practitioners from the public and protect clients from incompetent counselors, especially in malpractice issues. Counseling is not a value-free activity, so counselors must act with full recognition of the client's rights. Unethical behavior includes violation of confidentiality, exceeding one's level of professional competence, imposing one's values on a client, creating dependency, dual relationships, questionable financial arrangements such as excessive fees, and improper advertising. Clients can also use codes to evaluate questionable treatment.

  12. Explain confidentiality in counseling, its principles and its limitations.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Confidentiality is a central concept in the client-helper relationship and Schneiders (1963) termed information revealed in counseling an 'entrusted secret', noting that the obligation is relative rather than absolute and depends on the nature of the material. It should be discussed with clients from the onset of the relationship, and professionals should inform clients of its limits before counseling begins. Its limitations include that suspected child abuse must be reported and threats to harm others must be reported. Under the duty to warn, as in the Tarasoff case (1969), a counselor must warn when a client's conduct may be harmful to self or another.

  13. What are dual relationships in counseling and why are they considered unethical? How should counselors handle them?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A dual relationship refers to a professional assuming two or more roles simultaneously or sequentially with the person seeking help, such as friendship and business deals outside therapy. Because counselor and client are unequal in power and status, judgment is likely to be impaired and exploitation may occur, and conflict of interest can arise even in apparently harmless relations. For this reason sexual or business relationships with clients outside the therapeutic relationship are unethical. Herlihy and Corey suggest counselors secure informed consent, seek consultation, document and monitor the practice, and obtain supervision when dealing with such issues.

  14. Describe empathy, positive regard and genuineness as personal characteristics of an effective counselor.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The person-centered school of Rogers (1957) identifies empathy, positive regard and genuineness as necessary and sufficient conditions for therapeutic change. Empathy is seeing the world through another's eyes and experiencing the client's internal world through his or her frame of reference, while remaining separate from the client and different from sympathy. Positive regard is being able to recognize values and strengths in clients even when they hold widely different attitudes, and can be facilitated through respect, encouragement and strength assessment. Genuineness or congruence is consistency in values, attitudes and behaviors, where the counselor is authentic and real rather than presenting a facade.

  15. What did Combs et al. (1969) and Meier and Davis (2001) find about effective counselors?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Combs and his colleagues (1969) concluded that personal beliefs and traits differentiated effective from ineffective helpers, and that the major technique was the 'self as instrument'. Effective helpers perceive others as able rather than unable to solve their own problems, and as dependable, friendly, worthy, able to cope and self-disclosing, maintaining a positive view of human nature. Meier and Davis (2001) stated that effective helpers become aware of their personal issues, are open to supervision, avoid hiding behind the use of too many tests, and consult when presented with an ethical dilemma. Rogers also maintained that the counselor's attitude was far more important than theory and methods alone.

  16. Discuss beneficial and harmful motives for entering the counseling profession.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Beneficial motives include humanism, a commitment to create a better world by empowering others, people orientation associated with Holland's social personality type, intellectual curiosity about human behavior, worked-through emotional pain that gives extra sensitivity, and commitment to competence. Harmful motives include unresolved emotional pain, where the counselor seeks help rather than provides it, do-gooding, where counselors take charge and infantilize clients while seeking approval, and seeking intimacy through psychological closeness difficult to obtain otherwise. True believerism is also harmful because it implies lack of openness to conflicting evidence and to the specific needs of individual clients.

  17. List and briefly explain the main listening and understanding skills identified in the handouts.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The main listening and understanding skills are possessing an attitude of respect and acceptance, tuning into the client's internal viewpoint (empathy), using opening remarks and small rewards, asking open questions, paraphrasing, reflection of feeling, and summarization. Respect and acceptance mean suspending judgment about the client's goodness or badness and respecting the client as a separate person with rights to their own thoughts and feelings. Tuning into the internal viewpoint means responding from inside the client's frame of reference rather than from the counselor's external viewpoint. Together these skills show the client accurate understanding and encourage disclosure.

  18. Explain the role of nonverbal communication in counseling with reference to space, timing and touch.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Nonverbal communication regulates the counseling relationship and communicates caring, empathy or boundaries without words. In the use of space, distance and position matter: counselors should observe how much personal space a client needs and accommodate it as much as possible. Timing covers starting and ending on time and the timing of disclosures; for example, holding fast to the therapeutic hour communicates good personal boundaries, while a disclosure just before the session ends may signal ambivalence. Touch is powerful and can communicate caring and empathy, but it can be misunderstood, so beginning clinicians should refrain from touching, especially early on, or first ask the client if touch is acceptable.

  19. What is congruence? Explain with examples how a counselor can maintain congruence.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Congruence refers to the counselor's efforts to keep personal verbal and nonverbal expressions in line with each other, and it is critical to good therapeutic rapport. It is maintained by communicating verbal and nonverbal messages consistently for the intended communication. For example, to convey comfort the counselor uses a calm, soothing voice, relaxed posture and open facial expression while saying 'Please make yourself comfortable.' To express empathy the counselor nods, smiles, uses inviting gestures and open body posture while saying 'What a frightening experience.' To express understanding, matched kinesics and paralinguistics accompany the words 'Yes, I do understand.'

  20. Describe the Basic Listening Sequence (BLS) and its components.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The Basic Listening Sequence is the integrated sequence of listening microskills used to draw out the client's story and build understanding. Its components, in order, are open questions, closed questions, encouragement, paraphrasing, reflection of feeling, and summarization. Open and closed questions elicit information, encouragers and small rewards keep the client talking, paraphrasing checks understanding of content, reflection of feeling responds to the client's emotions, and summarization pulls the material together. Although the microskills of listening had been part of counseling for over 30 years, the BLS was not presented as an integrated sequence until the 1980s.

  21. Outline the stages of the counseling process, including the five-stage structure of the interview.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The counseling process develops in definable stages: first building a relationship, then a middle working phase of understanding and action, and finally termination. The five-stage structure of the interview is: (1) rapport and structure, (2) gathering data and identifying assets, (3) determining outcomes or goal setting, (4) generating alternative solutions, and (5) generalizing and transferring learning to daily life. In the first stage two struggles occur — the battle for structure, which the counselor should win, and the battle for initiative, which the client should win. In the middle phase the counselor uses skills such as accurate empathy, immediacy, self-disclosure and confrontation to help the client change perceptions and act.

  22. How should a counselor handle initial resistances from a client?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The counselor should first use non-judgmental listening and good active listening to convey understanding and build the trust needed to lower resistances. The counselor should communicate acceptance of the client's unwillingness to change rather than justifying themselves or entering a competitive contest. Joining with the client — for example, reflecting a young client's anger that his parents are the ones with problems — shows understanding of the client's internal viewpoint and may diminish resistance. The counselor can also discuss the client's reluctance and fears openly, invite cooperation by presenting counseling as a partnership, enlist the client's self-interest, and reward silent clients for talking, while remaining sensitive, flexible and tactful about pace.

  23. Discuss termination of the counseling relationship: its functions, timing and guidelines.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Termination is the decision, one-sided or mutual, to stop counseling, and it is the end of the professional relationship when the session goals have been met. A formal termination serves three functions: it signals that something is finished, it helps maintain changes already achieved and generalize problem-solving skills, and it reminds the client that they have matured and can face life's challenges independently. It is wise to spend the final 3–4 weeks discussing termination in a relationship lasting more than 3 months, and about one-sixth of the total time should focus on termination. Sessions themselves should start and end on time, leave about five minutes for a summary, and may include homework and setting the next appointment.

  24. Differentiate between paraphrasing and reflection of feelings.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Both paraphrasing and reflecting feelings are concerned with the meaning of the client's words, but they differ in focus. Paraphrasing is simple mirroring of verbal content: it rewords what the client said to check the counselor's understanding, but it excludes the client's voice and body messages. Reflecting feelings is concerned with feelings and emotions; because feelings are bodily sensations with word labels, the counselor must also pick up voice and body messages that may qualify or negate the verbal message. For example, a client who says 'I'm OK' softly with tearful eyes needs a reflection that responds to this 'music' and not just to the words. Reflecting feelings usually involves paraphrasing, but paraphrasing alone has distinct limitations.

  25. Explain empathy in counseling, distinguishing primary and advanced empathy.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Empathy is tuning into the client's internal viewpoint: Rogers (1961) defined it as entering the client's phenomenological world and experiencing it as if it were your own, without ever losing the 'as if' quality. It involves two skills — perceiving and understanding what is taking place emotionally, and communicating that understanding to the client so the client feels understood, as Martin (1983) emphasized regarding the intended emotional message. Primary empathy responds in a way that shows the counselor has understood the client's major themes. Advanced empathy goes one step further by exploring themes, issues, meanings and emotions below the surface of what the client is sharing.

  26. Describe the main influencing skills and explain how they differ from listening skills.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Influencing skills are change strategies that go beyond a strict listening approach to guide clients in changing their stories, thoughts and feelings. They include interpretation/reframing, which offers an alternative frame of reference; directive, which tells the client what action to take; advice/information; self-disclosure, in which the counselor shares personal experience or present reactions; feedback, which gives specific data on how the client is seen by others; logical consequences, which explain the if/then outcomes of behavior; and the influencing summary. Whereas listening skills such as paraphrasing and reflection mainly draw out and confirm the client's own meanings, influencing skills actively introduce the counselor's frame, suggestions or data to promote change.

  27. What is confrontation (challenging) in counseling, and how should a counselor challenge safely?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    In counseling, confrontation is not a hostile or aggressive act; it is a gentle process of pointing out discrepancies between or among a client's attitudes, thoughts or behaviors, and, following Egan, an invitation to examine self-defeating behavior and change it. Counselors may challenge mixed messages, distortions of reality, failure to speak for oneself with 'I' messages, and failure to acknowledge choice. To challenge safely, the counselor should first reflect and show understanding, help clients challenge themselves where possible, use only the minimum amount of 'muscle' required, and avoid threatening voice and body messages. The counselor should not talk down to the client, should not overdo challenging, and should leave the ultimate responsibility for accepting the challenge with the client.

  28. Explain the concepts of structure and initiative as factors influencing the counseling process.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Structure is the joint understanding between counselor and client regarding the characteristics, conditions, procedures and parameters of counseling, including time limits, fees, goals and recording permission. It clarifies the relationship, protects the rights, roles and obligations of both parties, and is especially important at the beginning and when a client has unrealistic expectations. Initiative is the motivation the client brings to the session and concerns motivation for change and client responsibility. In the first stage, the counselor should win the battle for structure while the client should win the battle for initiative, which the counselor supports through acceptance, understanding, persuasion and, where needed, confrontation.

  29. Define resistance and describe Otani's four categories of resistant behavior.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Resistance is unwillingness to change: resistant clients cling to the certainty of the present and do not want to face the pain that change demands, and resistance can appear at any stage of counseling. Reluctance is commonly seen in clients referred by a third party, such as school children or court-referred clients, who are unmotivated. Otani (1988) grouped 22 forms of resistance into four broad categories: amount of verbalization, such as silence and minimal talk; content of the message, such as intellectual talk, emotional display or symptom preoccupation; style of communication, such as false promising, thought editing, last-minute disclosure and externalization; and attitude toward the counselor and session, such as poor appointment keeping, payment delays and favor asking.

  30. What criteria did Krumboltz (1966) give for judging counseling goals, and who should decide the goals?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Krumboltz (1966) identified that effective counseling goals should be capable of being stated differently for each client, should be compatible with the values of the counselor, and the degree of attainment of the goals should be observable. The handouts stress that the ultimate decision about the goals must rest with the client and counselor as a team. This team approach respects the client's responsibility for improving himself or herself. The counselor acts as a facilitator who provides a safe and comfortable environment in which the client can find solutions.

  31. What are the major goals of counseling? Briefly explain any three.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The major goals of counseling include facilitating behavior change, enhancing coping skills, promoting decision making, improving relationships and facilitating the client's potential. Facilitating behavior change means enabling the client to live a more productive and satisfying life, as Rogers (1961) saw behavior change as a necessary result of counseling. Enhancing coping skills helps individuals cope effectively when developmental tasks are incompletely achieved or new role demands create overload and anxiety. Promoting decision making means the counselor provides information and clarifies characteristics and emotions but does not make decisions for the client, who learns to estimate consequences in terms of sacrifice, time, energy, money and risk.
