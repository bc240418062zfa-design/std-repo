  1. What is communication? Why is business communication important?
    Topic: Communication & Audience

    Communication is a process of sharing ideas, thoughts, feelings and information between a sender and a receiver through a suitable medium. It involves encoding a message, transmitting it through a channel and decoding it by the receiver, followed by feedback. Business communication is important because organizations run on the exchange of information: managers plan, organize and control through communication, employees coordinate their work, and the organization deals with customers, suppliers and other stakeholders. Effective business communication saves time, avoids misunderstandings, builds goodwill and improves productivity.

  2. What is effective communication?
    Topic: Communication & Audience

    Communication is effective when it achieves the desired goals and objectives from the receiver, i.e. the receiver understands the message exactly as the sender intended and responds in the desired way. An effective message is purposeful, audience-centered, clear, concise and complete, and it produces feedback that confirms understanding.

  3. What do you know about 'Phantom Readers'?
    Topic: Communication & Audience

    In some situations the most important readers may be hidden from you. Written communications addressed to one person are often read and used by others as well. These real but unnamed readers are called phantom readers. A writer should keep phantom readers in mind, because a document may be forwarded, filed or read later by people who were not its original audience.

  4. Into which four main categories can an audience be divided? Describe briefly.
    Topic: Communication & Audience

    An audience can be divided into four main categories: 1. Experts — they possess extensive knowledge of the subject. General experts possess extensive knowledge about a field in general, while specific experts share or surpass your knowledge of the document's subject matter. 2. Technicians — they have highly technical knowledge and build, operate or maintain things; they need brief introductions and clear explanations of unfamiliar terms. 3. Executives / Managers — they use documents to make decisions and evaluate projects and employees. 4. Laypersons — they have the least technical knowledge, so everything must be explained in simple, familiar language.

  5. How can you target your audience while planning a document?
    Topic: Communication & Audience

    You can target your audience by: • Identifying your audience type (experts, technicians, executives or laypersons). • Assessing the level of expertise your audience has. • Knowing the audience's purpose in reading the document. • Considering the audience's attitude towards you and towards the content of the document. Audience analysis is vital in a professional environment because the needs of the audience can be assessed only after identifying who the audience is.

  6. Which three roles does an audience play in an organization?
    Topic: Communication & Audience

    An audience plays three main roles: 1. Decision makers — they take the decisions in an organization; their role determines the action or reaction of the organization in a particular situation. 2. Advisors — they provide information and advice to the decision makers. 3. Implementers — they carry out the decisions that have been taken by the executives of an organization.

  7. What are the Seven Cs of effective communication? Explain briefly.
    Topic: The Seven Cs & Message Quality

    The Seven Cs are the principles that make business communication easy and effective: 1. Completeness — the message contains all the facts the reader needs. 2. Conciseness — the message is brief and to the point, without unnecessary words. 3. Consideration — the message is prepared with the receiver in mind (you-attitude). 4. Concreteness — the message is specific, definite and vivid rather than vague and general. 5. Clarity — the message is easy to understand; meaning is got across exactly as intended. 6. Courtesy — the message shows respect and concern for the reader; it is not merely mechanical politeness. 7. Correctness — the message uses the right level of language and accurate facts, figures and words.

  8. What is meant by 'Concreteness'? Explain with the help of examples.
    Topic: The Seven Cs & Message Quality

    Communicating concretely means being specific, definite and vivid rather than vague and general. It often means using denotative (direct) words, specific figures and vivid, image-building words. Examples: • Vague: 'He is a very rich man.' Concrete: 'He earns Rs. 200,000 a month.' • Vague: 'Please send the goods soon.' Concrete: 'Please send the goods by 15 June.' Concreteness makes the message clear, convincing and free from misinterpretation.

  9. Which characteristics ensure 'Completeness' of a document?
    Topic: The Seven Cs & Message Quality

    Your message is complete when it contains all the facts that readers or listeners need for the reaction you desire. To ensure completeness: • Provide all necessary information. • Answer all questions asked. • Give extra information, when desirable. • Assess the message from the receiver's eyes to be sure everything needed is included. A complete message eliminates the need for additional clarity and follow-up communication.

  10. What is coherence and why is it important?
    Topic: The Seven Cs & Message Quality

    In a coherent sentence the words are arranged so that the ideas clearly express the intended meaning. The correct modifier is placed as close as possible to the word it is supposed to modify. Example — Unclear: 'Being an excellent lawyer, I am sure that you can surely help us.' Clear: 'Being an excellent lawyer, you can surely help us.' Coherence is important because incoherent writing confuses the reader, changes the meaning and destroys the effectiveness of the message. The paragraph is one of the most powerful instruments of coherence.

  11. Describe briefly structural, stylistic and contextual clarity.
    Topic: The Seven Cs & Message Quality

    Structural clarity: At the level of the whole document, structural clarity makes it easy for the reader to get the large picture. Use abstracts and other forecasting strategies such as introductions that tell readers what is coming. Stylistic clarity: It is promoted by simple, direct language and careful word choice; it is achieved through a balance between precise language and familiar language. Contextual clarity: It means the message fits its situation and background, so the reader can relate every part of the document to its proper context.

  12. What are Functional Words and which function do they perform?
    Topic: The Seven Cs & Message Quality

    Functional words (structure words) include: • Conjunctions • Prepositions • Articles • Pronouns Functional words express relationships among content words. Content words (nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs) carry the real meaning and have both a denotative meaning and a connotative meaning, while functional words join and relate them in a sentence.

  13. Differentiate between denotative and connotative meanings of words.
    Topic: The Seven Cs & Message Quality

    Denotative meaning is the literal, dictionary meaning of a word — the direct meaning that points to a specific object or idea. For example, the denotative meaning of 'home' is a place where one lives. Connotative meaning refers to the associations and feelings that are connected to a certain word beyond its literal meaning. For example, 'home' connotes warmth, comfort, family and security. Writers should prefer denotative words where exactness is needed, because connotations differ from person to person and can cause misunderstanding.

  14. What are the characteristics of a well-organized message?
    Topic: The Seven Cs & Message Quality

    A well-organized message has the following characteristics: • The subject and purpose are clear. • All information is related to the subject and purpose. • The ideas are grouped and presented in a logical way. • All necessary information is included. Well-organized messages are efficient: they contain only relevant information, so the audience does not waste time with superfluous information, and they motivate the audience to accept the message.

  15. What are the main steps of the writing process for business messages?
    Topic: Planning, Composing & Revising

    The writing process in business messages consists of three main activities: 1. Planning — analyze the audience, define your objectives/purpose, and gather and organize the information. First group the ideas and then put them in sequence. 2. Composing — draft the message, putting the organized ideas into words, sentences and paragraphs with a suitable tone and style. 3. Revising — revise, edit and proofread the draft for clarity, conciseness, correctness and courtesy. Proofreading is the last thing you do before you get ready to distribute your document.

  16. Define the term 'Transitions'.
    Topic: Planning, Composing & Revising

    A transition is a word, phrase, sentence, or series of sentences connecting one part of a discourse to another. Such phrases as 'to continue the analysis', 'on the other hand' and 'in addition' are transitions. They are structural clues that guide the reader from one idea to the next and keep the writing coherent. Transitional words also show comparison and contrast (e.g. 'conversely'), addition, result and sequence.

  17. What is a business letter? Enlist at least three types of business letters.
    Topic: Letters & Memos

    A business letter is a formal written message sent to individuals outside an organization (letters are used to communicate outside your organization). Business letters are commonly either full-block formatted, with every line starting at the left margin, or modified-block formatted, with the heading and the closing aligned at the center of the page. Types of business letters include: • Job application letters • Acceptance letters • Transmittal letters • Inquiry letters • Technical-information letters • Letters of recommendation • Claim (complaint) letters and adjustment letters

  18. Enlist the parts of a business letter.
    Topic: Letters & Memos

    The main parts of a business letter are: 1. Letterhead — the preprinted stationery with the organization's name, logo and address. 2. Date line 3. Inside address — the name and address of the receiver. 4. Salutation — e.g. 'Dear Mr. Ahmad:' 5. Body — the message itself, organized in paragraphs. 6. Complimentary close — e.g. 'Yours sincerely,' or 'Yours faithfully,' 7. Signature and the writer's name/designation. Optional parts include the attention line, subject line, enclosure notation, copy notation, reference initials and the postscript (P.S.). A letter usually consists of three sections: front matter, body and end matter.

  19. What do you know about 'Adjustment Requests' (adjustment letters)?
    Topic: Letters & Memos

    An adjustment letter is the reply to a claim (complaint) letter. A customer is entitled to request an adjustment whenever he receives a product or experiences a service that does not live up to the seller's standards. In an adjustment letter the organization grants or refuses the adjustment. A good adjustment letter: • Begins with the good news if the claim is granted. • Explains what went wrong and what is being done. • Regains the customer's confidence and goodwill. • Avoids blaming the customer or the company's own staff.

  20. What are Claims and Adjustments?
    Topic: Letters & Memos

    A claim letter (also called a complaint letter) is written by a customer who is dissatisfied with a product or service and requests compensation, repair, replacement or refund. While writing a claim letter, use a direct request and a professional tone, give specific details, and avoid a complaining tone. An adjustment is the settlement offered by the organization in response to the claim, and the letter conveying it is the adjustment letter. Fair claims and prompt, courteous adjustments protect the goodwill of the organization.

  21. What is the difference between a cover letter and a resume?
    Topic: Letters & Memos

    A cover letter accompanies a larger item, usually a document (or a resume). It provides the recipient with a specific context in which to place the larger document and simultaneously gives the sender a permanent record of having sent the material. It is written for a specific job and reader. A resume is a structured summary of an applicant's education, experience, skills and achievements. Resume objectives require you to emphasize the points that are major evidence that you are qualified for the job you seek. Most resumes are organized around the applicant's experience.

  22. What is a memorandum? How does it differ from a letter?
    Topic: Letters & Memos

    A memorandum (memo) is a brief, informal report used for communication within an organization; it is the primary vehicle for communication inside an organization. A memo does not use a salutation or a complimentary close; it has headings: To, From, Date and Subject. If a memo is typed by a person other than the writer, reference initials are used. A letter, on the other hand, is used when the document is sent to outside individuals. It is more formal, uses salutations, and follows the full-block or modified-block format.

  23. What is a goodwill message? To whom are goodwill letters usually written?
    Topic: Letters & Memos

    A goodwill message is a message written to build and maintain friendly relations; it expresses thanks, congratulations, sympathy or appreciation without any direct business purpose. Goodwill letters are usually written to customers. They are generally organized by the direct approach and receive a favourable reaction, because they create a feeling in the audience that the writer has their personal welfare in mind.

  24. How many parts does the Indirect Plan consist of? Explain in detail.
    Topic: Message Approaches & Special Messages

    The indirect plan consists of four parts: 1. A buffer — a neutral, pleasant opening statement that establishes goodwill and provides a transition to the refusal. 2. Reasons supporting the negative decision — a convincing explanation before the decision is announced. 3. A clear, diplomatic statement of the negative decision — stated firmly but tactfully, preferably implied or sandwiched between positive statements. 4. A helpful, friendly, and positive close — ends on a goodwill note, possibly offering an alternative. The indirect plan presents the supporting data before the main idea and is used for bad-news and persuasive messages, especially when the audience is skeptical or hostile.

  25. What is AIDA?
    Topic: Message Approaches & Special Messages

    AIDA is a plan used in persuasive (especially sales) messages; it is the specialized version of persuasion. It has four phases: 1. Attention — attract the reader's attention with an opening that promises a benefit. 2. Interest — arouse the reader's interest by describing the product or idea and its benefits. 3. Desire — create desire by providing evidence, testimonials and convincing details. 4. Action — motivate the reader to take a specific action, such as placing an order or replying by a date.

  26. What is a bad-news message? How should it be written?
    Topic: Message Approaches & Special Messages

    A bad-news message is a message that tells the reader unpleasant news, such as a refusal of a request, claim or application. Such messages are challenging to write because the readers will not be happy to receive the news. A bad-news message: • Always begins with a neutral statement (buffer) that provides a transition to the refusal. • Gives reasons before the negative decision (indirect approach). • States the bad news clearly but diplomatically, cushioned in the middle of the message. • Closes on a helpful, friendly and positive note.

  27. If you are writing a persuasive message, what are the common ways of establishing your credibility?
    Topic: Message Approaches & Special Messages

    The common ways of establishing your credibility in a persuasive message are: • Naming your sources when you use information from others. • Demonstrating expertise — showing your knowledge and experience of the subject. • Supporting your message with factual evidence. Credibility comes from the Latin word 'credo' meaning 'I believe'; credible communicators are both expert and trustworthy. While writing persuasive messages, keep four things in mind about your audience: needs and appeals, emotion and logic, credibility, and semantics.

  28. Write down the techniques that are used to get the reader's attention or interest.
    Topic: Message Approaches & Special Messages

    In a persuasive letter, the opening section gets the reader's attention and the following section arouses interest. Techniques include: • Beginning with a question the reader will answer 'yes'. • Opening with a surprising fact, statement or statistic related to the reader's benefit. • Using a short story, incident or quotation. • Offering a gift, free sample or special benefit. • Asking a favour or paying a sincere compliment. The letter begins with such devices to catch the reader's attention and then leads up to the main point by arousing the reader's interest.

  29. Write down FIVE advantages of using the 'written medium' in business communication.
    Topic: Message Approaches & Special Messages

    The written medium is best when you do not need immediate feedback. Its advantages are: 1. You can write detailed and complex messages. 2. It creates a permanent record that can be referred to later. 3. It minimizes distortion when a message is passed from person to person. 4. It reaches a potentially unlimited audience over time and place (publication). 5. It allows the writer to plan, revise and control the message carefully, and the reader to read it at his own pace.

  30. Which format should one perceive while writing a report?
    Topic: Reports & Proposals

    Responsive format, style and organization. Before you write, decide: • Whether to use a letter, memo, or manuscript format. • Whether to group your ideas one way or another. • Whether to employ a formal or an informal style. When making decisions about the format, style and organization of a report, consider its origin, subject, timing, distribution, purpose and probable reception. Short reports within an organization usually use the memo format, while longer, formal reports use the manuscript format.

  31. What is a formal document?
    Topic: Reports & Proposals

    A document that is written in formal style and uses formal language is called a formal document, such as circulars, business letters and CVs. Formal reports produced by an organization usually include research work, collected ideas and visual aids, and they follow the organization's prescribed format and style.

  32. Explain the term 'Abstract'.
    Topic: Reports & Proposals

    An abstract is a condensation of an entire report, focusing on the main issues: what was done, what was found out, and its significance. Abstracts are self-sufficient. The procedure in many companies is to take the abstract from the analysis report, copy it a number of times, circulate it to readers, and allow readers to order the full report if they feel they need the information. An abstract also promotes structural clarity by giving the reader the large picture in advance.

  33. Which are the principal meeting documents? What do you know about task-facilitating roles in a meeting?
    Topic: Reports & Proposals

    The principal meeting documents are the agenda and the minutes. Formal minutes are often required by federal, state or local law, by-laws, charters or regulations. Task-facilitating roles, as the name shows, make the meeting process well planned and smooth. They include: • Initiating — getting the group started on a line of inquiry. • Information giving or seeking — offering (or seeking) information relevant to questions facing the group. • Coordinating — showing relationships among ideas, clarifying issues, summarizing what the group has done. • Procedure setting — suggesting decision-making procedures that will move the group toward the goal.

  34. What is oral communication? Discuss the methods of delivering an oral presentation.
    Topic: Oral Communication & Presentations

    Oral communication is communication through the spoken word. It is conversational in nature and brings back immediate results (feedback). Oral presentations in a professional environment generally fall into two categories: informative speaking (whose primary goal is audience learning) and persuasive speaking (used to influence what an audience thinks or does). There are four methods of making an oral presentation: 1. Extempore (extemporaneous) — a carefully prepared and researched speech delivered in a conversational style from key words; it is the most recommended method. 2. Impromptu — a speech delivered without preparation, e.g. when asked to speak at the last moment. 3. Memorization — the speech is learnt by heart and delivered from memory. 4. Reading (manuscript) — the speech is read from a written text, used when exact timing and wording are a must.

  35. How can visual aids be helpful in an oral presentation?
    Topic: Oral Communication & Presentations

    Visual aids help in delivering speeches and accommodating the audience's questions, and they help audiences in understanding the purpose and content of the presentation. They significantly improve the interest of a presentation, make complex data simple, reinforce the spoken words, and help the audience remember the key points. However, the visual aid must support the message — a prompt sheet with lengthy details, for example, can make a presentation ineffective, while a cue card with key words is effective.

  36. What is the goal of Content Listening?
    Topic: Oral Communication & Presentations

    The goal of content listening is to understand and retain the information that is delivered by a speaker. Moreover, your job as a listener is to identify the key points of the message. Content listening does not require the listener to evaluate or criticize the message; the focus is on comprehension and retention of facts and ideas.

  37. How can a presentation be made effective?
    Topic: Oral Communication & Presentations

    Effective oral communication is a combination of many skills: • Outlining and planning the presentation with clear objectives in mind. • Analyzing the audience — a good presenter knows the age, education and interests of his audience. • Preparing overheads or other display media. • Rehearsing — practice standing and speaking aloud, and check the timing of the speech. • Effective delivery — poise and enthusiasm, eye contact with the audience (which shows the confidence of the speaker), effective use of voice, and attention to personal appearance and grooming, because the presenter's appearance affects his credibility.
