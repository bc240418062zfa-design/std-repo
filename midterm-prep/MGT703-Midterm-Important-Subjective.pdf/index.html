  1. What is a mission statement? Discuss its nature and the components that a good mission statement should include.
    Topic: Questions from Actual Midterm Papers (Solved)

    A mission statement is an enduring statement of purpose that distinguishes one business from other similar firms. It is a declaration of an organization's 'reason for being' and answers the basic question: 'What is our business?' A good mission statement reveals what an organization wants to be and whom it wants to serve, and it is broad enough in scope to allow the generation and consideration of a range of alternative objectives and strategies. According to Fred David, a mission statement can include nine components: (1) Customers — who are the firm's customers? (2) Products or services — what are the firm's major products or services? (3) Markets — geographically, where does the firm compete? (4) Technology — is the firm technologically current? (5) Concern for survival, growth, and profitability — is the firm committed to growth and financial soundness? (6) Philosophy — what are the basic beliefs, values, aspirations, and ethical priorities of the firm? (7) Self-concept — what is the firm's distinctive competence or major competitive advantage? (8) Concern for public image — is the firm responsive to social, community, and environmental concerns? (9) Concern for employees — are employees a valuable asset of the firm?

  2. Discuss the benefits of horizontal diversification.
    Topic: Questions from Actual Midterm Papers (Solved)

    Horizontal diversification refers to adding new, unrelated products or services for present customers. Although the new products are technologically unrelated to the firm's current products, they can appeal to the firm's present customers. Its main benefits are: (1) the firm can offer the new products through its existing, trusted distribution channels, so market access is quick and inexpensive; (2) the firm's existing customer relationships, reputation, and brand loyalty help the new products gain acceptance; (3) revenue and profit can be increased by selling more products to the same customer base without acquiring new customers; (4) marketing costs per customer fall because the same sales force and channels serve a wider product range; (5) the strategy can be especially attractive when the firm's basic industry is in decline, because growth is achieved without depending on the declining product line; and (6) it reduces dependence on a single product market. However, unlike related diversification, it offers no operational synergy between the old and new businesses, so its success depends mainly on accurate identification of what present customers will buy.

  3. How can the Internet and intranet improve the working capacity of an organization?
    Topic: Questions from Actual Midterm Papers (Solved)

    The Internet and intranet improve an organization's working capacity in several ways. (1) Faster communication: e-mail, messaging, and shared platforms allow instant communication within the organization (intranet) and with customers, suppliers, and distributors (Internet), removing delays of traditional communication. (2) Access to information: employees can instantly access company policies, databases, manuals, and records from anywhere, which speeds up decision-making. (3) Coordination and teamwork: project teams at different locations can collaborate on shared documents and schedules, so coordination across departments and branches improves. (4) Cost reduction: paperless workflows, online meetings, and electronic filing reduce administrative, printing, and travel costs. (5) Better customer service and sales: through e-commerce, customers can obtain information, place orders, and receive support around the clock, and the firm can reach new geographic markets at low cost. (6) Knowledge sharing and learning: an intranet preserves organizational knowledge and training material in one place, so new employees learn faster and expertise is not lost. (7) Improved control: managers can monitor performance, inventories, and sales in real time. In short, these technologies increase speed, accuracy, reach, and coordination, thereby raising the overall productivity and working capacity of the organization.

  4. Explain the strategic management process.
    Topic: Questions from Actual Midterm Papers (Solved)

    The strategic management process consists of three stages. (1) Strategy formulation: this stage includes developing a vision and mission, identifying an organization's external opportunities and threats, determining internal strengths and weaknesses, establishing long-term objectives, generating alternative strategies, and choosing particular strategies to pursue. Issues in strategy formulation include deciding what new businesses to enter, what businesses to abandon, whether to expand operations or diversify, and how to avoid a hostile takeover. (2) Strategy implementation: this stage requires a firm to establish annual objectives, devise policies, motivate employees, and allocate resources so that formulated strategies can be executed. It includes developing a strategy-supportive culture, creating an effective organizational structure, redirecting marketing efforts, preparing budgets, developing information systems, and linking employee compensation to organizational performance. It is often called the 'action stage' and is considered the most difficult stage. (3) Strategy evaluation: this is the final stage, in which managers determine whether particular strategies are working properly. It includes reviewing external and internal factors that are the bases for current strategies, measuring performance, and taking corrective actions. The three stages are interrelated and are carried out at all hierarchical levels of an organization; the process is dynamic and continuous rather than a one-time activity.

  5. Define the major types of strategies and explain the situations in which each type is used.
    Topic: Questions from Actual Midterm Papers (Solved)

    Fred David classifies the main alternative strategies into four groups. (1) Integration strategies: forward integration (gaining ownership or control over distributors or retailers — used when present distributors are expensive, unreliable, or incapable), backward integration (gaining ownership or control over suppliers — used when present suppliers are expensive or unreliable and few in number), and horizontal integration (gaining ownership or control over competitors — used when the firm can gain economies of scale and monopolistic characteristics without government challenge). (2) Intensive strategies: market penetration (increasing market share for present products in present markets — used when markets are not saturated), market development (introducing present products into new geographic areas — used when reliable, inexpensive distribution channels are available and the firm has excess capacity), and product development (increasing sales by improving or developing new products — used when successful products are in the maturity stage and the firm has strong R&D capability). (3) Diversification strategies: related/concentric diversification (new but related products — used in slow-growth industries), unrelated/conglomerate diversification (new, unrelated products — used when the basic industry is declining), and horizontal diversification (new, unrelated products for present customers). (4) Defensive strategies: retrenchment (cost and asset reduction to reverse declining sales — used when the firm has consistently failed to meet its objectives), divestiture (selling a division — used when a division needs more resources than the firm can provide), and liquidation (selling all assets for their tangible worth — used when retrenchment and divestiture have failed).

  6. In the external audit, PSO realised that its profit is indirectly related to OPEC export policies. What kind of external force is important in this case, and can PSO do anything to neutralise this factor?
    Topic: Questions from Actual Midterm Papers (Solved)

    OPEC export policies are part of the political, governmental, and legal forces in the external environment (they also influence economic forces such as crude oil prices, because OPEC decisions are taken by governments of oil-exporting countries and directly affect the international price of oil, which determines PSO's cost of supplies). Key external factors of this type are completely beyond the control of a single firm: PSO cannot change OPEC's production quotas or export policies, so it cannot 'neutralise' the factor in the sense of removing it. However, PSO can reduce its harmful impact through strategic responses, for example: (1) identifying and evaluating this force properly in an external audit and EFE Matrix so that strategies are built around it; (2) diversifying supply sources and maintaining strategic petroleum reserves so that a supply cut does not stop operations; (3) using long-term supply contracts and price-hedging arrangements to reduce price uncertainty; (4) improving operational efficiency and reducing costs so that margins can absorb price shocks; (5) diversifying the business into related areas such as lubricants, LPG, and retail services so that profit does not depend only on regulated fuel margins; and (6) working collectively through industry associations to present the industry's case to the government and the regulator (OGRA) for timely price adjustments. Thus the force itself cannot be controlled, but its negative effect can be managed and minimised through sound strategies.

  7. What is blue ocean strategy, and how can a firm benefit from it?
    Topic: Questions from Actual Midterm Papers (Solved)

    Blue ocean strategy, developed by W. Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne, argues that firms should create new demand in an uncontested market space — a 'blue ocean' — rather than compete head-on in overcrowded industries ('red oceans') where rivals fight over a shrinking profit pool and competition turns the water bloody. Its cornerstone is value innovation: the simultaneous pursuit of differentiation and low cost, which breaks the traditional value-cost trade-off. A firm benefits from blue ocean strategy in the following ways: (1) it creates and captures new demand instead of fighting for existing customers; (2) it makes the competition irrelevant, so the firm can enjoy a period of uncontested growth and strong profits; (3) by offering a leap in value for buyers while keeping costs low, it achieves both higher margins and higher sales volume; (4) it aligns the whole system of the firm's activities in pursuit of differentiation and low cost; and (5) it can open entirely new markets and build a strong brand before imitators arrive. Examples commonly cited include Cirque du Soleil, which created a new market space combining circus and theatre while eliminating costly elements such as animal acts.

  8. What is concentric (related) diversification? Give guidelines for its effective use.
    Topic: Questions from Actual Midterm Papers (Solved)

    Concentric diversification, also called related diversification, means adding new but related products or services. The new products have technological and/or marketing synergies with the existing product lines, even though they may appeal to new groups of customers. Guidelines for its effective use — concentric diversification can be an especially effective strategy when: (1) an organization competes in a no-growth or slow-growth industry; (2) adding new, but related, products would significantly enhance the sales of current products; (3) new, but related, products can be offered at highly competitive prices; (4) new, but related, products have seasonal sales levels that counterbalance an organization's existing peaks and valleys; (5) an organization's products are currently in the declining stage of the product life cycle; or (6) an organization has a strong management team. Because of the synergy between old and new businesses, related diversification is generally considered less risky than unrelated diversification.

  9. Differentiate among the three intensive strategies with the help of business situations. Also explain why Porter's generic strategies are oriented towards gaining competitive advantage.
    Topic: Questions from Actual Midterm Papers (Solved)

    The three intensive strategies differ in terms of products and markets. (1) Market penetration seeks to increase market share for present products in present markets through greater marketing efforts — for example, a soft-drink company increasing advertising and sales promotion in its existing cities to sell more of the same drink; it is suitable when present markets are not saturated and the usage rate of present customers can be increased. (2) Market development means introducing present products into new geographic areas — for example, the same company launching its existing drink in a new province or a foreign country; it is suitable when reliable and inexpensive distribution channels are available and the firm has excess production capacity. (3) Product development seeks increased sales by improving present products or developing new ones for present markets — for example, introducing a sugar-free or improved-flavour version of the drink for existing customers; it is suitable when successful products are in the maturity stage and the firm has strong research and development capability. Porter's generic strategies are oriented towards gaining competitive advantage because Porter argues that a firm can outperform rivals only by establishing a difference it can preserve: either delivering comparable value at a lower cost (cost leadership) or delivering greater value that justifies a premium price (differentiation), applied either industry-wide or within a narrow segment (focus). Each generic strategy is a fundamentally different approach to creating and sustaining a superior position over competitors, which is exactly what competitive advantage means.

  10. AGP Pharmaceuticals is trying to introduce e-commerce practices for reducing the bargaining power of distributors across the country. Do you think that e-commerce based forward integration would be a suitable option for this company? Justify your answer with three appropriate reasons.
    Topic: Questions from Actual Midterm Papers (Solved)

    Yes, e-commerce-based forward integration is a suitable option for AGP Pharmaceuticals. Forward integration means gaining ownership or increased control over distributors or retailers, and selling directly to pharmacies, hospitals, and ultimately customers through an online platform is a modern form of this strategy. Three reasons justify this choice. (1) Reduced bargaining power of distributors: at present, distributors can pressure AGP on margins, credit terms, and shelf priority because AGP depends on them to reach retailers. By developing its own e-commerce channel, AGP gains an alternative route to market, so the distributors' bargaining power falls and AGP's dependence on them is reduced. (2) Lower distribution cost and better margins: forward integration is especially attractive when present distributors are expensive. An online ordering system removes several layers of margin, reduces order-processing and travelling costs of sales staff, and allows AGP to retain a larger share of the final price. (3) Greater control, information, and service quality: direct online contact gives AGP real-time data about demand, inventories, and the buying behaviour of pharmacies and doctors, which improves production planning and forecasting. It also ensures proper handling information, availability of medicines in remote areas not well served by distributors, and faster response to shortages or complaints — all of which strengthen AGP's competitive position. A caution is that AGP should manage channel conflict by introducing the system gradually, so that existing distributors do not retaliate before the new channel is established.

  11. Rostek is a multinational company that provides sustainable business and energy solutions around the world. Rostek wants to start its business operations in Pakistan, but its strategists are concerned about the potential negative impact of economic forces in Pakistan. Identify at least five economic factors of Pakistan and elaborate the potential impact of each on a foreign investment like Rostek's.
    Topic: Questions from Actual Midterm Papers (Solved)

    Five economic factors of Pakistan and their potential impact on Rostek are as follows. (1) Inflation rate: Pakistan has frequently experienced high inflation, which raises the cost of raw materials, wages, utilities, and local borrowing. For Rostek, this means project costs can overrun estimates and pricing of energy solutions becomes difficult, reducing profit margins. (2) Exchange rate instability: the Pakistani rupee has depreciated sharply against the US dollar. Since Rostek would import equipment and repatriate profits in foreign currency, depreciation increases the local-currency cost of imports and reduces the dollar value of earnings, creating exchange risk. (3) Interest rates: high interest rates set to control inflation make local financing expensive, raising the cost of capital for Rostek's projects and possibly making some energy projects unviable. (4) Energy prices and shortages: Pakistan has faced high electricity and fuel prices and, in the past, load-shedding. While this raises Rostek's operating costs, it also creates strong demand for its sustainable energy solutions, so the impact is mixed. (5) GDP growth and disposable income: slow economic growth and low per-capita income limit the purchasing power of businesses and households, restricting the market size for premium sustainable solutions. In addition, factors such as the balance-of-payments position, tax rate changes, and the size of the informal economy also matter. Rostek can manage these risks through local partnerships, phased investment, hedging, and agreements with the government on tariffs and repatriation.

  12. Staffing is an activity that is performed by almost all organizations. As a management function, how can it be made more effective? Which activities come under staffing?
    Topic: Questions from Actual Midterm Papers (Solved)

    Staffing is the management function concerned with obtaining, using, and maintaining a satisfactory and satisfied workforce; it is performed by all managers, not only by the human resource department. Staffing includes the following activities: recruiting, interviewing, testing, selecting, orienting, training, developing, counselling, evaluating, rewarding, disciplining, promoting, transferring, demoting, and dismissing employees, as well as developing job descriptions and job specifications, forecasting manpower needs, and maintaining employee records. Staffing can be made more effective in the following ways: (1) link staffing to strategy — manpower planning should be based on the organization's strategies and long-term objectives, so that the right number and type of people are available when strategies are implemented; (2) use systematic selection — valid tests, structured interviews, and clear job descriptions and specifications improve the match between the person and the job; (3) invest in orientation, training, and development so that employees quickly become productive and keep their skills current; (4) appraise performance fairly and link rewards to performance, which motivates employees and reduces turnover; (5) maintain good employee relations and communication so that grievances are handled before they become disputes; and (6) use modern tools such as e-recruitment and a human resource information system for speed and accuracy. Effective staffing directly supports strategy implementation because strategies can only be executed by competent and motivated people.

  13. What should be the characteristics of a firm that is successfully pursuing a cost leadership strategy? Enlist any six of them.
    Topic: Questions from Actual Midterm Papers (Solved)

    A firm successfully pursuing cost leadership aims to become the low-cost producer in its industry while maintaining acceptable quality and features. Six characteristics of such a firm are: (1) it builds efficiently designed, large-scale production facilities to obtain economies of scale; (2) it vigorously pursues cost reductions from experience, learning, and tight control of overhead and operational costs; (3) it minimises expenses in areas such as research and development, services, sales force, and advertising; (4) it has access to low-cost raw materials and labour, and designs products that are cheap and easy to manufacture; (5) it maintains a strong, centralised cost-control and reporting system, with managers frequently evaluated on cost performance; and (6) it offers a standard, no-frills product with limited models and features, priced at or slightly below competitors' prices, while investing enough in technology and process improvement to defend its cost advantage. Such a firm can earn above-average returns even in the presence of strong competitive forces, because its low costs protect it against rivals, powerful buyers, and suppliers.

  14. Identify the major stakeholders in a firm's pricing decisions and explain why each of them is interested in pricing.
    Topic: Questions from Actual Midterm Papers (Solved)

    Pricing is one of the key functions of marketing, and David identifies five stakeholder groups that affect, and are affected by, a firm's pricing decisions. (1) Consumers: price determines the value they receive for money; they want fair and affordable prices and react quickly to price changes, so consumer acceptance limits how high a firm can price. (2) Government: the government regulates prices of essential goods and services, monitors monopolistic and unfair pricing practices, and uses taxes and duties that enter the final price, so firms must keep pricing within legal and policy limits. (3) Suppliers: suppliers watch the prices a firm charges because the firm's ability to pay for raw materials and to sustain volumes depends on its sales revenue; high input prices also force the firm to raise its own prices. (4) Distributors: distributors and retailers are interested in pricing because their margins, commissions, and sales volumes depend on the final price set by the firm. (5) Competitors: competitors closely monitor and respond to price changes, since a price cut can trigger a price war and a price increase can shift market share. A strategist must therefore set prices that keep all these stakeholders reasonably satisfied while still earning an adequate return.

  15. OGRA regulates the prices of oil and gas in Pakistan. Identify the stakeholders of the oil and gas sector and briefly explain the interest of each stakeholder.
    Topic: Questions from Actual Midterm Papers (Solved)

    The Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority (OGRA) determines and notifies the prices of petroleum products and gas in Pakistan, so pricing in this sector affects many stakeholders. (1) The government: it sets overall energy policy, collects petroleum levy and sales tax on fuels, and wants prices that balance revenue needs with public welfare. (2) OGRA (the regulator): its interest is in fair, transparent, cost-based pricing that protects consumers while allowing companies a reasonable return. (3) Oil marketing companies such as PSO, Shell, and Total PARCO: they want prices and dealer margins that cover their import, storage, and distribution costs and give them a workable profit. (4) Refineries and exploration/production companies: their revenues depend on wellhead and ex-refinery prices, so they want prices that justify continued investment. (5) Distributors and dealers (petrol pump owners): their commission is fixed per litre, so they press for higher commissions and uninterrupted supply. (6) Consumers and industry: households, transporters, and factories want low and stable prices because fuel prices affect transport fares, production costs, and inflation. (7) Suppliers/importers and international market participants such as OPEC: international crude prices and import contracts determine the cost base on which OGRA builds local prices. (8) Society at large: it is affected through inflation, environmental effects, and the availability of energy. Because these interests conflict, OGRA's pricing decisions require balancing the return of companies against the purchasing power of consumers.

  16. What is strategic management? Why is it important for organizations?
    Topic: Core Midterm Topics (Solved)

    Strategic management is the art and science of formulating, implementing, and evaluating cross-functional decisions that enable an organization to achieve its objectives. It focuses on integrating management, marketing, finance/accounting, production/operations, research and development, and information systems to achieve organizational success. It is important because it allows an organization to be more proactive than reactive in shaping its own future; it enables the firm to initiate and influence activities and thus to exert control over its own destiny. Financial benefits include improved sales, profitability, and productivity, while nonfinancial benefits include enhanced awareness of external threats, improved understanding of competitors' strategies, increased employee productivity, less resistance to change, and a clearer understanding of performance-reward relationships. Research shows that organizations using strategic-management concepts are more profitable and successful than those that do not.

  17. Differentiate between a vision statement and a mission statement.
    Topic: Core Midterm Topics (Solved)

    A vision statement answers the question 'What do we want to become?' It sets out the long-term direction and the desired future position of the organization, and it should be short — ideally a single sentence — and developed before the mission statement. A mission statement answers the question 'What is our business?' It is an enduring statement of purpose that distinguishes one firm from other similar firms, identifies the scope of the firm's operations in product and market terms, and embodies its philosophy and concerns for customers, products, markets, technology, survival and growth, self-concept, public image, and employees. In short, the vision describes the destination the firm wants to reach in the future, while the mission describes the firm's present business, customers, and values that will guide it towards that destination.

  18. What is an external audit? Describe the five categories of external forces.
    Topic: Core Midterm Topics (Solved)

    An external audit (also called environmental scanning or industry analysis) focuses on identifying and evaluating trends and events beyond the control of a single firm, such as increased foreign competition, population shifts, and information technology developments. Its purpose is to develop a finite list of opportunities that could benefit a firm and threats that should be avoided. Key external forces fall into five categories. (1) Economic forces: interest rates, inflation, GDP trends, unemployment, disposable income, and stock market trends. (2) Social, cultural, demographic, and environmental forces: population growth and age distribution, ethnic and religious composition, attitudes towards work and leisure, and ecological concerns. (3) Political, governmental, and legal forces: government regulations, tax laws, political stability, special tariffs, and the actions of regulatory bodies. (4) Technological forces: new products, processes, automation, and the Internet, which can create new markets and make existing products obsolete. (5) Competitive forces: the strengths, weaknesses, objectives, and strategies of rival firms, potential entrants, and substitute products. Because these forces affect what products are bought and how firms compete, strategists must continuously monitor them.

  19. Explain Porter's Five-Forces Model of competition.
    Topic: Core Midterm Topics (Solved)

    Michael Porter's Five-Forces Model is the most widely used approach for analyzing competition in an industry. The five forces are: (1) Rivalry among competing firms — usually the strongest force; strategies pursued by one firm can be successful only to the extent that they provide competitive advantage over rival firms' strategies, and rivalry intensifies as competitors become more numerous and equal in size. (2) Potential entry of new competitors — barriers to entry such as economies of scale, capital requirements, product differentiation, and government policy can restrain new entrants; when entry is easy, incumbent firms must strengthen their positions. (3) Potential development of substitute products — products of other industries that can perform the same function place a ceiling on the prices firms can charge and intensify competition. (4) Bargaining power of suppliers — suppliers can raise prices or reduce quality when they are few in number, when there are no substitutes, or when they can integrate forward. (5) Bargaining power of consumers — customers bargain for lower prices and better quality when they buy in large volumes, when products are undifferentiated, and when switching costs are low. Together, the strength of these forces determines the profit potential of an industry and helps strategists decide where and how to compete.

  20. What is an EFE Matrix? How is it developed, and how are its scores interpreted?
    Topic: Core Midterm Topics (Solved)

    The External Factor Evaluation (EFE) Matrix allows strategists to summarize and evaluate economic, social, cultural, demographic, environmental, political, governmental, legal, technological, and competitive information. It is developed in five steps: (1) list 10 to 20 key external factors, including both opportunities and threats affecting the firm and its industry; (2) assign each factor a weight from 0.0 (not important) to 1.0 (very important), based on the factor's importance to success in the industry, with all weights summing to 1.0; (3) assign each factor a rating from 1 to 4 indicating how effectively the firm's current strategies respond to that factor (4 = superior response, 3 = above average, 2 = average, 1 = poor); (4) multiply each factor's weight by its rating to obtain a weighted score; and (5) sum the weighted scores. The total weighted score ranges from 1.0 to 4.0, with 2.5 as the average. A score of 4.0 indicates the firm is responding in an outstanding way to opportunities and threats, while 1.0 shows its strategies are not capitalizing on opportunities or avoiding threats.

  21. What is an internal audit? Which functional areas of business are examined in it?
    Topic: Core Midterm Topics (Solved)

    An internal audit requires gathering, assimilating, prioritizing, and evaluating information about a firm's operations in order to identify its strengths and weaknesses. Unlike the external audit, employees from all functional areas participate in performing it, which improves their understanding of how their jobs and departments fit into the whole organization. The functional areas examined are: (1) Management — planning, organizing, motivating, staffing, and controlling; (2) Marketing — customer analysis, selling, product/service planning, pricing, distribution, marketing research, and opportunity analysis; (3) Finance/Accounting — the investment, financing, and dividend decisions, assessed through financial ratio analysis; (4) Production/Operations — process, capacity, inventory, workforce, and quality; (5) Research and Development — developing new products and improving products and processes; and (6) Management Information Systems — receiving, storing, and providing information for decision-making. The results are summarized in an Internal Factor Evaluation (IFE) Matrix, in which strengths receive ratings of 3 or 4 and weaknesses ratings of 1 or 2.

  22. Differentiate between forward integration, backward integration, and horizontal integration.
    Topic: Core Midterm Topics (Solved)

    The three integration strategies differ in the direction in which a firm extends its control along the industry value chain. Forward integration means gaining ownership or increased control over distributors or retailers — for example, a manufacturer opening its own retail outlets or selling directly through its own website; it is attractive when present distributors are expensive, unreliable, or incapable of meeting the firm's needs. Backward integration means seeking ownership or increased control of the firm's suppliers — for example, a car manufacturer acquiring a tyre company; it is attractive when present suppliers are expensive or unreliable and the number of suppliers is small. Horizontal integration means seeking ownership or increased control over competitors — for example, acquiring or merging with a rival firm in the same industry; it is attractive when the firm can gain economies of scale and monopolistic characteristics in an area without being challenged by the government. Forward and backward integration are together called vertical integration strategies.

  23. What is retrenchment? When is retrenchment an appropriate strategy?
    Topic: Core Midterm Topics (Solved)

    Retrenchment occurs when an organization regroups through cost and asset reduction to reverse declining sales and profit. It is sometimes called a turnaround or reorganization strategy and may involve selling land and buildings to raise cash, pruning product lines, reducing employees, closing marginal businesses or obsolete factories, and instituting expense control systems. Retrenchment can be an especially effective strategy when: (1) an organization has a clearly distinctive competence but has failed to consistently meet its objectives and goals over time; (2) an organization is one of the weakest competitors in a given industry; (3) an organization is plagued by inefficiency, low profitability, poor employee morale, and pressure from stockholders to improve performance; (4) an organization has failed to capitalize on external opportunities, minimize external threats, take advantage of internal strengths, and overcome internal weaknesses over time; (5) an organization has grown so large so quickly that major internal reorganization is needed; or (6) a strategist is charged with evaluating the overall performance of the organization. Retrenchment is generally tried before divestiture or liquidation because it keeps the firm in business.

  24. Differentiate between divestiture and liquidation.
    Topic: Core Midterm Topics (Solved)

    Both divestiture and liquidation are defensive strategies, but they differ in scope and finality. Divestiture means selling a division or part of an organization; it is often used to raise capital for further strategic acquisitions or investments and can be part of an overall retrenchment strategy to rid an organization of businesses that are unprofitable, require too much capital, or do not fit well with the firm's other activities. The rest of the organization continues to operate after a divestiture. Liquidation means selling all of a company's assets, in parts, for their tangible worth; it is a recognition of defeat and can be an emotionally difficult strategy, but it may be better to cease operations than to continue losing large sums of money. Liquidation is used when both retrenchment and divestiture have been unsuccessful, and it ends the existence of the firm. In short, divestiture is a partial exit from a business, whereas liquidation is the complete termination of the firm.

  25. Explain Porter's generic strategies. What does Porter mean by a firm being 'stuck in the middle'?
    Topic: Core Midterm Topics (Solved)

    Porter proposes three generic strategies through which a firm can gain competitive advantage. (1) Cost leadership emphasizes producing standardized products at a very low per-unit cost for consumers who are price-sensitive; it requires efficient-scale facilities, tight cost control, and minimization of costs in R&D, services, and advertising. (2) Differentiation aims at producing products and services considered unique industry-wide, directed at consumers who are relatively price-insensitive; examples of differentiating features are an extremely high quality, an extensive service network, and a strong brand image. (3) Focus means producing products and services that fulfil the needs of small groups of consumers — a particular buyer group, geographic market, or segment of a product line. Fred David further divides these into five types: Type 1 cost leadership (low cost), Type 2 cost leadership (best value), Type 3 differentiation, Type 4 focus (low cost), and Type 5 focus (best value). A firm is 'stuck in the middle' when it fails to develop its strategy in at least one of the three generic directions — it achieves neither a cost advantage nor differentiation nor a defensible niche — and such a firm is almost guaranteed low profitability because it loses high-volume customers to low-cost firms, high-margin customers to differentiators, and niche customers to focused firms.

  26. What are long-term objectives? List the desirable characteristics of long-term objectives.
    Topic: Core Midterm Topics (Solved)

    Long-term objectives represent the results expected from pursuing certain strategies; strategies represent the actions to be taken to accomplish long-term objectives. The time frame for long-term objectives typically extends two to five years into the future. Without long-term objectives, an organization would drift aimlessly toward some unknown end, so objectives are essential for organizational success: they help in evaluation, create synergy, reveal priorities, focus coordination, and provide a basis for planning, organizing, motivating, and controlling. Desirable characteristics of long-term objectives are that they should be: (1) quantitative, (2) measurable, (3) realistic, (4) understandable, (5) challenging, (6) hierarchical (organized from corporate to divisional to functional levels), (7) obtainable, and (8) congruent among organizational units. Objectives should also be expressed in terms of accomplishments rather than activities, and should generally include both financial and strategic targets, because a firm that pursues only financial objectives may adopt short-sighted actions that harm its long-term position.

  27. What is value chain analysis? Identify the primary and support activities in a firm's value chain.
    Topic: Core Midterm Topics (Solved)

    Value chain analysis is the process whereby a firm determines the costs associated with organizational activities from purchasing raw materials to manufacturing products to marketing those products, and compares them with rivals' costs in order to identify where cost advantages or disadvantages exist. The aim is to identify where low-cost advantages or cost disadvantages occur along the value chain and which activities can be performed differently to create value. Porter's value chain divides activities into two groups. Primary activities are directly involved in creating and delivering the product: (1) inbound logistics (receiving and storing inputs), (2) operations (converting inputs into finished products), (3) outbound logistics (storing and distributing products), (4) marketing and sales (promoting and selling the product), and (5) service (installation, repair, and after-sales support). Support activities assist the primary activities: (1) firm infrastructure (general management, finance, and legal), (2) human resource management, (3) technology development, and (4) procurement (purchasing of inputs). A firm creates competitive advantage by performing these activities more cheaply or better than competitors.

  28. What are first mover advantages? Explain with the help of examples.
    Topic: Core Midterm Topics (Solved)

    First mover advantages are the benefits a firm may achieve by entering a new market or developing a new product or service prior to any competitor. Common first mover advantages include: (1) securing access to rare resources and distribution channels before rivals; (2) gaining new knowledge of key success factors and issues; (3) carving out market share and a position that is easy to defend but difficult for rivals to copy; (4) building brand loyalty and a reputation among customers who associate the product category with the pioneer; and (5) setting technical standards and customer expectations that later entrants must follow. For example, early entrants in online retail and mobile payments in many countries established customer bases, logistics networks, and brand recognition that later competitors found expensive to match. However, being a first mover also carries risks: the pioneer bears the cost of developing the market, later entrants can learn from its mistakes, and rapid technological change can make the pioneer's investments obsolete, which is why first mover advantages must be protected by continuous improvement.

  29. Differentiate between a merger and an acquisition. Also define a joint venture.
    Topic: Core Midterm Topics (Solved)

    A merger occurs when two organizations of about equal size unite into a single entity; the two firms combine their operations and ownership on a relatively co-equal basis, and a new name is often given to the combined firm. An acquisition occurs when a large organization purchases a smaller one, or vice versa; the acquired firm's operations are absorbed into the acquiring firm, and the acquired firm ceases to exist as a separate entity. In friendly cases both are negotiated, whereas a takeover is an acquisition that is not solicited by the acquired firm. A joint venture, by contrast, is a cooperative strategy in which two or more firms form a temporary partnership or consortium for the purpose of capitalizing on some opportunity; the participating firms do not merge and remain independent, but they often create a separate jointly owned organization for the specific project. Joint ventures are widely used, for example, when firms enter foreign markets or undertake large projects that require complementary resources and risk sharing.

  30. What is the resource-based view (RBV) of internal assessment? What conditions must internal resources satisfy to provide a sustained competitive advantage?
    Topic: Core Midterm Topics (Solved)

    The resource-based view (RBV) approach contends that internal resources are more important for a firm than external factors in achieving and sustaining competitive advantage. In contrast to the industrial organization (I/O) view, which emphasizes external industry factors, RBV argues that organizational performance is determined primarily by internal resources, which fall into three categories: physical resources (plant, equipment, location, technology, raw materials), human resources (employees, training, experience, intelligence, knowledge, skills, abilities), and organizational resources (firm structure, planning processes, information systems, patents, trademarks, copyrights, databases). For a resource to be a source of competitive advantage, empirical indicators suggest it should be: (1) valuable — it enables the firm to exploit opportunities or neutralize threats; (2) rare — possessed by few or no competitors; and (3) costly to imitate — competitors cannot easily or cheaply develop or obtain it. When a resource is valuable, rare, and costly to imitate, it can provide a sustained competitive advantage that rivals cannot duplicate even if they understand its importance.

  31. What is a Competitive Profile Matrix (CPM)? How does it differ from an EFE Matrix?
    Topic: Core Midterm Topics (Solved)

    The Competitive Profile Matrix (CPM) identifies a firm's major competitors and its particular strengths and weaknesses in relation to a sample firm's strategic position. In a CPM, the critical success factors of an industry — both internal and external — are listed, and the firm and its major competitors are each assigned ratings from 1 (major weakness) to 4 (major strength) on every factor. Weights indicate the relative importance of each factor to success in the industry, and weighted scores are summed so that the firm can be compared with each rival on a common scale. The CPM differs from the EFE Matrix in three important ways: (1) the critical success factors in a CPM are broader and include both internal and external issues, whereas the EFE covers external factors only; (2) the ratings in a CPM refer to strengths and weaknesses, whereas the ratings in an EFE indicate how effectively a firm's strategies respond to external factors; and (3) the CPM compares several competing firms against each other, while the EFE evaluates a single firm against its environment. Despite these differences, both tools use weights that sum to 1.0 and total weighted scores that range from 1.0 to 4.0.

  32. Explain the five functions of management in the context of an internal audit.
    Topic: Core Midterm Topics (Solved)

    In an internal audit, the management area is examined through the five basic functions of management. (1) Planning consists of all those managerial activities related to preparing for the future, including forecasting, establishing objectives, devising policies, and setting goals; it is the primary management function and the foundation for the others, providing a bridge from the present to the future. (2) Organizing includes all those managerial activities that result in a structure of task and authority relationships, such as job design, job specialization, departmentalization, span of control, unity of command, and coordination. (3) Motivating involves efforts directed at shaping human behaviour and includes leadership, communication, work groups, job enrichment, job satisfaction, and organizational change. (4) Staffing includes activities such as recruiting, interviewing, testing, selecting, orienting, training, developing, evaluating, rewarding, disciplining, promoting, transferring, demoting, and dismissing employees. (5) Controlling refers to all those activities directed at ensuring that actual results are consistent with planned results, including quality control, financial control, inventory control, and expense control. Weakness in any of these functions can become an internal weakness for the firm, while excellence in them is a source of strength.
