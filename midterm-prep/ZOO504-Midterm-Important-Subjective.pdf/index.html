  1. Write a note on the Indus Dolphin. (5 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Scientific Name: Platanista minor National Status: Endangered IUCN Red List Threat Status: Endangered The Indus River dolphin is one of the world's rarest mammals and the second most endangered freshwater river dolphin. Description: It has a long beak which thickens towards the tip, and large teeth. The body is stocky with a rounded belly. Flippers are large and paddle-shaped. The forehead is steep. It has a poorly seeing eye; the eye has not developed a lens, yet the dolphin still uses its eyes to differentiate between light and dark. Tail flukes are broad in relation to body size. Colour is grey-brown, sometimes with a pinkish belly. Males are smaller than females. Length is 1.5 to 2.5 m and weight ranges from 70-110 kg. It travels mostly in couples or solitary. Navigation is by echolocation system; physical touch gives the dolphins important information about their surroundings and helps them find food. Both sexes reach sexual maturity between six and ten years of age, or when they reach a length equal to or greater than 1.7 meters. Gestation period is 8-9 months and a single calf is born. Diet consists of fish and crustaceans. Habitat: These dolphins favour silt-laden, turbid waters of the Indus river system, at temperatures of 8°C to 33°C — the deepest river channel and deep, low-velocity water. They are less common in secondary channels and small braids. During the low-water season (October to April), barrages divert almost all river water, so dolphin habitat downstream of Sukkur Barrage and in some tributary segments has been eliminated. Distribution: Native to India, Bangladesh, Nepal and Pakistan — from Kotri, Sindh to Jinnah Barrage in north-western Punjab. Historically it occurred in the Indus mainstream and the Sutlej, Beas, Ravi, Chenab and Jhelum tributaries. Threats: Human development — construction of dams and barrages segments the populations, reducing mating of mature individuals and restricting free movement. By-catch: they commonly get trapped in gillnets, longlines or other fishing gear. Direct hunting for meat and oil, or for use as bait or in alternative medicines. Pollution from industrial, agricultural and domestic activities. River traffic causes collisions with boat propellers that injure them severely.

  2. Describe the conservation actions taken for the Indus Dolphin. (3 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    • The species is included in Appendix I of CITES. • The species is legally protected in all range states and occurs in a number of national parks and other designated areas, including dolphin reserves or sanctuaries. • In 1972, dolphins were protected under the Wildlife Act of Sindh. • In 1974, the Government of Sindh declared the Indus River between the Sukkur and Guddu Barrages a dolphin reserve. • The Government of Punjab prohibited deliberate killing of dolphins in the Punjab Wildlife Protection Act in 1974. • Establishment of the Taunsa Wildlife Sanctuary and Chashma Wildlife Sanctuary in 1983 and 1984 respectively. • A United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) project to rescue dolphins trapped in irrigation canals and return them to the Indus mainstem has had some success in reducing mortality. • Since 2000, WWF-Pakistan has been working in collaboration with the Sindh Wildlife Department under the Indus River Dolphin Conservation Project (IRDCP).

  3. Write a note on the Urial and its subspecies found in Pakistan. (5 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Urial (Ovis orientalis) is a wild sheep with long legs and relatively small horns, native to Central Asia. It is represented by three subspecies in Pakistan. 1. Ovis orientalis vignei — Ladakh Urial or Shapu: Fur tends to be more greyish in winter and less red. The chest ruff is comparatively short with black hairs predominating. The horns turn markedly inwards at their tips. Distribution: Found in Chitral and the Northern Areas, on mountains up to 3000 meters elevation. Status: Endangered. 2. Ovis orientalis blandfordi — Afghan or Baluchistan Urial: Luxuriantly developed neck ruff. The body fur is reddish. The rams have horns which often develop more than a complete arc, with the tips of the horns bending slightly outwards. Distribution: Found in Sindh, Baluchistan and the southern hilly regions of KPK. Status: Endangered. (The third subspecies represented in the region is the Punjab Urial, Ovis orientalis punjabiensis, found in the Salt Range and Kala Chitta hills of Punjab.)

  4. Write a note on the Hog Deer. (3 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Common Name: Para Scientific Name: Axis porcinus National Status: Vulnerable IUCN Red List Threat Status: Endangered Description: It is smaller and stouter in build and hog-like in appearance — a small deer with short delicate legs and a rather bulky, heavy body. Its pelage is coarse. General coloration is dark olive-brown, lacking any rufescent tinges when viewed from a distance. It has round ears fringed from inside with white hairs. Some adults (especially females) have scattered pale spots in their summer coat, particularly on either side of the darker dorsal line. The short tail is quite bushy. The surface of the horns is somewhat smooth with less corrugation. It lives solitary or in pairs. Average antler size is 30-38 cm. Females are slightly smaller and without antlers. Habitat and Ecology: Shrub-dominated wetlands, artificial terrestrial plantations like Changa Manga, and riverine areas. It prefers to live in grassy patches bordering forests. Distribution in Pakistan: Changa Manga Plantation, Head Qadirabad, Ferozewala, Head Marala, Taunsa Wildlife Sanctuary and the riverine forests of Sindh. Native to: Bangladesh, Bhutan, Cambodia, India, Nepal and Pakistan.

  7. Describe the Indian Spotted Eagle. (5 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Scientific Name: Clanga hastata Description: A medium-sized eagle, about 60 cm in length, with a wingspan of 150 cm and a short tail. Adults are essentially brown. The legs appear longer and thinner due to the tarsii being less thickly feathered. This species has a lighter coloration overall compared to its relatives, with a darker iris that makes the eyes appear darker than the plumage. The young birds are glossy brown with a spotted appearance; tips of the head and neck feathers are creamy. The upper tail coverts are light brown with white, giving a barred appearance, and the median coverts have large creamy spots. After about eighteen months the bird moults and becomes a darker shade with fewer spots. Habitat and Ecology: It is a powerful predator that seizes its prey from the ground. It preys on frogs, birds and mostly mammals. It is a tree-nesting species.

  12. Write a short note on the Leopard Cat, its distribution and threats. (3 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    IUCN Red List Threat Status: Least Concern Distribution: The leopard cat occurs throughout most of India, west into Pakistan and Afghanistan. Its range extends into the Himalayan foothills, across most of China, north to the Korean Peninsula and into the Russian Far East. It is found in most of Southeast Asia — Thailand, Vietnam and China — and on the islands of Sumatra, Java, Borneo and Taiwan, and on small islands off South Korea. The leopard cat is the only wild cat species native to Japan and the Philippines. Threats: Leopard cat skins are commercially traded internationally for the fur trade, primarily for coats. Skins are also used as decorations in some parts of its range. They are occasionally kept as pets, sometimes taken from the wild and sometimes from captive breeding. They have been interbred with domestic cats, particularly in the West, to make the popular Bengal breed. Their bones are used in some traditional medicines, in the same way as tiger bones are used in Asian medicine.

  13. What is the feeding habit of the Jungle Cat? (3 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Jungle Cats feed mainly on prey that weighs less than one kilogram — small mammals, principally rodents, with three to five rodents per day. Birds and waterfowl are the mainstay of the Jungle Cat's diet in winter. Other prey species are taken more opportunistically, including hares, nutria, lizards, snakes, frogs, insects and fish. They have also been seen to scavenge kills of large predators such as the Asiatic lion.

  14. What are the threats to the Jungle Cat? (3 marks)

Habitat destruction for agricultural purposes and infrastructure development are also major issues. Some farmers consider the Jungle Cat a pest which takes poultry.

  15. What is a Bobcat? Write its description and threats. (5 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Scientific Name: Lynx rufus IUCN Red List Threat Status: Least Concern Description: Generally, the "lynx" is a wild cat with yellowish-brown fur; body fur is not spotted. Body length is 70-76 cm and the tail is comparatively long. The back of the ears is entirely black. The body is covered with brick-red fur, with generally an off-white colour on the lips, chin and underparts, black streaks on the body and dark bars on the forelegs and tail. Bobcats in the desert regions of the southwest have the lightest coloured coats; in the northern, forested regions they are darkest. Bobcat eyes are yellow with black pupils. The cat has sharp hearing and vision and a good sense of smell. It is an excellent climber. The bobcat is crepuscular, being active mostly during twilight. Distribution: Canada, Mexico and the United States. Habitat: Adaptable predators inhabiting wooded areas, semi-desert, urban edge, forest edges and swampland environments. Like most cats, the bobcat is territorial and mostly solitary. Feeding: The bobcat hunts anything from insects and small rodents, rabbits and hares, to deer. Prey selection depends on location, habitat, season and abundance. Threats: The biggest threat to the bobcat is its fur. Habitat loss is another primary threat in all of its range countries. Increasing urbanization results in direct habitat loss, although bobcats have proven fairly adaptive to urbanization; indirect effects include vehicle collisions, which can be a primary source of mortality in urban populations, exposure to common rodenticides (anticoagulant toxicosis), and increased susceptibility to severe notoedric mange. In localized areas bobcats take domestic livestock and are persecuted as pests. Conservation actions: Listed on Appendix II of CITES. More research needs to be undertaken to gain knowledge of current distribution, both in and outside of protected areas.

  16. Write a note on the Indian Vulture. (5 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The Indian vulture (Gyps indicus) is an Old World vulture native to India, Pakistan and Nepal. It has been listed as Critically Endangered on the IUCN Red List since 2002, as the population severely declined. Description: A medium-sized, bulky bird. Its body and covert feathers are pale, while the quills are darker. Its wings are broad and its tail feathers short. Its head and neck are almost bald. Its bill is pale yellow and long. It is usually 80-103 cm long. Habitat and Ecology: It is found in cities, towns and villages near cultivated areas, and in open and wooded areas. This species feeds almost entirely on carrion. It nests almost exclusively in colonies on cliffs and ruins, and breeds mainly on hilly crags in central and peninsular India. Distribution: Native to Pakistan and India; vagrant to Nepal. Threats: These vultures were found dead and dying in Pakistan and throughout India. The anti-inflammatory drug diclofenac has been identified as the cause of mortality — vultures are exposed to diclofenac and other NSAIDs through scavenging on the carcasses of cattle and buffalo that had been treated with the drugs prior to death and left for scavengers to consume. Other likely contributory factors are poisoning, pesticide use, and conversion and disturbance of forested habitats within its range.

  17. Write a note on the Brown Bear. (5 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Scientific Name: Ursus arctos Synonym: Grizzly Bear IUCN Red List Threat Status: Least Concern Description: Large animals, dark in colour, with long guard hair over the shoulder, characterized by a distinctive hump on the shoulders and long claws on the front paws. Sexual maturity is at 4.5 to 7 years of age. Litter size ranges from one to four, but two are common. Cubs remain with their mothers for at least two and a half years. Females breed every three years. Feeding habit: Chiefly vegetarian — feeds mostly on fruits, honey and ants; occasionally kills sheep and goats. Distribution: The brown bear ranged across a large portion of North America and North Africa; presently it occupies North America, Europe and much of northern Asia. Its native countries include Afghanistan, Iran, Canada, Iraq, China, Pakistan, Georgia, Italy, India and France. Threats: Brown bears are attracted to areas with available human-related foods; being large and somewhat aggressive, they may threaten life and property (often agricultural products) and may be killed as a consequence. They are sometimes hunted for sport and killed for control purposes. Brown bears are poached for the commercial trade in gall bladders and paws. Habitat fragmentation with increasing human populations is also a threat. Conservation actions: Conservation actions vary greatly among nations and regions. In Russia, Japan, Canada, Alaska and parts of eastern and northern Europe, large populations of this species are legally hunted and thus managed as a game animal.

  21. Write a note on the Great Indian Bustard. (5 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Scientific Name: Ardeotis nigriceps National Status: Critically Endangered IUCN Red List Threat Status: Critically Endangered Description: It is a tall, long-legged bird — a large, brown-and-white bustard with a black crown and wing markings. There is a black crown on the forehead and the upper body is brown; the wings are marked with black, brown and grey. The sexes are similar in appearance, but males are bigger than females: males have a larger black crown, long hind crown feathers and a black band across the breast, and are about 122 cm in length; females are smaller, with a greyer neck, typically no or an incomplete breast-band, and are about 92 cm in length. Regarding voice, it gives booming moans during display and barking or bellowing sounds when alarmed. Habitat: Arid and semi-arid grasslands with scattered short scrub, bushes and low-intensity cultivation, in flat or gently undulating terrain. Birds congregate in traditional grassland patches. Biology and Behaviour: Nests are situated in the open ground and males take no part in incubation or care of the developing young. Only a single egg is usually laid. They feed on a wide range of items depending on seasonal availability; grasshoppers and beetles are the preferred diet. Alternatively, they can take grass seeds, berries, rodents and reptiles. In Rajasthan they are known to take Indian spiny-tailed lizards; in cultivated areas they feed on crops such as exposed groundnut, millets and pods of legumes. Distribution: Native to India and Pakistan. Threats: High-intensity poaching; widespread hunting for sport and food; vehicular access to remote areas; egg-collecting, which was rampant in many states of India; habitat loss and degradation; and lack of community support. Conservation action: This bustard is included in Appendix I of CITES. In India it is legally protected and there are severe penalties for killing an individual. Other measures include establishment of protected areas specifically for the species, rehabilitation of grasslands, removal of invasive weed species, school and large-scale community awareness programmes, and publicity materials and media stories to raise awareness of the species.

  22. Describe Capra hircus (Wild Goat). (3 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Females are beardless but carry backward-curving horns; these measure up to 15 cm and bear fine annulations towards their base. The younger males and females show a characteristic pattern with a dark stripe running from the corner of the eye to the muzzle. Mature males are spectacularly beautiful and may have horns over one meter in length. In adults the hair coat is reddish-buff in colour; the belly and the outside of the lower limbs, beard and fore part of the face vary from black to chestnut brown. Shoulder height is 85 to 95 cm in males and 55 to 60 cm in females. The weight of adult males is up to 120 kg and of females 50 to 55 kg. They are capable of surviving in very arid desert conditions and inhabit mountain crests up to 3300 m.

  26. Write three rules of a zoo.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 marks marks

    1. No zoo shall acquire any animal in violation of the Wildlife Act. 2. No zoo shall exhibit any animal that is seriously sick or injured. 3. No zoo shall use any animal, other than the elephant in plains and the yak in hilly areas, for riding purposes. 4. Each zoo shall have a wall at least two meters high from the ground level. 5. The built-up area in any zoo shall not exceed twenty-five percent of the total area of the zoo.

  27. How should animal enclosures be designed in a zoo? (2 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    • All animal enclosures in a zoo shall be so designed as to fully ensure the safety of the animals, the caretakers and the visitors. • Stand-off barriers and adequate warning signs shall be provided for keeping the visitors at a safe distance from the animals. • All animal enclosures in a zoo shall be so designed as to meet the full biological requirements of the animals housed therein. • The enclosures shall be of such size as to ensure that the animals get space for their free movement and exercise.

  28. Write a note on breeding in zoos. (5 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Breeding in zoos (captive breeding) is an important tool for the conservation of endangered species. Zoos breed animals in captivity so that healthy populations of rare species can be maintained outside the wild, and surplus animals can be reintroduced into their natural habitats. • Only healthy animals of sound genetic stock should be selected for breeding, and inbreeding should be avoided to maintain genetic diversity. • Breeding pairs should be of the same species/subspecies; cross-breeding between different subspecies is not allowed, as it destroys the purity of the stock. • Proper records of parentage (studbooks) are maintained for each animal so that related animals are not paired. • Enclosures for breeding animals should provide space, shelter, nesting material and privacy, so that the animals can display natural courtship and parental behaviour. • Young ones should, as far as possible, be reared by their parents rather than by hand, so that they learn natural behaviour. • Animals bred in excess of the carrying capacity of the zoo are exchanged with other zoos or released into suitable natural habitats under approved reintroduction programmes.

  29. How are hygienic conditions maintained in a zoo? (3 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    • Every zoo shall maintain a healthy, hygienic and natural environment, so that the visitors get an opportunity to experience a natural environment. • For the maintenance of hygienic conditions, each zoo shall have a graveyard where carcasses of dead animals can be buried, without affecting the hygiene and cleanliness of the zoo. • The large and medium zoos shall have an incinerator for disposal of the carcasses and other refuse material.

  30. What full-fledged veterinary facilities should a zoo have? (2 marks)

  31. What is the purpose of a post-mortem in a zoo? (2 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Every zoo shall have a post-mortem room run under an expert veterinarian. • Any animal that dies in a zoo shall be subjected to a detailed post-mortem. • The findings shall be recorded and maintained for a period of at least six years.

  32. How is record keeping done in a zoo? (2 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Maintenance of Records: • Every zoo shall keep the record of all animals in terms of their birth, acquisitions, sales, disposals and death. • Every zoo shall prepare and maintain an inventory record: Zoo ID, Animal ID, Scientific name, Sex, Origin, Birth, Parents, Death, Out of stock. • Every zoo shall prepare and maintain a daily record: Health, Behaviour, Feed and Feeding, Management, Health Care, Problems if any. • Every zoo shall also prepare a brief summary and an annual report of the activities of the zoo for every financial year. • Death of animals in the zoo, with the reason of death identified on the basis of post-mortem reports and diagnostic tests.

  33. How do zoos play a role in education and research? (5 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Zoos exist in today's society for a myriad of reasons including conservation, education, science and recreation. Education is one of the main roles of zoos and aquaria today. Millions of people visit zoos and aquaria worldwide each year, which means that the zoo community can reach a very large group of people and explain the importance of conservation. Zoos enable visitors to learn about our natural world and thereby respect nature in all its forms. • Every enclosure in a zoo shall bear signboards displaying scientific information. • Every zoo shall publish literature and make the same available to the visitors, either free of cost or at a reasonable price — leaflets, brochures, guidebooks. • Every large and medium zoo shall make arrangements in the form of recordings and writings to study biological behaviour, population dynamics and veterinary care of the animals. • Every large and medium zoo shall make a detailed database, and the database shall be exchanged with other zoos.

  34. Write a note on the Saker Falcon. (5 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Scientific Name: Falco cherrug IUCN Red List Threat Status: Endangered Description: Great variation in colour and pattern exists, from a fairly uniform chocolate-brown colour to a pale sandy colour with brown bars or streaks. Some are almost pure white individuals, which are particularly prized by Arab falconers. Saker females are markedly larger than males: females typically weigh 970 to 1300 g, are 55 cm long, with a wingspan of 120 to 130 cm; males usually weigh from 730 to 990 g, are 45 cm long, with a wingspan of 100 to 110 cm. Sakers have sharp, curved talons, used primarily for grasping prey, and they use their powerful, hooked beak to sever the prey's vertebral column. The saker falcon is a large, powerful bird of prey with an exceptionally broad wingspan for its size. Range lifespan: Wild — 10 (high) years; Captivity — 25 (high) years. Range Distribution: Wide range across the Palearctic region, from eastern Europe to western China. Conservation action: Controls of illegal trade are implemented in various countries in the western range. Captive breeding has been developed strongly in some countries, including the U.A.E., as a means of substituting farmed for wild-caught birds. Clinics have also been set up to improve the longevity and availability of wild-caught birds in various Gulf States. Threats: This species suffered mainly from electrocution on power lines, and decreased prey availability due to the loss and degradation of steppes and dry grasslands through agricultural intensification.

  35. Describe the biology and behaviour of the Falcon. (3 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    This bird is physically adapted to hunting close to the ground in open terrain, combining rapid acceleration with high manoeuvrability. It specialises on mid-sized diurnal terrestrial rodents (especially ground squirrels). Its habitats include semi-desert and steppes; in some areas it is found particularly near water and even in urban environments. This species usually builds no nest of its own; it uses an old stick nest in a tree which was previously used by other birds. Clutch size varies from three to five, exceptionally two to six. The species usually occurs singly or in pairs. Range lifespan: Wild — 10 (high) years; Captivity — 25 (high) years. The bird's lifestyle largely depends on the extent of food supply. They are sedentary, part-migratory or fully migratory.

  41. What conservation actions are underway or proposed for Kingfishers? (3 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

The fur is typically reddish-orange on the upper parts, with whitish underparts; the backs of the ears and the front of the legs are black. They dig burrows (earths) or use those of other animals for shelter and for rearing the young (cubs). They communicate through scent marking, body posture and vocalizations. Importance and threats: The red fox controls rodent populations, but it may also prey on poultry and game birds and can transmit diseases such as rabies. It is hunted and trapped for its valuable fur, and is killed in retaliation where it attacks livestock and chickens.

  50. What are the categories of endangered (threatened) species? Define each. (3 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Species of organisms which are in danger of extinction are classified into three categories: • Critically Endangered (CR): When a taxon is facing an extremely high risk of extinction in the wild in the immediate future. • Endangered (EN): Those which are abundant in their range but are gradually declining in total numbers (facing a very high risk of extinction in the wild in the near future). • Vulnerable (VU): When a taxon is not Critically Endangered or Endangered but is facing a high risk of extinction in the wild in the medium-term future.

  51. Describe the categories of the IUCN Red List. (5 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Extinct (EX): When there is no reasonable doubt that the last individual has died. Extinct in the Wild (EW): When a taxon is known only to survive in cultivation, in captivity, or as a naturalized population (or populations) well outside the past range. When exhaustive surveys in known and/or expected habitat, at appropriate times (diurnal, seasonal, annual), throughout its historic range have failed to record an individual. Surveys should be over a time frame appropriate to the taxon's life cycle and life form. Critically Endangered (CR): When a taxon is facing an extremely high risk of extinction in the wild in the immediate future. Endangered (EN): When a taxon is not Critically Endangered but is facing a very high risk of extinction in the wild in the near future. Vulnerable (VU): When a taxon is not Critically Endangered or Endangered but is facing a high risk of extinction in the wild in the medium-term future. Data Deficient (DD): When a taxon has inadequate information to make a direct or indirect assessment of its risk of extinction based on its distribution and/or population status. Not Evaluated (NE): When a taxon has not yet been evaluated against the criteria.

  52. What is meant by Data Deficient (DD)? (2 marks)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    When a taxon has inadequate information to make a direct, or indirect, assessment of its risk of extinction based on its distribution and/or population status, it is listed as Data Deficient. A taxon in this category may be well studied and its biology well known, but appropriate data on abundance and/or distribution is lacking. Data Deficient is therefore not a category of threat or Lower Risk.
