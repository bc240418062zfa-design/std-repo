  1. Define advertising according to Bovee (1992) and explain the six segments of its definition.
    Topic: Introduction to Advertising

    Bovee (1992) defined advertising as: "Advertising is the non-personal communication of information, usually persuasive in nature, about products, services or ideas by identified sponsors through the various media." The definition has six segments: (1) it is a paid form of communication; (2) the sponsor is identified; (3) most advertising tries to persuade or influence the consumer to do something, though in some cases it merely makes consumers aware of the product or company; (4) the message is conveyed through different types of media; (5) advertising makes the message reach a large audience of potential consumers; and (6) advertising is a form of mass communication, therefore it is non-personal.

  2. Differentiate between personal selling and non-personal selling. Also state the advantages and disadvantages of personal selling.
    Topic: Introduction to Advertising

    There are two basic ways to sell: personally and non-personally. In personal selling the seller and buyer have to be together, face to face. In non-personal selling the seller is approached through a medium, i.e. advertising. Advantages of personal selling: the seller has time to discuss everything about the product; the buyer has time to ask questions, get answers and examine evidence for or against the product; the seller can see the person to whom the product is being sold; and the seller can easily locate potential buyers. Disadvantages: personal selling is expensive because it is labor intensive, and it is time consuming. Despite these advantages of personal selling, advertising (non-personal selling) remains more effective and is far cheaper per potential customer.

  3. Explain the growing importance of advertising in the modern world.
    Topic: Introduction to Advertising

    Advertising has assumed great importance in today's fast developing and competitive world. We see hundreds of ads every day, and consciously or subconsciously advertisements have a tremendous effect on us: advertising can not only change buying decisions but also changes the mindset and attitude of people. Advertising reflects the culture of a nation, and advertising and culture affect each other. The advertising industry also supports the media industry - in fact media survives on advertising because the two go hand in hand. Advertising can be used as an effective tool for a social cause, and it helps in spreading information about new technology and innovations. Today advertising is not only for spreading brand awareness; it also shows us where we stand in the world today.

  4. How are advertisements classified? Explain the bases of classification.
    Topic: Introduction to Advertising

    Advertisements are classified on four bases. (1) Target audience: consumer, business, industrial, trade, professional and agricultural advertisements. (2) Area: international, national, regional and local advertisements. (3) Media used: print, newspaper, magazine, electronic, radio, television, out-of-home, outdoor, transit, direct mail, directories and other media. (4) Purpose: product or non-product; commercial or non-commercial; primary or selective demand; and direct or indirect action advertisements. Before designing and classifying an ad, three questions are essential: what do I want my advertising to accomplish (goals), whom should my advertising address (target audience - demographics, behaviors, needs and desires), and what should my advertising say (the message).

  5. What is puffery, shock advertising and a weasel claim? Explain with examples.
    Topic: Advertising and Society

    These are three ethically questionable practices in advertising. Puffery means exaggerated, fantastical or impossible claims, e.g. a cosmetic ad saying "There are only 3 steps between you and the beauty." Shock ads try to captivate the audience through a shocking message, e.g. "Death cigarettes." A weasel claim is a probable claim shown in such a way that a word weakens it, e.g. "probably the best show of the year" - here the word 'probably' is the weasel; or an ad saying a medicine "helps" stop gas before it starts - here the word 'helps' is the weasel word.

  6. Explain the code of ethics in advertising.
    Topic: Advertising and Society

    The code of ethics in advertising lays down the standards that advertisements must follow: (1) advertisements will not offend morality, decency and the religious views or beliefs of the viewers; (2) advertisements should not be directed towards any religious or political end; (3) advertisements should not endanger the safety of children or create in them any interest in unhealthy practices; and (4) indecent, vulgar, suggestive, repulsive or offensive treatment of themes shall be avoided.

  7. Differentiate between needs, wants and demands. Also explain the basic equation of value and the marketing process.
    Topic: Advertising and Society

    A need is a basic human necessity. When a need becomes specific, it is a want. A want for a specific product supported by an ability to pay is a demand. Marketers must understand and interpret the needs, wants and demands of the target market. Value is expressed by the basic equation: Value = Benefit / Cost ("value for money"). Exchange is the core concept of marketing - the exchange of a product between the seller and the buyer; when an agreement is reached between two parties, a transaction takes place. The marketing process has four major stages: the marketing environment analysis, the target market and positioning process, marketing mix decisions, and the ultimate consumer.

  8. What is marketing mix? Explain the five factors for assembling the marketing mix and its components.
    Topic: Marketing Tools, Mix and Product Life Cycle

    Marketing mix is the set of marketing variables that the firm uses to achieve its marketing objectives in the target market. Assembling the marketing mix means identifying and creating the best possible combination of the 4 P's, choosing appropriate marketing activities, allocating adequate marketing efforts and resources to each 'P', and identifying the effectiveness of different combinations in terms of possible sales volume and net profit. Five factors for assembling the mix: it should be separate for every brand; the weightage for each 'P' must be decided; the decision should be customer/market oriented; the mix has to be dynamic; and it is the most visible part of marketing strategy. Components: Product (ingredients, quality, design, features, brand, packaging, services, warranties etc.), Price (list price, discounts, credit terms, payment mode), Physical Distribution (channels, coverage, locations, inventory, transportation, warehousing) and Promotion (advertising, personal selling, direct marketing, publicity, sales promotions, public relations).

  9. Explain the four stages of the Product Life Cycle (PLC).
    Topic: Marketing Tools, Mix and Product Life Cycle

    Every product has a life cycle: a product has a limited life, its sales pass through different stages, profits rise and fall in different stages, and products require different marketing, manufacturing, financial and purchasing strategies in each stage; the competitive environment is also different in every stage. The four stages are: (1) Introduction - the product is launched in the market; (2) Growth - sales and profits rise rapidly; (3) Maturity - sales reach their peak and profits begin to stabilize or fall; and (4) Decline - sales and profits fall and the product loses its market.

  10. What is SWOT analysis? Explain its external and internal components.
    Topic: Marketing Tools, Mix and Product Life Cycle

    SWOT analysis studies the external and internal environment of a business. (1) External environment analysis (Opportunity and Threat analysis): it covers macro factors, which are uncontrollable - social, legal, economic, political and technological - and micro (task) factors, which have a direct impact - customers, employees, competitors, distributors, creditors, suppliers and other stakeholders. (2) Internal environment analysis (Strength and Weakness profile): it is related to the functional areas of the business - marketing, finance, manufacturing, purchase, research and development, information technology, human resource, leadership and image.

  11. Explain the different types of advertising agencies.
    Topic: Advertising Agencies

    There are four types of advertising agencies. (1) Full Service Agency: provides all marketing communication services. (2) Creative Agency: provides writing of ad copy and design service. (3) A Media Independent: offers buying of space and time and related research. (4) A Composite Agency: offers both creative and media services with research. The purpose of advertising agencies is to advise and counsel on marketing strategy and on advertising and media strategy, to prepare and develop print, outdoor and electronic advertisements, to carry out collateral designs such as display material, and to help and counsel on sales promotion and other communication tasks.

  12. Describe the structure of an advertising agency, explaining the role of each key position.
    Topic: Advertising Agencies

    The structure of an advertising agency consists of the following positions. Account Executive: organizes the resources of the agency to solve the problems of the client. The Creative Team: basically comprises of a writer and a designer. Account Planner or Researcher: responsible for the data and market intelligence on the account. Media Planner: this team actually makes the campaign come live. Account Director: overall responsible for providing service to the clientele of the agency. Creative Director: overall in charge of the creative work and the artwork of the agency's clients. Media Director: responsible for media planning and the buying activities of the agency. Production House: the department which brings alive the creative artwork of the creative team. All finished material is then released to the media several days before the launch date.

  13. How are advertising agencies paid? What points should be considered while selecting an advertising agency?
    Topic: Advertising Agencies

    Advertising agencies are paid by the media and not by the client, by an agreed percentage of commission for the advertisements released; an agreed commission is also charged on services like production cost etc. Before selecting an agency, the following points should be considered: name and address of the agency, agency history, year of establishment, ownership pattern, team of professionals, size of turnover (annual reports), growth trends, revenue and billing for the last 5 years, clients won and lost in the last 3 years, and the agency mission.

  14. What are advertising objectives? Explain sales, communications and behavior-related objectives.
    Topic: Advertising Planning and Positioning

    An advertisement, to be effective, should be seen, read, understood, believed and acted upon. There are three types of advertising objectives. (1) Sales objectives: an immediate increase in the sales percentage. (2) Communications objectives: an increase in market share, achieved through the communication response pyramid - moving the audience from unawareness to awareness, knowledge, liking, preference, trial, purchase and regular buying. (3) Behavior-related objectives: increasing the company's brand usage rate among existing consumers, and encouraging non-users to have trial consumption and/or purchase.

  15. What is positioning? Explain the seven approaches to positioning strategy.
    Topic: Advertising Planning and Positioning

    Positioning is the foundation upon which all other marketing mix decisions are built. It involves a decision to emphasize or highlight certain aspects of a brand. Positioning is not what you do to the product, but what you do to the minds of the consumers through marketing communications. In Philip Kotler's words, it is "the act of designing the company's offering and image to occupy a distinctive place in the target market's mind." The seven approaches to positioning strategy are: (1) using product characteristics or customer benefit approach; (2) the price-quality approach; (3) the use or application approach; (4) the product user approach; (5) the product class approach; (6) the cultural symbol approach; and (7) the competitor approach.

  16. Explain the nine steps for deciding the positioning strategy and the three components of positioning.
    Topic: Advertising Planning and Positioning

    The nine steps for deciding the positioning strategy are: (1) identify the competitor; (2) find how competitors are perceived and evaluated by consumers; (3) determine the positioning strategy used by each competitor for the specific product category; (4) understand the customer thoroughly; (5) identify the various alternative positioning strategies available; (6) find which position is still unused; (7) evaluate own strengths and weaknesses; (8) select the position; and (9) monitor and evaluate the position. Economic analysis should guide the decision, and the broad rules are: keep it focused, keep it consistent and keep it long term. The three main components of positioning are: (1) Benefit - the emotional reason to purchase from your company; (2) Target - your best potential customer; and (3) Competition - anyone else in the market who is vying for the same or similar customers.

  17. Explain the three types of message content used in designing an advertising message.
    Topic: Advertising Message and Media Planning

    While designing a message, four questions are answered: what to say (content), how to say it logically, how to say it symbolically (format), and who should say it (source). Message content is of three types. (1) Rational content: appeals through benefits like quality, value, performance and economy. (2) Emotional content: negative appeals such as fear, guilt and shame, and positive appeals such as love, pride and joy. (3) Moral content: directed to the audience's sense of what is right, and used for social causes. An attractive source is the best source, e.g. celebrities and professionals are used as spokespersons.

  18. Define the following media terms: media planning, medium, media mix, media class, media vehicle, media option, reach, coverage and frequency.
    Topic: Advertising Message and Media Planning

    Media planning is a process of determining how to use time and space of media to achieve advertising objectives; the media plan is a guide for media selection - a plan of action. A medium is a single form of communication, e.g. TV, radio, billboards, online media. Media mix is the combination of media to be used. Media class is the type of medium, like TV or radio. A media vehicle is a single program, magazine or radio station. A media option is the choice within a vehicle, e.g. full page or half page, color or black and white. Reach is the measure of the number of different audience members exposed at least once to the message. Coverage refers to the potential audience that might be exposed to the ad message. Frequency refers to the number of times the audience is exposed to the advertisement message.

  19. Explain the four methods of determining the advertising budget.
    Topic: Advertising Budget and Research

    There are four methods to determine the advertising budget. (1) Affordable method: the company spends what it can afford; this is a top-down approach in which management decides how much to spend, but it ignores market reality. (2) Percentage of sales method: the budget is set as a fixed percentage of current or expected sales. (3) Competitive parity method: the budget is set by matching the spending of competitors. (4) Objective and task method: an activity-based and objective-oriented approach in which the objectives are defined first, the tasks needed to achieve them are decided, and the cost of those tasks becomes the budget. Budgets are decided through two approaches: the top-down approach and the bottom-up approach.

  20. Explain the steps of the advertising research process.
    Topic: Advertising Budget and Research

    The advertising research process consists of the following steps to collect relevant information: (1) Problem definition. (2) Exploratory or informal research. (3) Determining research objectives. (4) Research design - sampling methods and size, scaling, and the data collection method. (5) Data collection. (6) Data tabulation and analysis. (7) Interpretation, conclusion and reporting. Through research the advertiser finds out who the customers are, what are the likes and dislikes of customers, how the company's brand is perceived, and how to improve the customer's perception. Advertising research helps in appropriate positioning decisions for the brand, in selecting target markets, in finding the acceptability of creative ideas, and in pre-testing and post-testing of ads.

  21. Differentiate between pre-testing and post-testing of advertisements, giving the methods of each.
    Topic: Advertising Testing and Media Research

    Pre-testing is done before the campaign is run, to establish whether the message content and presentation will perform as desired. Methods for print advertising include direct questioning, focus groups, paired comparison tests, order of merit test, direct mail test, depth interview and story telling; for TV and radio they include the central location test, trailer test, theatre test, live telecast test and sales experiment. Post-campaign testing is done after the campaign to measure its effectiveness, and its methods are: (1) Inquiry test - the same offer is placed in different copies in different issues/media to determine the pulling power of different copy; (2) Sales test - through point of purchase displays or direct mail, or through two matched groups collecting data; (3) Recall test - aided recall, in which the respondent is shown a picture of the ad with the sponsor or brand name blanked out, and unaided recall, in which only the product or service is given; and (4) Recognition test - e.g. mail surveys through questionnaires.

  22. Explain the AIDA model. Which stages are common to all the response hierarchy models?
    Topic: Working of Advertising and Response Models

    The AIDA model was developed by E. K. Strong. AIDA stands for Attention, Interest, Desire and Action: an advertisement must draw or attract attention, be interesting, create desire and initiate action. There are four response hierarchy models: the AIDA model, the hierarchy of effects model, the innovation-adoption model and the information processing model. All four models start with the cognitive stage and pass through three common stages: the cognitive stage (thinking/learning - Learn), the affective stage (feeling/liking - Feel) and the behavior stage (acting/doing - Do, also called the conative stage).

  23. Explain the basic communication model of advertising, including the role of noise.
    Topic: Working of Advertising and Response Models

    In the basic communication model, the source (sender) is the advertiser, who decides the objectives of the campaign; the message is encoded by the agency as strategy and tactics; the channel is the media mix (one-way and two-way channels); and the receiver is the target audience, whose response passes through perception, learning, persuasion and action. Feedback flows back from the receiver to the source. Noise is any factor that hinders or distorts the delivery of the advertising message to the target audience. External noise factors include public opinion, marketing strategy, the general pattern of consumer trends and competitors' marketing communication; internal noise factors include perceived needs, information processing ability, attitudes and opinions, and the level of avoidance of advertising. The effects aimed at are perception, learning, persuasion and behavior (sales).

  24. What is the RACE formula of John Marston and the ROPE formula of Jerry Hendrix? Explain both.
    Topic: Problem Solving and Persuasive Strategies

    Both are formulas for problem solving and planning in communication. The RACE formula was given by John Marston: R - Research, A - Action, C - Communication, E - Evaluation. The ROPE formula was given by Jerry Hendrix: R - Research, O - Objectives, P - Programming, E - Evaluation. In problem solving, one starts by assembling readily available facts, determining which audience is affected or involved, and deciding whether additional research is needed to define the problem and evaluate its scope; then a hypothesis is formulated, facts are assembled to test it, and the hypothesis is revised if disproved. Evaluation covers impact (informational, attitudinal and behavioral) and output (media efforts and results).

  25. Explain the three types of advertising appeals.
    Topic: Consumer Behaviour, Appeals and Media Terms

    There are three types of advertising appeals. (1) Informational / Rational / Logical appeals: these focus on the consumer's practical, functional or utilitarian need for a product or service; they emphasize the features of a product or service and inform about the benefits or reasons for owning or using a particular brand. (2) Emotional appeals: these relate to the consumer's psychological and/or social needs for purchasing a product or service, because many purchase motives relate to emotions and feelings about a brand; they are of two types - positive emotional appeals and negative emotional appeals. (3) Moral appeals: these attempt to draw the audience to "what is right" and are generally used to urge people to support or show concern for social causes such as polio eradication, environment protection, population control and donations.

  26. Define clutter, zapping and zipping. Also explain the three scheduling strategies: continuity, flighting and pulsing.
    Topic: Consumer Behaviour, Appeals and Media Terms

    Clutter is the number of ads in a medium. Zapping is changing channels to avoid commercials. Zipping is fast-forwarding through commercials while watching programs on video etc. A fleeting message is a commercial that passes very quickly. There are three scheduling strategies: (1) Continuity - spreads advertising continuously over the period; (2) Flighting - alternates between intense advertising and no advertising; and (3) Pulsing - a combination with peaks and valleys, i.e. a continuous base level with periodic bursts of heavier advertising. Scheduling decisions depend on the brand, buying cycles, budget, advertising decay and competitive spending.

  27. What are program rating, Gross Rating Point (GRP) and Target Rating Point (TRP)? Explain with formulas.
    Topic: Consumer Behaviour, Appeals and Media Terms

    Program rating is a number expressed as a percentage; it measures the potential reach of TV or radio. Program rating = (Number of households viewing the program / Number of households owning TV sets) x 100. For example, if in a city 1 million households own TV sets and a program is watched by 300,000 households, the program rating is 300,000/1,000,000 x 100 = 30%. Gross Rating Point (GRP) tells how many potential audiences may be exposed to a series of commercials; it is the sum of total exposure potential expressed as a percentage of the audience population: GRP = Reach x Frequency, and Frequency = Total Exposures / Reach = GRP / Reach. Target Rating Point (TRP) refers to the number of people in the primary target audience that will be reached, and the number of times they will be reached; one TRP is equal to 1% of the total TV audience, so a program with 50 TRPs was watched by 50% of the audience in that week.

  28. Explain the five steps of the creative process.
    Topic: Creativity and Copywriting

    Creativity means being novel and appropriate; it is the ability to generate fresh, unique and appropriate ideas that can be used as a solution to a communication problem. The creative process has five steps: (1) Immersion - gathering raw material information through background research and immersing yourself in the problem; (2) Digestion - taking the information, working with it and thinking about it in the mind; (3) Incubation - putting the problem out of your conscious mind and letting the information do the work; (4) Illumination - as a result of the first three steps, the birth of an idea takes place; and (5) Verification - studying the idea to see if it still looks good and solves the problem, and shaping the idea for practical benefit.

  29. What is a Unique Selling Proposition (USP)? Explain its three essential characteristics. Also explain the other approaches to major selling ideas.
    Topic: Creativity and Copywriting

    A major selling idea is the central theme of an ad campaign - the "big idea" that attracts consumer attention, gets a reaction and sets the product or service apart from the competition. The Unique Selling Proposition approach has three essential characteristics: (1) each advertisement must make a proposition to the customer - it should say "buy this and you will get this benefit"; (2) the proposition must be unique, either in the brand or in the claim; and (3) it must be strong enough to move the masses, i.e. pull new customers to the brand. The other approaches to major selling ideas are: creating a brand image (developing a strong, memorable identity through image advertising, used when competing brands are very similar), finding the inherent drama (building the campaign around the drama inherent in the product), and positioning (determining what place the product will occupy in the market, e.g. by price, attributes, ability to surpass competition, application, product users or product class).

  30. Differentiate between hard sell and soft sell advertising.
    Topic: Creativity and Copywriting

    Hard sell advertising coaxes the receiver with an immediate demand to buy the product, e.g. "buy now" - it creates pressure to buy. Soft sell advertising is just the opposite: instead of an immediate demand, it uses a wider and more suitable approach to persuade the customer to buy the product. Soft sell is considered better because it does not irk (irritate) the customer. The style of selling is decided according to the nature of the product, the competition and the target audience, and it differs from agency to agency and campaign to campaign.

  31. Who is a copywriter? Explain the work and characteristics of a copywriter.
    Topic: Creativity and Copywriting

    A copywriter is the person who writes the most interesting and original copy that will get people appreciating and buying; copywriters write slogans and punchy phrases to coax the target audience into buying products and services. A copywriter works in creative partnership with the art director to conceive, develop and produce effective advertisements - the art director deals mainly with visual images, while the copywriter provides the verbal or written aspect. The work of a copywriter includes meeting the account management team to discuss the client's requirements, generating workable concepts and ideas, writing clear, persuasive and original copy, discussing progress with the creative director, amending and revising campaigns according to feedback, proofreading copy for spelling and grammar, and overseeing the campaign through production to final completion. The characteristics of a copywriter include writing creatively and persuasively, working under pressure, meeting deadlines, working in a team, working cohesively with clients, keeping abreast of market trends, translating the client's preferences, having an amazing sense of humor, working on various projects simultaneously, and being a wordsmith.

  32. Explain the anatomy (components) of an advertisement.
    Topic: Creativity and Copywriting

    The components of an advertisement are: (1) Headline - to grab attention. (2) Illustration - to retain interest. (3) Body copy - contains the details and the sales pitch. (4) Signature line - the brand name, slogan and trademark. (5) Standing details - the address of the company. In the detailed anatomy of an ad, the visual part works as a facilitator and includes the overline, headline, underline and lead, while the elaborator part contains the body, subheads, wrap-up, caption, product name and the slogan/logo. The language of advertising is normally very positive and uses common adjectives such as new, good/better/best, free, fresh, delicious, full, sure, clean, wonderful, special, crisp, fine and great, and verbs such as make, get, give, have, see, buy, come, go, know, keep, look, need and use.

  33. What are weasel words? Explain "helps", "virtually", "new and improved" and "like" with examples.
    Topic: Language, Weasel Words and Market Education

    Advertisers often use special words or phrases that are often misleading; such words are called weasel words. (1) Helps: people often think "help" means to stop or cure, but in advertising it is not necessarily true - e.g. a product can "help" indigestion, but in fact the product does not cure indigestion. (2) Virtually: it means not really, or not in fact - e.g. an ad claiming a cold medicine "virtually relieves all symptoms" could mean it does not relieve any symptoms. (3) New and improved: the change makes the product "new", and a product remains new for 6 months. (4) Like: "like" means "not in fact" - e.g. products claiming they work "like magic"; if things are like magic, they are not magic.

  34. What is market education? Explain its two kinds and the effects of adopting a market education strategy.
    Topic: Language, Weasel Words and Market Education

    Market education is the effort of informing, educating and creating understanding of a product or service in the market before and along with advertising, so that advertising becomes more acceptable and productive. There are two kinds of market education: the first kind prepares the market in advance, and is also called pre-selling; the second kind occurs with products and services that are already on the market. Correspondingly, there are two types of market: trade (the people who sell your products or services) and consumers or users (the people who buy or use your products). The effects of adopting a market education strategy are: it helps to minimize sales resistance; it makes it easier for the sales force to achieve adequate distribution prior to advertising; it helps to reduce the cost of advertising; and it makes advertising more effective. Techniques adopted for market education include a media relations campaign in press, radio and TV; testing of prototypes by potential users; production of slides, videos and other audio-visual aids for dealers and customer conventions; work visits for dealers; special campaigns aimed at opinion leaders; and printed material to support these activities.

  35. How does advertising differ from Public Relations?
    Topic: Language, Weasel Words and Market Education

    Advertising and Public Relations differ in the following ways: (1) PR is not a form of advertising; it has a broader perspective and advertising is only a part of it. (2) PR relates to all the communications of the total organization, while advertising is mainly used for sales objectives and marketing functions. (3) At times advertising may not be used by an organization, but every organization is involved in PR. (4) PR embraces everyone and everything, while advertising is used for special selling and buying tasks. Advertising presents the most persuasive possible selling message, whereas the role of PR is informing, educating and creating understanding through knowledge; advertising is more successful when prior PR activity has created knowledge and understanding of the product or service being promoted.

  36. Explain the seven W's of an advertisement campaign.
    Topic: Language, Weasel Words and Market Education

    For an effective advertisement campaign, seven relevant questions, known as the seven W's, must be tackled: (1) What is the objective of the campaign? (2) What problems are to be addressed? (3) What extent do you anticipate to achieve results? (4) What media will you prefer to use, for different target audiences? (5) What will be the likely cost? (6) Who is going to launch the campaign? (7) When is the campaign planned for launch? Campaigns must be devised prudently to be cost effective and result oriented, because there is stiff competition among manufacturers and similar or competitive products.

  37. What is sponsorship? Why do companies sponsor, and what can be sponsored?
    Topic: Sponsorship and Advertising Media

    Sponsorship is an important tool of advertising - a technique not only to win customers but also to retain them. As Sir Francis Bacon said, "A wise man will make more opportunities than he finds"; sponsorship is basically about creating more opportunities rather than finding the prevalent ones. Four questions must be answered: what should be sponsored, what are the total costs involved, how can sponsorship be made effective, and how can the sponsorship be organized. Companies sponsor because they want to enter a new segment of the market, increase familiarity with their name, improve customer relations, enhance their corporate image, show how their products perform, get maximum media coverage, support distributors, become known in export markets, and increase goodwill. Things that can be sponsored include sports, arts and culture, publications, professional awards, educational activities and charitable causes.

  38. Explain the advantages and disadvantages of newspapers as an advertising medium.
    Topic: Sponsorship and Advertising Media

    Advantages of newspapers: (1) Flexibility - advertising space from 1 inch to multiple pages can be used, ads can be scheduled on any day of the week, and ads can be prepared on very short notice. (2) Range of market coverage - newspapers provide geographical flexibility. (3) Competitive advantage - newspapers are relatively cheap as against other media. (4) Positive consumer attitudes - high reader interest and approximately 80% coverage. (5) Interaction of national and local - newspapers provide a bridge between the national advertiser and the local retailer. There are three major types of advertising in newspapers: classified advertising (want ads, primarily for the convenience of readers, generally without headlines or illustrations), retail advertising (sales promotion advertising based on price features and specials, and image advertising designed to position a product better), and national advertising (used by manufacturers to support their products as part of their national advertising plans). Disadvantages of newspapers: (1) no audience selection - they do not provide audience selectivity; (2) high waste circulation, which makes them too expensive on a national basis; (3) short life span - a newspaper is unlikely to be put aside and read later like a magazine; and (4) poor reproduction - generally poor color reproduction.

  39. Explain the advantages and disadvantages of magazines as an advertising medium.
    Topic: Sponsorship and Advertising Media

    Advantages of magazines: (1) Target audience - magazines can reach specialized audiences, e.g. men's health magazines target men and women's magazines target women. (2) Audience receptivity - magazines have a very high level of audience receptivity; an ad in Fortune magazine would impress a business audience. (3) Long life span - magazines have the longest life span of all media, some are never discarded (e.g. National Geographic), and they have high reach potential as they are passed along to family, friends, customers and colleagues. (4) Format - the magazine format allows creative advertising variety through multiple pages, inserts and other features. (5) Visual quality - production quality is excellent, printed on top class paper with very good reproduction. (6) Sales promotions - advertisers can use magazines to distribute coupons, samples and information cards. Disadvantages of magazines: (1) limited distribution, which means lack of penetration; (2) lack of immediacy - ads may take a long time to have an effect because readers may not look at an issue until long after it arrives; (3) limited flexibility - long lead time, with closing dates often 2 to 3 months prior to the date of issue; and (4) high cost, because of high quality color production.

  40. Explain the advantages and limitations of television advertising. Also state the forms of TV advertising.
    Topic: Sponsorship and Advertising Media

    Television provides the ability to communicate sight, sound, motion and emotion, gives the product a "larger than life" image, and being visual has more impact on customers - "seeing is believing." Advantages: (1) Creativity and impact - the blend of sight and sound offers tremendous creative flexibility and makes a deep impact. (2) Coverage and cost effectiveness - TV can reach large audiences; nearly everyone, regardless of age, gender, income or educational level, watches TV, and the large coverage makes it cost effective. (3) Captivity and attention - the combined power of sight, sound, motion and emotion creates a very good effect. (4) Selectivity and flexibility - some selectivity is possible due to variations in audience composition resulting from program content, time of telecast and geographical coverage. Limitations: (1) Cost - it is an enormously expensive medium. (2) Lack of selectivity - TV does not offer as much audience selectivity as radio, magazines, newspapers or direct mail. (3) Fleeting message - commercials usually last 30 seconds or less and leave nothing tangible for the viewer. (4) Clutter - the advertiser's message is only one of many spots, which reduces effectiveness. (5) Limited viewer attention - the viewing audience shrinks during commercial breaks. (6) Deception - art and technology are used to create simulations, amounting to saying what is not actually true. The ingredients of a good TV campaign are celebrities, humor (especially a surprise or a twist), a story line and a simple concept. The forms of TV advertising are sponsorships (sponsoring programs), participations (frequent spots of 5, 10, 20 or 30 seconds) and spot announcements (spots appearing during breaks).

  41. Explain the advantages and disadvantages of radio advertising. How is the radio audience measured?
    Topic: Sponsorship and Advertising Media

    Advantages of radio advertising: (1) it is a low cost option; (2) it has definable target markets based on the station's format; (3) radio stations offer considerable flexibility and a short lead time; (4) intimacy - e.g. FM stations and liking for presenters and DJs; and (5) mobility - its portability makes it mobile and it can be taken anywhere. Disadvantages: (1) short exposure time - radio ads normally last only 15 or 30 seconds and listeners busy with other activities may not register them; (2) target duplication - several stations may try to reach the same target market, and advertising on all of them may not be financially feasible; (3) overloading of ads - too much information is put in one ad, so very little is retained; and (4) loyal listenership - radio stations have loyal listeners who do not prefer to listen to other stations. The radio audience is measured through coverage (the most basic measure - the number of homes in a geographic area able to pick up the station clearly), circulation (the number of listeners listening regularly), competing programs, and the timings of programs.

  42. Explain transit advertising and billboard advertising, with their advantages and disadvantages.
    Topic: Sponsorship and Advertising Media

    Transit advertising consists of paper posters placed inside or on transit vehicles and in transit stations. Its advantages: exposure to one ad can be long if it is inside a transit vehicle; high frequency; ads outside the vehicle are seen by large and diverse audiences; the ad message can be timely; the method tends to be quite inexpensive; and ads can be somewhat lifestyle targeted by passing through specific neighborhoods. Its disadvantages: ad design is usually limited to the size of the space; people on mass transit are not generally in a receptive mood; transit ads are hard to target; surroundings may distract from the message; and the mass transit environment may not suit the message. Billboard advertising uses big outdoor boards to splash advertisements. Its advantages: big, splashy messages attract attention; the ad has impact and technology has made it more interesting; and the ad reaches a lot of people as they travel the same route every day. Its disadvantages: it is hard to reach a specific audience; creativity is inhibited by space limitations; it is hard to measure its effectiveness; the ad may become weathered and vandalized; and costs, though reasonable, could become quite expensive through innovative adaptations.
