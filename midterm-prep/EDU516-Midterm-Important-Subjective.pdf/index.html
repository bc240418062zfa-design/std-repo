  1. What do you know about scanning?
    Topic: Reading Skills

    When scanning takes place, everything comes in a detailed form. At the primary level, children can be given to recognize the letters, words and the meanings of words; for example, words may be given in colours and students are asked to look at the words and find a particular letter. For secondary school students it can be the use of words, their meanings and spellings, and for even higher level students they may be asked to look for the meaning of words in context; high achievers can even read between the lines. Thus there are levels of scanning according to the level of the students, and guidance and clear instructions are very important for scanning.

  2. Explain skimming with an example.
    Topic: Reading Skills

    Skimming is done when we just want to get a gist of what is going on; for example, we skim through the newspaper to see the headlines or read the news lines on the TV screen. It is not done for exam preparation or to comprehend any sort of content. We need to inculcate the skill of skimming in our students so that they know how to go through content when they do not have enough time to read it thoroughly. It gives students a good idea of what they are going to read, so it is also called pre-reading. It is also done in brainstorming sessions; some students create new words and ideas come to their mind, it boosts creativity, and they make conclusions about what they are reading.

  3. What are the objectives of close reading?
    Topic: Reading Skills

    Close reading is related to the development of writing skills. A particular text is given to the students and no other choice is given to them; for example, if we want them to develop the skill of report writing, we give them lots of reports in the newspapers to read. The teacher needs to let them know the objectives of close reading: they are supposed to read the features of that writing and then design a similar text. The students should sequence the features; for example the incident, title, date and timing, venue, relate the incident and in the end relate what is going to happen. The teacher should also put these features on the board so that the students know what they are going to observe in the given text. The three important factors in close reading are literal meaning, inferences and contextual meaning.

  4. What is personal response?
    Topic: Reading Skills

    Personal response is the response that the teacher expects from the students after the reading activity. While doing the reading activity, students need to know what the comprehensive text is. In order to do that, the teacher needs to do a lot before starting the reading activity: the pre-reading, prediction and skimming help the students get an idea of what they are going to read and thus help in comprehending the text. The teacher needs to develop the whole environment of understanding of the text to prepare them for what they are going to read. Students should be made familiar with the reading material, maybe by giving them the words that they are going to encounter in the reading material. Giving the reading pieces in bits and reading in bits does not allow the students to comprehend the text.

  5. Define elision.
    Topic: Reading Skills

    The time gap that we give while talking is called elision. It is actually related to speaking; however, speaking skills are greatly affected by our reading skills, and thus it is important to be careful about noticing elision while reading. This is especially true for literature reading, as there are elision points in the dialogues. They may be shown as a dot, dash or sometimes empty quotation marks, which show that the speaker was taking a pause. The teacher should read the text with elision in a similar manner as the speaker would have said it. This is natural language, as we take pauses to think while speaking, and the teacher should bring the attention of students towards elision during the reading activity so that the students use elision themselves when they write stories and dialogues. Elision is an important language feature.

  6. Name and briefly describe the types of text.
    Topic: Writing Skills and Types of Text

    There are six types of text: (1) Narrative, (2) Argumentative, (3) Dialogue, (4) Exposition, (5) Report, and (6) Discussion. Narrative writing is like telling a story. An argumentative text presents an argument with two sides of a point, supported by dialogues and counter-arguments. Dialogue writing presents the exact words of speakers in inverted commas. Exposition explains and gives information about a topic. A report is based on reported speech and transfers information about an event in one's own words. A discussion text discusses an issue, presenting opinions of different sides.

  7. What do you know about argumentative text?
    Topic: Writing Skills and Types of Text

    An argumentative text is similar to debates, but debates are oral and argumentative writing is a written form of debates. It is usually an argument between two people. There is a point taken with two sides of a coin, while both sides are trying to prove a point. There is a sequence in the dialogues from both sides: one point is being discussed and then there is a counter-argument. Both sides of the argument are to be taken into consideration, and the opinion of both or more parties should be included in the dialogue writing. Punctuation is very important in the conversation to make the dialogues clear; without punctuation the arguments will not be shown like a dialogue. Some words which are used in spoken language can be used in argumentative writing as well, for example "In my opinion", "I think"; such phrases will make the argument evident. Polite phrases can be used according to the scenario, for example "thank you for your opinion but..."

  8. Write a note on dialogue writing.
    Topic: Writing Skills and Types of Text

    Dialogue is used in all types of texts and can sometimes be used in narrative writing. The dialogue should be written in inverted commas to clarify that these are the exact words used by the speaker. Reported speech may be used without inverted commas, for example "He said that..." In an argumentative text a lot of dialogues are used. A statement will be using a full stop, an exclamation can be ended with an exclamation mark, and a question should end with a question mark. An elision can be shown by using a dash. The state of the speaker can be mentioned in parenthesis after the spoken words, for example: he said "...." (angrily).

  9. What are gap filling exercises? How are they useful?
    Topic: Writing Skills and Types of Text

    Gap filling exercises, traditionally called fill in the blanks, are exercises to develop the reading and writing skills and how an essay is to be read. This exercise can be useful for all levels. They enable students to form meaningful sentences and also develop the skill for looking for vocabulary. Instead of students looking for words and wasting time, the teacher should provide them with the list of words; in order to achieve the aim, she should facilitate the students with a word bank. The teacher can mention how many words are required in the gap by mentioning the number in the bracket at the end of the sentence, and she can also fill the first one to help the students. In primary school, the teacher should supervise the students in this exercise.

  10. How can a teacher help students in grammar and vocabulary during creative writing?
    Topic: Writing Skills and Types of Text

    Writing should encourage creativity, and the skill of creating must be instilled in children of all social backgrounds. Teachers need to teach grammar and spellings yet polish the imagination of students. Students should be taught to note down creative ideas whenever they come to their mind; this habit can be developed by carrying a notebook with them. Sometimes students are creative in their thinking but not in writing; here the teacher needs to help them with the grammar of sentences to remove the barrier to creative writing. Students should not be discouraged if there are grammar mistakes; the teacher should build confidence in students to enhance their creative writing skills. Vocabulary is like hunting for words: teachers can display words on the walls of the classroom to reduce the students' struggle for the right words. Although reading is a great way to increase vocabulary, words close in meaning to the idea in mind should be easily available, as lesser vocabulary would discourage the student from writing creatively.

  11. What is an essay?
    Topic: Writing Skills and Types of Text

    An essay is a collection of sentences and paragraphs. It is usually a part of the literature syllabus, but students should know how to analyze an essay. The beginning of each paragraph must express the purpose of that paragraph, and a new idea must begin in a new paragraph. The linking of paragraphs is also significant to learn. The sentence formation usually has all the parts of speech in it. Students should use different kinds of adjectives; with the use of nouns it is difficult to use different words, e.g. a cupboard will always be a cupboard. It has been seen that students mostly stick to the words "good" and "bad" for adjectives, so teachers should teach students more alternative words, as too much use of the same word would not carry an authentic message. The teacher should encourage creative skills and rich language, and students should be encouraged to write their own opinions and imagination. By reading good essays, students would be able to write good essays.

  12. What do you understand by formal writing? Provide an example.
    Topic: Formal and Informal Writing

    Formal writing has different definitions in different regions in the world. Academic writing like assignments and essays is mostly considered formal. Words in spoken English may not be used in formal writing. There is a proper format for formal writing: there is a beginning, title and ending with a formal greeting. Writing in an official context is an example of formal writing, for example an official letter, an application or a CV written to an office or an institution.

  13. What are the benefits for students to know about the difference between formal and informal writing? Where can these writings be used?
    Topic: Formal and Informal Writing

    Formal writing has different definitions in different regions in the world. Academic writing like assignments and essays is mostly considered formal; words in spoken English may not be used in formal writing, there is a proper format for formal writing, and writing in an official context is an example of formal writing. Informal writing is the opposite of formal writing. Emails are usually written informally, without a title or name, but they can be formal as well; it depends on the receiver. If he or she is in a higher position, for example a boss or a parent, we tend to use formal writing. There is nothing wrong with informal writing; it just has different features and characteristics, and it is mostly used online among young people with their peers. Knowing the difference helps students choose the right style for the right place: formal writing for official and academic purposes, and informal writing for personal communication.

  14. What are salutations?
    Topic: Formal and Informal Writing

    Salutations are phrases like "Dear Father" or "Dear Friend" etc. Sometimes we use different salutations for respectful relations. For informal writing, we can just use hi or hello. Phrases like "Good morning" and "Good day" can be used in both formal and informal writing. Some salutations are fixed for particular persons like a legal officer, political leader and royal family member; in that case "Your Highness" or "Most Respected" would be used when the position of that person is limited in number. These can be used for a mayor, governor, judge and sometimes a teacher.

  15. What is reflective writing?
    Topic: Formal and Informal Writing

    Reflective writing can be both under formal and informal writing; it depends on the organization and culture. It is something that we write reflecting back on what we have done in order to keep a record for further references. It is usually demanded by an institution, and then it is formal writing; it might be published somewhere and read by many people. Any kind of academic writing is formal, and a prospectus is a good example of formal reflective writing. You can write your own reflective writing as well, as in the case of a personal diary where you can jot down your own reflections for improving yourself. This may be only bullet points or like spoken language, for only the writer to read.

  16. How is diary writing considered a form of reflective writing?
    Topic: Formal and Informal Writing

    Diary writing is another form of reflective writing. It can be done while doing an action or after the action has taken place. It is different from reflective writing in the sense that it can be current. It can be called note writing also. It can be used both formally and informally, but mostly it is informal. Students can note down words in a diary for a word bank and a vocabulary building exercise; for formal writing you can use those words for a formal purpose. This is recommended for secondary level students. They can use technology to note down words too, and then they can always convey it into formal writing.

  17. What do you understand by statement of interest?
    Topic: Formal and Informal Writing

    A statement of interest provides information that has to be used at a formal place. It is usually used in applications for jobs or admissions. Teachers should know the structure and format of applications. It should give precise information about the applicant. Students should exhibit the statement of interest, which means telling why they are interested in the job or admission. The statement of interest is based on the attitude and aptitude of the person. It is an important question in job interviews: what is the priority reason for you? Along with being precise, it should have information that is written formally.

  18. What are the elements of CV writing?
    Topic: Formal and Informal Writing

    A CV (Curriculum Vitae) is formal informative writing, as it is about providing facts based on reality. You have to inform the person about yourself, about facts that are always going to stay with you, like name, date of birth, nationality and religion. The layout of the CV is very important, be it for higher education or a job. Its elements are: your details, personal statement, work experience, education, skills, hobbies and interests, and references, presented in a clear and simple layout. It should be organized, starting with educational qualifications with the dates; you should specify if it is a degree, course or certification, and the name of the institute must also be mentioned, whether it was full time or part time. Qualifications should be divided in columns and in chronological order. Extracurricular activities, volunteer and charity work also come under it, and one should check for spelling or typographical errors.

  19. What is a "report"? Elaborate an informal report.
    Topic: Formal and Informal Writing

    A report is a type of text based on reported speech, in which you transfer the information in your own words; it does not use the actual words spoken, known as direct speech. Sometimes a few important dialogues may be used, as in a journalist's report, but usually dialogues are not part of this kind of text. Words like claimed, said and addressed can be used. The date, time and location must be mentioned in a report of a certain event; if the location is mentioned in the title, it may not be used separately. The expressions and personal feelings of the writer should not be included; facts and figures should be used instead of personal feelings. An informal report presents the same factual account in a simpler and less rigid way for familiar readers: it keeps the essential facts, date, time and place of the event, but it is written in an easy, natural style without a strict formal format.

  20. What is the difference between a formal email and an informal email?
    Topic: Formal and Informal Writing

    Email is a very common means of communication and is used in offices mostly; it is a useful substitute for memos which were used to let the staff know about any order by the higher authority. An email is formal when you have to email a person who is higher in position and age, or in a senior office position; in a formal email one cannot take the risk of using words of one's mother tongue or informal language. An informal email is like a chain of communication, just like a conversation; the thread can continue informally within an online group, and it is like responding as if we are talking to a person. Emails have the advantage of replying instantly. Students must be able to identify and compare these differences.

  21. How do you teach students to write a formal letter?
    Topic: Formal and Informal Writing

    When it comes to formal letters, we must address the person with title or surname. Upon ending, we should use greeting phrases like "Best Wishes" or "Yours Sincerely" etc. Usually letters are formal when the recipient is a professional senior, doctor or teacher; it is usually somebody with whom you have a distance of respect. In the content of the letter, it should be within limits and there should be no unnecessary details. Teachers should provide an outline of a formal letter - beginning, middle and end - for the ease of students. The formal manner should be according to the relationship of the recipient and sender. The details in the letter should be relevant to the issue at hand and of course respectful; it has to be to the point and precise.

  22. How can you create a favorable classroom environment for developing speaking skills?
    Topic: Speaking and Listening Skills

    The environment must be natural, not artificial, where people are mocking at each other and looking down upon the speaker. Even if students are making mistakes, there must be no shame in that; mistakes are supposed to help us improve. The atmosphere should not be of shame and guilt; hence the environment must be friendly and accepting of the errors of others, as English is a second language. This way students will not feel hesitation to speak up. The teacher should encourage students to speak in English only, and she herself should avoid the mother tongue; this would make the classroom more favorable. She can put words on the board, walls or charts to help students use them in sentences. The teacher should understand the problems of grammar, pronunciation and punctuation and help them accordingly. She should have encouraging body language and gestures like a warm smile, and she should herself use the language she expects her students to use.

  23. What is the importance of pair work in speaking skills?
    Topic: Speaking and Listening Skills

    Pair work plays an important role in developing speaking skills. Students often feel shy speaking to a higher authority. If this activity is done in pairs, they speak to their partners, and the teacher can supervise where they lack. She can note down their mistakes and give them collective feedback so as not to embarrass the students; this way they would not become conscious. Personal feedback can be given at a later level. In the beginning, teachers should always give collective feedback without taking names; otherwise it would become very demotivating for the students. The teacher should explain her point and then she can observe their progress. Teachers should develop a structure of giving feedback when supervising pair work. It is also important to give positive feedback and praise where due; this would make the feedback authentic, that the teacher was not only pointing out mistakes but also appreciating the effort.

  24. How do language games play a role in learning a language?
    Topic: Speaking and Listening Skills

    Speaking skills are best learnt by fun. It is a natural instinct for people to speak comfortably in their own language, but in order to learn a foreign language it is good not to force people, but to use comedy and fun. When there is no stress on the brain, it is accepted freely. More words are picked up by the learners in a game; funny words are very quickly absorbed by young learners. Words used in movies often become cliches and become popular among the masses, mostly in a comical manner. Games in a classroom are a subtle way to convey the message. A teacher can find many language games online or in books. If a teacher likes to play games, she would become one of them; students can enjoy and learn at the same time. Teachers should, however, maintain discipline in the classroom.

  25. Which different activities are used for "match the words"?
    Topic: Speaking and Listening Skills

    Match the words exercises are useful as they build up vocabulary and understanding. It is started by matching pictures with words; this way students can learn names of things, even in the classroom. In secondary school, the teacher can provide two words which are similar in meaning but the sequence is jumbled up; column A has to be matched with column B. There can be collocations to match the words. This is a useful exercise to enable students to look for another similar word; students can also think of a third word and write that down side by side, and this way they are going to enhance their vocabulary. Teachers should accept those words and not consider them wrong. This is done while reading as well; students can underline words which are similar in meaning.

  26. Define word stress, intonation and collocation.
    Topic: Speaking and Listening Skills

    Word stress is the stress on a word to get meaning; it is important because you can change the meaning of sentences by stressing a particular word, and you can also change its tone. Intonation is the high or low pitch of a spoken sentence; students should know the importance of intonation to bring out the meaning of the sentence, and we are able to explain the meaning of a poem by using the correct stresses. A collocation is two or more words that go well together and are commonly used together, for example "get well"; advertisements use collocations like "buy one get one", and people tend to learn these quickly as they are quite catchy. Collocations are relevant to higher level students and should not be done with primary grades.

  27. What is netiquette?
    Topic: Speaking and Listening Skills

    Netiquette is the etiquette to use language online; the net language also has some manners. Students are usually speaking that language too. Casual and informal language can be used, but there should be restrictions on its use. Since the words are spread to a lot of people, some words should be censored, as some words are not accepted by society members like senior citizens or children.

  28. A teacher should be very patient in a speaking class and should not discourage or cut the conversation of students. How does it help students?
    Topic: Speaking and Listening Skills

    A lot of confidence is needed to speak a foreign language, and it becomes easy when students get into the habit of speaking that language. If the teacher is patient, does not cut the conversation and does not discourage the students, they get enough time and courage to express their ideas without the fear of being stopped or corrected at every step. The teacher is mainly responsible for building confidence for speaking in English; if she appreciates the effort, it can boost the confidence of students. Unfortunately, teachers can hinder the progress of students by being overly critical; therefore praise is very important. Encouragement makes students speak more, and the more they speak, the more fluent and confident they become.

  29. How can a teacher remove hesitation from students?
    Topic: Speaking and Listening Skills

    The teacher can remove hesitation by creating a favorable classroom environment which is natural and friendly, where nobody mocks at the speaker and there is no shame or guilt in making mistakes, as mistakes help us improve. She should encourage students to speak in English only and herself avoid the mother tongue. Activities like pair work help, because students feel shy speaking to a higher authority but speak freely with a partner; feedback should be collective in the beginning, without taking names, so that students do not become conscious or demotivated. She should appreciate the effort of students, give positive feedback and praise where due, and use encouraging body language and gestures like a warm smile. Putting helpful words on the board, walls or charts also supports hesitant students while speaking.

  30. How does a good speaker become a good listener? (Why is speaking a product of listening?)
    Topic: Speaking and Listening Skills

    It is common to give sheets of paper to students to write new words they have encountered; this way students will try to focus more and become good listeners. Speaking is a product of listening, and this way speaking would also be improved. The content of the audio also helps students gain insight into how words are used. A person who listens carefully learns the correct pronunciation, intonation and use of words, and this listening practice in turn makes him a good speaker.

  31. What is a conditional? Also mention the types of conditionals.
    Topic: Grammar Use

    A conditional is a sentence that presents a condition (usually with "if") and its result; the result depends on the condition. There are three main types of conditionals. The first conditional is based on natural and factual things that are possible to happen, e.g. the water will boil at 100 C. The second conditional is based on wishes and things that are not really possible, e.g. "If I had money, I would buy a house." The third conditional is based on regret about the past, e.g. "If I had worked hard, I would have scored maximum marks."

  32. Why is the third conditional called "regret with a condition"?
    Topic: Grammar Use

    Third conditionals are mostly based on regret. Modal words are also used along with the use of "if", e.g. "If I had worked hard, I would have scored maximum marks." The condition here is of working hard; now I cannot work hard, the time has gone. The verb is in the past as it is showing regret. These regrets cannot be undone now; they have already caused harm. The condition is that this action should have been done in the past, therefore the verb is in the past. To put it simply, it is regret with a condition.

  33. What is the first conditional? Write an example.
    Topic: Grammar Use

    First conditionals are easy to understand. These are things that would be possible to happen; they are natural and factual. For example, the water will boil at 100 C. It is different from future tense as it has a condition: it is a scientific fact, it is going to happen, but it will only boil at 100 C, otherwise it will not boil. The teacher can ask the students to underline the words that make the sentence a conditional.

  34. What is the second conditional? Give an example.
    Topic: Grammar Use

    Second conditionals are on a second level degree. They are based on wishes, for example "If I had money, I would buy a house"; the condition is if the person had the money. The sentence carries a meaning of wish, and the use of "if" shows the wish. "Would" is the modal word here. Second conditionals are also used when things are not really possible; usually the facts are in first conditionals, and impossible wishes are in second conditionals - things that are never going to happen, e.g. "If I was a billionaire, I would buy the world."

  35. What is present simple tense? Give two examples.
    Topic: Grammar Use

    Present simple is the first tense students learn. What is happening at present is being described in present simple tense. An action verb and a subject are what make a present simple tense; a subject can be a noun or a pronoun, and an object may be added. The best way to learn it is with examples like: I eat, I go, I run, I laugh; e.g. "I eat an apple." Students must be aware of proper grammar use in writing, and while reading, students should identify grammar usage, as tenses are the most important feature of grammar.

  36. How can you teach past simple tense to students?
    Topic: Grammar Use

    Past simple is something similar to present simple, but the difference is in time; the time has passed now. "I eat" now becomes "I ate". A teacher can draw a timeline, as it is a useful tool in grammar lessons and a common practice at all levels. Jumping from present to past would make students understand it well. The past tenses of verbs must be taught to students; the teacher can make columns of verbs in random order and ask students to match them. It is not a good practice to memorize the past forms of verbs; students should be able to consult the teacher for help.

  37. What strategy would you use to teach future tense to your students?
    Topic: Grammar Use

    Future tense is about what is going to happen in the future; the action is a planning or a wish. Future tense consists of a subject, the future form of the verb and the object, for example "I will eat an apple tomorrow." The use of "will" and "shall" with the verb is very important. Words like later, tomorrow and afterwards specify that the action will be done in the future. The teacher can teach it through such examples and add the extensions of the future tenses according to the need of the students; students can add phrases of their own choice as well.

  38. What is the present perfect tense? State an example.
    Topic: Grammar Use

    The present perfect tense has to be in present form but it uses a past participle. For example, "I have written a letter"; here "written" is the past participle and the tense is present. It indicates that this action was done not a long time ago but in the recent past. It can be in answer to the question: "Have you written the letter?" It is taught in secondary school mostly.

  39. What is team teaching (team teach)?
    Topic: Planning, Resources and Assessment

    Team teach means you can have an assistant teacher in your class. Both of you can work together and supplement each other, and it is going to benefit both the players. Classroom management can be much better in this way: one teacher can handle individual tasks and the other teacher can manage the overall class. Junior school teachers can team teach with senior school teachers to learn their techniques. It is a matter of team playing, learning from each other's experiences and also appreciating each other's efforts. A change in the classroom environment is a good way to motivate the students, especially those who are shy. Later the teacher can do a survey to assess if the students enjoyed the team teach.

  40. What is needs analysis?
    Topic: Planning, Resources and Assessment

    Needs analysis means finding out what the learners need while learning; this can be for students as well as for the teacher herself. Primary school children show their needs and emotions very well, while secondary students sometimes do not show what they need while learning. There are different ways to do a needs analysis: the teacher can form a questionnaire to ask students what type of learners they are and how they learn best - while reading, listening, memorizing, or looking at pictures. She can herself evaluate how the students have responded to assess how they learn, notice which students need help in which activity, and then make those students do extra work and activities. Some students may need more appreciation than others; this should not be ignored, but it should also not look like she is doing favoritism.

  41. What are auxiliaries? Give definition and examples.
    Topic: Planning, Resources and Assessment

    Auxiliaries are helping verbs that are used with the main verb to form tenses, questions, negatives and the passive voice; they help the main verb express time, mood and voice. The primary auxiliaries are be (am, is, are, was, were), do (do, does, did) and have (has, have, had). For example: "She is reading a book" (be), "Do you speak English?" (do), "They have finished their work" (have). The modal auxiliaries - can, could, shall, will, may, might, must, ought to - express ability, permission, possibility, obligation etc., for example "You ought to respect your teachers."

  42. What is the difference between assessment for learning and assessment of learning?
    Topic: Planning, Resources and Assessment

    Assessment for learning is formative assessment: it takes place during the learning process, while teaching is going on. Its purpose is to check students' understanding, identify their difficulties and give them feedback so that learning can be improved; it is a part of teaching itself. Assessment of learning is summative assessment: it takes place at the end of a lesson, unit or course, for example in tests and examinations. Its purpose is to measure and report how much the students have achieved against the set objectives, usually in the form of marks or grades. In short, assessment for learning helps learning to happen, while assessment of learning measures the learning that has happened.

  43. Give three examples of reflexive pronouns.
    Topic: Planning, Resources and Assessment

    Reflexive pronouns are pronouns that refer back to the subject of the sentence; they end in -self (singular) or -selves (plural): myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves. Three examples are: (1) I completed my homework myself. (2) She prepared the lesson plan herself. (3) The students solved the exercise themselves.

  44. Why are resources used in the classroom?
    Topic: Planning, Resources and Assessment

    Resources are used in the classroom to support and enrich teaching and learning. Vocabulary, for example, can be provided by resources: for primary school students, teachers can provide word banks on charts, board and slides, and for higher level students there can be a resource corner in the classroom with newspapers and magazines for consulting when students are short of vocabulary. This reduces the burden on the teacher and enables students to work independently, and students develop a sense of responsibility and confidence. Resources also cater to different types of learners - pictures and visuals help visual learners, audio tapes help audio learners - and make the lesson interesting and clear.

  45. What are the benefits of recording a lesson for a teacher?
    Topic: Planning, Resources and Assessment

    Recording a lesson helps the teacher in self-reflection. By watching or listening to her own recorded lesson, the teacher can observe her lesson delivery, her language, questioning, explanations and classroom management objectively, just as an observer would. She can identify her strengths and the areas that need improvement, such as unclear instructions, uneven participation of students or mistakes in language use. It also allows her to compare her progress over time and to share the lesson with a critical friend or senior teacher for feedback and professional development.
