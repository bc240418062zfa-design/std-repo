  4. Create a conflict situation where conflict is competitive. (Marks: 3)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A competitive conflict is a conflict in which the disputants believe that when one disputant helps him- or herself, the other disputant is humbled, quieted or made less powerful; it is a win-lose situation. Situation: The sales team of a toy manufacturing company claims that it boosted sales in the current year as compared to previous years, so there should be extra bonuses for the welfare and encouragement of the team, but the management is not ready to announce extra bonuses according to the efforts of the sales team. In this situation a competitive (win-lose) conflict will arise, as the staff demands more bonuses after claiming increased sales and the management is not prepared to announce more bonuses.

  16. What are the advantages of collaborative negotiation? (Marks: 3)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    People tending towards a collaborative style try to meet the needs of all the people involved. This style is useful when you need to bring together a variety of viewpoints to get the best solution, when there have been previous conflicts in the group, or when the situation is too important for a simple trade-off. Its advantages are that it produces solutions that satisfy the interests of all disputants, preserves and strengthens relationships, and creates durable settlements because no party feels defeated.

  17. In which situation may the competitive style of negotiation be used? Justify. (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    People who tend towards a competitive style take a firm stand and know what they want. They usually operate from a position of power, drawn from things like position, rank, expertise, or persuasive ability. This style can be useful when there is an emergency and a decision needs to be made fast; when the decision is unpopular; or when defending against someone who is trying to exploit the situation selfishly. However, it can leave people feeling bruised, unsatisfied and resentful when used in less urgent situations.

  18. Describe in which situations avoiding may be the best approach to conflict resolution. (Marks: 3)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    People tending towards the avoiding style seek to avoid the conflict completely. This style is characterized by delegating controversial decisions, accepting default decisions, and not wanting to hurt anyone's feelings. It can be suitable when victory is impossible, when the argument is unimportant, or when someone else is in a better position to solve the problem. However, in many situations this is a weak and ineffective approach to take.

  19. What basic points should be considered before consulting an interest tree? (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Tips for interest trees; an interest tree must include the following points: 1. There must always be needs; other elements are optional. 2. There may be multiple levels of underlying interests. 3. Each position, aspiration, interest, and principle/value rectangle must logically relate (directly or indirectly) to one or more need rectangles. 4. Don't confuse interests with facts or contentions.

  20. Describe the elements of business relationships. (Marks: 3)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A business relationship is designed for people who must be involved in a relationship but who have very low levels of trust in each other. A business relationship has the following components: 1. Explicit and detailed agreements 2. Formal, preferably written communication 3. No sharing of emotion 4. Balanced, neutral assessment methods

  21. Define displaced conflict, give an example, and explain its resolution. (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Displaced conflict: There is an unacknowledged conflict, and the disputants are disputing over something else. Example: Business partners who have an unacknowledged conflict over the allocation of rights and responsibilities dispute about a minor aspect of the business. Resolution: A displaced conflict cannot be settled by resolving the surface dispute, because the real issue remains hidden. It is resolved by diagnosing the conflict correctly, bringing the unacknowledged underlying conflict into the open, and then addressing the real dispute about rights and responsibilities directly.

  22. What do you know about the Dual Concern Model? Discuss in detail. (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The Dual Concern Model explains negotiating styles on the basis of two concerns: concern for self and concern for others. Its five styles are: 1. The avoiding style, which represents a low level of concern for both self and other. 2. The dominating (or competing) style, which represents a high level of concern for self and a low level of concern for other. 3. The obliging (or accommodating) style, which represents a low level of concern for self and a high level of concern for other. 4. The integrating (or collaborating or problem-solving) style, which represents a high level of concern for both self and other. 5. The compromising style, which represents a moderate level of concern for self and other.

  23. Differentiate between low level trust and mistrust. (Marks: 3)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Mistrust is a state in which one individual actively believes that the other is likely to harm him or her, whereas low level of trust is simply the absence of a positive belief that the other intends to be helpful. Trust is difficult to establish when mistrust is present. Mistrust and low levels of trust are the engines driving conflict escalation: low levels of trust create the suspicion, circumspection, and defensive tactics that promote inefficiency, bad feelings and disputants' efforts to undermine one another.

  24. Explain meta dispute and loss of ripeness. (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Meta-dispute: Meta-disputes are disputes about the way a conflict is being handled. Unresolved and escalating conflict breeds meta-disputes; the more the conflict is complex, the more the chances of evolving meta-disputes. Meta-disputes are generally based on misunderstandings. A simple meta-dispute is an interpersonal conflict (dispute) over the way another interpersonal conflict is being handled. Loss of ripeness: Resolving a conflict is perceived as costly, difficult, and unpleasant. Many times, disputants won't confront the work needed to resolve a conflict until they find no alternative. This situation of conflict is called ripeness; when disputants lose this readiness and stop feeling that the time is right to settle, it is a loss of ripeness, which becomes an impediment to settlement.

  25. Discuss the managerial actions that can give rise to a workplace conflict. (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Types of managerial actions that cause workplace conflicts: 1. Poor communications: (a) Employees experience continuing surprises; they aren't informed of new decisions, programs, etc. (b) Employees don't understand reasons for decisions; they aren't involved in decision-making. (c) As a result, employees trust the “rumor mill” more than management. 2. The alignment or the amount of resources is insufficient: (a) Disagreement about “who does what”. (b) Stress from working with inadequate resources. 3. “Personal chemistry”, including conflicting values or actions among managers and employees, for example: (a) Strong personal natures don't match. (b) We often don't like in others what we don't like in ourselves. 4. Leadership problems, including inconsistent, missing, too-strong or uninformed leadership (at any level in the organization), evidenced by: (a) Avoiding conflict, “passing the buck” with little follow-through on decisions. (b) Employees see the same continued issues in the workplace. (c) Supervisors don't understand the jobs of their subordinates.

  26. Discuss the basic needs of humans regarding the conflict onion. (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Lying even more deeply at the heart of the conflict onion than principles and values are basic human needs. People are not able to express easily the basic human needs that lie at the heart of a conflict onion; they must be inferred from what people say and do and the circumstances they are in. A good interest analysis should include basic human needs. Basic human needs are basic, and a failure to deal with and address them creates pressure for them to be expressed in other ways. Theories about human development and human drives provide a conflict diagnostician with guidance about basic human needs.

  27. How can we analyze the interests of agents and advocates? (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    An interest analysis should explore the interests, values, and needs of the agents and advocates on all sides of the conflict. The basic reason that interest analysis should include the agents and advocates of one's team is to clarify whether they have problematic conflicts of interest with their principal. It also helps to better understand the complexities of what is motivating the “other team” and to develop coping strategies, and to develop an understanding of the motivators of other team members and strategize to cope with such conflicts.

  28. How are conflict gamers different from conflict professionals? (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Conflict gamers: Conflict gamers love interpersonal conflict and feel the most alive when up to their necks in it. They don't seem to need to prepare for a negotiation; their innate personality and temperament alone seem to be preparation enough. They jump at the chance for a rumble. In a negotiation, they seem utterly fearless. They are always ready to inflict punishment on their adversaries. After litigation is over, win or lose, over drinks or lunch conflict gamers express what a profound pleasure it all was, what a rush, and how it resembled the happy days they once spent in high school. Conflict professionals: Conflict diagnosis is also for conflict professionals and professionals-in-training seeking to enrich their understanding of their field. For example, for a lawyer, a judge, a paralegal etc., the course helps a lot in diagnosing conflict and applying ADR techniques for conflict management. Applying these techniques to conflicts enables conflict professionals to find the magic keys to unlocking their clients' potential power to settle their differences.

  29. Why does ADR fail? (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Following are the four reasons why ADR (strategies of change) fail: 1. Resistance and lack of support from others. 2. Application of existing (traditional) ways and means, rather than innovative solutions. 3. Lack of proficiency in using innovative tools. 4. Support by social structures of traditional ways to resolve disputes.

  30. Elaborate BATNA and the radical perspective in conflict diagnosis. Also list the steps of conflict diagnosis. (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    BATNA means the Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement. A disputant should develop a list of alternatives to a negotiated agreement, including the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, or BATNA. If you are a disputant, agent or an advocate, develop plans for clarifying these alternatives and improving them. An accurate estimate of BATNA protects the disputant from making irrational choices and from being unduly pressurized into a settlement. Steps in conflict diagnosis: 1. Describe/map the conflict 2. Identify sources 3. Analyze interests 4. Characterize the conflict 5. Consider trust 6. Identify impediments to settlement 7. Address negotiation styles and preferences 8. Consider power 9. Identify/maximize the Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement (BATNA) 10. Consider diversity issues

  31. Explain the merits of a high level of trust. (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A high level of trust is given importance because it has certain advantages, such as: 1. Less guesswork 2. Fewer formalities; greater efficiency 3. Pleasant feelings and a comfortable work environment 4. It fosters cooperation 5. Better quality of life for everyone

  32. What are the techniques to transform competitive conflict into cooperative conflict? (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The methods and techniques to transform competitive conflict into cooperative conflict are: 1. Choose language with care 2. Assign joint tasks 3. Expand the pie 4. Establish ground rules for civility in communication 5. Create or focus on a common enemy 6. Point out areas of agreement 7. Focus blame away from the disputant and towards process 8. Prepare “the case” 9. Use trust-building exercises 10. Set up structure to create sharing of information

  33. What are the basic components of the conflict onion? (Marks: 3)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The components of the conflict onion are: 1. Position: The demand the disputant makes to others. 2. Aspiration: The bottom line the disputant is looking for. 3. Underlying interest: The reason for the aspirations. 4. Principles and values: Beliefs and moral codes that influence the interests. 5. Basic human needs: Underlying needs that drive the motivations of the disputant.

  34. You desire your work team to complete a project within 2 weeks. Doing extra time will be needed. Most of the team members are resisting the extra time. How will you perceive this conflict (cooperative or competitive) and why? (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    This conflict should be perceived as cooperative. Conflict situations offer each of us an opportunity to choose a style for responding to the conflict. The cooperative problem-solving style enables people to work together so everyone can win. Using this style, people try to find a solution that will help everyone meet their interests and help everyone maintain a good relationship. The manager can discuss the deadline openly with the team, understand the members' concerns about extra time, and jointly find a way, such as sharing the extra work or adjusting priorities, so that the project is completed in time without treating the team as an opponent.

  35. What aspect enhances the importance of conflict management? (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Conflict management involves implementing strategies to limit the negative aspects of conflict and to increase the positive aspects of conflict at a level equal to or higher than where the conflict is taking place. Furthermore, the aim of conflict management is to enhance learning and group outcomes (effectiveness or performance in an organizational setting). Because conflict is unavoidable in organizations and can either damage or improve performance depending on how it is handled, the ability to manage it constructively enhances its importance.

  36. One of your subordinates has denied to perform a certain task assigned by you to him. What sort of heuristics may occur in determining your reaction to this situation? (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    In reacting to the subordinate's refusal, several heuristics (mental shortcuts used in the interpretational phase of perception) may occur: 1. I may assume the refusal was intentional defiance, although the actor may not have intended his action that way; for example, he may be overloaded or may not have understood the task. 2. Negative heuristics associated with escalated conflict may operate; under stress and with reduced mental and emotional resources, I may interpret the refusal as laziness or disrespect. 3. A self-fulfilling prophecy may follow: if I treat him as uncooperative, he may actually become defensive and uncooperative. A manager should therefore check his interpretation, use his legitimate power through the proper way, and consider the subordinate's previous performing attitude before reacting.

  37. What is a caucus?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A caucus is a meeting between a mediator and one disputant (with or without the disputant's representatives), out of the earshot of the other disputant and his or her representatives. A caucus is different from a joint session, in which all the disputants involved in mediation, and/or their representatives, participate together.

  38. What is trust? Also describe the three levels of trust.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Trust can be defined as a state of mind in which a person believes that another person intends to be helpful and, accordingly, that it is appropriate to take risks in the relationship. Trust can be described as a basic component of human relationships. The three levels of trust are: 1. Calculus-based trust 2. Knowledge-based trust 3. Identification-based trust

  39. What is ADR? Give its definition.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    ADR (Alternative Dispute Resolution) is the general term that includes conflict management, mediation, arbitration, and other processes that are alternatives to the judicial system.

  40. Discuss the quality of ADR.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Though saving time and money are important goals, if the process is faulty, long-term efficiency is lost, and so the quality of the dispute resolution process and outcome must be considered. There is little evidence that this longer-term assessment of long-term efficiency and effectiveness is taking place. Assessments of the quality of ADR are confounded by the lack of empirical research to sufficiently distinguish among forms of ADR. Conflict diagnosis ideas will also help researchers and policy makers to design better studies and to interpret studies more effectively.

  41. What are the perspectives on the handling of interpersonal conflict?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    An evaluation of interpersonal conflict depends on how it is handled. Conflict diagnosis allows the user to choose the best blueprint and the best tools to handle a conflict well. Conflict can have positive and negative consequences, and perspective is critical in discussing them. The perspectives are: a. Time perspective: short-term, intermediate-term, long-term. b. Person perspective: individual-disputant, joint-disputant, systemic, institution or society-wide. c. Issues perspective: process versus outcome; narrow versus broad focus; monetary or economic versus non-monetary, tangible or intangible; prospective versus retrospective.

  42. What are the types of assisted negotiation?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The various types of assisted negotiation are: 1. Agent or advocate-assisted negotiation: disputants' representatives conduct the negotiation. 2. Mediation: a neutral third party assists the disputants in settling the dispute. 3. Nonbinding evaluation: a neutral third party renders a nonbinding evaluation of the conflict.

  43. Differentiate between simple negotiation and assisted (facilitated) negotiation.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    There are two forms of negotiation: Simple negotiation: In this type of negotiation, the only participants are the disputants themselves. Assisted (or facilitated) negotiation: In assisted negotiation, the disputants are joined by others, such as agents, advocates or a neutral third party, who help them in conducting the negotiation.

  44. What is interest analysis?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Interest analysis is perhaps the most critical step in the conflict diagnosis process. A systematic exploration of a conflict participant's interests is called interest analysis. Briefly stated, interest analysis is the development of an accurate and complete understanding of each conflict participant's positions, aspirations, interests, needs and values in relation to the interpersonal conflict. Interest analysis includes an explanation of all the underlying interests, needs, and values of each conflict participant, as well as an exploration of how all link together and are organized.

  45. Differentiate between competitive and cooperative conflicts.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    1. Competitive conflicts: Competitive conflicts occur when two parties believe that they are right and the other one is wrong and will not agree in the middle. It is a win-lose situation. These conflicts can ruin a relationship and lead to a dysfunctional, destructive outcome. 2. Cooperative conflicts: Cooperative conflicts occur when two parties agree to compromise to end a conflict; each party may need to give up something to get something in return. It is a win-win situation. These conflicts build trust for the future and allow parties to accomplish their present goals together.
