  1. Define cloud computing according to NIST and list its five essential characteristics.
    Topic: Introduction to Cloud Computing

    According to NIST, "Cloud computing is a model for enabling ubiquitous, convenient, on-demand network access to a shared pool of configurable computing resources (e.g., networks, servers, storage, applications, and services) that can be rapidly provisioned and released with minimal management effort or service provider interaction." The five essential characteristics are: (1) On-demand self-service — a consumer can unilaterally provision computing capabilities automatically without human interaction with the provider; (2) Broad network access — capabilities are available over the network through standard mechanisms for heterogeneous devices such as mobile phones, tablets, laptops and workstations; (3) Resource pooling — the provider's resources are pooled to serve multiple consumers, who generally have no control or knowledge over the exact location of the resources; (4) Rapid elasticity — capabilities can be elastically provisioned and released with demand and appear unlimited to the consumer; (5) Measured service — resource use is automatically controlled and optimized through a metering capability, typically on a pay-per-use basis.

  2. Differentiate between cluster, grid and cloud computing.
    Topic: History, Cluster and Grid Computing

    A computer cluster is a collection of interconnected stand-alone computers which cooperate to work as a single resource pool; the nodes are usually homogeneous, owned by one organization and located together. In grid computing, computational resources spread across the nation or the globe, owned by different organizations, are shared by multiple users to solve large-scale computational problems, giving the illusion of a virtual supercomputer; several organizations may unite to form a Virtual Organization (VO). Unlike the cloud, grid resources do not join or leave dynamically and the majority of resources are not provisioned from data centers. Cloud computing, on the other hand, provides dynamically scalable, virtualized IT resources as a charged service over the Internet from data centers, with rapid elasticity and measured (pay-per-use) service.

  3. What is a computer cluster? State the important benefits of cluster computing.
    Topic: History, Cluster and Grid Computing

    A computer cluster is a collection of interconnected stand-alone computers which cooperate to work as a single resource pool of computing resources. Clusters became popular in the 1990s when mainframes and traditional supercomputers were becoming less cost-effective for high performance computing (HPC); in 2010, 85% of the top 500 supercomputers were computer clusters built with homogeneous nodes. Important benefits of cluster computing are: (1) Scalability — more nodes can be added to increase computing power; (2) High availability and fault tolerance — failure of one node does not stop the whole system; (3) Use of commodity computers — inexpensive off-the-shelf machines are used instead of costly special-purpose hardware. Cluster computing has laid the foundation of modern supercomputers, computational grids and cloud computing.

  4. Describe the layered architecture of grid computing.
    Topic: History, Cluster and Grid Computing

    The grid middleware layered architecture, deployed on the participant computers, consists of the following layers: (1) Application layer — the top layer, consisting of user applications to be run on the grid; (2) Collective services — focus on interaction among the resources and implement functions such as resource discovery, scheduling and brokering; (3) Resource service layer — deals with the aggregated computing resources (software and hardware) available for user applications in collective operations; (4) Connectivity layer — provides the core networking among the computational resources of the fabric layer through physical or virtual networking; (5) Grid fabric — consists of all the computational resources such as storage systems, catalogs, network resources, servers and their network connections.

  5. Explain the Lead, Lag and Match strategies adopted in IT capacity planning.
    Topic: Business Drivers and Capacity Planning

    IT capacity planning is the estimation and fulfillment of future IT requirements of an organization. Companies usually adopt one of the following strategies: (1) Lead strategy — adding new IT capacity in anticipation of future needs, i.e., capacity is added before the demand actually arises; (2) Lag strategy — adding new IT capacity when the IT resources reach full utilization, i.e., capacity is added only after the existing resources are fully used; (3) Match strategy — adding IT capacity in small increments, closely following the growth in demand. Over-provisioning (acquiring more equipment than the estimated requirements) causes over-expenditure, while under-provisioning leaves the equipment inadequate for future requirements. Capacity planning may lead an organization to adopt cloud computing and plan for renting cloud resources instead of purchasing IT equipment.

  6. Differentiate between simplex, half-duplex and full-duplex data flow with examples.
    Topic: Basics of Data Communication

    (1) Simplex: It is unidirectional communication in which only one of the devices can transmit and the other can only receive. Examples: keyboard (input only) and monitor (output only). (2) Half duplex: Both devices can communicate but only one at a time; the entire capacity of the transmission medium is available to the transmitting device. Example: walkie-talkies. (3) Full duplex: Both devices can send and receive at the same time; the transmission medium provides separate paths (channels) for transmission of each device. Example: a telephone conversation.

  7. Compare LAN, MAN and WAN.
    Topic: Basics of Computer Networking

    LAN (Local Area Network): A privately owned network with the scope of an office, building or a campus; each host has a unique identifier/address; bit rate ranges from 1 Mbps to 1 Gbps; media may be wired/wireless and typical LANs include Ethernet, Token Ring, FDDI and ATM. MAN (Metropolitan Area Network): A network covering a large geographical area bigger than a LAN and smaller than a WAN, with a diameter of 5 to 50 km covering several buildings or a whole city; it is generally not owned by a single organization and its equipment is usually owned by a service provider; it provides high-speed connectivity for sharing regional resources. WAN (Wide Area Network): A network that spans a large geographical area such as towns, cities, states or even countries and usually interconnects multiple LANs; unlike a LAN, a WAN is normally created and run by communication companies and leased to user organizations; its types are point-to-point WAN and switched WAN.

  8. Differentiate between circuit-switched and packet-switched networks.
    Topic: Basics of Computer Networking

    Circuit-switched network: A dedicated physical connection (circuit) is established, maintained and terminated through a carrier network for each communication session. It is used extensively by telephone companies. It is only useful when all the circuits are being utilized simultaneously; otherwise the network is underutilized. Packet-switched network: It is a WAN switching method in which a single link is shared among multiple network devices. Statistical multiplexing is used to enable the devices to share the packet-switching circuits, so the available capacity is used efficiently and no dedicated connection is reserved for a session.

  9. Explain the classification of IPv4 addresses.
    Topic: TCP/IP, IP Addressing and Ethernet

    The IPv4 address consists of a prefix part (Network ID) and a postfix part (Host ID). The classes are: Class A — 8 bits for Network ID; the network ID starts with binary '0'; total networks are 2^7; first byte range is 0 to 127. Class B — 16 bits for Network ID; the network ID starts with '10'; total networks are 2^14; first byte range is 128 to 191. Class C — 24 bits for Network ID; the network ID starts with '110'; total networks are 2^21; first byte range is 192 to 223. Class D — used for multicasting; there is no prefix or Network ID; first byte range is 224 to 239. Class E — reserved for future use; first byte range is 240 to 255. IPv4 is a 32-bit address, while IPv6 is a 128-bit address.

  10. What is Network Address Translation (NAT)? How does it work?
    Topic: TCP/IP, IP Addressing and Ethernet

    Organizations use private IP addressing for the LAN devices and can use the Network Address Translation (NAT) mechanism to have a single or a few registered global IP addresses for Internet communication. The NAT-enabled router replaces the local address of the sending device with the registered global IP address before sending the packets on the Internet. The mapping of incoming Internet packets is done through a NAT table, which contains the source device local address and the port number of the program along with the corresponding IP address of the Internet device. The internal network is supposed to initiate the Internet communication in the NAT mechanism for the mapping to take place.

  11. Differentiate between TCP and UDP.
    Topic: TCP/IP, IP Addressing and Ethernet

    TCP (Transmission Control Protocol): It is a connection-oriented protocol that provides flow control, congestion control and error control, ensuring reliable, end-to-end and error-free transmission. A TCP message is called a segment. UDP (User Datagram Protocol): It is a lightweight protocol which is not connection-oriented; it does not provide flow, congestion or error control. A UDP message is called a datagram. Both protocols work at the transport layer of the TCP/IP suite and transport a message from an application program on the source host to the corresponding application program on the destination host using port numbers.

  12. What is virtualization? Discuss the role of a hypervisor (VMM).
    Topic: Virtualization

    Virtualization is a technology used to enhance the utilization of computing resources, in which a single hardware machine is multiplexed among multiple virtual machines (VMs). A software-based Virtual Machine Monitor/Manager (VMM), also called a hypervisor, is a program that manages the hardware resources for the VMs and keeps each VM from disrupting other VMs. In hypervisor (bare-metal) architecture, the hypervisor sits between the hardware and the VMs and manages the VMs, e.g., Xen and VMware. Virtualization can be implemented at several levels: instruction set architecture (ISA) level, hardware abstraction level, operating system level, library support level and application level. Reasons for virtualization include sharing of resources, isolation of users of a shared resource, aggregation of smaller resources into a single big virtual resource, easier dynamic relocation/provisioning and easier management of virtual resources.

  13. Differentiate between full virtualization and para-virtualization.
    Topic: Virtualization

    Full virtualization: The guest operating system (the VM's OS) does not know that it is installed on a VM. The virtualization layer manages the hardware acceleration and can be installed on the hardware or on the host's OS. Some instructions of the guest VM are directly run on the hardware to enhance performance. Example: VMware. Para-virtualization: The guest OS is modified to comply with the virtualization layer, and all calls for hardware acceleration are handled by the virtualization layer. Example: KVM. Thus, the main difference is that in full virtualization the guest OS runs unmodified and remains unaware of virtualization, whereas in para-virtualization the guest OS is modified to work with the virtualization layer.

  14. Explain the different options available for VM migration.
    Topic: Virtualization

    VMs can be migrated from one host to another for server load balancing, server consolidation, as a remedy for failover hosts and VMs, and as a remedy for resource shortage for a VM. A VM is made of two basic components: the VM state (processor and RAM contents) and the virtual hard disk. The migration options are: (1) Cold migration — the VM has to be powered-off before migration; (2) Warm migration — migration of a suspended VM; (3) Live migration — migration of a powered-on VM with zero downtime and full availability; during live migration the VM state is copied from the source to the destination host while the state and storage of the VM keep working on the source host to avoid downtime, and modern hypervisors provide unbroken network connectivity during the migration. If the virtual hard disk is on network-accessed shared storage, it need not be copied when the destination host can access that storage.

  15. What is server consolidation? How does virtualization help in data center automation?
    Topic: Virtualization

    Server consolidation is a technique by which more VMs are aggregated on a single server (by migrating jobs/VMs to it) while assuring performance and QoS. This increases resource utilization across the data center, makes more servers available to take more workload, and otherwise idle servers can be shut down to save power. A data center is a facility with networked computers used by organizations to process, store and share large amounts of data, and data center automation refers to the dynamic provisioning of hardware and software resources to millions of users simultaneously. Virtualization helps data center automation because data center automation is triggered by the growth of virtualization products: enhanced resource allocation to jobs/VMs assures performance and QoS, virtualization helps in setting up virtual storage to offer virtual disks to other VMs, and virtualization can synchronize with cloud management systems to dynamically provision cloud services and billing systems. The data center owner has three major considerations: assuring performance and QoS, increasing resource utilization, and saving costs. Hence, virtualization is essential for cloud computing.

  16. Differentiate between horizontal scaling and vertical scaling.
    Topic: Cloud Characteristics, Scaling and Key Terms

    Horizontal scaling: It is the scaling out or scaling in of IT resources of the same type; the number of resources increases or decreases according to the load. Commodity hardware can do the work, IT resources are instantly available and it is not limited by hardware capacity. Vertical scaling: An IT resource is replaced with a resource of higher capacity (scaling up) or with a resource of lower capacity (scaling down) according to the workload. Specialized servers are required, additional setup is required (downtime is required during replacement), it is limited by maximum hardware capacity, and it is less common in the cloud.

  17. Define multitenancy and resiliency in cloud computing.
    Topic: Cloud Characteristics, Scaling and Key Terms

    Multitenancy: It is a software architecture consisting of software executing over a server and serving different users (tenants), whereby each tenant is isolated from the others. The cloud provider pools the IT resources by using multitenancy technology to dynamically assign and reassign these resources according to cloud consumers' demands; the physical as well as virtual resources are multi-tenanted (shared) by using statistical multiplexing. Resiliency: The ability of a computer system to recover from a failure is called resiliency. The redundant implementation of IT resources paves the way to a resilient system; the whole system is pre-configured so that as soon as a resource fails, the processing is automatically handed over to the redundant resource. In cloud computing, the redundancy of IT resources is implemented at different physical locations and/or in different clouds, e.g., data kept at two different locations and replicated so that if the primary hard disk fails, the secondary drive takes over.

  18. Discuss the benefits of cloud computing for cloud consumers.
    Topic: Benefits, Risks and Roles in Cloud Computing

    The immediate benefit of using the cloud is the reduction in initial cost: the cloud saves the initial upfront ownership (capital) costs such as IT equipment, software, networking, construction and installation costs, and the cloud rental can replace the proportional/operational costs. Common measurable benefits for cloud consumers include: pay-as-you-go rental for short-term usage; availability of virtually unlimited resources on demand with negligible wait time for provisioning; IT resources can be added or removed at a fine-grained level, e.g., 1 GB storage increments; applications and resources can be migrated across regions if required; increased scalability, as the cloud can dynamically and instantly provide and release resources, avoiding over-provisioning and under-provisioning; and increased availability and reliability through the modular structure and resource redundancy of the cloud, guaranteed through a Service Level Agreement (SLA) between the cloud provider and the cloud user.

  19. Explain reduced operational governance control and limited portability as risks/challenges of cloud computing.
    Topic: Benefits, Risks and Roles in Cloud Computing

    Reduced operational governance control: The cloud consumer gets a lesser privileged control over the resources leased from the cloud. Risks can arise from how the cloud provider manages the cloud; an unreliable cloud provider may not abide by the guarantees offered in the SLA, which will directly affect the quality of the cloud consumer's solutions that rely upon these services. The cloud consumer should keep track of the actual level of service being provided, and SLA violations can lead to penalties receivable from the cloud provider. Limited portability between cloud providers: Due to the lack of industry standards for cloud computing, public cloud environments remain proprietary to their providers. It is quite challenging to move a custom-built software from one cloud to another if it has dependencies upon the proprietary environment (such as the security framework) of the former cloud.

  20. Describe the roles of cloud provider, cloud consumer, cloud auditor, cloud broker and cloud carrier.
    Topic: Benefits, Risks and Roles in Cloud Computing

    Cloud provider: The organization that provides the IT resources; it is responsible for providing IT resources as per the SLA and performs management and administrative tasks to assure flawless provisioning of cloud services; a cloud provider usually owns the IT resources, though it may also resell services leased from other providers. Cloud consumer: The organization or individual who has contracted with the cloud provider to lease/rent the cloud IT resources through a user interface and/or through software API calls. Cloud auditor: Provides an unbiased assessment of the trust-building features of the cloud, including the security, privacy impact and performance of the cloud; the cloud consumer may rely upon the cloud audit report for choosing a cloud. Cloud broker: A party that provides mediation services to cloud providers (seller) and cloud consumers (buyer) for the purchasing of cloud services. Cloud carrier: The party responsible for providing connectivity between the cloud provider and the cloud consumer; ISPs can be assumed as cloud carriers, and the provider and carrier are in a legal agreement (SLA) to assure a certain level of connectivity and network security.

  21. Compare IaaS, PaaS and SaaS on the basis of the cloud consumer's level of control and common activities.
    Topic: Cloud Service Models

    Control level: In SaaS the consumer has control of usage and usage-related configuration only; in PaaS the consumer has limited administrative control over the programming platform; in IaaS the consumer has full administrative control over the virtual resources of the VMs. Functionality provided: SaaS provides access to a front-end user interface; PaaS provides a moderate level of administrative control over the programming platform; IaaS provides full administrative control over virtual resources. Common activities of the cloud consumer: SaaS — use and configure the service; PaaS — develop, debug and deploy cloud services and cloud-based solutions; IaaS — installation and configuration of software and configuration of the infrastructure of the VM. Common activities of the cloud provider: SaaS — implementation, management and maintenance of the cloud service; PaaS — providing the pre-configured programming platform, middleware and other needed IT resources; IaaS — provisioning and managing the VMs and the underlying physical infrastructure. The three delivery models can also be combined, e.g., PaaS over IaaS, SaaS over PaaS, or SaaS over PaaS over IaaS.

  22. What is SaaS? Give examples of SaaS and state which types of software are not suitable for public SaaS offerings.
    Topic: Cloud Service Models

    According to NIST, SaaS is "software deployed as a hosted service and accessed over the Internet." SaaS is a software solution having the code and data executing and residing on the cloud, hosted over cloud infrastructure and offered as a reusable utility service commercially available to different users; a user accesses the SaaS through a browser. Examples: Salesforce.com for Customer Relationship Management (CRM), Taleo for Human Resources Management, ADP for payroll processing and HRM, Carbonite for file backups, and Microsoft Office 365 for document creation, editing and sharing. Classes of SaaS include business logic (invoicing, CRM), collaboration (email, calendar systems), office productivity (word processors, spreadsheets) and software tools. Software not suitable for public SaaS offerings (according to NIST): (1) Real-time software, which requires precise response time, e.g., flight control systems and factory robots, because of variable response time and network delays; (2) Bulk-consumer data software, where extremely large amounts of data originate physically at the consumer's side, e.g., patient monitoring data, which cannot feasibly be transferred in real time over a WAN; (3) Critical software, whose failure or delay can cause loss of life or property, because continuous acceptable reliability is challenging over unreliable public network access.

  23. What is PaaS? Give examples of PaaS and state the basis of PaaS billing.
    Topic: Cloud Service Models

    PaaS delivers a programming environment containing preconfigured tools to support the development lifecycle of custom applications. According to NIST, PaaS provides a toolkit for conveniently developing, deploying and administering application software which can support a large number of users, process large volumes of data and can be accessed over the Internet. PaaS clouds provide a set of software building blocks, development tools (languages and compilers), supporting runtime environments, tools to deploy the developed applications, and processing, storage and networking resources. PaaS consumers include application developers, application testers, application deployers, application administrators and application end users. Examples: Google App Engine (Java, Python and Go), Force.com of Salesforce.com, LongJump, OpenShift from Red Hat (PHP, Python, Ruby, C), and Windows Azure and SQL Azure from Microsoft. PaaS billing is usually based on: the number of consumers, the kind of consumers (e.g., developers vs. application end users), storage, processing or network resources consumed by the platform, requests serviced, and the time the platform is in use.

  24. What is IaaS? State the scope of control of the provider and the consumer in IaaS, and how IaaS consumers are billed.
    Topic: Cloud Service Models

    In IaaS, the provider makes available the computing resources in the form of VMs; the consumer has the duty of installing the OS and software. The provider also provides stable network access, network components such as firewalls, and data storage. Scope of control: The provider has administrative control over the hypervisor and total control over the hardware layer, and no control over the top three layers (application, middleware/OS as installed by the consumer). The consumer has total control over the top three layers, can request the provider to deliver a VM from the hypervisor layer, and has no control over the hardware layer. IaaS benefits include saving in upfront cost and full administrative control over the VM (start, shut down, pause, installation of OS and applications). Customer billing in IaaS is based on: per CPU hour, data GB stored per hour, network bandwidth consumed and network infrastructure used (e.g., IP addresses) per hour, and value-added services used (e.g., monitoring, automatic scaling).

  25. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of cloud-based data storage.
    Topic: Data Storage and Miscellaneous Services

    In cloud storage, instead of storing data locally, the data is stored on the cloud and accessed through the web, using web browser interfaces, a mounted disk drive that appears local, or API calls. Advantages: (1) Scalability — the user can scale the storage capacity up or down according to requirements; (2) Various convenient costing models are available, from one-time payment to monthly payment to pay-as-per-use; (3) Reliability — storage providers assure data reliability through replication; (4) The data can be accessed worldwide using the Internet; (5) Various methods of data access are available. Disadvantages: (1) Performance — because of Internet-based access, cloud storage can never be as fast as SAN or NAS based local storage; (2) Security — not all users may be able to trust the cloud provider with their data; (3) Data orphans — the user has to trust the data deletion policies of the provider; files deleted by the user may not be immediately (or ever) deleted from the cloud storage.

  26. What is OpenID? How does it work?
    Topic: Data Storage and Miscellaneous Services

    OpenID is a popular example of Identity as a Service (IDaaS). It allows the users to sign in to multiple websites by using a single account, solving problems related to multiple log-in accounts per user: avoiding too many user names and passwords, overcoming the scarcity of desired user names, difficult account management, and avoiding filling long forms again and again. OpenID is not controlled by any organization or person; providers include Google, Microsoft, Yahoo, Amazon and Salesforce, and there are more than 1 billion OpenID accounts accepted by over 50,000 websites. How it works: (1) A user creates an OpenID login through a suitable provider; (2) the user visits a website compatible with OpenID; (3) the site prompts the user to sign in with the OpenID credentials; (4) the user is redirected to the OpenID provider's website; (5) the user opts to share the credentials/token with the visited website; (6) the user provides login and password at the OpenID provider's website; (7) if the user is verified, the OpenID provider confirms to the visited website; (8) the user is redirected to the visited website, which accepts the user as an authenticated user.

  27. Compare the public, private, community and hybrid cloud deployment models.
    Topic: Cloud Deployment Models

    Private cloud: The cloud infrastructure is provisioned for exclusive use by a single organization comprising multiple consumers (e.g., business units); it may be owned, managed and operated by the organization, a third party, or a combination, and may exist on or off premises. Community cloud: The infrastructure is provisioned for exclusive use by a specific community of consumers from organizations that have shared concerns (e.g., mission, security requirements, policy and compliance considerations); at least one member of the community should provide cloud services. Public cloud: The infrastructure is provisioned for open use by the general public and exists on the premises of the cloud provider; the consumer generally has limited upfront cost, limited visibility of the provider's procedures, and is generally not aware of the location of the IT resources. Hybrid cloud: The infrastructure is a composition of two or more distinct cloud infrastructures (private, community or public) which remain unique entities but are bound together by standardized or proprietary technology that enables data and application portability, e.g., for load balancing between clouds.

  28. What is cloud bursting? How is it related to the hybrid cloud?
    Topic: Cloud Deployment Models

    Cloud bursting is the phenomenon whereby a consumer uses a private cloud in routine but may use the services of other types of clouds for load balancing at peak times. Hybrid clouds are often formed when cloud bursting is applied, because the private cloud and the additional public/community cloud together compose a hybrid cloud. Hybrid clouds are also formed when one type of cloud is used to provide backup to another type of cloud; for example, an organization may process sensitive data on an outsourced private cloud but choose new software testing on a public cloud. Challenges for hybrid clouds exist in security management, identity management and access control of multiple clouds, and the scenario becomes more complex when clouds dynamically join and exit the hybrid cloud.

  29. What are web services? Name and explain the core technologies of web services.
    Topic: Service Oriented Architecture

    Web services are independent units of software (code) which allow network-based machine-to-machine interaction. They have no user interface; they process data between computers through API calls, and examples include SOAP and REST based web services. To use a web service residing on a remote server, a program exchanges messages with the service, sending parameters (through an API call) and waiting for the reply; web services are treated as a black box by the programmer and are interoperable, meaning programs written in dissimilar languages can call their API functions. Service Oriented Architecture (SOA) is usually a collection of such services which communicate with each other for the exchange of data and processing. The core technologies of web services are: (1) WSDL (Web Service Description Language) — a markup language to define the API of the web service, including the functions and the input/output messages associated with each function; the messages are in the form of XML and defined by XML schema; (2) SOAP or REST — the common messaging format, i.e., Simple Object Access Protocol or Representational State Transfer; (3) UDDI (Universal Description, Discovery and Integration) — a standard which regulates the service registries in which WSDL definitions can be published so that they can be discovered by the users.

  30. Explain any three prominent security threats to cloud computing.
    Topic: Cloud Security Threats and Trust Issues

    (1) Denial of Service (DoS): The purpose is to overload the IT resources to the stage where they cannot work properly. It can be launched by artificially increasing the workload on a cloud service through fake messages or repeated communication requests, overloading the network with traffic to cripple performance and increase response time, or sending multiple cloud service requests each designed to consume excessive memory and processing resources. (2) Virtualization attack: Based upon the administrative privileges provided to cloud consumers and multi-tenancy, it is possible to compromise the underlying physical hardware, e.g., by an imposter in disguise of a legitimate consumer who cracks a weak password, or by a trusted but malicious consumer; the vulnerabilities in the virtualization platform are exploited over a single VM to take control of the physical server, making all VMs hosted on the compromised server vulnerable, and if the infected VM is migrated to another server, a number of servers may get compromised. (3) Overlapping trust boundaries: Moving consumer data to the cloud means the provider now shares with the consumer the responsibilities of availability, confidentiality and integrity of data, so the consumer extends the trust boundary to include the cloud provider; when multiple consumers share an IT resource, the trust boundaries overlap and the provider may not be able to provide security features satisfying the security requirements of all consumers. Other threats include traffic eavesdropping, malicious intermediary, insufficient authorization and weak authentication attacks, and flawed implementations.

  31. Define privacy, security and trust, and list the privacy issues of cloud computing.
    Topic: Cloud Security Threats and Trust Issues

    Privacy: The confidentiality of data related to a person or organization. Security: The preservation of confidentiality, integrity and availability of data. Trust: The state of accepting a vulnerability on the basis of positive expectations. The consumer's trust in the cloud is affected by the privacy and security vulnerabilities of the cloud; the lack of trust is the key factor for user reluctance to use cloud services, and consumers feel a lack of control when shifting to the cloud. Privacy issues of cloud computing include: (1) Lack of user control; (2) Lack of training and expertise; (3) Possibility of secondary (unauthorized) use of consumer data; (4) Legal compliance. The consumers' trust can be assured through safeguarding personal/confidential/sensitive data, and the existence and enhancement of transparency and accountability can increase trust.

  32. Explain the cloud usage monitor and its generic types.
    Topic: Cloud Infrastructure Mechanisms

    The cloud usage monitor is a software used to collect and process the data related to cloud-based IT resources; the reporting and analysis requirements determine the scope and volume of data collected. Its generic types/formats are: (1) Monitoring agent — it transparently monitors and analyzes the dataflow over communication paths; it measures the network traffic and messages. (2) Resource agent — it collects the resource usage data related to certain events such as initiating, suspending, resuming and vertical scaling; it interacts with the cloud resource management module. (3) Polling agent — it collects the cloud service usage data after periodic polling to IT resources, for example the uptime/downtime of a cloud service, and records the updated status of the resource.

  33. What is a Service Level Agreement (SLA)? What promises are made by the provider in service agreements, and what are the remedies for failure to perform?
    Topic: Service Agreements and Data Center Design

    NIST identifies that the consumer and provider are under a legal agreement or terms of service, which has two parts: the Service Agreement, which contains the legal terms of the contract, and the Service Level Agreement (SLA), which contains the technical performance promises by the provider and the remedies for performance failures. Promises made to the consumer include: (1) Availability — usually 99.5% to 100% availability is assured for time intervals of a billing cycle (e.g., 15 minutes, 1 hour, 1 year); (2) Remedies for failure to perform — in case of violation of the availability promise, the customer is compensated in terms of service credit for future use of the cloud service; a refund is usually not given, and the consumer is responsible for monitoring the availability of the service and claiming compensation; (3) Legal care of consumer information — the provider assures not to disclose/view/use/share the consumer's data except in case of legal requirement. Limitations included by the provider include scheduled outages (not considered service failure and informed in advance), force majeure events (power failure, natural disaster, unreliable connectivity), service agreement changes and service API changes.

  34. Describe the layered networking structure of a cloud hosting data center.
    Topic: Service Agreements and Data Center Design

    A cloud hosting data center has a layered architecture for Internet access. At the core of a cloud is a cluster of VMs/physical servers; there can be tens of thousands of physical servers in a data center, and a large data center can host hundreds of thousands of physical servers. The servers are physically connected to layer 2 switches; there is a Top of Rack (TOR) switch in each rack, and one server is connected to only one TOR switch. The TORs are connected to aggregate switches (AGS), which provide the cross-rack inter-VM connectivity. There are a few access routers (AR) and border routers (BR) at layer 3; the layer 2 AGSs are connected to the BR through the AR, and the BRs are connected to the Internet. The cluster nodes are used for computations, while some nodes are used for workload allocation, some for monitoring and some for load balancing; gateway nodes provide the interface of cloud services to the outside world through the Internet.
