  1. Define social psychology according to Gordon Allport.
    Topic: General

    Social psychology is a discipline that uses scientific methods to understand and explain how the thoughts, feelings, and behaviors of individuals are influenced by the actual, imagined, or implied presence of others. It focuses on the interaction between individuals and their social environment.

  2. Differentiate between individual-level explanations and mediating variables.
    Topic: General

    While both explain behavior, they function at different levels. 1) Individual-level explanations focus on heredity, physiology, and past experiences, such as the effect of testosterone on aggression or childhood upbringing. 2) Mediating variables are internal, unobservable states like beliefs, attitudes, and schemas that link external factors to behavior. 3) Individual-level factors are often rooted in biology or history, whereas mediating variables are the cognitive and emotional processes occurring within the person. 4) For example, a person's history (individual level) might lead them to hold a specific schema (mediating variable) about others. Both are necessary to fully explain human social behavior.

  3. What quote did Floyd Allport use to define his view on social psychology?
    Topic: General

    Allport stated that only within the individual can we find the behavior mechanisms and consciousness fundamental to interactions. He argued there is no psychology of groups that is not entirely a psychology of individuals.

  4. Explain the differences and similarities between social psychology and
    Topic: General

    personality psychology. Social psychology and personality psychology are considered allies because both focus on the individual. 1) They are so similar that the American Psychological Association often includes them in the same journals. 2) Personality psychology focuses on individual differences, whereas social psychology focuses on common humanity. 3) Social psychology specifically examines how people view and affect one another. 4) Personality psychologists might study why one person is more aggressive than another, while social psychologists study how social situations lead most people to act in certain ways. For example, a social psychologist studies conformity, while a personality psychologist studies traits. In conclusion, while they overlap in subject matter, their research objectives remain distinct.

  5. Define research according to the handout.
    Topic: General

    Research is defined as the method of asking and answering a question with a set of systematic procedures which assist researchers in avoiding biases. This systematic approach ensures that the inquiry is structured and reliable. It aims to reduce uncertainty and ambiguity through the acquisition of knowledge.

  6. Explain the scientific method and its three primary goals in social psychology.
    Topic: General

    The scientific method is a set of systematic procedures used to gather, analyze, and interpret information to reduce error and reach dependable generalizations. 1) Description involves classifying and cataloguing events, often using a nomothetic approach to identify similarities. 2) Prediction uses these descriptions to forecast future outcomes, such as performance or IQ changes, though it cannot establish causation. 3) Understanding is achieved when the causes of a phenomenon are identified through causal inferences. This requires satisfying conditions like time-order relationships and eliminating alternative explanations. For example, researchers might study the 'pace of life' in different countries to describe cultural differences. The scientific method is essential because it relies on empirical evidence rather than intuition. (Pg 9-10)

  7. Differentiate between anonymity and confidentiality.
    Topic: General

    Anonymity exists when neither the researcher nor the reader can identify a given response with a respondent. Confidentiality occurs when the researcher can identify a person's response but does not do so publicly.

  8. Discuss the significance of Milgram's experiment and the controversy
    Topic: General

    surrounding it. Milgram studied obedience to authority to explain the horrors of World War II. 1) He used a shock generator to see if people would obey harmful orders. 2) The study found obedience is a seemingly universal feature of human behavior. 3) It was controversial because it did not satisfy informed consent. 4) Participants experienced high tension and stress during the procedure. For example, 26 out of 40 subjects continued to the end of the shock sequence. The study remains a landmark in ethical debates. (Pg 13-14)

  9. Explain the concept of 'representative sample'.
    Topic: General

    A sample is representative of the population to the extent that it exhibits the same distribution of characteristics as the population. Representativeness is critical for the ability to generalize from a sample to the population .

  10. Discuss the various probability sampling strategies used in social psychology.
    Topic: General

    Probability sampling ensures every element has an equal chance of being included. 1) Simple random sampling: all items have an equal chance of inclusion, often chosen by random numbers. 2) Systematic random sampling: subjects are selected according to a regular pattern, such as every tenth person. 3) Stratified sampling: includes quotas of strata like gender or ethnicity to ensure adequate representation. 4) Cluster sampling: a convenient group is selected and then sub-sampled randomly. 5) Hybrid or multistage selection: involves choosing a sampling frame cluster randomly, then randomly selecting individuals within it. These methods increase the likelihood of representativeness.

  11. Define the term compliance as used in social psychology research.
    Topic: General

    Compliance is defined as a situation where a person immediately does what is asked of him or her. This concept is considered an essential element in conducting research.

  12. Explain the concept of naturalistic observation and its advantages in social
    Topic: General

    psychology research. Naturalistic observation is a research method that investigates behavior in its natural environment. 1) Observers remain as unobtrusive as possible so that their presence does not influence the behavior. 2) In some cases, researchers are not present at all and use hidden video cameras to record events. 3) The behavior recorded is analyzed later by the researchers. 4) One advantage is that behavior remains unobtrusive. 5) Another advantage is that it can be used during the initial phases of a project. For example, a researcher might observe playground interactions without interfering. Naturalistic observation provides a realistic view of human behavior in everyday settings.

  13. Differentiate between open-ended and closed-ended questions.
    Topic: General

    An open-ended question requires a narrative response, whereas a closed-ended question is answered with a yes/no or by selecting from alternatives. Closed-ended questions are easier to score, while open-ended questions may capture information that structured questions miss. Open-ended responses require time- consuming coding by trained judges.

  14. Describe the different types of survey designs.
    Topic: General

    Survey research is carried out according to specific designs to track data over time or across groups. 1) Cross- sectional design involves drawing samples at one point in time, such as comparing different age groups. 2) Longitudinal designs track data over a period. 3) Trend studies use successive, different samples of respondents, though they do not track individual changes. 4) Cohort studies focus on specific groups like age groups or people married in a specific year. 5) Panel studies survey the same respondents repeatedly to examine individual changes, though they suffer from practice effects and dropouts. For example, a panel study might track the same students over four years of college. In conclusion, the choice of design depends on whether the researcher wants to see individual changes or group trends.

  15. Explain the concept of a confederate in social psychology experiments.
    Topic: General

    A confederate is an individual who poses as a fellow participant in an experiment but is actually working for the researcher. They are used to manipulate the social environment, such as by insulting a participant to induce anger. This helps in studying specific behavioral responses under controlled conditions.

  16. Describe the process of counterbalancing and its purpose in experimental
    Topic: General

    design. Counterbalancing is a technique used to maintain the integrity of an experiment when participants are exposed to multiple conditions. 1) It is specifically applied to reduce the order effect when individuals are exposed to two or more conditions. 2) A common method is the ABBA design, which involves changing the sequence of conditions for different participants. 3) By varying the order, researchers ensure that the sequence itself does not influence the results. 4) The advantages include less subject variability and the ability to draw stronger conclusions about causality. 5) This control is essential for ensuring that the independent variable is the true cause of the observed effect. It is a fundamental practice in rigorous experimental design.

  17. What is the relationship between the frontal lobes and self-awareness?
    Topic: General

    Neuroscientists have identified the frontal lobes as the primary source for cognitive abilities like self- awareness. Specifically, the anterior cingulate of the frontal lobe is involved in this process.

  18. Describe the role of the brain in cognitive abilities related to the self.
    Topic: General

    The brain, specifically the cerebral cortex, is the center for self-related cognitive abilities. 1) The cerebral cortex is the outer layer that integrates brain functions. 2) It is significantly larger in humans than in other animals. 3) The frontal lobes are the primary source of higher mental processes. 4) These processes include planning, social skills, and abstract thinking. 5) The anterior cingulate of the frontal lobe is specifically linked to self-awareness. For instance, the ability to plan a future event relies on these frontal lobe functions. The brain acts as the biological foundation for the self.

  19. Define evaluation apprehension in the context of public awareness.
    Topic: General

    Evaluation apprehension is the anxiety experienced when one realizes they are the object of others' attention. It occurs because public examination can lead to either positive or negative outcomes.

  20. Describe the development of self-awareness across the lifespan.
    Topic: General

    1) Self-awareness is not inborn but develops over time. 2) Infants (9-12 months) initially fail to recognize their mirror image as themselves. 3) By 18 months, children recognize they are separate individuals, possibly linked to the growth of spindle cells. 4) In childhood, individuals begin labeling their personal qualities. 5) During adolescence, the self becomes critical for life decisions. 6) In adulthood, the self continues to change, though less extensively. Example: A toddler recognizing their reflection as 'me' marks a key developmental milestone. Conclusion: The self-concept evolves from infancy through adulthood.

  21. Differentiate between an independent self and an interdependent self.
    Topic: General

    An independent self is fostered in individualist cultures and focuses on personal uniqueness. An interdependent self is fostered in collectivist cultures and focuses on group identification and cooperation. These concepts define how individuals relate to their society.

  22. Explain the four categories used to code responses in the Twenty Statements
    Topic: General

    Test. The Twenty Statements Test (TST) uses four categories to analyze self-concept. 1) Physical self-descriptions identify the self through physical qualities like being male or overweight. 2) Social self-descriptions identify the self through roles or memberships like being a student or a daughter. 3) Attributive self-descriptions identify the self through psychological traits like being intelligent or assertive. 4) Global self-descriptions identify the self vaguely, such as being a human being or simply 'me'. These categories allow researchers to categorize how individuals perceive their identity. For example, a student might list 'I am a student' (social) and 'I am tired' (attributive). This categorization helps in understanding the dominant aspects of a person's self-concept.

  23. Define a schema in the context of self-understanding.
    Topic: General

    A schema is a set of beliefs that provides an organizing framework for understanding a topic, event, or person. Self-schemas are beliefs that help us organize and understand our own personality traits, attitudes, values, and characteristics.

  24. Explain the concept of self-schemas with examples.
    Topic: General

    1) A schema is a set of beliefs providing an organizing framework for understanding topics or persons. 2) Self-schemas are beliefs that help individuals organize their personality traits, attitudes, and values. 3) These schemas summarize characteristics attributed to the self, including actions and relationships. 4) Research shows individuals are 'schematic' for traits they value and 'aschematic' for those they do not. 5) For example, a student may have a strong schema for 'independence' if they define themselves by it, whereas another might not. Self-schemas are essential for understanding how we categorize our own identity.

  25. Define person perception.
    Topic: General

    Person perception is the process by which we come to know about others' temporary states, such as emotions, intentions, and desires, and enduring dispositions, such as beliefs, traits, and abilities. It is an ongoing process involving impression formation and attribution.

  26. Explain the process of impression formation and its dynamic nature.
    Topic: General

    Impression formation is the process by which observers integrate various sources of information about others' self-presentations into a unified and consistent judgment. 1) It begins with rapid assessments of salient and observable qualities. 2) Observers attend to nonverbal cues like facial expressions and body posture. 3) It incorporates descriptive characteristics like traits to form an overall impression. 4) The process is dynamic, meaning judgments are continually updated as new information becomes available. 5) It functions like a working model of a person to guide social interaction. An example is updating your opinion of a colleague after seeing them handle a difficult project differently than expected. Impression formation is a continuous, evolving cognitive process.

  27. Explain the principle of evaluative consistency.
    Topic: General

    Evaluative consistency is a tendency to view others in a way that is internally consistent. Even when contradictory information is available, people persist in viewing others as consistently good or bad, often distorting or explaining away the contradictory data.

  28. Discuss the impact of individualism and collectivism on nonverbal
    Topic: General

    communication and behavior. Individualism and collectivism are cultural dimensions that influence how emotions are expressed and how individuals monitor their behavior. 1) Collectivists are more likely to monitor their behavior to ensure it does not disrupt group functioning. 2) Both groups may display positive emotions publicly, but collectivists are more uncomfortable expressing negative emotions. 3) These cultural differences manifest in nonverbal cues. 4) For instance, Americans and Asians are less touch-oriented than other cultures. 5) Space requirements also differ, with Asians preferring more distance than Middle Easterners. For example, a Middle Eastern person standing close to someone may be seen as friendly, while that same distance might be perceived as intrusive by a North American. In conclusion, cultural background dictates the social rules for both nonverbal cues and the public expression of emotion.

  29. Define person perception in the context of initial categorization.
    Topic: General

    Person perception begins with using salient categories like sex, race, or age to make initial assumptions about an individual. Perceivers often interpret subsequent information as consistent with these first impressions. This process is biased toward viewing people as category instances rather than unique individuals.

  30. Discuss the stability and consequences of having a baby face.
    Topic: General

    Baby faces are defined by features like large eyes and small chins, and their perception is highly stable. 1) Research shows that perceivers agree consistently on who has a baby face across different age groups. 2) These features tend to remain stable over time, though women may be less likely to retain them into their thirties. 3) Baby-faced individuals are often perceived as warm and trustworthy but weak and incompetent. 4) This leads to a self-fulfilling prophecy where they may internalize these traits. 5) These perceptions affect life outcomes, such as hiring preferences for specific jobs. For example, baby-faced individuals are often hired for teaching roles while mature-faced people are chosen for leadership. Overall, baby-faced individuals are judged differently based on evolutionary biases that equate their features with childhood dependency. (Pg 47-48)

  31. Define the covariation principle.
    Topic: General

    The covariation principle states that for something to be the cause of a particular behavior, it must be present when the behavior is present and absent when the behavior is absent. It is the basis for Kelley's model.

  32. Explain the conditions under which we make dispositional attributions.
    Topic: General

    According to the correspondent inference theory, we are likely to make dispositional attributions under three specific conditions. 1) Social desirability: we are more likely to make an internal attribution when observing socially undesirable behaviors. 2) Choice: an internal attribution is more likely when the actor has freely chosen the behavior. 3) Non-common effects: an internal attribution is more likely when the outcome of a behavior has a unique effect. 4) These conditions help us filter out situational noise. 5) This process allows us to make stable predictions about personality. For example, if someone chooses to volunteer for a difficult task when others do not, we attribute this to their generous personality. These conditions are essential for interpreting social behavior accurately.

  33. Define the actor-observer bias.
    Topic: General

    The actor-observer bias is the tendency for people to attribute their own behavior to external causes while attributing the behavior of others to internal causes. Actors focus on the situation, while observers focus on the actor.

  34. Describe the actor-observer bias and the evidence provided by Storms (1973).
    Topic: General

    The actor-observer bias is the tendency for people to attribute their own behavior to external causes while attributing others' behavior to internal causes. 1) Actors overestimate the importance of the situation for their own behavior. 2) Observers overestimate the importance of an actor's dispositions. 3) Storms (1973) conducted a study with participants as observers and actors. 4) Observers focused on the actor and emphasized dispositional factors. 5) Actors focused on the situation and emphasized situational factors. 6) The bias was reversed when participants were shown videotapes from the opposite perspective, as actors then saw their own faces and made internal attributions. This confirms that perceptual salience and information access drive this bias.

  35. Differentiate between explicit and implicit cognition.
    Topic: General

    Explicit cognition refers to deliberate judgments or decisions of which we are consciously aware. Conversely, implicit cognition involves judgments or decisions that are under the control of automatically activated evaluations.

  36. Discuss the dual-process models of social cognition.
    Topic: General

    Dual-process models describe the strategies used for social cognition, divided into explicit and implicit cognition. 1) Explicit cognition involves deliberate, conscious judgments or decisions where the individual is aware of the process. 2) Implicit cognition involves judgments or decisions under the control of automatically activated evaluations. 3) These processes allow humans to manage complex information efficiently. 4) We often switch between these based on the time and information available. For example, deciding on a career path is explicit, while judging a stranger's mood is often implicit. This model explains how we balance conscious thought with automatic reactions.

  37. Explain the base rate fallacy.
    Topic: General

    The base rate fallacy is the tendency for individuals to ignore base-rate information when making probability judgments if they are provided with specific descriptive information. In the Tversky and Kahneman study, participants ignored the actual frequency of engineers in a group when given personality descriptions. This shows that stereotypes often override statistical data.

  38. Discuss the False Consensus Effect and explain why it occurs.
    Topic: General

    1) The false consensus effect is the tendency to exaggerate how common one’s own opinions are in the general population. 2) It is rooted in the belief that our personal opinions are fairly typical. 3) Ross, Greene, and House (1977) demonstrated this by asking students if they would wear a sandwich board on campus. 4) Those who agreed estimated that most others would agree, while those who disagreed estimated that most others would disagree. 5) One explanation is the availability heuristic, where our own self-concepts serve as a lens to view others. 6) Because our own beliefs are easily recalled, they become the most accessible information when we judge others. 7) Another factor is the false-uniqueness belief, which is a product of the self-serving bias. 8) The conclusion is that we project our own views onto others because our own thoughts are the most available to us.

  39. Explain the behavioral component of attitudes.
    Topic: General

    The behavioral component describes how a person tends to act towards an object. It acknowledges that while attitudes influence behavior, past behavior can also determine attitudes.

  40. Explain the relationship between behavior and attitudes.
    Topic: General

    The relationship between behavior and attitudes is reciprocal and complex. 1) Influence: Attitudes are considered one of the primary determinants of how a person acts toward an object. 2) Feedback: Conversely, past behavior serves as a determinant for shaping future attitudes. 3) Tricomponent view: The behavioral component is one of the three pillars of the traditional attitude model. 4) Complexity: Because behavior and attitudes influence each other, they are often studied together to understand social actions. 5) Example: A person's positive attitude toward exercise may lead to the behavior of going to the gym, while the habit of going to the gym reinforces a positive attitude toward physical fitness. This interaction highlights that attitudes are not static but are influenced by our actions.

  41. Explain the significance of the Mita, Dermer, & Knight (1977) experiment.
    Topic: General

    This study showed that people prefer mirror images of themselves over actual photos because they are most familiar with their own mirror reflection. Friends, however, prefer the actual view of the person because that is what they see. This explains why individuals often dislike standard photographs of themselves.

  42. Discuss the role of Classical Conditioning in attitude formation with reference
    Topic: General

(1992) found that aversive stimuli paired with nonsense words created a stronger effect. 5) This indicates that classical conditioning is a more powerful determinant when knowledge about the object is limited. 6) Subliminal conditioning is a subset where stimuli are presented without conscious awareness. 7) Krosnick et al.   43. Define functional attitude theory.
    Topic: General

    Functional attitude theory suggests that attitudes are formed and changed based on the degree to which they satisfy different psychological needs. For example, an individual might change their attitude toward a brand of coffee if they discover the company engages in anti-environmental practices. This reflects a shift in how the attitude meets the person's needs.

  44. Describe the findings of the LaPierre (1934) study and its significance.
    Topic: General

    LaPierre's 1934 study was a landmark investigation into the attitude-behavior relationship during a time of widespread prejudice against Asians in the US. 1) Methodology: LaPierre traveled with a Chinese couple for three months, visiting numerous hotels and restaurants to see if they would be refused service. 2) Results: Only 1 out of 66 hotels and 1 out of 250 restaurants refused them service. 3) Contrast: Later, when these establishments were sent a letter asking if they would serve Chinese people, 90% of the 128 respondents claimed they would refuse. 4) Significance: This demonstrated a clear gap between stated attitudes and actual behavior. 5) Interpretation: It highlighted that general attitudes often fail to predict specific, real-world behaviors. For example, social pressure in person leads to different results than a hypothetical survey. In conclusion, this study created a crisis of confidence in the concept of attitude as a central psychological construct. (Pg 65-66)
