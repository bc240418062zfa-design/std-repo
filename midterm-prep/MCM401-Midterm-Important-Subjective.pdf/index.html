  1. What is a press release?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A press release, also known as a news release, is a written or recorded communication directed at members of the news media for the purpose of announcing something claimed as having news value. It is a condensed article written in a journalistic format. It is not an advertisement, but can often be more powerful for delivering attention. The purpose of the press release is to highlight what is interesting and newsworthy about your company, organization, services or products.

  2. What does Public Relations accomplish?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    There are many objectives that may be achieved through expert public relations activity. Any one of them, any group of them, or all of them may be the basis for a company's public relations program. Professional public relations directs every activity toward reaching a selected objective; extraneous efforts are avoided and mere volume of press clippings is meaningless. Objectives that may be sought include: prestige or favorable image and its benefits; promotion of product and sales, ideas and rewards; goodwill of employees; preventing and solving labor problems; goodwill of stockholders/shareholders; overcoming misconceptions and prejudices against the company, group or organization; goodwill of customers/consumers; and formulation and guidance for making policies.

  3. How is Public Relations associated with mass communication?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Mass communication is the term used to describe the various means by which individuals and entities relay information through mass media to large segments of the population at the same time. It is usually understood to relate to newspaper and magazine publishing, radio, television and film, as these are used both for disseminating news and for advertising. The basic difference between mass communication and public relations is that mass communication is used to disseminate information to a larger audience with the help of newspapers, TV and radio etc., whereas public relations is a planned and sustained effort to establish and maintain goodwill and understanding between an organization and its publics. Thus mass communication is a tool through which public relations can be established and enhanced.

  4. Why do we classify Public Relations?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    We classify Public Relations because different institutions or organizations — business corporations, educational institutions, religious groups, government agencies and others — are directly or sometimes indirectly involved with the public, and each needs an established department to create a good image of the organization among people by removing misunderstandings or contradictions. The objectives of all institutions where the need of a Public Relations department is observed are the same: to build, maintain and enhance the image of the organization with its key publics. Their style of operation may vary according to the nature or classification of the organization, yet the basic and principal objective remains common — to build, maintain and enhance the institution's image through mutual understanding with its publics.

  5. What are some examples of Public Relations in old ages?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Public Relations is not new; it was also practiced in old times. In primitive times people used to engage in Public Relations activity through their writings, paintings and buildings. Examples include: writings on tablets of stone; ancient buildings and pyramids; early temples and mosques; cave paintings in Zimbabwe bearing pictorial messages; pictograms such as Chinese characters, which are still an example of PR means; and Holy Books, which also communicate and tell people about faith.

  6. What is the difference between PR and propaganda?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Propaganda is the means of gaining support for an opinion, creed or belief. It is another form of communication and by no means must be PR. The key difference is credibility: successful PR must be credible, whereas propaganda is liable to invite suspicion or at least disagreement. PR is a planned and sustained effort to establish mutual understanding based on truthful information, while propaganda may use false or partly false information to sway the opinions of the population.

  7. What is the difference between PR and publicity?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Publicity results from information being made known. Publicity is the result of information being made known by a PR plan. PR is very much about the behavior of individuals, organizations, products and services. Publicity only yields an image, subject to PR which provides adequate knowledge for that image. In short, publicity is only one possible outcome or tool of public relations, while PR is the complete planned activity of building goodwill and understanding.

  8. What are the five points for planning a PR budget?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    1. To determine what it will cost to carry out a PR Plan. 2. To determine what sort of plan can be carried out for a given sum of money. 3. After agreeing to a cost and plan, the budget provides a check list of tasks to be done as per a time table. 4. The budget sets a discipline for both expenditure and over-expenditure. 5. After completion of the campaign, results can be measured against the budget.

  9. What are the principles of good public relations?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The principles of good public relations, particularly in dealing with the press, are: maintain a two-way relationship; establish a reputation of reliability by conduct; provide good copy; cooperate in providing material; provide verification facilities; and build personal rapport with the media.

  10. How should news releases be written?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The easiest way to learn how to write a news release is to study newspaper reports and observe how they are written, because there is a special technique. Two fundamental characteristics are apparent from reading newspapers: (A) the subject is stated in the opening words, and (B) the opening paragraph is a summary of the whole story. The 7-Point Formula is: 1. Subject — what is the story about? 2. Organization — what is the name of the organization? 3. Location — where is the organization located? 4. Advantages — what is new, what are the benefits? 5. Details — what are the colors, sizes, prices, performance figures or other details? 6. Applications — what are the uses and who are the users? 7. Source — is this different from location, e.g. location may be where the work is done and source will be the head office.

  11. What is private media?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Private media are the media created and controlled by the organization itself for its own publics. They include: 1. House journals, 2. Videos, 3. Slides, 4. Audio cassettes, 5. Spoken word, 6. Private exhibitions, 7. Seminars, and 8. Educational literature.

  12. What are the different types of house journals?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The different types of house journals are: 1. Magazines — A4 size. 2. Newspapers — like a tabloid. 3. Newsletters — 2 to 8 pages. 4. Wall newspapers — like a poster.

  13. What are the different types of research?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Desk Research — it is basically a study of existing statistics and survey reports. Field Research — this is normally done by interviewing people 'in the field', as when interviewers contact respondents. Ad hoc Research — this means one-off surveys, complete in one project only. Continuous Research — these are the surveys carried out regularly, e.g. monthly, thus recording changes or trends.

  14. Why is there a need for budgeting in Public Relations?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    1. To learn what it will cost to carry out a PR program. 2. To learn what sort of program can be carried out for a given sum of money. 3. Budgeting helps in controlling the program cost. 4. Budgeting provides a discipline for both expenditure and over-expenditure. 5. After completion of the campaign, results can be measured against the budget.

  15. How can Public Relations personnel prepare for a public relations crisis?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    PR personnel can prepare for a crisis by: identifying things that can go wrong and become highly visible, and assessing weaknesses throughout the organization; assigning priorities based on which weaknesses are most urgent and most likely; drafting questions, answers and resolutions for each potential crisis scenario; focusing on the two most important tasks — what to do and what to say — during the first critical hours following a crisis; and developing a strategy to contain and counteract, not react and respond.

  16. What are the 7 C's of PR used as a strategy to counter crisis?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The 7 C's of PR are an effective means of strategy to counter crisis: 1. Credibility, 2. Context, 3. Content, 4. Clarity, 5. Continuity & Consistency, 6. Channels, 7. Capability of the Audience.

  17. Describe the Six-Point PR Planning Model. (Marks: 10)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The Six-Point PR Planning Model consists of the following steps: 1. Appreciation of the situation — assessing the current situation through opinion, attitude or image surveys, press clippings, sales figures and similar methods, converting hostility, prejudice, apathy and ignorance into sympathy, acceptance, interest and knowledge. 2. Definition of objectives — deciding what is to be achieved, e.g. changing the image, improving community relations or establishing a new corporate identity. 3. Definition of publics — identifying the internal and external publics to be targeted, such as employees, investors, distributors, consumers and opinion leaders. 4. Selection of media & techniques — choosing suitable print, electronic and other media to reach the defined publics. 5. Planning of a budget — determining the cost of carrying out the plan. 6. Assessment of results — evaluating the results and comparing them against the objectives.

  18. How does Public Relations help educational institutions? (Marks: 10)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Public Relations helps educational institutions in many ways. It builds and maintains a favorable image of the institution among its publics — students, parents, teachers, the local community, government and funding bodies. Through planned communication it creates goodwill and mutual understanding, removes misconceptions and prejudices about the institution, and publicizes its achievements, programs and facilities, which helps in attracting better students and staff. It promotes admissions and new courses, wins the goodwill of students and employees, prevents and solves internal problems, and supports the institution in times of criticism or crisis by presenting truthful information. In this way PR helps an educational institution to be known, understood and trusted by all its publics.

  19. What do you understand by publics in Public Relations? (Marks: 3)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    In Public Relations, 'publics' are the people with whom an organization has to do business and maintain relationships. They are the groups of people who are affected by, or can affect, the organization. Publics are broadly classified into internal publics (e.g. employees) and external publics. The expanded publics include: the community, potential employees, employees, suppliers of services and materials, investors (the money market), distributors, consumers and users, and opinion leaders.

  20. Describe sponsorship as an important PR tool. (Marks: 3)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Sponsorship is the donation of funds to an organization or event in exchange for supportive association in order to generate publicity. It is an important PR tool because, by associating the organization's name with a popular event, cause or activity, it creates goodwill, builds a favorable image and keeps the organization's name before its publics in a positive way. Sponsorship also helps in developing good communications with the publics and provides opportunities for media coverage that money cannot directly buy.

  21. What do you understand by corporate identity? (Marks: 3)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Corporate identity is the physical identity of an organization — how one sees or identifies it. It is the total of the visual and physical elements by which an organization is recognized, such as its logo, color, special typeface, name displays, stationery, packaging, uniforms, vehicles and house journals. A corporate identity scheme is often a PR responsibility because it applies to all aspects of an organization and is a significant part of its total communication.

  22. Explain the two-way communication process. (Marks: 3)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Communication is a two-way process. In it, a sender encodes a message and transmits it through a channel to a receiver, who decodes the message and understands it. The process is completed only when the receiver sends feedback to the sender, showing that the message has been received and understood. Good public relations is based on this two-way relationship: the organization not only informs its publics but also listens to them, monitors outside opinion and conveys this intelligence back to management, so that policies can be adjusted and mutual understanding maintained.

  23. What makes Public Relations different from advertising? (Marks: 3)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Public Relations and advertising are different in several ways: 1. PR is not a form of advertising; it is in fact a bigger activity than advertising. 2. PR relates to all the communications of the total organization, while advertising is mainly limited to marketing functions. 3. PR is neither 'free advertising' nor 'unpaid for advertising'; it is time consuming and time costs money. 4. At times advertising may not be used by an organization, but every organization is involved in PR. 5. PR embraces everyone and everything, while advertising is limited to special selling and buying tasks. 6. Advertising presents the most persuasive possible selling message to the right prospects for a product or service at the lowest possible cost, while the PR role is informing, educating and creating understanding through knowledge — also known as market education.

  24. Explain the term 'internal publics'. (Marks: 3)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Internal publics are the publics inside the organization itself, the foremost among them being the employees. They also include management and shareholders connected with the organization from within. An organization must first maintain good relations with its internal publics, because employees who are well informed and satisfied act as ambassadors of the organization to the outside world. House journals and other private media are largely produced to inform and entertain the internal publics.

  25. What is the importance of a house journal in Public Relations? (Marks: 3)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A house journal is a publication produced by an organization in order to inform and entertain its internal publics. It is one of the oldest forms of organized PR — the printed house journal has existed for more than 170 years — and it remains important because print has permanence and portability: it can be retained and read almost anywhere, at any time, and by a wide readership. A house journal keeps employees informed about the organization's activities, policies and achievements, builds a sense of belonging and goodwill, and provides a channel of two-way communication between management and staff.

  26. How does corporate image differ from corporate identity? (Marks: 3)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Corporate image and corporate identity are two different things. Corporate image is mental — it is what one thinks about the company, i.e. the impression or picture of the organization formed in the minds of its publics, built from elements such as its founding date, industrial relations record and share performance. Corporate identity is physical — it is how one sees or identifies the company, i.e. the visible elements such as its logo, colors, stationery, packaging, uniforms and name displays. In short, identity is what the organization presents, while image is what the publics perceive.
