  27. Explain Object-Oriented Analysis using Abbott's Textual Analysis (mapping of parts of speech to model components). Also: what are the meanings of Proper Noun, Improper Noun, Being Verb and Having Verb in textual analysis? (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Object-Oriented Analysis, Object Selection & Peter Coad Methodology

    In Abbott's textual analysis, parts of speech in the problem statement are mapped to model components: Part of speech = Model component Proper noun = Instance (a specific object, e.g. Mehdi Hassan) Improper (common) noun = Class / type / role (e.g. student, teacher) Doing verb = Operation (e.g. buy) Being verb = Classification (e.g. is a horse, is a book) Having verb = Composition (e.g. fan has wings) Adjective = Attribute value or class (e.g. this ball is green) Adjective phrase = Association (e.g. the customer with children, the customer who bought the kite) So, in textual analysis: a proper noun identifies a specific instance/object; an improper noun identifies a class; a being verb shows classification (is-a relationship); and a having verb shows composition (whole-part relationship).

  33. Write the difference between coupling and cohesion.

  34. What is coupling? Explain (long question).
    Topic: Software Design, Coupling & Cohesion

    Coupling is a measure of independence of a module or component. Loose coupling means that different system components have loose or less reliance upon each other. Hence, changes in one component would have a limited effect on other components. High (tight) coupling, on the other hand, means components are strongly dependent on each other, so a change in one component strongly affects the others. A good design aims at low coupling.

  45. Using the architecture of a house or building, draw a comparison with software architecture. (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Software Architecture & Design Quality

    The architecture of a house/building is a plan (blueprint) that shows the overall structure of the house before it is built: its foundation, rooms, floors, plumbing and wiring, and how all these parts are connected. Software architecture is the same idea for a software system: it is the high-level plan/structure of the system that shows its major components (sub-systems), how they are organized and how they communicate with each other, before detailed design and coding begin. In both cases: (i) the architecture is decided first and is very costly to change once construction/coding has started; (ii) it must satisfy the owner's/user's requirements and constraints (space, cost, safety in a house; functionality and quality attributes in software); (iii) many different detailed designs can be built on the same architecture; and (iv) specialists (architects) produce it so that builders/developers can construct the actual product from it.   46. Differentiate between a sub-system and a module. (Marks: 3)
    Topic: Software Architecture & Design Quality

    A sub-system is a larger, relatively independent part of a system; it is itself composed of several modules/components that work together to provide a major functionality of the system, and it can often be designed, developed and tested fairly independently. A module is a much smaller, well-defined unit inside a sub-system that performs one specific function; it has high cohesion, a well-defined interface, and hides its internal details (information hiding). In short: a system is decomposed into sub-systems, and each sub-system is further decomposed into modules. (Answer written from course knowledge — not in sources)

  65. What is the difference between Aggregation and Association? (Marks: 3)
    Topic: UML & Modelling (Use Case, Class, Sequence, Collaboration Diagrams & DFD)

    An association is a general structural relationship between two classes which shows that objects of one class are connected to objects of another class (e.g. a student is associated with a course). Aggregation is a special kind of association that represents a whole-part relationship: one class (the whole/aggregate) is made up of other classes (the parts), e.g. a basket (whole) contains fruits (parts). In aggregation the parts can exist independently of the whole, whereas in a simple association there is no whole-part ownership at all. Every aggregation is an association, but every association is not an aggregation. (Answer written from course knowledge — not in sources)

  68. Define the following: 1) Synchronous Messages 2) Asynchronous Messages (Marks: 3)
    Topic: UML & Modelling (Use Case, Class, Sequence, Collaboration Diagrams & DFD)

    Asynchronous messages are "signals", denoted by a half arrow. They do not block the caller. Asynchronous messages typically perform the following actions: - Create a new thread - Create a new object - Communicate with a thread that is already running Synchronous messages are "call events" and are denoted by the full arrow. They represent nested flow of control, which is typically implemented as an operation call. In case of a synchronous message, the caller waits for the called routine to complete its operation before moving forward.

  94. Differentiate between the action-oriented and object-oriented approaches of decomposition.
    Topic: Software Design, Coupling & Cohesion

    wer: In the case of action-oriented approach, data is decomposed according to functionality requirements. That is, decomposition revolves around function. In the OO approach, decomposition of a problem revolves around data. Action-oriented paradigm focuses only on the functionality of a system and typically ignores the data until it is required.
