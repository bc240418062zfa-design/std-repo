  1. What is the literal meaning of Islam?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The handout associates the root with peace, harmony, security, obedience and submission.

  2. What is Tawheed?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Tawheed is belief in the oneness of Allah and directing worship to Him alone.

  3. What is the role of Islamic beliefs in life?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    They guide a Muslim’s worldview, worship, character and accountability.

  4. Name the five pillars of Islam.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Shahadah, salah, zakat, fasting in Ramadan and Hajj.

  5. Why is prayer important in Islam?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The handout emphasizes prayer as a central obligatory act and the first matter of accountability.

  6. Is worship limited to ritual acts only?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    No. The handout presents worship broadly: lawful livelihood, truthfulness and good conduct can also be worship when consistent with Shariah.

  7. What is the Qur'an?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The word of Allah revealed to Prophet Muhammad (PBUH), preserved in the Mushaf and transmitted continuously.

  8. Name some names/attributes of the Qur'an mentioned in the handout.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Al-Qur'an, Al-Furqan, Al-Zikr, Al-Noor and Al-Kitab.

  9. Differentiate descending order and Tawqifi order.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Descending order follows the order of revelation; Tawqifi order is the current arrangement of the Qur'anic surahs.

  10. What is meant by the miraculous status of the Qur'an?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Its inimitability; the handout discusses its eloquence and the challenge to produce a comparable surah.

  11. Name the two types of tafsir mentioned in the handout.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Tafsir bil-Ma'thur and Tafsir bil-Ra'y.

  12. Which scientific topics are discussed in this lecture?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The handout discusses the universe, motion/orbits of Earth, Sun and Moon, embryonic development and predictions of future events.

  13. What etiquette does Surah Al-Hujurat 49:1–5 teach regarding the Prophet?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Do not raise voices above his; speak respectfully and observe proper etiquette.

  14. Why should believers follow the Qur'an and Sunnah?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The handout describes them as central sources of guidance in religious matters.

  15. What lesson is drawn from lowering one's voice?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    It reflects respect and is associated in the handout with piety.

  16. What actions are prohibited in Qur'an 49:12 as discussed here?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Excessive suspicion, spying and backbiting.

  17. When does the handout mention that speaking about another may be permitted?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    For seeking justice/advice, protecting someone from harm, and examining Hadith narrators for reliability.

  18. According to Qur'an 49:13, what is the standard of honour?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Piety, not race or tribal distinction.

  19. List the qualities of successful believers in Surah Al-Mu'minun 23:1–11.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Humility in prayer, avoiding vain talk, giving zakat, guarding chastity, keeping trusts/covenants and guarding prayers.

  20. How should the servants of the Most Merciful respond to ignorant people?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The handout says they respond peacefully and with humility.

  21. What is the lesson about behaviour?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Behaviour reflects a person's thoughts and character.

  22. Which verse is central to the lecture's discussion of finality of Prophethood?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Surah Al-Ahzab 33:40.

  23. What does Khatam mean in the handout's explanation?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Seal; it is explained as indicating the completion and finality of prophethood.

  24. What is the instruction in Qur'an 33:56 as discussed in the handout?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Believers are instructed to send blessings and peace upon the Prophet.

  25. What is the literal meaning of Hadith in the handout?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Conversation/speech and a new thing.

  26. Name the basic kinds of Hadith discussed.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Hadith Qawli (saying), Fi'li (action) and Taqriri (approval).

  27. What is a weak Hadith?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A weak (Daif) Hadith is a narration that does not meet the required conditions of acceptance, due to defects in its chain of narrators or in the narrators' reliability and memory.

  28. What duties of the Prophet are described in the cited Qur'anic verse?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Reciting revelations, teaching the Book and wisdom, and purifying/training people.

  29. What two terms does the Qur'an use in relation to religious commandments, as noted here?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The two terms are Ita'at (obedience) and Ittiba (following).

  30. What does the handout discuss about early Hadith preservation?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Instructions about writing Hadith and reports/collections associated with Companions including Abu Hurairah, Ibn Abbas, Ayesha, Ibn Umar and Abu Bakr.

  31. What is the meaning of 'Actions depend on intentions'?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The value and purpose of an action are connected to the intention behind it.

  32. How does the handout describe practical love of the Prophet?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    By following his guidance and holding fast to the religious teachings.

  33. What topic about the Last Day is included?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Signs of Doomsday are discussed in the lecture.

  34. What responsibility regarding prayer does the cited Hadith assign to parents?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    It says to instruct children to pray at seven and gives further instruction for age ten.

  35. What is an Ummah?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A community of people united by a shared religious identity.

  36. What does enjoining good and forbidding evil mean?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Encouraging right actions and discouraging wrong actions, with attention to the circumstances described in the handout.

  37. What does the Hadith about lawful livelihood teach?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Seeking lawful earnings is emphasized alongside other religious duties.

  38. What does the handout say about unlawful earnings?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Haram sources of income and taking others' property unlawfully are prohibited.

  39. Name the three signs of a hypocrite mentioned in the Hadith section.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The well-known narration mentions lying when speaking, breaking promises and betraying a trust.

  40. What is the literal meaning of Fiqh?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Understanding and comprehension.

  41. What are the two broad types of Shariah rules discussed?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Shariah rules are broadly of two types: (1) rules relating to beliefs and ethics, and (2) practical/legal rulings that govern worship, transactions and social dealings.

  42. What is Ijma?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Agreement of qualified jurists on a legal ruling.

  43. What is Ijtihad?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A qualified jurist's effort to derive a ruling for an issue without a clear text-based ruling.

  44. State one guiding principle of Ijtihad.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    There is no room for Ijtihad where a clear ruling already exists in Qur'an or Sunnah.

  45. Name causes of sectarianism mentioned in the handout.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Causes of sectarianism include religious causes - differences in the interpretation of texts, extremism and blind following of personalities - and worldly causes such as political disputes, lust for leadership and personal or group interests, which divide the Ummah and spread hatred.

  46. What is the meaning of Seerah?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The handout gives meanings including path, method, attitude, character and habits.

  47. Why study the Seerah?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    To understand the Prophet's life, character and guidance and learn practical lessons.

  48. What early-life topics are covered?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The lecture introduces Seerah and discusses the Prophet's early life and upbringing.

  49. How did the Prophet's public preaching begin?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The handout discusses the beginning of preaching, first to close relatives and then more broadly.

  50. What happened during the journey to Taif?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The Holy Prophet (PBUH) travelled to Taif to invite Banu Thaqif to Islam. They rejected his message, mocked him, and set their slaves and children to pelt him with stones until he was wounded and his shoes filled with blood. He took shelter in an orchard and prayed to Allah Almighty. When Jibraeel (A.S) offered to crush Taif between the two mountains, the Prophet (PBUH) refused and instead prayed for the guidance of its people - a supreme lesson in patience and perseverance.

  51. What were the pledges of Aqabah connected with?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Commitments by people from Yathrib/Madinah to support and follow the Prophet.

  52. What important steps did the Prophet take after reaching Madinah?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Three important steps were taken: (1) construction of Masjid-e-Nabawi, the centre of the new Muslim community; (2) Muwakhat - establishing brotherhood between the Muhajirin (emigrants from Makkah) and the Ansar (the helpers of Madinah); and (3) the Treaty/Charter of Madinah (Misaq-e-Madinah), which defined the rights and duties of all groups of Madinah, including the Jews.

  53. Who were the Ansar?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The Muslims of Madinah who supported the Prophet and the emigrants.

  54. What was the purpose of the Treaty of Madinah?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    To establish agreed rules and responsibilities among the community's groups.

  55. Name rulers who received letters inviting them to Islam.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The handout lists Najashi of Abyssinia, Heraclius of Rome, Khusrau Parvez of Iran, Maqquqas of Egypt and Hoza of Yamama.

  56. What is the meaning of 'Aslim Taslam' as given in the handout?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Come to believe/submit and you will be safe.

  57. What events are covered after Hudaybiyyah?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The lecture discusses the breach of the agreement, entry into Makkah/general amnesty, completion of religion and later campaigns including Khyber/Mutah.

  58. What is Khilafat-e-Rashidah?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The period of the rightly guided caliphs after the Prophet (PBUH).

  59. Why was Abu Bakr Siddiq appointed the first caliph, according to the handout?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Hazrat Abu Bakr Siddiq (R.A) was appointed because of his seniority and unmatched services to Islam: he was the first adult male to accept Islam, the closest companion of the Holy Prophet (PBUH), accompanied him during the Hijrah, and the Prophet (PBUH) himself appointed him to lead the prayers during his final illness. The Companions therefore elected him by consultation at Saqifah Bani Sa'idah.

  60. Name important actions of Abu Bakr's caliphate.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Dispatching the army of Hazrat Usama bin Zaid (R.A); the campaigns of Ridda against apostasy and the false prophets; jihad against those who refused to pay Zakat; compilation of the Holy Qur'an into one volume (Mushaf); and the beginning of the campaigns towards Iraq and Syria.

  61. What administrative feature is highlighted in Khilafat-e-Siddiqi?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The handout highlights consultation (Shura) in important matters.

  62. How was administration organized?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The handout describes division into provinces for administrative convenience.

  63. What does the handout discuss regarding Umar's agreements?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Muwaafiqqat-e-Umar are the matters in which Hazrat Umar's (R.A) opinion was later confirmed by revelation; for example, taking Maqam-e-Ibrahim as a place of prayer, the verses of Hijab (veiling), and his opinion regarding the prisoners of Badr.

  64. Who became caliph after Abu Bakr Siddiq?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Umar Farooq (RA).

  65. What are key features of Umar's caliphate in the handout?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Vast territorial expansion (Persia, Syria, Egypt and beyond); a highly organized administration with provinces and appointed governors; separation of the judiciary from the executive; establishment of departments (Diwan) for the army and stipends; the Hijri calendar; census, police, canals, roads and rest houses; and strict accountability of public officials.

  66. What does the handout mention about the caliphates of Uthman and Ali?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Hazrat Uthman (R.A): preparation of the standard recension of the Holy Qur'an (Mushaf-e-Usmani) and dispatch of its copies to the provinces, expansion of Masjid-e-Nabawi, establishment of the first Muslim naval fleet, and continuation of Shura-based administration. Hazrat Ali (R.A): shifting of the capital to Kufa, administrative reorganization, appointment and removal of governors, and efforts to preserve the unity of the Ummah during internal conflicts, with Shura maintained in important matters.

  67. What two benefits of Taqwa have been described in the verse: "Wa man yattaqillaha yaj'al lahu makhraja wa yarzuqhu min haythu la yahtasib" (Surah At-Talaq)?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The verse (65:2-3) describes two benefits of Taqwa: (1) Allah Almighty makes a way out (deliverance) for the God-fearing person from every difficulty and distress; and (2) Allah provides for him sustenance from sources he could never imagine or expect.

  68. Describe the main characteristics of the Islamic social system in the light of the Hadith: "Laysa minna man lam yarham sagheerana wa yuwaqqir kabeerana wa ya'mur bil-ma'roof wa yanha anil-munkar."
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    According to this Hadith, the Islamic social system is characterised by: (1) mercy and kindness towards the young; (2) respect and honour for the elders; (3) enjoining good (amr bil-ma'roof); and (4) forbidding evil (nahi anil-munkar). One who lacks these qualities is not counted among the true followers of the Prophet (PBUH).

  69. Translate the following verse: "Wa ma ramayta iz ramayta wa lakinnallaha rama" (Surah Al-Anfal 8:17).
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    "And you (O Muhammad PBUH) did not throw when you threw, but Allah threw." The verse refers to the Battle of Badr, when the Holy Prophet (PBUH) threw a handful of dust towards the enemy; its effect was produced by Allah Almighty, teaching that real success comes from Allah, not from human means.

  70. What is the literal and technical meaning of the word "Ummi" used in the Holy Qur'an regarding Hazrat Muhammad (PBUH)?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    Literally, "Ummi" means one who has not learned reading and writing - the unlettered. Technically, in the Qur'an it is the title of the Holy Prophet Muhammad (PBUH), who did not learn reading or writing from any human teacher; he was taught directly by Allah Almighty through revelation. His being Ummi is itself a proof of the miraculous nature of the Qur'an, which came through a Prophet who had never read any book.

  71. What is a Mawdu (fabricated) Hadith?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    A Mawdu Hadith is a narration that is forged and falsely attributed to the Holy Prophet (PBUH) - i.e., it was invented by a liar and is not the saying, action or approval of the Prophet (PBUH) at all. It is the worst category of weak narrations and it is unlawful to narrate it except to expose its fabrication.

  72. Who is called Zun-Noorain and why?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    Hazrat Uthman bin Affan (R.A) is called Zun-Noorain, "the possessor of two lights", because he married two daughters of the Holy Prophet (PBUH) one after the other: Hazrat Ruqayyah (R.A), and after her demise, Hazrat Umm Kulthum (R.A).

  73. "Islam is a complete code of life." Explain.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    Islam is not merely a set of rituals; it provides guidance for every sphere of human life - beliefs, worship, morality, family, society, economy, law and state. The Qur'an and Sunnah lay down principles for individual as well as collective life, so a Muslim does not need to look outside Islam for direction in any field of life.

  74. Translate the opening verses of Surah Al-Alaq.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    "Read in the name of your Lord who created. He created man from a clot of blood. Read, and your Lord is the Most Generous, Who taught by the pen - taught man that which he did not know." (96:1-5) These were the first verses revealed to the Holy Prophet (PBUH) in the cave of Hira.

  75. Write down the salient characteristics of the Khulafa-e-Rashideen (the rightly guided caliphs).
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    The rightly guided caliphs were distinguished by: deep piety and Taqwa; complete adherence to the Qur'an and Sunnah; government by consultation (Shura); justice and equality for all citizens; simplicity of personal life; accountability of themselves and their officials before Allah and the people; and selfless service of Islam and the welfare of the Ummah.

  76. Write any two Ahadith about controlling anger.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    (1) The Holy Prophet (PBUH) said: "The strong is not the one who overcomes the people by wrestling, but the strong is the one who controls himself when he is angry." (2) The Prophet (PBUH) advised a man who asked for counsel: "Do not become angry" - and repeated it several times. In another narration, the one who swallows his anger while being able to act upon it will be rewarded greatly by Allah on the Day of Judgment.

  77. What is Khilafat? Define it.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Khilafat is the system of government in Islam in which a caliph (Khalifa), elected or nominated with the consultation and consent of the Muslims, runs the affairs of the state according to the Qur'an and Sunnah. The caliph is the successor of the Holy Prophet (PBUH) in the administration of religion and worldly affairs, not in prophethood.

  78. Enlist five points of the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    (1) The Muslims would return to Madinah that year without performing Umrah, and come back the next year for three days. (2) There would be a truce (no war) between the two parties for ten years. (3) Any tribe could join either party as an ally. (4) A person coming from Makkah to the Prophet (PBUH) without his guardian's permission would be returned, while a Muslim going to Makkah would not be returned. (5) The Muslims would not carry weapons except sheathed swords during the next year's Umrah.

  79. Write five important steps of the Usmani era (the caliphate of Hazrat Uthman R.A).
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    (1) Preparation of the standard recension of the Holy Qur'an (Mushaf-e-Usmani) and dispatch of official copies to the provinces, ending recitation disputes. (2) Expansion and renovation of Masjid-e-Nabawi and Masjid al-Haram. (3) Establishment of the first Muslim naval fleet. (4) Further territorial expansion in Persia, Armenia, Azerbaijan and North Africa. (5) Continuation of the Diwan system, stipends and Shura-based administration.

  80. Write the features of Jami Tirmidhi.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    Jami Tirmidhi, compiled by Imam Abu Isa Muhammad bin Isa al-Tirmidhi, is one of the six canonical books of Hadith (Sihah Sitta). Its features: it is arranged in jurisprudential (fiqhi) order; the author states the grade of most narrations (Sahih, Hasan, Daif); he mentions the opinions of the jurists on the issues discussed; and he frequently adds the practice prevalent on a narration. The use of the grade "Hasan" for Hadith was popularised by Imam Tirmidhi, who also authored Al-Shama'il al-Muhammadiyyah on the qualities of the Prophet (PBUH).

  81. What was the reaction of Hazrat Umar (R.A) on hearing the news of the demise of the Holy Prophet (PBUH)?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    Hazrat Umar (R.A) was so overwhelmed with grief that he could not believe the news; standing in the mosque he declared that the Prophet (PBUH) had not died and would return, like Hazrat Musa (A.S). Hazrat Abu Bakr (R.A) then came, confirmed the demise, and recited the verse "Muhammad is only a Messenger; messengers have passed away before him..." (3:144), upon which Hazrat Umar (R.A) accepted the reality, saying it was as if he had never heard that verse before.

  82. Name three cities founded during the caliphate of Hazrat Umar (R.A).
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Basra and Kufa in Iraq, and Fustat in Egypt were founded as garrison/administrative cities during the caliphate of Hazrat Umar Farooq (R.A).

  83. Write Awwaliyat-e-Umar (R.A) - the firsts introduced by Hazrat Umar (R.A).
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    Hazrat Umar (R.A) was the first to: adopt the title Ameer-ul-Momineen; start the Hijri calendar; establish the Diwan (register for army stipends); separate the judiciary from the executive and appoint Qadis; establish the police and night patrols; conduct a census; fix stipends for the public; and establish departments such as Bait-ul-Mal (public treasury) in an organised form.

  84. Who was Hammam bin Munabbeh and what is the name of his book?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 3 Marks marks

    Hammam bin Munabbeh was a famous student of Hazrat Abu Hurairah (R.A) and a Taabi'i scholar of Yemen. He compiled the narrations he heard from Abu Hurairah (R.A) in a collection called Sahifa Hammam bin Munabbeh, also known as Sahifa al-Sahiha, which is preserved within the Musannaf of Abdur Razzaq and the Musnad of Imam Ahmad.

  85. Name any three martyrs each of Ghazwa-e-Badr and Ghazwa-e-Uhud.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Martyrs of Badr include Hazrat Ubaidah bin al-Harith (R.A), Hazrat Umair bin Abi Waqqas (R.A) and Hazrat Mihja (R.A), the freed slave of Hazrat Umar (R.A) - 14 Muslims were martyred in total. Martyrs of Uhud include Hazrat Hamza bin Abdul Muttalib (R.A), Hazrat Mus'ab bin Umair (R.A) and Hazrat Abdullah bin Jahsh (R.A) - 70 Muslims were martyred in total.

  86. Write a Hadith on Kasb-e-Halal (lawful earning) and explain it.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The Holy Prophet (PBUH) said: "Seeking lawful (halal) earning is an obligation after the (other) obligations." In another narration he said that no one eats better food than that earned by the work of his own hands, and that the truthful, honest trader will be with the prophets, the truthful and the martyrs. Explanation: Islam does not separate worship from economic life; earning a lawful livelihood through honest effort is itself an act of worship, while unlawful income corrupts both worship and society.

  87. Name the people who accepted Islam at the invitation of Hazrat Abu Bakr Siddiq (R.A).
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Through the invitation of Hazrat Abu Bakr Siddiq (R.A), many prominent Companions accepted Islam, including Hazrat Uthman bin Affan, Hazrat Zubair bin al-Awwam, Hazrat Talha bin Ubaidullah, Hazrat Saad bin Abi Waqqas, Hazrat Abdur Rahman bin Awf, Hazrat Abu Ubaidah bin al-Jarrah, Hazrat Uthman bin Mazoon and Hazrat Khalid bin Saeed bin al-Aas (R.A).

  88. Why was the Holy Qur'an revealed in Arabic?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The Qur'an was revealed in Arabic because it was first addressed to the Arabs, in the language of the Holy Prophet (PBUH) and his people, so that they could understand it clearly and convey it to others; the Qur'an itself says it was sent down as an Arabic Qur'an so that people may understand. Arabic was also the most eloquent and expressive language of its time and audience, suited to the Qur'an's miraculous eloquence (I'jaz).

  89. How did Allah Almighty help the believers in Ghazwa-e-Ahzab, as described in Surah Al-Ahzab?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    In the Battle of Ahzab (the Trench), Allah helped the believers by sending a severe windstorm and unseen hosts (angels) against the confederate armies, which uprooted their tents, extinguished their fires and filled them with terror, so the enemy withdrew without achieving its aim despite besieging Madinah - a help granted without a major battle, as a favour (minnah) of Allah upon the believers.

  90. What are the signs of the wise people (Ulul Albab) in the light of Surah Aal-e-Imran?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics • 5 Marks marks

    According to Surah Aal-e-Imran (3:190-191), the signs of the people of understanding are: they remember Allah standing, sitting and lying on their sides; they reflect deeply on the creation of the heavens and the earth; they conclude that Allah has not created this in vain and glorify Him; and they seek refuge in Allah from the punishment of the Fire - combining remembrance (Zikr) with reflection (Fikr).
