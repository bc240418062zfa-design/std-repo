  13. Write short notes on the three theories of communication: electronic theory, social environment theory and rhetorical theory.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Electronic Theory: The message is transmitted as a signal through a channel, where it may be distorted by noise (such as smudged typing or acoustical problems). As a last step, the receiver decodes the message. This theory is helpful because it introduces the ideas of senders and receivers and of possible interference, and it emphasizes one important aspect of communication: accuracy. Its usefulness is limited, because people are not machines. Social Environment Theory: We need to understand the rules, or the 'culture', of the environment in order to communicate — both the official rules, such as company policies and practices, and the unwritten rules regarding to whom, how, when and for how long it is appropriate to communicate within a certain organization. This theory adds the important dimension of the specific social situation. Rhetorical Theory: Communication is not linear, but circular; not just sending a message to be received, but producing a response; not static, but dynamic. Rhetorical theorists provide an important addition to a communication model for business communicators.

  14. Describe the flow of communication in an organization.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    An exchange of information within an organization is called internal communication. It takes place in the following directions: Downward Flow: Organizational decisions are made at the top level and then flow down to the people who carry them out. Upward Flow: To solve problems and make intelligent decisions, managers need to know what is going on in the organization. Many executives want comments from employees in addition to the usual periodic reports. Horizontal Flow: It takes place between peers in organizations in order to solve problems, perform job duties, prepare for meetings and cooperate on important projects. Informal Communication (Grapevine): Every organization has an informal communication network — a grapevine — that supplements the official channels. It is the casual conversation of an organization and an important source of information. Communication that takes place outside the organization is called external communication, e.g. letters, pamphlets, annual reports and interviews with the news media.

  15. Do you think it is important for a good communicator to have certain traits? Write down the traits of a good communicator.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Yes. Following are the traits of good communicators: Perception: They are able to predict how you will receive their message. They anticipate your reaction and shape the message accordingly. Precision: They create a 'meeting of the minds'. When they finish expressing themselves, you share the same mental picture. Credibility: They are believable. They have faith in the substance of their message. You trust their information and their intentions. Control: They shape your response. Depending on their purpose, they can make you laugh or cry, calm down, change your mind or take action. Congeniality: They maintain friendly, pleasant relations with you, regardless of whether you agree with them or not.

  17. Point out some barriers in communication which arise due to attitude and value differences of sender and receiver.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The term attitude describes people and explains their behaviour. More precisely, an attitude can be defined as a persistent tendency to feel and behave in a particular way towards some object. A receiver's attitude toward a message can determine whether it is accepted or rejected. The effectiveness of communication is influenced also by the values, attitudes and opinions of the communicators. People react favourably when they receive an agreeable message, and occasionally people react according to their attitudes toward a situation rather than to the facts. For example, a person who does not like the night shift has a negative attitude towards his work assignment, so a message about it is likely to be rejected. Similarly, a closed-minded person, who holds rigid views on certain subjects regardless of the circumstances, is very difficult to communicate with.

  18. What type of barriers in communication will be faced by a foreigner in a country he visited for the first time, and how will he be able to overcome them?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    People in the world are not exactly alike; cultures or countries are not the same. These differences can cause problems in conveying meanings and become barriers in communication for a foreigner. He will mainly face the following barriers: 1. Semantic Barriers: A basic principle of communication is that the symbols the sender uses must have the same meaning in both the sender's and the receiver's minds. Words have denotative meanings (the dictionary definition) and connotative meanings (qualitative judgements and personal reactions). For example, 'slim' has a positive meaning in 'slim girl' and a negative one in 'slim chances'. 2. Physical Barriers: These are caused by your own physical appearance, your audience, or the context of the document or the presentation. They include psychological barriers (each person's unique perception of reality), emotional barriers, barriers involving values and attitudes, and the barrier of perception of reality (abstracting, slanting and risky inferences). To overcome these barriers, the foreigner should learn the language and culture of the host country, use simple, clear and denotative words, avoid slanting and risky inferences, keep an open mind, respect local values and customs, and seek feedback to confirm that his message has been understood as intended.

  19. 'Environmental factors can greatly affect the process of communication.' Discuss.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Organizations give consideration to office space, factory area and layout, the sales area and conference venues. The environment should put people at ease and match their expectations; an uncomfortable environment can produce 'noise' that causes communication barriers and interferes with the communication process. Therefore, the desires for privacy, familiarity and security need to be satisfied. Careful design of the working area meets these needs and in so doing improves communication, productivity and morale. Light, colour, tables, chairs, desks, temperature, plants, sound, artwork and wall coverings all have a good impact on people and on the image of the organization.

  21. What are the main points which should be kept in mind while writing a message carefully?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Before writing a message, the following planning steps are necessary for effective communication: 1. Define the purpose of the message. 2. Analyze your audience — readers or listeners. 3. Choose the ideas to include. 4. Collect all the facts to back up these ideas. 5. Outline — organize your message. During the careful creation of the message, focus on the receiver: keep the reader in mind, use the 'you attitude', and apply the seven Cs — clarity, conciseness, consideration, concreteness, correctness, courtesy and completeness. Choose the direct approach when the reader will respond favourably, and the indirect approach (with a buffer) for bad news. Finally, revise, edit and proofread the message before sending it.

  22. Which aspects should be kept in mind while drafting a message?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A message is the main idea that a writer wants to communicate. A message should be clear, complete, concise and correct. While drafting a message you should keep in mind the reader. If it is good news, the message should start with the main idea. In case of bad news or a refusal, there should be a buffer. The closing of the message should be effective.

  23. Write a note on editing and proofreading.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Editing means revising the message that is still in the rough draft. At this stage the writer reviews the content, organization and style of the message: ideas are added, deleted or rearranged, wordy expressions are removed, and the message is checked for clarity, conciseness, completeness and correctness. Proofreading is the final checking of the finished copy before it is distributed. It means reading the final draft carefully to make sure that it is free from errors of spelling, grammar, punctuation, capitalization and typing, and that names, figures and facts are accurate. Editing improves what the message says and how it says it; proofreading makes sure that the final copy is error-free.

  24. 'When a message is vivid, definite and specific, it conveys meaning effectively.' Discuss the importance of concreteness in a business message.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Concreteness means that a message should be vivid, definite and specific. If a message convinces the reader, the sender will get the reply and feedback; if a message lacks these qualities, it will be vague and general and may be ignored by the reader. Concrete facts and figures make the reader know exactly what is required or desired by the sender. For example, 'The chief executive decided' is clearer than 'A decision has been made.' To achieve concreteness, denotative words should be used instead of connotative words, and specific facts and figures and active verbs should be used. A concrete message always leaves a positive impression on the reader.

  25. 'Conciseness means saying what you want to say in the fewest possible words.' Discuss.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Conciseness can be achieved in the following ways: Eliminate wordy expressions: Use single words whenever possible. For example, the wordy expression 'I want to take this opportunity to tell you that we are grateful to you' can be concisely written as 'Thank you'. Include only relevant material: Your reader will lose interest in your message if he finds irrelevant things in it. Avoid information obvious to the reader, long introductions, excessive adjectives and pompous words. Avoid unnecessary repetition: Avoid repetition by using pronouns, short names or acronyms. Stick to the purpose of the message. Writing concisely means using only necessary, meaningful words, which saves the reader's time and effort.

  31. Write a note on beginnings and endings in a letter.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Beginning: When you begin your message, decide whether your reader will respond favourably or unfavourably to the message. Keep the main idea or good news in the beginning; in case of bad news, begin with a buffer. The opening must be impressive in a way that it captures the attention of the reader, so always choose appropriate openings that suit the purpose of your message. Ending: An effective ending will motivate the reader to act as requested. If no direct request is required, leave the reader with some expression of regard, assurance, appreciation or willingness to help. Always remember that closings should be strong, clear and polite; they should leave a sense of closure and goodwill with the receiver.

  32. What are the main points to be kept in mind about a close paragraph?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The following points should be kept in mind about a close paragraph: 1. Make the action you desire easy and clear for the reader. 2. Keep the last paragraph concise and correct. 3. End on a positive, courteous thought. 4. Do not make a refusal statement or bring in apologies in the closing; end with goodwill.

  33. Write a note on different punctuation styles.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    There are three types of punctuation styles: 1. Closed Punctuation: In this style the heading, date, inside address, salutation and complimentary close are punctuated. 2. Standard Punctuation: No line of the heading or inside address is punctuated; a comma is placed only after the salutation and the complimentary close. 3. Open Punctuation: In this style no punctuation is used, even after the salutation and the complimentary close.

  37. What are the different parts of an interoffice memorandum? Describe briefly.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    An interoffice memorandum has the following parts: Heading: It contains TO (to whom the memo is addressed), FROM (who is writing the message), DATE, and SUBJECT of the memo. Body: The message itself is written in this part. Signatory: At the end, the signature or initials of the writer are given. Thus, a memorandum usually consists of three main parts: the heading, the subject and date, and the message.

  38. What is a buffer?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A buffer is a statement that helps you bring your reader in a positive frame of mind, and the message becomes readable for the reader. It means a positive, pleasant rather than negative statement. A buffer may be based on agreement, appreciation, assurance, compliment, cooperation, good news, neutral courtesy or understanding.

  39. Write a detailed note on buffer.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A buffer is defined as a statement that helps to bring the reader in a positive frame of mind, and the message becomes readable and easy to understand for the reader. Bad-news messages begin with a buffer. However, the buffer should be neutral in tone, and the writer should try to avoid misleading the reader into thinking that the message is good. A good buffer is relevant to the message, shows appreciation or agreement, and leads the reader smoothly towards the bad news that follows.

  40. Which things should we avoid while writing a buffer?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    While writing a buffer, the following things should be avoided: 1. Avoid misleading the reader into thinking that the message contains good news. 2. Avoid apologizing for the bad news. 3. Avoid repeating or anticipating the bad news itself. 4. Avoid negative words and statements that accuse or blame the reader. 5. Avoid making the buffer so long that the real news is delayed too much; keep it brief, relevant and neutral in tone.

  41. What is the direct (deductive) approach?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    In the direct or deductive approach, the main idea of the message is stated at the very beginning, and the supporting details and explanation follow it. This approach is used for good-news messages and direct requests, when the reader will respond favourably, will be pleased, or is interested and willing to cooperate. It saves the reader's time and gives him a clear picture of the message at once.

  42. What is the organizational plan for order letters? Discuss each point briefly.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    1. Give the information in a clear format: Write a separate, single-spaced paragraph for each item, with double spacing between paragraphs, or arrange the order in a tabular form. When several sets of numbers, items and prices are given, tabular form is clearer than writing the information in sentences. 2. Write orders, not just hints: Legally, an order letter is the 'offer' portion of a contract; the 'acceptance' is completed when the seller sends the merchandise. Use specific and direct openings such as 'Please send me' rather than vague phrases such as 'I am interested in…'. 3. Give a complete description of each item: quantity ordered, catalogue (or model or stock) number, name of product, description (colour, size, material, grade or quality, pattern, finish), unit price and total price. 4. Tell how you will pay for the order: personal cheque, COD, money order or credit card; add any shipping charges and sales tax. 5. Tell where, when and how you want the merchandise shipped.

  48. What points should be kept in mind while writing a letter refusing an order?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A letter refusing an order calls for the indirect plan. The following points should be kept in mind: 1. Begin with a buffer — thank the customer for the order and show appreciation. 2. Explain the reasons for the refusal clearly and honestly (for example, the item is out of stock or has been discontinued). 3. State the refusal tactfully, in positive language, without blaming the reader. 4. Offer an alternative or a substitute product, or tell the customer when the item will be available again, if possible. 5. Close with goodwill, expressing hope for future business.

  49. Which points should be kept in mind while writing get-well wishes and sympathy letters?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Following points should be kept in mind while writing get-well wishes and sympathy letters: Be optimistic when you write to someone who is ill. Mention once at the beginning of the letter how sorry you are that the person is ill. From then on, talk about a return to normal life. If the illness is not serious and recovery is expected, a cheerful, happy letter can be written; if the illness is serious, the tone should be sympathetic and serious, not humorous.

  50. What are the four stages of the AIDA plan? Describe briefly.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    One way to organize persuasive messages is the AIDA plan, which has four stages: 1. Attention 2. Interest 3. Desire 4. Action. In the attention stage, you convince the reader that you have something interesting or useful for him. In the interest stage, you explain how your message is related to your reader. In the desire stage, you provide relevant evidence to prove your claim, drawing attention to any enclosures. In the action stage, you close the message with an action ending that suggests a specific action the reader may take.

  52. What are the advantages and disadvantages of an e-mail?

    Advantages: 1. E-mail is a faster and more efficient channel than regular mail (sometimes referred to as snail mail); most messages reach anywhere in the world within minutes of being sent. 2. It saves paper. 3. It can be sent at any convenient time. 4. A message can be written and edited quickly by several people before it is sent. 5. The system is inaccessible to those who are computer illiterate or not online. 4. Its contents may reappear later in a variety of printed forms. 5. It lacks nonverbal communication cues to add meaning. 6. It can be overused.

  54. Explain the term culture and point out the main aspects of the intercultural communication model.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Culture refers to the behavioural characteristic typical of a group; it can be defined as all the ways of life, including arts, beliefs and institutions of a population, that are passed down from generation to generation. The main aspects of the intercultural communication model are: 1. Background to intercultural communication: the sender and the receiver are both affected by their own cultures, so the same message may be encoded and decoded differently. 2. National cultural variables: education, politics, social norms, language, economics and regulations. 3. Individual cultural variables: time, space, food, accepted dress, manners and decision making. To communicate successfully across cultures, a communicator must understand both sets of variables and avoid ethnocentrism — judging other people or countries by the cultural standards of his own group.

  55. Name the national and individual cultural variables in the intercultural communication model.

  56. Individual cultural variables are very important to understand intercultural communication. Discuss with three individual cultural variables.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Individual cultural variables are very important to understand intercultural communication, because while communicating, the message sender and the receiver are both affected by external and internal stimuli. Three individual cultural variables are discussed below: 1. Food: Food is one of the individual cultural variables. Before visiting your host country, it may be a good idea to visit various ethnic restaurants in your home country; then you will have an initial idea of the kinds of foods available and how they are served, prepared or eaten. 2. Manners: Knowing manners is also very important. You must follow the manners according to the country. Children shake your hand in Germany and hug you in Italy, so different countries have different manners of greeting. 3. Time: Persons in Latin America and the Middle East treat time more casually than do Americans, who prefer promptness. Germans are time-precise. In Latin America and in Buddhist cultures you may wait an hour, just reflecting a different concept of time; arriving late is a socially accepted custom.

  57. What points should we consider while discussing the Space (proxemics) as an individual cultural variable?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Space means nearness in physical space. Personal space varies according to culture, status, role and gender. Some cultures consider those who stand close to you as intrusive, rude, pushy and overbearing, while in other cultures standing close is normal and friendly. The space between the sender and the receiver of a message influences the way the message is interpreted, so a communicator should respect the space conventions of the other culture.

  58. What is the role of time, space and silence in effective communication?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Time: Time plays a vital role in making communication effective. Time is one of the most central differences that separate cultures and cultural ways of doing things. In the West, time tends to be seen as quantitative, measured in units that reflect the march of progress. Germans are considered time-precise, whereas persons in Latin America and the Middle East treat time more casually than do Americans, who prefer promptness. Space: Space is also very important in communication. How close strangers may stand to you differs from culture to culture. The space between the sender and the receiver of a message influences the way the message is interpreted. Some cultures consider those who stand close to you as intrusive, rude, pushy and overbearing. Silence: Silence is also used as feedback. Although it is not very effective, it still gives feedback to the sender; silence for a while should also be maintained in order to know the response to your communication.

  59. Explain the term artifacts.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Artifacts are objects used to convey nonverbal messages about self-concept, image, mood, feeling or style. For example, lipstick, clothes, perfumes, glasses and hairpieces reflect the style or mood of the user. Many artifacts are common to the group, but we also use artifacts, particularly clothing, as an individual form of communication.

  60. What is meant by globalization? Discuss its implications for organizational behaviour.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Globalization means that for a company to survive, it must establish markets not only in its own country but also in many foreign countries. It describes a process by which regional economies, societies and cultures have become integrated through a globe-spanning network of communication and trade; it is the process of increasing the connectivity and interdependence of the world's markets and businesses. The implications of globalization on organizational behaviour are profound and direct: 1. New organizational structures. 2. Different forms of communication. 3. Need for more sensitivity to cultural differences. 4. More competition, as companies compete with foreign competitors and the world is viewed as a single market. 5. A diverse workforce, whose management becomes more challenging as organizations expand internationally; people from different countries face difficulties in nonverbal communication due to differences in their cultures.
