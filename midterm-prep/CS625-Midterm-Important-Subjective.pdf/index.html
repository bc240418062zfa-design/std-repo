  1. Define profession, professional and professionalism.
    Topic: The Profession and Professionalism

    Profession: A paid occupation, especially one that involves prolonged training and a formal qualification. Professional: A professional is a member of a profession or any person who earns their living from a specified professional activity; the term also describes the standards of education and training that prepare members of the profession with the particular knowledge and skills necessary to perform their specific role within that profession. Professionalism: Professionalism is a way of thinking and living rather than an accumulation of learning - a profession is not just what you do, it is who you are.

  2. List and briefly explain any five traits of a professional.
    Topic: The Profession and Professionalism

    (1) Seriousness: the professional is serious about the job. (2) Wanting to do better: a never-ending quest to improve performance in every variable, every project, every relationship and every detail. (3) Dealing with the unexpected: when things change, the true professional rises to the occasion. (4) Communication skills: clear, concise and confident communication. (5) Enthusiasm: attitude is everything; a professional greets each day with a positive attitude and inevitably becomes a leader. (Other traits: helpfulness, taking the initiative, cool under pressure, remains focused, and don't follow - lead.)

  3. Differentiate between law, morals and ethics.
    Topic: The Profession and Professionalism

    Law: Rules that mandate or prohibit certain behavior in society; breaking the law can earn a fine or jail time. Moral values: The fixed moral attitudes or customs of a particular group; breaking a moral can ruin your reputation. Ethics: Ethics define socially acceptable behavior; they mean something more than 'law' and 'morals' and carry an additional connotation of 'rightness'; breaking an ethic can ruin your conscience. It is possible to break all three simultaneously.

  4. What is a code of ethics? What does it address?
    Topic: Professional Ethics and Code of Ethics

    A code of ethics is established by various professional organizations. It produces a positive effect on judgment and establishes the responsibility of professionals to act ethically according to the policies and procedures of their employers, professional organizations and the laws of society; organizations assume the responsibility to develop, disseminate and enforce these policies. The code provides an aid to individual decision making and addresses nine different areas (with some overlap): intellectual property, privacy, confidentiality, professional quality, fairness or discrimination, liability, software risks, conflicts of interest, and unauthorized access to computer systems.

  5. Write down five specific professional responsibilities of an ACM computing professional.
    Topic: Professional Ethics and Code of Ethics

    As an ACM computing professional I will: (1) Strive to achieve the highest quality, effectiveness and dignity in both the process and products of professional work. (2) Acquire and maintain professional competence. (3) Know and respect existing laws pertaining to professional work. (4) Accept and provide appropriate professional review. (5) Give comprehensive and thorough evaluations of computer systems and their impacts, including analysis of possible risks. (Further responsibilities: honor contracts, agreements and assigned responsibilities; improve public understanding of computing and its consequences; access computing and communication resources only when authorized to do so.)

  6. Explain the principles of the IEEE Code of Ethics.
    Topic: Professional Ethics and Code of Ethics

    1. Public: Software engineers shall act consistently with the public interest. 2. Client and Employer: Software engineers shall act in a manner that is in the best interests of their client and employer, consistent with the public interest. 3. Product: Software engineers shall ensure that their products and related modifications meet the highest professional standards. 4. Judgment: Software engineers shall maintain integrity and independence in their professional judgment. 5. Management: Software engineering managers and leaders shall subscribe to and promote an ethical approach to the management of software development and maintenance. 6. Profession: Software engineers shall advance the integrity and reputation of the profession, consistent with the public interest. 7. Colleagues: Software engineers shall be fair to and supportive of their colleagues. 8. Self: Software engineers shall participate in lifelong learning regarding the practice of their profession and shall promote an ethical approach to the practice of the profession.

  7. Ali is a database programmer whose company needs a large statistical program. Stuck on a problem for several months and under pressure from his manager to finish in a few days, Ali directly incorporates segments of code from a co-worker's program and from an early version of a commercial software package into his own program, without telling anyone or mentioning it in the documentation. Which issues of the code of ethics are involved in this case, and how did Ali violate them?
    Topic: Professional Ethics and Code of Ethics

    This case highlights issues involving intellectual property. Ali violated professional ethics in two areas: (1) Failure to give credit for another's work - he used his co-worker's code without acknowledgment; even if he had only 'looked' at the co-worker's source code and then written his own program, he would still have had an obligation to give credit in the documentation. (2) Using code from a copyrighted commercial package - Ali should have checked to determine whether or not the company was authorized to use that source code before using it. In general, it is desirable to share and exchange intellectual materials, but using software in this way is definitely a violation of the code.

  8. Aisha's company has been hired by a client to build a security system. Because of cost overruns, the client has decided to opt for a less secure system, although Aisha believes the information to be stored is extremely sensitive and could be accessed by employees and online intruders. What should Aisha do?
    Topic: Professional Ethics and Code of Ethics

    This case highlights issues involving privacy. Company officials have an obligation to protect the privacy of the information and of their employees; therefore they should not accept inadequate security. Aisha's first obligation is to attempt to educate the company officials about the risk. If that fails, she needs to consider her contractual obligations in honoring assigned responsibilities. She may ultimately have to choose between her contract and her obligation to honor privacy and security.

  9. Describe the legal forms of commercial organizations.
    Topic: The Structure of Organizations

    (1) Sole trader: An individual who is the sole person responsible for all debts; all assets, including 'private' assets, are at risk. A sole trader does not have to be the only employee (examples: local shop, plumber). (2) Partnership: Two or more people, all at risk, similar to a sole trader but with more than one person; normally professionals such as doctors, lawyers and accountants; inflexible in the normal commercial world because of the movement of key personnel and the risk involved. (3) Limited company: May be private (Company Limited - cannot sell shares to the public, but can sell shares privately) or public (Public Limited Company - PLC, trades shares to the public). Companies may be limited by shares (commercial companies), limited by guarantee (charities, professional bodies) or unlimited. A company has an independent legal existence, divided among its shareholders or members.

  10. Differentiate between the Memorandum of Association and the Articles of Association of a company.
    Topic: The Structure of Organizations

    Memorandum of Association: It controls the external relations of the company. It contains the company name (with restrictions), the country of registration (England & Wales, Wales or Scotland), the objects of the company, a liability clause (the liability of members is limited) and the authorised share value (nominal share value and number). Articles of Association: They control the internal relations of the company. They contain the rules of share capital, transfer of shares, meetings of members, rules governing directors' appointments, powers of directors, and dividends and reserves.

  11. What are the functional units of an organization? Briefly describe each.
    Topic: The Structure of Organizations

    Five groups of functions exist in almost any organization: (1) Production: Activities that directly contribute to creating the products or services that the company sells. (2) Quality management: Quality activities necessary to ensure that the quality of the products and services produced is maintained at the agreed level. (3) Sales and Marketing: Sales is concerned directly with selling the product, while marketing is concerned with establishing the environment in which the product is sold (e.g. through advertising) and with deciding how the range of products sold by the company should develop. (4) Finance and Administration: To pay bills, to look after the funds of the organization, and all central services. (5) Research and Development: How the company can do better the things that it already does, and what other things it might profitably be doing.

  12. What are the responsibilities of the directors of a company?
    Topic: The Structure of Organizations

    Directors are elected by the shareholders. They must act in the best interest of the company, be honest, declare their interests, and be aware of the company's trading position. A board includes executive and non-executive directors. The company secretary, who could also be a director, is responsible for the company's formal administration.

  13. Differentiate between centralization and decentralization in an organization.
    Topic: The Structure of Organizations

    In a centralized organization, the detailed operational decisions are taken at the centre. In a decentralized organization, as many details as possible are settled at the local level. In a company of the size of Syniad, the distinction between centralization and decentralization has little meaning: centralized policies and procedures are widely used, but they have usually been developed within one part of the company and have been adopted by general consent.

  14. Describe the roles of the Operations Director and the Technical Director in Syniad Software Company.
    Topic: Anatomy of a Software House

    Operations Director: The Operations Director is responsible for all the revenue earning operations of the company. It is his job to ensure that all projects are completed satisfactorily and that resources are available to carry out the projects that the company wins; the personnel reports to him. Technical Director: The Technical Director is responsible for quality management; research and development; marketing at a technical level (e.g. arranging for staff to give papers at conferences); and technical training (as opposed to training in, say, project management or presentational skills, which is the responsibility of the personnel function).

  15. Which two reports are used for assessing and monitoring the sales position of a company? Explain them.
    Topic: Anatomy of a Software House

    (1) The confirmed sales report shows, for each grade, the number of staff in that grade who are committed to contracts in each of the following twelve months and the total expected revenue from that grade in each month. (2) The sales prospects report shows, for each sales prospect, the potential value of the sale, its likelihood and the likely start date.

  16. How does Syniad Software Company monitor its financial performance?
    Topic: Anatomy of a Software House

    Syniad monitors its performance against the budget in three areas: (1) Costs and revenue: Each month, the income and expenditure under the various heads are compared and, if significant deviations are observed, corrective action is taken; in practice this is difficult because of random fluctuations, staff hiring, large projects and fixed price project estimation. (2) Project costing: The costs and revenue of each project are calculated each month and the cumulative gross margin (i.e. the difference between total costs and total revenue to date on the project) is calculated as a percentage of the total revenue. (3) Sales: The forward sales position is monitored through the confirmed sales report and the sales prospects report.

  17. Why does a new company need capital? For what purposes is cash needed in the starting period?
    Topic: Organizational Financial Practices

    A client is unlikely to pay an invoice within less than one month of receiving it, and some large companies are notorious for not paying invoices for as much as six or even twelve months. The group therefore needs enough cash in hand to be able to live for at least three months, and additional money will be needed for the expenses of starting the company. For large projects or packages, a much larger sum of money is likely to be needed while they are being developed, because there will be no revenue coming into the company. In the starting period, cash will be needed for: salaries; rent, rates, heating and lighting of the premises used; equipment and consumables; costs of advertising and marketing the products; and miscellaneous expenses, ranging from company stationery to travelling expenses.

  18. What sources of funds are available to a company? Differentiate between grants, loans and sale of equity.
    Topic: Organizational Financial Practices

    Sources of funds can be grouped into grants, loans and sale of equity. Grant: A grant is a sum of money given to the company; while the company is obliged to demonstrate that it has been used for the purposes for which it was intended, it is not intended that the grant should ever be paid back to the organization which gave it. Loan: A loan is a sum of money lent to the company; interest is payable on it, at a rate that may be fixed or variable, and the loan is usually for a fixed period. The company is liable to pay back the loan and, if the company goes into liquidation, the lender is entitled to recover the loan from the sale of the assets of the company. In most cases, security is required for the loan. Sale of equity: Equity capital is money paid to the company in exchange for a share in the ownership of the company. Shareholders are at a much greater risk of getting a poor return on their capital, or even losing it completely, than are lenders, but in compensation they stand to make a greater profit than lenders if all goes well.

  19. What is working capital? Why can a company be profitable and yet unable to pay its bills?
    Topic: Organizational Financial Practices

    Accounting normally operates on an accrual basis, so it is perfectly possible for a company to be consistently profitable and yet be unable to pay its bills. Cash has to be found to cover the gap between what a company has to pay out in cash and what it receives in cash - this is the working capital. A cash flow prediction (the amount of cash expected to be received and disbursed in each of the next twelve months) is used to manage this. It is also usual to negotiate stage payments rather than leaving all payment until the work is completed, and the bank specifies the maximum that can be borrowed on an overdraft, although interest is only payable on the amount actually owed.

  20. How does HR play an important role in the management of a software house?
    Topic: Human Resource Management

    Human resource management is a function in organizations designed to maximize employee performance in service of an employer's strategic objectives, and management of people, staff training and development with a strategic approach suggest that it is particularly appropriate for software work, because in a software house the products are created entirely by people and it is teams, not individuals, who complete projects. HR plays its role by: establishing the numbers of staff the company needs; ensuring maximum utilization of the human resources available; arranging staff training and development (important because IT graduates often lack transferable or 'people-handling' skills); carrying out staff appraisals and career development planning; shifting employee attitudes from mere compliance with rules to commitment and self-motivation; and building comprehensive HRM policies that are responsive to immediate needs and also serve the medium- and long-term corporate requirements. Human resource management is the responsibility of all managers, not only of a personnel department.

  21. What do you know about the unitary perspective?
    Topic: Human Resource Management

    In the unitary perspective, the entire enterprise is regarded as analogous to a team, with one focus of loyalty and one focus of authority: everyone in the organization is seen as working together towards the same shared goals, like the members of a single team. A crucial part of keeping effective workers content under this perspective is a system where they can be promoted without having to become managers. For example, at Microsoft a talented software developer can stay just that, and yet rise to the top tier of elite 'architects'; these architects are not company directors despite their seniority.

  22. Write down any three features of the Health and Safety at Work Act 1974.
    Topic: Human Resource Management

    Any three of the following: (1) The Act applies to premises, i.e. factories, offices etc., and employment is the only necessary criterion for its application. (2) Along with specific requirements, it imposes general (and far-reaching) requirements, instead of relying only on regulations for specific industries and processes, which were rigorous but difficult to keep up to date in the face of rapidly changing technology. (3) It creates comprehensive new duties for manufacturers and suppliers of articles and substances for use at work (the earlier law had no such requirements). (4) Specific regulations are couched in general terms and supplemented by approved codes of practice that are more easily updated.

  23. What are the remedies for breach of copyright?
    Topic: Intellectual Property Rights

    A copyright owner has all the usual civil remedies of search, injunction, damages and an action for an account of profits made in breach of copyright. If it is shown that at the time of the infringement of copyright the defendant did not know and had no reason to believe that copyright subsisted in the work, then the plaintiff is not entitled to damages against the defendant. A copyright owner is also given an important power to enter premises without using force in order to seize infringing copies, or articles specifically designed or adapted for making copies.

  24. Give any four acts of plagiarism.
    Topic: Intellectual Property Rights

    Plagiarism is the practice of taking someone else's work or ideas and passing them off as one's own. Any four of the following are considered plagiarism: (1) Turning in someone else's work as your own. (2) Copying words or ideas from someone else without giving credit. (3) Failing to put a quotation in quotation marks. (4) Giving incorrect information about the source of a quotation. (5) Changing words but copying the sentence structure of a source without giving credit. (6) For a computer program, changing variable names only, or not changing the structure or flow of a program.

  25. How can the different elements of a software product be protected as intellectual property?
    Topic: Intellectual Property Rights

    Intellectual property rights are often the most valuable assets owned, used and developed by a software house. The name under which a product is sold may be registered as a trade mark; the hardware or a process used in its manufacture may be protected by a patent; the look of the product may be registered in the Designs Registry; software can be protected by copyright; and the know-how which goes into the development of the product may be protected as confidential information. Unauthorized use of intellectual property can be stopped by an injunction, and damages may be sought for infringement of these rights.

  26. What conditions must be satisfied before an action for breach of confidence can succeed?
    Topic: Intellectual Property Rights

    Confidential information is information 'which is not public property and public knowledge'. Three conditions must be satisfied before an action for breach of confidence can succeed: (1) The information must be confidential. (2) The information must have been disclosed in circumstances which give rise to an obligation of confidence. (3) There must be an actual or anticipated unauthorized use or disclosure of the information.

  27. What conditions must be satisfied before a patent can be granted? What rights does a patent give?
    Topic: Intellectual Property Rights

    A patent may only be granted if: (1) The invention is new. (2) It involves an inventive step. (3) It is capable of industrial application. (4) The subject matter of the invention does not fall within an excluded class. A patent gives to an inventor a monopoly in an invention: the inventor is given the exclusive right to use or exploit the invention for a defined period. The monopoly granted by patent law is so strong that the owner of a patent may even exclude independent inventors from the market.

  28. What exclusive rights does copyright law give to the owner of copyright?
    Topic: Intellectual Property Rights

    Copyright law gives six exclusive rights to the owner of copyright: (1) To copy the work. (2) To issue copies to the public. (3) To rent or lend the work to the public. (4) To perform, play or show the work in public. (5) To broadcast the work or include it in a cable programme service. (6) To make an adaptation of the work, or to do any of the above with an adaptation. These rights apply equally to published and to unpublished works. Some acts are nevertheless permitted under the 1988 Act, such as fair dealing, making back-up copies of computer programs, de-compilation for the purpose of interoperability and error correction.

  29. What are the four types of contractual arrangement widely used in connection with the provision of software services? Describe each briefly.
    Topic: Computer Contracts

    (1) Fixed price: The supplier agrees to develop the software for a fixed price; software suppliers try to use standard form contracts for this, consisting of a short introductory section, a set of standard terms and conditions, and a set of appendices or annexes. (2) Contract hire: The supplier agrees to provide the services of one or more staff to work for the client, and the staff work under the direction of the client. The supplier's responsibility is limited to providing suitable competent people and replacing them if they become unavailable or are said unsuitable by the client. Payment is on the basis of a fixed rate for each man day worked, and issues such as delay payments and acceptance tests simply do not arise. (3) Time and materials: It is somewhere between a contract hire agreement and a fixed price contract. The supplier agrees to undertake the development of the software in much the same way as in a fixed price contract, but payment is made on the basis of the costs incurred, with labor charged in the same way as for contract hire. The supplier is not committed to completing the work for a fixed price, although a maximum payment may be fixed beyond which the project may be reviewed. (4) Consultancy: Consultants are typically used to assess some aspect of an organization and to make proposals for improvements. The end product of a consultancy project is therefore usually a report or other document. Consultancy projects are usually undertaken for a fixed price, but the form of contract is very much simpler.

  30. Globalization is at its peak, and organizations are hiring freelancers to perform day-to-day tasks on low wages instead of hiring full-time office staff. Do you think the security of the organization is affected by freelancers? Discuss.
    Topic: Computer Contracts

    Yes, the security of the organization is affected. Freelancers usually work away from the organization's premises, on their own systems and networks, and outside its direct supervision, so the risk of unauthorized access to computer systems and of leakage of confidential information and trade secrets increases. They are also less bound by the organization's code of ethics, culture and day-to-day security policies than permanent staff, and their loyalty and commitment to the organization are generally weaker, so information may be handled carelessly or even disclosed deliberately; when the engagement ends, the organization may lose control of copies of its information altogether. The risk can be reduced by signing proper contracts and confidentiality (non-disclosure) agreements with freelancers, giving them access to computing and communication resources only when authorized and only to the information they need, monitoring their access, and withdrawing all access as soon as the work is finished.

  31. Consider an example in which person A got settled well, luckily, right after his graduation. Person B is in the social circle of person A on Facebook. B is jobless and struggling hard for making his bread and butter. A is very frequent in posting his daily enjoyments to gather applause from friends, although A has no intention to hurt anyone's feelings. Comment on whether posting such activities on social media sites can be a source of encouragement or despair for people who do not have the same opportunities, and state what precautions should be taken while posting on social media.
    Topic: Computer Contracts

    Such posting can work in both ways. For some people, a friend's success is a source of encouragement: it motivates them to work harder and shows them what is possible. For others, like B, who are struggling hard without success, repeated displays of enjoyment and luxury can become a source of despair, jealousy and a feeling of failure, even though A has no intention to hurt anyone. A computing professional should therefore be sensitive to the feelings of others and avoid causing harm. Precautions while posting on social media: avoid showing off wealth and enjoyments excessively; keep posts modest and purposeful; respect the privacy of others and never post someone's pictures or information without permission; never share confidential information belonging to an employer or client; think about the possible effect of a post on different readers before posting; and prefer sharing useful, positive and encouraging content.
