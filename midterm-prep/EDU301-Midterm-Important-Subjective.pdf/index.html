  1. What is the application level in the cognitive domain? Explain with examples.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Application means using the knowledge in a new situation. A child has learnt about transparent, translucent and opaque objects: if he tells the definitions exactly as taught, it is the knowledge level; if he tells them in his own words, it is comprehension; but when he is given five things — a cellophane sheet, paper, table, book and wall — and he correctly identifies which are opaque, transparent and translucent, he is at the application level. Similarly, classifying given foods into vegetable, fruit and meat groups, and using the formula Breadth x Length = Area to actually find the area of a geometry box, are application-level tasks. Words often found in application-level questions are: apply, show, demonstrate, choose, illustrate, classify, use, draw a diagram, observe and record.

  2. Define instructional planning and curriculum planning. Differentiate between them.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    In curriculum planning, the needs of society are identified. There are three foundations of curriculum — sociological, philosophical and psychological — and on the basis of societal needs the curriculum is developed. Benchmarks and standards are written in sequence for grade levels, and assessment and resources are decided at this level. In instructional planning, the needs identified are those of the students (pupils). The teacher writes and sequences instructional objectives — which should be derived from the standards and benchmarks of the curriculum — identifies the methods and strategies to be used in class, decides the assessment procedures, and arranges the resources. So curriculum planning works at the level of society and standards, while instructional planning works at the level of the pupil and the lesson, and the two are interlinked.

  3. What is the important thing that we use in Tyler's model and in Bloom's Taxonomy?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The important thing in Tyler's model and Bloom's taxonomy is that you observe the ultimate behaviours of the students. In both, you identify the objectives first, then plan the activities and experiences for attaining them, and eventually assess the students against those objectives. Tyler's rationale asks four questions: (1) What is the purpose of education? (2) What educational experiences should attain the purpose? (3) How can the educational experiences be effectively organized? (4) How can we determine that the purposes are met? Objectives actually provide the criteria to evaluate the lesson.

  4. What is inductive reasoning? Differentiate between the deductive and inductive modes of presentation.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Deductive reasoning goes from general to specific: we start with a general statement and then go to the specific, e.g. 'All humans are mortal; Umar is a human, so Umar is mortal.' Inductive reasoning goes from specific to general: Ahmed is a man and he is mortal, Ali is a man and he is mortal, so we conclude that all men are mortal. In teaching a noun, the deductive mode starts with the definition of a noun, then its characteristics, then examples, then its place in the hierarchy. The inductive mode starts with brainstorming on names — students tell names of birds, animals and places — then the teacher asks for the commonality, and the students themselves reach the definition that a noun is the name of a person, place or thing.

  5. Give an example of the deductive method.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The teacher tells the students that 'water is necessary for plants to grow.' Now the teacher asks them to prove it. He gives them a task: put two plants in the sunlight, give water daily to one of the plants, and observe them after 15 days and tell which plant grows and which one is withered. The students thus prove that plants really need water to grow. Because the lesson started from the general statement (rule) and moved to the specific proof, this is the deductive method.

  6. What is sequencing? What are the effects if the sequence is not followed in a lesson plan?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Sequencing is the art of developing a logical plan for instructional activities; such a plan consists of interlinked steps which are in progression — they start from simple and go to complex. Sequencing helps to make learning more manageable: when you teach a child a complex concept or process, you isolate it into pieces, because after understanding the bits and parts the child can understand the big picture. It also relates information to the bigger picture. If we do not sequence the lesson — for example, if we start instruction with complex things and then move to something simple — the sequencing is not logical, and the student will not be able to understand what he is going to learn and what the terminal objectives are.

  7. Which methods are used for effective teaching regarding sequencing?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The principles of sequencing are: (1) The teacher needs to start the lesson with simple steps, and must come up with at least one example so that the students can understand the concept easily. (2) Use concrete examples — examples which the students can see and can relate to their learning. (3) Add complexity to the lesson: you may start with simple things, but complexity must be added, otherwise it is not a sequenced lesson. (4) Introduce abstractions — things which are not tangible, such as heartbeat and blood pressure — because abstract thinking is very important and children should be able to recognize and relate abstract things.

  8. Explain Tyler's model of curriculum and curriculum alignment.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    There are four steps in Tyler's model of curriculum: (1) What is the purpose of the education? (2) Which educational experiences will attain the purposes? (3) How can these experiences be effectively organized? (4) How can we determine when the purposes are met? Curriculum alignment means that whenever we write performance objectives, they must be aligned with the curriculum — with the curriculum of the year (short-term goals) and with the broader goals, i.e. school goals. For example, if the purpose of education is that students should be empowered citizens, the affective domain will be important in the objectives. The educational experiences must be organized so that they fulfil the objective — e.g. for recording in a lawn, students first observe and then record — and assessment must always be against the performance objectives, in which all three elements must be present.

  9. What is elaboration? Explain its methods and differentiate it from maintenance rehearsal.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Elaboration is a form of rehearsal which means 'adding on': it is a deliberate thought during which we relate the new information with the information already known and understood, so that the knowledge in working memory becomes part of long-term memory. It is different from maintenance rehearsal: when we learn something and do some kind of drill or cramming to maintain it, that is maintenance rehearsal. The methods of elaboration are: (1) Drawings — students read a text and give a pictorial demonstration of it; the adding on must not be confusing. (2) Metaphors and analogies — e.g. after teaching the cell, students compare it with a football game: nucleus is like the referee who controls the game, the cell wall is like the goalkeeper, and mitochondria, which give energy to the cell, are like sports drinks. (3) Summarizing in one's own words. (4) Questioning.

  10. What is reflective practice? Differentiate between reflection-in-action and reflection-on-action.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Reflective practice means answering some key questions to create an image of the classroom teaching and learning. The teacher must notice his classroom environment: how many students are answering the questions asked at the end of the lesson, how many participate in discussion, and how many participate in activities. The answers to these questions tell the teacher what the environment of his class was. Reflective practice is very important because, being a professional, a teacher needs to raise the standard of his profession. Reflection is of two types: reflection-on-action means answering questions about a lesson after it has happened, so that you may know what the environment of your class was; reflection-in-action comes from experience — it is the reflection on every action at the same moment when the action is being taken, while the teacher is in the class.

  11. Write a note on the history of reflective practice.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Reflective practice is a concept used in education studies and pedagogy. It is a continuous process and involves the learner considering critical incidents in his or her life's experiences. Philosophers, theorists, educators and researchers have contributed to the development and growth of reflective practice. John Dewey (Dewey, 1933) is recognized as the eminent 20th-century influence on reflection in education.

  12. How can a teacher develop social capital in students?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    It is the responsibility of a teacher to increase the social capital of every student. Most of the time, teachers come to class with a lesson plan, teach the lesson, give written work and leave; there is no time for students to communicate with each other except in break or after school. The teacher should increase the social capital of students by organizing group activities. Instead of doing things alone, students communicate with each other, and in this way their thinking skills also increase.

  13. What is an educational goal?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A goal is a statement of intent stated in broad terms. For example, the goal of a teacher is: 'A teacher is responsible to produce good citizens.' Can we produce good citizens in a 40- or 45-minute lecture? Definitely not; but daily class lectures of 45 minutes can, over a long time, achieve the goal. Goals are therefore long-term and broad, unlike objectives, which are specific and short-term.

  14. What aspects make a teacher a professional?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A teaching professional must possess the relevant knowledge base. Students enrolled in the 4-year B.Ed programme study a curriculum based on all the concepts and principles to be learnt — how students learn, philosophy of education, methods of teaching, educational research, assessment and evaluation, etc. All these subjects provide the knowledge which is a core requirement to be a teaching professional. A profession also requires extensive training and specialized knowledge, allows autonomy, and requires the teacher to raise professional standards, e.g. through reflective practice.

  15. What are performance objectives? Write their three characteristics.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Performance objectives are precise statements of what you expect students to do; they communicate the exact intent of the lesson. Three characteristics (elements) of performance objectives are: (1) Performance — what the learner will do. (2) Condition — a description of the conditions under which the learner's performance is to occur; conditions determine how, when, where and with what the performance will occur. (3) Criterion measure — the standard against which the performance is judged. All three elements must be present in a performance objective, and students should be assessed according to those three elements.

  16. What are the components of an advance graphic organizer?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    An advance graphic organizer has three major components or attributes: (1) It is a statement of those elements that the learner will be required to master in a lesson — it may be a statement or a word, e.g. 'food' or 'noun'. (2) It presents the 'big picture' to establish relationships between different concepts. (3) The advance organizer model is based upon deductive learning.

  17. What is the horizontal dimension of planning?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    In the horizontal dimension (level) of planning, planning includes the selection of activities, resources, assessment and homework. It deals with how the components of a lesson or unit at the same level are arranged and coordinated with each other.

  18. Define lesson planning. What are the requirements of a lesson plan?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A lesson is a piece of a unit, NOT a block of time: a whole unit (e.g. the unit of light) cannot be taught in one lesson, so it is divided into pieces, and the plan for each piece is called a lesson plan. A lesson plan defines objectives; materials, resources and activities are mobilized for the efficient attainment of the selected objectives; and finally, evaluation criteria are also identified. It is a systematic design for the development, implementation and evaluation of instruction — basically 'a rehearsal for delivering actual instruction.' The requirements (components) of a lesson plan are: Subject, Instructional objectives, Rationale, Content and process, and Instructional procedures.

  19. Differentiate between a unit plan and a lesson plan.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A unit plan and a lesson plan have many similarities because the lesson plan is a part of the unit plan. Both mention the subject, rationale, instructional objectives, content and processes, and resources. The unit plan contains the unit topic, learning activities and evaluation for the whole unit, which consists of a series (hierarchy) of lesson plans. The lesson plan contains the lesson topic and detailed instructional procedures: (1) focusing event, (2) teaching procedures, (3) student activities and participation, (4) formative check, (5) closure, along with assessment and notes. Overall, a lesson plan is actually the detailed form of a unit plan: it originates from a unit but is much more detailed.

  20. What are the benefits of unit planning? Why is unit planning important?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Unit planning is important because we need to know the reasons for dividing the whole thing into components. Most of the time teachers complain about shortage of time; this problem can be solved by making detailed unit plans. In a unit plan you can observe all the topics given in the yearly plan, identify the most important topics and the overlapping topics, and omit the overlapping topics whose level of difficulty is the same as taught previously. In this way, unit planning helps you to go through the whole yearly plan systematically, and it is the guarantee of effective instruction and effective learning.

  21. Write the components of a unit plan.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    There are 8 components of a unit plan: (i) Subject/Topic, (ii) Rationale, (iii) Instructional objectives, (iv) Content, (v) Processes, (vi) Resources, (vii) Learning activities, (viii) Evaluation.

  22. What is the Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD)?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The gap between the actual level and the potential level of a child is called the Zone of Proximal Development. Vygotsky is very famous for this concept of his theory. At the second stage of ZPD, a child gets assistance from a more knowledgeable other, which helps the child reach the potential level.

  23. What is Bloom's taxonomy? Name its six levels and explain the lower and higher levels of learning.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Bloom's taxonomy is a taxonomy of the cognitive domain with 6 levels: Knowledge, Comprehension, Application, Analysis, Synthesis and Evaluation. Knowledge is the first level, then comprehension, and then application; these three levels are categorized as the lower level of learning. The next three levels — analysis, synthesis and evaluation — are categorized as the higher level of learning.

  24. What is CAP? Explain the three domains of learning.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    CAP stands for the three domains of learning: Cognitive, Affective and Psychomotor. The cognitive domain (Knowledge) encompasses objectives that deal with recall or recognition of knowledge. The affective domain (Attitude) deals with feelings, values and appreciation. The psychomotor domain (Skills) is skill-oriented and engages our body movements and motor muscles.

  25. How many levels are there in Simpson's psychomotor domain? Name them.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    There are seven levels in Simpson's psychomotor domain: (i) Perception, (ii) Set, (iii) Guided response, (iv) Mechanism, (v) Complex overt response, (vi) Adaptation, (vii) Origination. Perception, the first level, means attending to a stimulus.

  26. Name the levels of Bloom and Krathwohl's taxonomy of the affective domain.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    There are five levels of the affective domain: (i) Receiving, (ii) Responding, (iii) Valuing, (iv) Organization, (v) Characterization. Characterization is the highest level of the affective domain. The taxonomy of the affective domain was presented by Bloom and Krathwohl in 1964.

  27. What is kinesthetic learning?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Kinesthetic learning is the learning in which the student carries out a physical activity, rather than listening to a lecture or watching a demonstration. It is also known as tactile learning.

  28. What are the qualities of a good teacher?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A good teacher is one who plans a lesson, implements it and evaluates it after implementation. A good teacher engages the students in active learning. A good teacher must know assessment and evaluation, teaching techniques and skills, and pedagogical content knowledge. Content knowledge is the competency important for a teacher in learning, and good teachers focus most on teaching strategy.

  29. Why are activities important in a lesson?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Activities are important because we do not teach processes to our students unless we include learning activities in our lessons. Learning takes place through activities: students participate, communicate with each other and develop their thinking skills, instead of only listening to the teacher.

  30. Why is school important for the social development of a child?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    In social development, very important things are sharing (like making friends), observing rules and arrangements, and physical development. For all of these, school plays an important role: the child learns to live and work with others in a group, which is why schooling is a group orientation and learning is social by nature.

  31. How are growth and self-respect factors of motivation for teachers?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Motivation among teachers is promoted by factors such as recognition, growth and self-respect. A teacher who sees opportunities for professional growth — learning new methods, improving skills and developing as a professional — remains committed and motivated. Similarly, self-respect matters: the warmth, respect and appreciation in the eyes of the students for the teacher are the actual recognition, and this recognition energizes the teacher. Research shows that the best recognition a teacher gets is from the students.

  32. How is the apprenticeship model effective for the teacher?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The apprenticeship model is based on Vygotsky's work. It involves peers working closely together with a teacher in joint problem solving, e.g. in workshops and skill-learning centres. The learner works with a more knowledgeable other, observes and practises the skill under guidance, and gradually becomes independent, so it is an effective way of learning teaching skills.

  33. Do learners learn best alone?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    No. Learning is social by nature and cannot be separated from the social context in which it occurs. Instead of doing things alone, when learners communicate with each other — in group activities and peer learning — their thinking skills increase. Children actually learn a great deal from their peers: peer learning is learning in which students interact with other students to attain educational goals.

  34. What is an evaluation instrument? Why are taxonomies important for evaluation?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    We need evaluation instruments to assess students and teaching. Evaluation instruments are always developed when we have knowledge of the taxonomies. For example, if I want to evaluate students in sketching, I must know the taxonomies: if I have decided to assess the students at the mechanism level, I will have a different checklist; but if I want to assess them at the origination level, my checklist will be totally different. So if we do not know the taxonomies, we will only assess whether the students have sketched something or not. Taxonomies are important to learn because through them we know the level of students' learning.

  35. How does a teacher serve a social function? Explain teaching perceptions as culturally and socially embedded.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Teaching has a social function: perceptions about teaching are culturally and socially embedded. In a survey, teachers from rural and urban areas, and from private and government schools, were selected, and the ideas of teachers and parents were analysed to see whether they differ or not. The teachers from urban schools said that it is their responsibility to teach discipline to the students; the parents had the same perception — they said teachers are very respectable, must teach students discipline and behaviour, and students must obey them. Teaching is essentially a social activity, and the teacher also works for change in society by sharing ideas with the students.

  36. What is meant by multi-methodology?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Different types of mental functions occur in the left and right hemispheres of the brain; our brain is composed of a right and a left hemisphere. Because learners process information through both hemispheres, teaching should not rely on a single method: multi-methodology means using multiple methods of teaching so that both sides of the brain are engaged and learning becomes effective.

  37. Name the processes of modelling. Explain the production process.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The modelling processes (Bandura) are: Attention, Retention, Production and Motivation. Production: once you have paid attention to the model and retained the information, it is time to actually perform the behaviour you observed. Further practice of the learned behaviour leads to improvement and skill advancement.

  38. Teaching is a process, not an object. Comment.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Teaching is the process to use different objects and ideas. It is not a thing or object that can be delivered once; it is a continuous process of planning, implementing and evaluating through which desirable changes are brought in the behaviour of students. That is why a good teacher plans a lesson, implements it and evaluates it after implementation.

  39. What is peer learning?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Peer learning means that children learn from their peers. They actually learn more from their peers (other students) than they learn in school from their teachers. It is a type of learning in which students interact with other students to attain educational goals.

  40. What is observational learning? What is the inhibitory effect?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Observational learning is something that we learn by observing others; there are a number of people who learn things by just observing other people. Bandura believed that much human behaviour is learnt by observing the behaviour of others. The inhibitory effect occurs when a learner observes a model being punished or discouraged for a behaviour; the learner then thinks that the behaviour is not good to perform and is inhibited from doing it. Anything that detracts attention has a negative effect on observational learning, while retention is important because it helps in storing information for later use and acting upon it.

  41. Teaching is a social, political and ethical activity. Discuss.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    As a social activity, teaching needs people with whom teaching can be done; it is essentially a social activity. As a political activity, the teacher needs to have some perceptions and must discuss those ideas with students; both should work together for a social change, because politics means a set of ideas, and the teacher must share ideas with the students so that they can work for a change in society. As an ethical activity, it is the duty of a teacher to fulfil his responsibilities ethically: teachers need to be committed to their profession, honest and dedicated. If teachers do not exhibit the above, they are not taking teaching as an ethical activity.

  42. What is declarative knowledge? Write its two parts.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Declarative knowledge is the knowledge that is declared in books, research papers and newspapers, or that you acquired from your elders/teachers. There are two parts in this knowledge: facts and concepts. Facts are something that you memorize. Concepts are, for example, Newton's laws, rules of grammar and writing patterns — these are also part of our declarative knowledge. Verbal or factual information are the other names for declarative knowledge.

  43. What is IPT (Information Processing Theory)? Discuss the role of working memory in IPT.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Nothing is learnt unless it is in long-term memory. In information processing theory, we have short-term and long-term memory, and between both of them there is working memory. Working memory is our conscious memory; we do not know much about working memory and how it operates. This theory says that every learning should become part of long-term memory, and automation is the ultimate learning. The short-term store is named as the sensory register in information processing theory. Humans can keep only 5-9 unrelated pieces (chunks) of information in their working memory at one time.

  44. What is short-term planning? What are the instructional aims for lesson planning?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Lesson planning is short-term planning. The instructional aims for lesson planning are student-oriented: instructional aims are broad statements of what the teaching is intended to achieve for the students, from which the specific instructional objectives of a lesson are derived.

  45. What is the scientific method?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The scientific method is a tool that helps scientists investigate phenomena systematically: they observe a problem, formulate a hypothesis, conduct experiments to test it, analyse the results and draw conclusions. In teaching, it develops inquiry among students, and an educator must work to stimulate the spirit of inquiry among the students.
