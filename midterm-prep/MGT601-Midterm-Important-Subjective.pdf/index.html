  1. What does the technical feasibility of a project cover? (Marks: 3)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Technical feasibility covers identification of the critical technical specifications of a product: its functional design (meeting the customer's functional requirements in the best possible way), adaptability to customer demand, durability, reliability of performance, safety, reasonable utility (an acceptable level of obsolescence) and standardization. It also covers the optimum utilization of all kinds of resources - men, materials, power and finance - the quality of the product, and the cost of production.

  2. You are a manager of a fan manufacturing company and your sales volume is low. How will you improve your sales volume? (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    To improve the sales volume, I will take the following steps: (1) check the quality of the product and improve it, as product quality is the main thing in a fan; (2) reduce the cost of the product; (3) make the price of the product attractive for the customers; (4) motivate the sales team by giving bonuses and incentives; (5) do proper marketing of the product; (6) appoint good distributors in every area; (7) maintain a good stock level; and (8) make the product easily available in the market.

  3. What is market myopia? (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Market myopia is a shortsighted approach of concentrating on production rather than on the marketability of the product. A myopic management team focuses only on the product and not on the customers. When identifying market opportunities, an entrepreneur must fully understand the requirements of the market and the consumers; without a proper understanding of market conditions, failure becomes certain. Example: the railroads in the USA saw themselves in the railroad business rather than in the transportation business, so when the automobile age came, the railroad companies lost their major share of passenger traffic.

  4. What are the environmental causes among the post-operative problems faced by a new enterprise? (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The environmental causes of post-operative problems are: (1) changes in government policy with regard to fiscal measures, import-export, industries, agriculture, etc.; (2) changes in the social and political conditions of the country, particularly where the change adversely affects the growth and expansion of the unit; and (3) inflationary pressure, which increases the cost of production of the unit.

  5. What benefits do SMEs enjoy in environmental issues, and what benefits do they not enjoy? (Marks: 5 + 2)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Benefits enjoyed: SMEs can make economic gains from environmentally friendly production - for example, reducing material waste lowers the cost of production, and saving water and energy increases the competitiveness of the firm. There is also a direct link between the effectiveness of technology transfer and the stabilization of global climate change and natural resource depletion, and special credit lines and training may be provided to SMEs for this purpose. Benefits not enjoyed: SMEs in developing countries often ineffectively exploit the available technology options and inefficiently utilize transferred technologies, because of the high transaction costs of developing the capacities and capabilities to manage and generate technological change.

  6. A drain having polluted water from one factory may be used by some other industry, which suffers the bad effects of pollution. This is an example of 'externality'. What do you understand by the term externality? Are externalities positive or negative or both? Explain with examples. (Marks: 3)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    An externality is a cost or benefit of an economic activity that is experienced by an unrelated third party. Externalities can be negative or positive, or both. Negative externality: a drain carrying polluted water from one factory may be used by some other industry, which suffers the bad effects of the pollution; air pollution and noise pollution are also negative externalities. Positive externality: an individual who maintains an attractive garden may confer benefits on neighbours - the benefit enjoyed by the neighbours, for which they did not pay, is a positive externality.

  7. When you plan a new business, you create a contingency fund to guard against unforeseen natural calamities. Write different natural calamities that are a constant threat for your business. (Marks: 3)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The natural calamities that are a constant threat to a business include floods, earthquakes, fire, lightning, storms, hurricanes and tsunamis. A contingency fund is created while planning a new business to guard it against such unforeseen natural calamities.

  8. How will you overcome your lack of experience to avoid the failure of your SME venture? (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The key ingredient for starting a business successfully is knowing the business. A thorough knowledge of the business one intends to pursue, coupled with a thorough study of the market, is a powerful factor in avoiding failure; most business owners attribute their success to their prior work experience in the same field. A new entrepreneur can overcome lack of experience by spending considerable time learning about the products, the competition and the customers, by evaluating the marketability of the products, and by hiring professional consultants to guide the venture in the right direction. Knowledgeable management is essential to the success of a business.

  9. Due to inadequate study of the SME sector, entrepreneurs fail to sustain their new business. Give different reasons which may cause the failure of a new business. (Marks: 5)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The reasons which may cause the failure of a new business include: (1) lack of management experience or poor management - the managers of a failed business usually lack the experience, knowledge or vision essential to operate a successful firm; (2) undercapitalization - not having enough money to operate the business normally; (3) lack of experience - not knowing the business or the industry well; (4) poor location; (5) poor inventory management - carrying too much inventory, or inventory that becomes outdated and cannot be sold; (6) over-investment in fixed assets - excessive amounts of cash tied up in land, buildings, plant and equipment; (7) poor credit-granting practices; (8) taking money out of the business for personal use; (9) unexpected growth - growing too rapidly without having planned for it; and (10) wrong competitive strategy - wrong location, target market, product mix or pricing.

  10. Who is an entrepreneur? Discuss.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    An entrepreneur is a person who creates something new, undertakes risk, handles uncertainty and is always looking for change. He responds to change as an opportunity and leads the organization in such a way that it excels. As Michael E. Gerber describes, the entrepreneur is the personality who turns a trivial condition into an exceptional opportunity: the visionary, the dreamer, the energy behind every human activity, the imagination that sparks the fire of the future, and the catalyst for change. The entrepreneur lives in the future, is never satisfied with the past, and is happy when controlling the flow of changes in the present - and when the market has a need for change, he always meets the need.

  11. Describe the problems of small-scale industries. (Marks: 4)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The problems of small-scale industries are of two types. Internal problems: problems in planning and implementation (improper technical feasibility and economic viability studies); production problems (non-availability of raw materials, under-utilization of capacity, lack of trained labour, poor quality and high cost); labour problems; marketing problems; financial problems; and administrative problems. External problems: infrastructural problems (non-availability of land, power, water and transport); financial problems (inadequate and untimely finance, high cost of credit, inadequate working capital, low return, recovery and sickness); government controls and policies; market constraints (competition from large and medium industries, changing technology, recession and global competition); and extraneous problems (strikes, lockouts, power breakdowns and government restrictions).

  12. How would you differentiate cottage industries from small industries?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Cottage industries are mainly rural in character, whereas small industries are generally set up in urban and semi-urban areas. Cottage industries are owned and run by the members of a family and are traditional in nature, while small industries may be run by individuals or on a partnership basis. Cottage industries generally use traditional techniques and do not use power, whereas small industries use modern techniques and power. Cottage industries mainly produce goods for local markets, while small industries produce goods for wider markets.

  13. Differentiate between small and medium enterprises. Also explain the role of SMEs in a developing economy like Pakistan. (Marks: 10)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    An SME is a business entity which does not employ more than 99 persons, with productive assets (excluding land and building) of Rs 2 to 40 million. A small enterprise employs 10 to 35 persons with productive assets of Rs 2 to 20 million, while a medium enterprise employs 36 to 99 persons with productive assets of Rs 20 to 40 million. Role of SMEs in a developing economy like Pakistan: SMEs are engines of economic growth; they provide low-cost employment, as the unit cost of persons employed is lower than in large units; they promote regional and local development; they help in the equitable distribution of wealth; they earn valuable foreign exchange through exports; they improve the trade balance by generally using indigenous raw materials; they promote self-help and an entrepreneurial culture; and they are resilient in recessions because they are less affected by economic disruptions and are more or less self-adjusting.

  14. What are the long-term issues regarding SMEs? Why are they considered important, and how can we perform better in the presence of these issues? (Marks: 10)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The long-term issues regarding SMEs are literacy, law and order, intellectual property rights (IPR) and infrastructure. Literacy: low literacy and poor quality of education mean that the workforce is ill-trained, which affects the productivity and growth of SMEs. Law and order: one in five respondents reported that their business was the target of at least one crime during 2002, and SMEs spend a significant share of their revenue on security, which raises the cost of doing business. Intellectual property rights: strong IPR protection can protect indigenous products such as rice, kinno, traditional knowledge and pottery, and can attract foreign direct investment in research and development intensive sectors. Infrastructure: energy and transport inefficiencies impose heavy costs - a typical business loses about 5.6 percent of its annual sales revenue due to power outages alone. These issues are important because they determine the long-run competitiveness of the sector; performance can be improved through better education and training, improved security, effective IPR enforcement and investment in power and transport infrastructure.

  15. What are the pre-operative, mid-operative (construction phase) and post-operative problems that an entrepreneur has to face while starting a new enterprise? Explain. (Marks: 15)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Pre-operative problems: selecting an appropriate form of business organization; acquisition of basic facilities such as raw materials, power and transport; and other related problems. Construction-phase (mid-operative) problems: acquisition of land; building construction and other civil works; acquisition and installation of machinery and equipment; identification of sources of supply of inputs; market prospecting; sources of working capital; and coordination problems. Post-operative problems: lack of profits; lack of experience; lack of sales; expense problems; neglect; lack of capital; customer problems; personnel problems; natural calamities; government regulations; unmindful expansion; environmental problems; and production problems.

  16. (a) Define training and development. (b) State the objectives of training. (c) Explain the methods of training. (Marks: 15)
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    (a) Training seeks to upgrade an employee's knowledge so as to keep abreast of changes in the competitive business environment and to prepare the employee for advancement; development builds the overall competence of employees for future responsibilities. (b) Objectives of training: increasing employees' job knowledge; improving employee productivity; improving the quality of work; improving the work climate and organizational health; improving health and safety; preventing obsolescence; and achieving the personal growth of employees. (c) Methods of training - on-the-job training includes demonstration, performance and inspection (its three phases), apprenticeship, job rotation, the conference method and the heir apparent (understudy) method; off-premises training programs are also used to train employees away from the workplace.

  17. Describe the role of the World Trade Organization (WTO) in Pakistan. What are the benefits of the WTO for the trade of Pakistan?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The World Trade Organization (WTO) is an international organization established to supervise and liberalize world trade; it is the successor of the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT) created in 1947. It deals with the global rules of trade between nations, and its main function is to ensure that trade flows as smoothly, predictably and freely as possible. Pakistan, as a founding member of GATT, has been working closely with the WTO; the WTO Secretariat provides technical assistance to Pakistan in the form of training, workshops and seminars for officials of the Ministry of Commerce, and helps in trade research and in drafting trade-related legislation. Benefits of the WTO for the trade of Pakistan: (1) reduction in the cost of living; (2) a wider choice of goods and services; (3) increase in incomes; (4) stimulation of economic growth and employment; (5) making life more efficient; (6) encouragement of good government; (7) protection and lobbying for weaker countries; (8) contribution to peace and stability; (9) constructive settlement of disputes; and (10) help for governments to take a balanced view of trade policy.

  18. Discuss SMEs and the making of import-export decisions.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    In today's global economy, internationalization can make a small company more competitive and can expand its market potential. Exporting is the most common entry mode for SMEs, though it is risky and requires commitment, market knowledge, a realistic budget, time and patience. The strongest indicator of export potential is success in the domestic market: if a company can compete at home, it has a good chance of competing internationally. Export readiness also requires a mature product and demand for it in the target market. Regional and bilateral trade agreements (such as the EU, NAFTA, ASEAN, MERCOSUR and ECOWAS) help SMEs by reducing trade barriers, but the rules of origin and standards set by such agreements can also affect an SME's decision to enter a foreign market.

  19. What topics are addressed through marketing research studies? Describe the important marketing research questions for a small business.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Marketing research studies address the areas of product, market, sales, promotion, competition, and basic economic and industry trends. The crucial marketing research questions for a small business are: (1) Who is the customer? (2) Where is the customer? (3) What does the customer buy? (4) Who does the customer buy from? (5) Why does the customer buy? (6) How does the customer buy? (7) How much will the customer buy? (8) Who is the competition? (9) What prices are competitors charging? (10) What are the substitutes for my product or service?

  20. Discuss the role of micro-financing in boosting SMEs.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Microfinance is the provision of very small loans (microcredit) to impoverished borrowers who typically lack collateral, steady employment and a verifiable credit history. It is designed not only to support entrepreneurship and alleviate poverty, but also to empower the poor and uplift entire communities. Access to even small amounts of credit at reasonable interest rates, instead of the high rates charged by moneylenders, improves the condition of the poor, enables them to start or expand micro-enterprises, creates employment, and thereby boosts the SME sector.

  21. What are the various factors that hamper the way of SMEs to enter the export market?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The factors that hamper SMEs from entering the export market include: lack of finance; non-tariff barriers such as stringent product standards; lack of awareness of international opportunities and markets; lack of knowledge of international marketing; lack of export knowledge and skills; shortage of personnel trained in export matters; lack of access to imported raw materials, inputs and machinery; and government regulations.

  22. Are the current economic conditions of Pakistan conducive for small and medium enterprises? Give at least five arguments in support of your answer.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    No, the current economic conditions of Pakistan are not favourable for small and medium enterprises. The government is not playing an effective role in promoting the sector, and load shedding is a major cause of declining production. Inflation has made it difficult for people to start new businesses, and the level of education and literacy is not encouraging. Tax rates are high, which discourages new business start-ups, and strikes and law-and-order problems hamper the economy and discourage foreign investors from investing in Pakistan.

  23. Being the owner of a small firm, how can Mr. Malik Amjad face the challenge of the rising cost of doing business?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Mr. Malik Amjad should adopt cost-cutting techniques in all operations of his business - for example, avoiding unnecessary expenses and cutting extra expenditures on labour and transport - while ensuring that cost reduction does not lower the quality of the product, because quality is the most important factor for retaining customers and increasing the market share of the firm.

  24. Which institutions are supporting the development of SMEs in Pakistan? Describe their functions.
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    SMEDA is the premier institution for SME development, providing help desks, business development services, training and online facilitation. The Business Support Fund (BSF), established in 2005 by the Ministry of Finance, supports SMEs in acquiring business development services such as ERP systems, ISO certification and intellectual property registration. The SME Bank focuses on providing financial assistance to small and medium enterprises. The Punjab Small Industries Corporation (PSIC) provides small industrial estates and loans of up to Rs 7.5 lac at a debt-equity ratio of 70:30, while the Sindh Small Industries Corporation (SSIC) provides similar facilities in Sindh.

  25. What is a Quality Management System (QMS)? How does a company become registered under ISO 9001?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    A Quality Management System (QMS) is a set of policies, processes and procedures required for planning and execution (production, development and service) in the core business area of an organization. ISO 9000 is a series of standards that defines, establishes and maintains a quality assurance system for manufacturing and service industries. To become registered under ISO 9001, a company designs and documents its quality management system, has it assessed by an accredited registration body, and - once it passes the assessment - receives a certificate of registration; periodic surveillance audits then confirm continued compliance.

  26. What is the expanded form of LDC?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    LDC stands for Least Developed Country.

  27. What is meant by antidumping?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    When a manufacturer in one country exports a product to another country at a price which is either below the price it charges in its home market or below its cost of production, it is called dumping. To protect the domestic industry, the importing country imposes additional duties (antidumping duties) to bring the price to a fair level.

  28. Who are drone entrepreneurs?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Drone entrepreneurs are inert and traditional entrepreneurs who are hurdles in economic development. They struggle to exist, not to grow.

  29. What are imitating entrepreneurs?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Imitating entrepreneurs copy the innovations originated by innovative entrepreneurs. They are suited to developing countries, which are not able to take up expensive research, as they bring the technology and innovations of developed countries into their own country.

  30. What are countervailing duties?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Countervailing duties are imposed by a country on imported products which have been subsidized by the governments of the exporting countries, in order to offset the subsidy and protect the domestic industry.

  31. What is the default rate?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    The default rate is the percentage of loans that are not repaid by the borrowers. In Pakistan, the default rate of SMEs is about 15 percent, as against 65 percent for large-scale enterprises.

  32. What is meant by value added?
    Topic: Core Subjective Topics

    Value added is the difference between the value of the output produced and the value of the inputs (raw materials and intermediate goods) used in producing it.
