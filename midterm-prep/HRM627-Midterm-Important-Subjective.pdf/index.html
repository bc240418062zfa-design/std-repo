  1. Define human resource development (HRD).
    Topic: Human Resource Development: Concepts

    HRD is the process of enabling people to make things happen. It deals both with the process of competency development in people and with the creation of conditions - through public policy, programs and other interventions - that help people apply these competencies for their own benefit and for the benefit of others.

  2. What competencies are developed through HRD?
    Topic: Human Resource Development: Concepts

    Competencies may include knowledge, skills, attitudes and values. They may deal with any field - agriculture, industry, science, technology, management, various professions (like medicine, law, engineering and teaching), politics, public administration, home science, labour, telecommunications, research and tourism - and they may be developed in individuals, groups, communities or collectives.

  3. Differentiate between human development and human resource development.
    Topic: Human Resource Development: Concepts

    Some writers treat human development as a much larger and all-encompassing concept, while HRD is viewed as limited to skill development and knowledge acquisition demanded by organizations for employment purposes. Another objection to the term HRD is that the word "resource" seems to imply that human beings are treated like material resources and as instruments of development rather than its beneficiaries. However, the differences are more linguistic than conceptual: UNDP prefers the term "human development", while agencies such as UNESCAP, ILO, CIDA and the Commonwealth Secretariat prefer "human resource development", and the distinction is slowly disappearing.

  4. What are the dimensions of development implied in the concept of HRD?
    Topic: Human Resource Development: Concepts

    Implied in the concept of HRD are all facets of the development of people: physical, intellectual, emotional, social, moral, political and spiritual development. People cannot function unless they are physically well developed, healthy and free from disease; intellectual development comes through education and socialization; social development is the ability to live as a member of society and contribute to it; political development ensures human dignity through freedom of expression and democratic participation; and moral and spiritual development brings order, discipline and peace into life.

  5. Why are women and girls considered a strategically important target group for HRD?
    Topic: Human Resource Development: Concepts

    Women and girls form a group of strategic significance because of the multiplier effect their development has on others: they influence the education and the well-being of the entire family. Developing women and girls therefore accelerates the development of the whole household and, through it, of society.

  6. What is governance? List the major characteristics of good governance.
    Topic: Human Resource Development: Concepts

    Governance means the process of decision-making and the process by which decisions are implemented. Good governance has eight major characteristics: (1) Participation, (2) Rule of Law, (3) Transparency, (4) Responsiveness, (5) Consensus Orientation, (6) Equity, (7) Effectiveness and Efficiency, and (8) Accountability.

  7. What are attitudes? Describe the three components of an attitude.
    Topic: Human Behavior, Attitudes and Personality

    Attitudes are evaluative statements - either favorable or unfavorable - concerning objects, people or events; they reflect how an individual feels about something. An attitude has three components: the cognitive component, made up of the beliefs, opinions, knowledge or information held by a person; the affective component, the emotional or feeling part of an attitude; and the behavioral component, an intention to behave in a certain way toward someone or something.

  8. What is the Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI)?
    Topic: Human Behavior, Attitudes and Personality

    The Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI) is one of the most popular personality tests. It consists of more than a hundred questions that ask people how they usually act or feel in different situations, and it places a person at one end or another of four dimensions: social interaction (Extrovert or Introvert), preference for gathering data (Sensing or Intuitive), preference for decision-making (Feeling or Thinking), and style of making decisions (Perceptive or Judgmental). Combining these preferences provides descriptions of 16 personality types.

  9. List and briefly describe the Big Five personality traits.
    Topic: Human Behavior, Attitudes and Personality

    (1) Extraversion: the degree to which one is sociable, talkative and assertive. (2) Agreeableness: the degree to which someone is good-natured, cooperative and trusting. (3) Conscientiousness: the degree to which someone is responsible, dependable, persistent and achievement-oriented. (4) Emotional stability: the degree to which someone is calm, enthusiastic and secure (positive) or tense, nervous, depressed and insecure (negative). (5) Openness to experience: the degree to which someone is imaginative, artistically sensitive and intellectual.

  10. What is emotional intelligence? Name its five dimensions.
    Topic: Human Behavior, Attitudes and Personality

    Emotional intelligence (EI) is an assortment of non-cognitive skills, capabilities and competencies that influence a person's ability to succeed in coping with environmental demands and pressures. It is composed of five dimensions: self-awareness (being aware of what you are feeling), self-management (managing one's own emotions and impulses), self-motivation (persisting in the face of setbacks and failures), empathy (sensing how others are feeling) and social skills (handling the emotions of others). EI has been shown to be positively related to job performance at all levels.

  11. Explain locus of control and its two types.
    Topic: Human Behavior, Attitudes and Personality

    Some people believe that they control their own fate, while others see themselves as pawns, believing that what happens to them is due to luck or chance. In the first case the locus of control is internal - these people believe they control their own destiny. In the second case it is external - these people believe their lives are controlled by outside forces. Research shows that employees high on externality are less satisfied with their jobs, more alienated from the work setting and less involved in their jobs than those high on internality.

  12. What is Machiavellianism?
    Topic: Human Behavior, Attitudes and Personality

    Machiavellianism (Mach) is named after Niccolo Machiavelli, who wrote in the 16th century on how to gain and manipulate power. An individual who is high in Machiavellianism is pragmatic, maintains emotional distance, and believes that ends can justify means; "if it works, use it" is consistent with a high-Mach perspective. High Machs are productive in jobs requiring bargaining skills or offering substantial rewards for winning, such as purchasing and commission-based sales.

  13. Define perception and explain the factors that influence it.
    Topic: Perception and Attribution

    Perception is a process by which individuals give meaning to their environment by organizing and interpreting their sensory impressions. The factors reside in: (1) the perceiver - personal characteristics such as attitudes, personality, motives, interests, experiences and expectations; (2) the target - the characteristics of the object being observed and its relationship to its background; and (3) the situation - the context, including time, location, light, heat, color and other situational factors.

  14. What is attribution theory? On which three factors does the determination of internal or external causation depend?
    Topic: Perception and Attribution

    Attribution theory was developed to explain how we judge people differently depending on the meaning we attribute to a given behavior. When we observe an individual's behavior, we attempt to determine whether it was internally caused (under the personal control of the individual) or externally caused (resulting from outside factors). The determination depends on three factors: distinctiveness (whether the behavior is unusual for that situation), consensus (whether everyone in a similar situation responds the same way) and consistency (whether the person responds the same way over time).

  15. Differentiate between fundamental attribution error and self-serving bias.
    Topic: Perception and Attribution

    The fundamental attribution error is the tendency, when we make judgments about the behavior of other people, to underestimate the influence of external factors and to overestimate the influence of internal or personal factors. The self-serving bias is the tendency for individuals to attribute their own success to internal factors such as ability or effort, while putting the blame for failure on external factors such as luck.

  16. Explain the shortcuts frequently used in judging others.
    Topic: Perception and Attribution

    (1) Selective perception: individuals cannot assimilate all they observe, so they selectively take in bits and pieces depending on their interests, background, experience and attitudes. (2) Assumed similarity (the "like me" effect): the observer's perception of others is influenced more by the observer's own characteristics than by those of the person observed. (3) Stereotyping: judging someone on the basis of our perception of a group he or she is part of. (4) Halo effect: forming a general impression about a person on the basis of a single characteristic, such as intelligence, sociability or appearance.

  17. Define intrinsic and extrinsic motivation.
    Topic: Motivation and Intrinsic Motivation

    Intrinsic motivation refers to doing an activity or behavior voluntarily for its own sake, and the inherent pleasure and satisfaction derived from participation; it is engaging in a task for its engagement value. Extrinsic motivation refers to activities engaged in as a means to an end, such as to gain reward or avoid criticism, rather than for the satisfaction of the activity itself. In intrinsic motivation the motivational force is provided by the self, whereas in extrinsic motivation it is controlled through rewards or feedback.

  18. How do rewards affect intrinsic motivation according to Cognitive Evaluation Theory?
    Topic: Motivation and Intrinsic Motivation

    Cognitive Evaluation Theory identifies two needs - autonomy and competence - and two types of rewards: controlling and informational. Controlling (task-contingent) rewards decrease intrinsic motivation, while informational feedback that affirms competence increases it. In their 1999 meta-analysis of 128 controlled experimental studies, Deci, Koestner and Ryan concluded that tangible rewards had a significant negative effect on intrinsic motivation for interesting tasks, while verbal rewards (positive feedback) had a significant positive effect. This confirms the overjustification theory: providing external rewards for an activity decreases intrinsic motivation.

  19. Define motivation and explain its three key elements.
    Topic: Motivation and Intrinsic Motivation

    Motivation is the willingness to exert high levels of effort to reach organizational goals, conditioned by the effort's ability to satisfy some individual need. The three key elements are: effort - a measure of intensity or drive (the quality of effort is as important as its intensity); goals - the organizational goals toward which effort is directed; and needs - an internal state that makes certain outcomes appear attractive. An unsatisfied need creates tension that stimulates drives within the individual, leading to search behavior to find goals that will satisfy the need and reduce the tension.

  20. Describe Maslow's hierarchy of needs theory.
    Topic: Motivation and Intrinsic Motivation

    Maslow proposed a hierarchy of five human needs. (1) Physiological needs: food, drink, shelter, sexual satisfaction and other physical needs. (2) Safety needs: security and protection from physical and emotional harm. (3) Social needs: affection, belongingness, acceptance and friendship. (4) Esteem needs: internal factors such as self-respect, autonomy and achievement, and external factors such as status, recognition and attention. (5) Self-actualization needs: the need to become what one is capable of becoming.

  21. Differentiate between Theory X and Theory Y.
    Topic: Motivation and Intrinsic Motivation

    Theory X is the assumption that employees dislike work, are lazy, avoid responsibility and must be coerced to perform. Theory Y is the assumption that workers are creative, enjoy work, seek responsibility and can exercise self-direction. These contrasting assumptions about human nature, proposed by McGregor, shape a manager's style of motivating and managing people.

  22. Explain Herzberg's motivation-hygiene theory.
    Topic: Motivation and Intrinsic Motivation

    Herzberg's motivation-hygiene theory proposes that intrinsic factors are related to job satisfaction and motivation, whereas extrinsic factors are associated with job dissatisfaction. Hygiene factors are the factors that eliminate job dissatisfaction but do not motivate. Motivators are the factors that increase job satisfaction and motivation; these factors are intrinsic to the job itself.

  23. Explain McClelland's three-needs theory.
    Topic: Motivation and Intrinsic Motivation

    McClelland's theory says that three needs - achievement, power and affiliation - are major motives in work. Need for achievement (nAch) is the drive to excel, to achieve in relation to a set of standards and to strive to succeed. Need for power (nPow) is the need to make others behave in a way that they would not have behaved otherwise. Need for affiliation (nAff) is the desire for friendly and close interpersonal relationships. Of these three needs, the need for achievement has been researched most extensively.

  24. Differentiate between job enlargement and job enrichment.
    Topic: Motivation and Intrinsic Motivation

    Job enlargement is the horizontal expansion of a job by increasing job scope - the number of different tasks required in a job and the frequency with which those tasks are repeated. Job enrichment is the vertical expansion of a job by adding planning and evaluating responsibilities, thereby increasing job depth - the degree of control employees have over their work.

  25. Explain Buber's three levels of communication.
    Topic: Communication, Transactional Analysis and NLP

    Buber distinguished three levels of communication. In I-It communication we treat others impersonally, almost as objects, and do not acknowledge the humanity of the other person. I-You communication accounts for the majority of our interactions: people acknowledge one another as more than objects but do not fully engage each other as unique individuals. I-Thou communication is the rarest kind of relationship and the highest form of human dialogue, in which each person affirms the other as cherished and unique and we meet others in their wholeness and individuality; Buber believed that only in I-Thou relationships do we become fully human.

  26. Differentiate between content meaning and relationship meaning.
    Topic: Communication, Transactional Analysis and NLP

    Interpersonal communication has two levels of meaning. The content meaning deals with the literal or denotative meaning of the message - for example, "clean your room now" means the room is to be cleaned. The relationship meaning refers to what communication expresses about the relationship between the communicators - the same command expresses that the parent has the right to order the child. Relationship-level meanings have three dimensions: responsiveness, liking (affection) and power or control.

  27. What is nonverbal communication? List its types.
    Topic: Communication, Transactional Analysis and NLP

    Nonverbal communication is all aspects of communication other than words themselves. It includes gestures and body language, how we utter words (inflection, pauses, tone, volume, accent), features of environments, personal objects, physical appearance and facial expressions. Scholars estimate that nonverbal behavior accounts for 65% to 93% of the total meaning of communication. Its nine types are: kinesics, haptics, physical appearance, artifacts, environmental factors, proxemics, chronemics, paralanguage and silence.

  28. Explain the three ego states in Transactional Analysis.
    Topic: Communication, Transactional Analysis and NLP

    Transactional analysis (TA), developed by Eric Berne, identifies three ego states. In the Parent ego state a person behaves as a parent - as a Critical Parent (evaluative, critical, judgmental, demanding, disciplining) or as a Sympathetic Parent (protecting, permitting, consoling, caring, nurturing). In the Child ego state a person behaves as a Natural Child (curiosity, intimacy, fun, joyfulness, impulsiveness) or as an Adapted Child (rebelliousness, pouting, anger, fear, anxiety, blaming others). In the Adult ego state a person behaves in a thinking, rational, calculating, factual and unemotional manner, gathering information and estimating probabilities. Generally, the most effective behavior, human relations and performance come from the Adult ego state.

  29. Differentiate between complementary, crossed and ulterior transactions.
    Topic: Communication, Transactional Analysis and NLP

    A complementary transaction occurs when the sender of the message gets the intended response from the receiver; such transactions generally result in more effective communication with fewer hurt feelings and arguments. A crossed transaction occurs when the sender does not get the expected response from the receiver; it results in surprise, disappointment and hurt feelings and often ends in arguments. An ulterior (hidden) transaction occurs when the words seem to be coming from one ego state but in reality the words or behaviors are coming from another ego state.

  30. What are submodalities in NLP? Give examples for each modality.
    Topic: Communication, Transactional Analysis and NLP

    Submodalities are the special elements of pictures, sounds or other sensations that can be changed in order to increase or decrease the intensity of our experience. Visual submodalities include size, color, brightness, distance and movement. Auditory submodalities include volume, tempo, pitch and tonality. Kinesthetic submodalities include temperature, texture, breathing and weight or pressure. By manipulating submodalities we can change how we feel about virtually anything - for example, brightening a mental image generally intensifies the emotion attached to it.

  31. What is organizational democracy? What are its principles?
    Topic: Leadership, Groups, Teams and Human Development

    Organizational democracy means that members of an organization participate in the processes of organizing and governance. In summary, its principles are: participative management practices; increased "voice" of employees; and focus on change. It is argued that increased employee voice leads to higher organizational commitment, better implementation of decisions, reduced dysfunctional behavior and upgraded skills, ultimately improving organizational performance and productivity.

  32. Differentiate between transactional and transformational leadership.
    Topic: Leadership, Groups, Teams and Human Development

    Transactional leadership is primarily task-focused: transactional leaders motivate individuals primarily through contingent-reward exchanges - they set goals, articulate explicit agreements regarding what the leader expects from organizational members and how they will be rewarded, and provide constructive feedback to keep everyone on task; they operate within the existing system. Transformational leadership is primarily people-focused: transformational leaders are charismatic, inspirational, intellectually stimulating and individually considerate; they articulate a vision, inspire others and appeal to higher ideals and moral values such as liberty, justice and equality, helping individuals transcend their self-interest for the sake of the larger vision of the firm.

  33. Describe the five stages of group development.
    Topic: Leadership, Groups, Teams and Human Development

    (1) Forming: the first stage, in which people join the group and define the group's purpose, structure and leadership. (2) Storming: the second stage, characterized by intragroup conflict. (3) Norming: the third stage, characterized by close relationships and cohesiveness. (4) Performing: the fourth stage, in which the group is fully functional and performs. (5) Adjourning: the final stage for temporary groups, during which members are concerned with wrapping up activities rather than task performance.

  34. Name and describe the types of work teams.
    Topic: Leadership, Groups, Teams and Human Development

    (1) Functional team: a manager and his or her subordinates from a particular functional area. (2) Self-managed team: a team that operates without a manager and is responsible for a complete work process or segment. (3) Virtual team: a team that uses computer technology to link physically dispersed members in order to achieve a common goal. (4) Cross-functional team: a hybrid grouping of individuals who are experts in various specialties and who work together on various tasks.

  35. What is the Human Development Index (HDI)? How is it calculated?
    Topic: Leadership, Groups, Teams and Human Development

    The Human Development Index (HDI) is a comparative measure of life expectancy, literacy, education and standard of living for countries worldwide, used to indicate whether a country is developed, developing or underdeveloped. It was developed in 1990 by Indian Nobel prize winner Amartya Sen and Pakistani economist Mahbub ul Haq, and is used by the UNDP in its annual Human Development Report. The HDI is the average of three indices: the Life Expectancy Index (life expectancy at birth, with minimum 25 and maximum 85 years); the Education Index (the adult literacy rate with two-thirds weight plus the combined gross enrollment ratio with one-third weight); and the GDP Index (the log of GDP per capita at PPP, from $100 to $40,000). An HDI of 0.8 or more represents high development, while below 0.5 represents low development.

  36. What is the Gini coefficient?
    Topic: Leadership, Groups, Teams and Human Development

    The Gini coefficient is a measure of inequality of income distribution or inequality of wealth distribution, developed by the Italian statistician Corrado Gini and published in his 1912 paper. It is defined as a ratio with values between 0 and 1, based on the Lorenz curve: a low Gini coefficient indicates more equal distribution, 0 corresponds to perfect equality (everyone has the same income) and 1 corresponds to perfect inequality (one person has all the income). The Gini index is the Gini coefficient expressed as a percentage. The coefficient satisfies four principles: anonymity, scale independence, population independence and the transfer principle.
