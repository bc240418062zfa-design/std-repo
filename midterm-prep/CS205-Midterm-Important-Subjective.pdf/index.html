  1. What is Information Security? Give the SANS definition of Information Security.
    Topic: Information Security Fundamentals

    According to SANS, Information Security means protecting information and information systems from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification or destruction.

  2. Differentiate between IT Security and Information Security.
    Topic: Information Security Fundamentals

    Information Technology Security (IT Security) is information security applied to technology; it is a part of Information Security. Its functions are: Network Security, System Security, Mobile Security, and Application & Database Security. Information Security is broader: besides technology it also covers physical security, human resource security, legal & compliance, and organizational and process-related aspects, such as Governance, Policies & Procedures, Risk Management and Performance Reviews.

  3. What is Cyber Security?
    Topic: Information Security Fundamentals

    Cyber Security is protecting information and information systems from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification or destruction through the internet. In other words, the prevention of crime related to the internet is taken care of with the help of cyber security.

  4. What are the three pillars (main parts) of Information Security? Explain each. (Long question)
    Topic: Information Security Fundamentals

    1. Confidentiality — keeping information secret; information must not be disclosed to a third/unauthorized person ("don't out the secrets"). 2. Integrity — keeping information in its original form; do not add any illegal or irrelevant data. 3. Availability — keeping information and information systems available for use whenever needed.

  5. Which combination can make Information Security?
    Topic: Information Security Fundamentals

    Information Security is a combination of People, Process and Technology.

  6. What are the challenges of Information Security?
    Topic: Information Security Fundamentals

    There are three challenges of information security: 1. Lack of ownership; 2. Department silos; 3. Missing security hardening.

  7. Who is Information Security for?
    Topic: Information Security Fundamentals

    1. Personal use — e.g. social media passwords and safe usage, online banking and email passwords, home PC/laptop and mobile security. 2. Organizational use — the Board and executive leadership; the CISO (Chief Information Security Officer), who is responsible to drive security programs; IT staff and business users. 3. Government and National departments — law enforcement, legal and policy making; national databases, critical infrastructure, regulation, standards and certifications, capacity-building and coordination.

  8. Why is Information Security needed? Explain with the help of famous attack examples.
    Topic: Information Security Fundamentals

    The need for information security is shown by real incidents: (1) Bangladesh Bank SWIFT Hack — February 2016: hackers used the SWIFT credentials of a Bangladesh Central Bank employee to send more than three dozen fraudulent money transfer requests; USD 81 million was stolen, and the total impact could have been USD 1 billion. (2) NHS Cyberattack — May 2017: described as the biggest ransomware outbreak in history; the ransomware attack hit 99 countries, with UK hospitals among the targets. Such incidents show why information and information systems must be protected.

  9. Write two characteristics of Information.
    Topic: Questions from Current Midterm Papers (Unsolved in the Sources) • 3 marks marks

    Two important characteristics of information are: 1. Accuracy — information must be correct and free from errors, because decisions are made on the basis of information. 2. Timeliness — information must be up-to-date and available at the time it is needed; outdated information loses its value. (Other characteristics commonly listed are relevance, completeness and accessibility.)

  10. Write two characteristics of Risk. (This question appeared in two different midterm papers.)
    Topic: Questions from Current Midterm Papers (Unsolved in the Sources) • 3 marks marks

    1. Uncertainty — a risk is an uncertain future event; it may or may not happen. 2. Potential loss/impact — if the risk does occur, it causes loss or harm to the organization's assets; therefore risk is measured through the likelihood (probability) of the event and the impact it would cause.

  11. Explain Non-repudiation with examples.
    Topic: Questions from Current Midterm Papers (Unsolved in the Sources) • 5 marks marks

    Non-repudiation is the security service that prevents a party from denying a transaction or communication afterwards. The sender cannot deny having sent the message, and the receiver cannot deny having received it, because proof of origin and delivery is available. Example: when a sender digitally signs an email or a contract, the signature proves who sent it, so the sender cannot later deny it — just as a registered-post receipt proves the delivery of a letter.

  12. Explain Cost Benefit Analysis and write its formula.
    Topic: Questions from Current Midterm Papers (Unsolved in the Sources) • 5 marks marks

    Cost Benefit Analysis (CBA) is used to decide whether a security control is worth implementing: the cost of the control is compared with the loss it will prevent. If the benefit of the control is greater than its cost, the control should be implemented. Formula: CBA = (ALE before the control − ALE after the control) − Cost of the control, where ALE (Annualized Loss Expectancy) = SLE × ARO. A positive result means the control is cost-effective.

  13. Explain the inside and outside attacks.
    Topic: Questions from Current Midterm Papers (Unsolved in the Sources) • 5 marks marks

    An inside attack is carried out by a person inside the organization — e.g. an employee or another trusted user — who already has authorized access to the systems and misuses it (stealing data, damaging systems, etc.). An outside attack is carried out by an external person (an outside hacker) who has no authorized access and attacks the organization's systems from outside, e.g. through the internet. Inside attacks are considered more dangerous because the attacker already knows the systems and has legitimate access.

  14. What is meant by Security? Also write the three goals of Security.
    Topic: Questions from Current Midterm Papers (Unsolved in the Sources) • 5 marks marks

    Security means protecting information and information systems from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification or destruction. The three goals of security are the three pillars of information security: 1. Confidentiality — keeping information secret from unauthorized persons; 2. Integrity — keeping information in its original form; 3. Availability — keeping information and information systems available for use.

  15. What is Risk Management? Also write three types of Risk Management.
    Topic: Questions from Current Midterm Papers (Unsolved in the Sources) • 5 marks marks

    Risk Management is the process of identifying, assessing and controlling the risks to an organization's information assets, so that the loss from security incidents is reduced to an acceptable level. Three types (treatments) of risk management are: 1. Risk Avoidance — removing the activity or asset that creates the risk; 2. Risk Mitigation (Reduction) — reducing the likelihood or impact of the risk by implementing security controls; 3. Risk Transfer — shifting the risk to a third party, e.g. through insurance or outsourcing. (Risk Acceptance — knowingly accepting the risk — is also commonly listed.)

  16. How to secure a system? Why do projects fail?
    Topic: Questions from Current Midterm Papers (Unsolved in the Sources) • 5 marks marks

    A system is secured by applying layered security controls: security hardening of IT assets, regular patching and vulnerability management, access control, antivirus and firewalls, monitoring, and security validation, together with security policies and user awareness. Projects (security transformation projects) fail mainly because of the challenges of information security — lack of ownership, department silos and missing security hardening — along with lack of management support and poor planning.

  17. What is Business Continuity Management?
    Topic: Questions from Current Midterm Papers (Unsolved in the Sources)

    Business Continuity Management (BCM) is the management process that prepares an organization to continue delivering its products or services at acceptable, predefined levels following a disruptive incident. It includes identifying potential threats, making business continuity plans, and arranging disaster recovery, so that mission-critical functions can be maintained or quickly resumed after a disaster. (Business Continuity itself is defined in the Qalb-e-Momin file — see the question "What is Business Continuity (BC)?" in this document.)

  18. What is the significance of the Vulnerability Management information model?
    Topic: Questions from Current Midterm Papers (Unsolved in the Sources)

    The Vulnerability Management information model organizes the information used in the VM lifecycle — information about IT assets, the vulnerabilities found in them, scan results and patch status. Its significance is that it gives a complete and structured view of where the weaknesses are, helps in assessing and prioritizing the scan results, and supports decisions about patching systems and verifying them (re-scan), so that vulnerabilities are managed systematically instead of randomly.

  19. Enlist any five names of security services provided by the OSI Security Architecture.
    Topic: Questions from Current Midterm Papers (Unsolved in the Sources)

    The OSI Security Architecture (X.800) provides these security services: 1. Authentication; 2. Access Control; 3. Data Confidentiality; 4. Data Integrity; 5. Non-repudiation. (Availability is also listed as a security service.)

  20. Enlist any five basic tracks of a Hardening Strategy.
    Topic: Questions from Current Midterm Papers (Unsolved in the Sources)

    A hardening strategy is applied along these basic tracks: 1. Operating system / server hardening; 2. Workstation and end-user device hardening; 3. Network devices and infrastructure hardening; 4. Application and software hardening; 5. Database and data hardening. On every track, the IT assets are configured to maximize security and minimize security risks.

  21. What is the first step in the automated mechanism of Security Hardening Validation?
    Topic: Questions from Current Midterm Papers (Unsolved in the Sources)

    The first step is the automated scanning/discovery of the IT asset: an automated (SCAP-validated) scanner collects the current configuration of the system or device, which is then compared automatically against the applicable hardening checklist/benchmark (e.g. CIS/DISA STIG) to confirm whether the security controls have actually been implemented correctly.

  37. What is a Patch?
    Topic: Security Hardening, Vulnerability Management, Patching, Validation & Testing

    A patch is a piece of software designed to update a computer program or its supporting data, to fix or improve it. This includes fixing security vulnerabilities and other bugs.

  40. Who conducts Security Validation?

  53. Differentiate between Remote Exploit and Local Exploit.
    Topic: Enterprise Security, Networks & Software Security

Local Exploit: A local exploit requires prior access to the vulnerable system and usually increases the privileges of the person running the exploit past those granted by the system administrator.

  60. What is Security Engineering?
    Topic: Additional Important Subjective Questions (Created from Course Topics)

    Security Engineering is the third layer of the Security Transformation Model. It consists of more in-depth and complicated security activities which take more time and effort, and is many times related to security architecture. Types of activities for security engineering: FW granular access lists; building an effective DMZ architecture; segregating the network with VLANs; adding a security tool such as SIEM, FW, DLP, NAC, etc.; and App-DB encryption.

  61. What is the objective of Security Engineering?
    Topic: Additional Important Subjective Questions (Created from Course Topics)

    Security architecture as per best practices; the right security devices in the right places; effective security configuration of security devices (features); optimum operation of security devices; and aggregate controls. Examples: FW first and then IPS; edge FW and data center FW; malware protection at the network edge.

  62. What is Patch Management? Write its tasks and the risk of not patching.
    Topic: Additional Important Subjective Questions (Created from Course Topics)

    Patch management is an area of systems management that involves acquiring, testing, and installing multiple patches (code changes) to an administered computer system. Patch management tasks: maintaining current knowledge of available patches; deciding what patches are appropriate for particular systems; ensuring that patches are installed properly; testing systems after installation; and documenting all associated procedures, such as specific configurations required. Risk of not patching: by not applying a patch you might be leaving the door open for a malware attack, because malware exploits flaws in a system in order to do its work; in addition, the timeframe between an exploit and when a patch is released is getting shorter.

  63. Write the key leadership qualities of the Information Security Head.
    Topic: Additional Important Subjective Questions (Created from Course Topics)

    The key leadership qualities of the Information Security Head (the key resource driving the Security Transformation Program) are: Authenticity; Candidness; Fairness & fair play; Team environment; Recognizing talent and hard work; and Celebrating success. Authenticity — IT is complex and no one person "knows-it-all"; communicate that each individual has limitations, admit mistakes and failures, and give credit where it is due. Candidness — call a spade a spade; honesty and straight-talk; hear feedback and give respect to the views of everyone. Fairness & Fair Play — promote performance and merit; adjust players in the right positions based on their strengths; coach and guide the team to perform and achieve results. Team Environment — discourage solo-flight and promote team consensus, team reviews, and team achievements; single out and coach individuals playing turf tactics. Recognize Talent & Hard Work — identify self-promotion versus talent combined with hard work, and encourage hard workers who are team players. Celebrate Success — hold team celebrations; recognize quiet workers and background workers as well; promote team achievements.
